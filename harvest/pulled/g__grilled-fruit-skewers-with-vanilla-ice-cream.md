This is a car-camping dessert because of the ice cream, and there is no honest way around that. Everything else here is trail-simple: fruit, a grate, five minutes. The heat does the work by driving off water at the cut surfaces and caramelizing the sugar already in the fruit, which is why the chunks need to be big. Small pieces cook through before the outside browns and you end up with warm fruit salad on a stick.

Honey goes on at the end, not the start. Sugar on the grate over direct heat blackens fast, so a minute of melting is all it gets. Firm nectarines matter more than ripe ones — a soft nectarine tears when you push a skewer through it and drops into the fire the first time you turn it.
