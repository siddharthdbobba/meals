Red lentils are the one legume that cooks on trail fuel. Split, they break down in about ten minutes and thicken their own water, so you never drain anything and never carry a strainer. Couscous asks for nothing but boiled water and a lid, which is why the two go together: one ingredient does the cooking, the other does the waiting.

The split into two pots is about texture, not effort. Cook the couscous in with the lentils and it turns to paste. Keep it separate and you get a dry grain under a wet stew, which holds up better when the whole thing has gone cold halfway through eating. Add salt last — bouillon carries more than you expect, and lentils salted early stay stubborn.
