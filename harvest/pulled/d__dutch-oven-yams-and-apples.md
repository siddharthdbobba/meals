This is a car-camping dish and it stays one. Whole roots and apples are heavy, and there is no dehydrated version of this that still works — the point is slices that hold their shape and soften in cider, which powdered ingredients cannot do. If you are carrying it on your back, cook something else.

The layering matters more than it looks. Cider poured over each quarter of the mixture means the liquid works down through all of it instead of pooling at the bottom and steaming one layer while the top dries out. The oil in the bottom of the oven is doing one job: keeping the first layer of yam from welding itself to the cast iron. Yams and sweet potatoes cook at about the same rate; the apples go softer, which is what thickens the whole thing by the end.
