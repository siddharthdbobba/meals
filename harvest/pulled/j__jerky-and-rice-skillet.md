Home cooks eat wild game two or three nights a week and mostly do it with a freezer, a skillet, and gravy. None of that travels. Jerky is the version of that meat that already survives without a fridge, so the trail recipe starts there and works backward: the drying is the preservation, and hot water puts some of the chew back.

The order matters more than anything else here. Jerky rehydrates slowly and instant rice rehydrates fast, so the meat, mushrooms, and onion get four minutes of actual simmering before the rice ever touches the pot. Skip that and you get soft rice with leather in it. The bouillon and the oil are doing what pan drippings would do at home, which is give the whole thing fat and salt so it reads as a meal instead of wet grain.
