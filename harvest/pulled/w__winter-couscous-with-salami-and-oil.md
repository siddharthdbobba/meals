Couscous is the fastest hot dinner that exists on a stove. It rehydrates off residual heat, so you burn fuel for one boil and nothing else. That matters in cold weather, when the same canister has to melt or warm water for everything else too.

The fat and the cured meat are the point. Plain couscous is close to empty calories and goes cold in your bowl in about four minutes. Oil slows that down and roughly doubles the energy in the pot. Dry-packed sun-dried tomatoes go in with the water rather than at the end, since they need the full five minutes to stop being leather.
