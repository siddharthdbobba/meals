Bannock is a fire bread with no yeast and no rise time, which is why it survived a century of canoe trips. Baking powder does the lifting the moment water hits it, so the dough goes straight from bag to pan. Cutting the fat into the flour first coats the starch and keeps the crumb short instead of tough.

The trail version swaps milk for milk powder and butter for shortening or ghee, both of which sit happily in a food barrel for a month. Low heat is the only real skill involved. Bannock cooked fast is charcoal on the outside and paste in the middle, and there is no fixing it once it gets there.
