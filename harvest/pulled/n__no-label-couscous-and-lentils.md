Everything here is a single ingredient with a name you already know: a legume, a grain, a dried vegetable, oil, salt, spice. That is the whole point. Packaged trail dinners lean on emulsifiers, hydrogenated fat, and flavor enhancers to survive a warehouse; dry lentils and couscous survive on their own and cost a fraction of a retort pouch.

The timing is what makes it one pot. Red lentils break down in about eight minutes, and couscous only needs to sit in hot water, so the lentils get the boil and the couscous gets the residual heat. Add the oil at the end rather than the beginning — it carries the spice and the calories, and stirring it into cold-ish food keeps it from going flat over the flame.
