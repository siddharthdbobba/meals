This is car-camping cooking, not trail cooking, and it stays that way on purpose. Fresh okra is the whole point of the dish and there is no shelf-stable version of it worth eating. Everything here rides in a cooler and gets cut at the trailhead table.

The roux is the only part that can fail. Oil and flour cooked past blond is what gives gumbo its color and body, and coals under a dutch oven run hotter than a home burner, so stand there and stir. Adding the water slowly matters for the same reason: dumped in all at once it seizes the roux into lumps you will not get out later. The okra thickens the rest on its own over the 30 minute simmer, which is why the chicken goes in last and only for 20 minutes.
