Retort-packed cooked beef is the one shelf-stable meat that still eats like meat. It sits on a shelf for a year and a half, weighs a pound in flavor and about ten ounces on the scale, and needs no cooking at all. That last part is what makes this work: nothing in the pot has to reach a safe temperature, so the whole meal rides on one pot of hot water.

The bean flakes are doing the structural work. Rice and beef alone leave you with wet grains and grease; the flakes bind the liquid and turn it into something a spoon can hold. Add them slowly, because they thicken late and there is no fixing an over-thick pot without more water and another boil. This is not a lightweight dinner, so it belongs in a boat, on a bike, or at a car camp where nine ounces of dinner is not an argument.
