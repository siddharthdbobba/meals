This works because a potato is mostly water. The flesh steams before it scorches, and the wall between the coals and the egg acts as a buffer, so the egg sets slowly instead of hitting the pan-hot temperatures that make it rubbery. The skin chars, and that char is what you handle rather than eat.

It is a car-camping and basecamp recipe, not a backpacking one. Raw eggs and whole potatoes are heavy and fragile, and the payoff is a half hour of fire tending. What you get in return is no pot, no pan, and no washing up. Don't throw out the scooped flesh — fry it in the morning or bury it alongside in foil.
