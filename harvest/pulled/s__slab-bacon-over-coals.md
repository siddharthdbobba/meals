Slab bacon is worth the extra work over the pre-sliced kind. You cut it as thick as you want, which means the fat renders slowly instead of flashing off, and a slab holds up in a pack better than a floppy vacuum pack does. Cut the slices at night by lantern so the morning is only fire and food.

The fat is the second half of the recipe. Dried hash browns want a fatty pan to brown in, and the bacon hands you exactly that, so nothing gets carried in a bottle and nothing gets poured into the fire. Soak the potatoes cold while the coals form; they take twenty minutes to soften and you are not doing anything else with that time. This one rides in a cooler or a cold pack for the first days out.
