steps:
  - "At home: brown the beef in a nonstick pan with no added oil. Drain it, then blot the pan and the meat with paper towels until nothing looks greasy."
  - "Add the onion, carrot, pepper, and garlic. Cook until soft. Stir in the tomato paste, herbs, salt, and a cup of water. Simmer ten minutes."
  - "Boil the pasta two minutes past the package time. It should be soft. Drain and rinse cold, then fold it into the sauce."
  - "Spread the whole mix thin on lined dehydrator trays. Dry at 145F for 6 to 8 hours, breaking up clumps once an hour, until the meat crumbles and nothing is tacky. Cool fully, then bag."
  - "At camp: pour the bag into the pot with 400 ml of water. Let it sit ten minutes cold, then bring it to a boil."
  - "Kill the heat, cover, and wait five minutes. Stir in the oil and crumble the cheese over the top."

Drying an assembled meal beats drying six ingredients and reassembling them in the dirt. The sauce coats everything as it dries, so the pasta and the beef take water back at close to the same rate. Dry the parts separately and you get soft vegetables next to gravel.

The lean beef and the empty nonstick pan are the whole trick. Fat does not dry, it just sits in the mix and goes off, and a rancid bag is a wasted evening four days from a road. So the fat comes along in a bottle and goes in at the last minute, where it does more for the calories anyway. The cold soak before the boil matters too: dry pasta hitting boiling water scorches on the bottom of a thin pot.
