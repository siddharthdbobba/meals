The source of this one is a deer, a grinder, and a Saturday in the garage. Ground venison alone will not make sausage — it is too lean to bind or to taste like much — so roughly 40% fatty pork butt goes into the grind, plus a cure and seasoning, before it is stuffed and dried. That work happens at home months earlier. What you carry is the finished log, which is shelf-stable because it was cured and dried, not because it is cold.

On the trail it becomes one pot. The potato flakes do the thickening, and simmering the coins first pulls the pork fat into the water so it ends up in the mash instead of sitting on top. Mustard and caraway are there because game sausage is a strong flavor and blunt potatoes will otherwise swallow it. If you buy the sausage instead of making it, read the label and skip anything with breadcrumb filler.
