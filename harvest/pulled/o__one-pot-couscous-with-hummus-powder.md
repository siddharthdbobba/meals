Couscous is the only grain that cooks on residual heat. It is already steamed and dried at the factory, so hot water alone finishes it, which means the stove runs for two minutes instead of ten. On a long trip that difference is measured in canisters you do not have to buy.

The hummus powder is doing two jobs. It carries most of the protein, and its chickpea starch binds the loose grain into something that stays on a spoon instead of scattering. The oil goes in last, off the heat, because it coats the couscous better once the water is gone. Everything here is dry-packed and lives out of a fridge indefinitely, so the same bag works on night one or night nine.
