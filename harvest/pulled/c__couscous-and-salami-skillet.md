Couscous is pre-steamed and dried at the factory, so it does not need to simmer. Boil water, add grain, walk away. That means one short burn on the stove instead of the ten to fifteen minutes rice or pasta wants, which matters when you are rationing fuel at the end of a long stretch.

The fat is the point. Couscous alone is thin, quick calories that leave you hungry by dark, so the salami, cheese, and oil are what make this a dinner rather than a side dish. Dry-cured salami keeps for days out of a fridge, and the sun-dried tomatoes rehydrate in the same water as the grain, which is why they go in during the boil rather than after.
