This started as a car-camping soup eaten with fresh bannock. On the trail the bread is the part that costs you: a bake means a second pot, more fuel, and standing outside in the weather. The soup alone holds up, so long as it is thick enough to eat with a spoon rather than drink.

Quinoa is the reason it works. It cooks through in about twelve minutes, thickens the broth on its own as the grains open, and brings real protein without meat. Fresh spinach is water and leaf, so the dried version loses almost nothing here. The oil goes in off the heat, at the end, where it stays a fat and not a fuel.
