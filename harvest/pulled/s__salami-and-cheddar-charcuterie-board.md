Dry-cured sausage is shelf-stable for the same reason it was invented: salt and time pulled the water out, so there is not enough left for bacteria to work with. The "keep refrigerated" label on the package is about texture and legal caution, not safety. A firm salami chub hanging in a warm deli case is already living the life it will live in your pack.

The rest of the board is chosen the same way. Aged hard cheese has low moisture and will weep oil in heat but not spoil; fresh cheese will not survive an afternoon. Mustard packets and dried fruit add acid and sugar against three ounces of salt and fat, which is the difference between a lunch and a chore. Cut only what you are about to eat — an uncut chub keeps for a week of summer, a sliced one does not.
