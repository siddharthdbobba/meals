Cold nights punish thin dinners. This one is built around potato flakes because they turn a pot of broth into something with structure, and structure holds heat far longer than soup does. The milk powder is doing the chowder work — whole milk powder, not skim, since the fat is half the point and there is no fridge involved.

Order matters more than technique. Milk powder goes in while the water is still loose, or it seizes into lumps you will not stir out with a titanium spoon. The corn needs its two minutes off the heat before the starch goes in, since nothing rehydrates once the pot has thickened. Fried onions on top are the only crisp thing in the bowl, so keep them out of the bag until the end.
