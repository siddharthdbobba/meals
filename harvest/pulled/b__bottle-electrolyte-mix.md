Sugar in a hydration bladder is a mistake you make once. The hose and bite valve never fully rinse in the field, and a sweet film in a warm reservoir grows something furry within a day or two. So the bladder carries plain water and this mix goes in a separate bottle, where you can see the inside and scrub it with a finger.

The ratio is doing the work. Roughly four percent carbohydrate and 500 to 700 mg of sodium per liter empties from the stomach about as fast as plain water does, so the calories arrive without sloshing. Push the sugar much higher and the drink starts pulling water into your gut instead of out of it. The citric acid is not decoration either: without acid the salt reads as salty, and with it the whole thing reads as lemonade.
