Small pots punish anything that needs a simmer. Two cups of capacity boils over fast, and a thin stainless base scorches starch the second it sticks. Couscous sidesteps both problems: it cooks entirely off the heat, absorbing water from a single boil under a lid. Fuel use is one short burn, and the pot comes clean with a wipe because nothing ever cooked against the metal.

The ratio is the whole recipe. Roughly equal volumes of couscous and water, plus a little extra to cover what the dried vegetables drink back. Too much water and it goes to paste; too little and the grain stays gritty in the middle. Oil and cheese go in after the lid comes off, since fat added early coats the grains and slows the rehydration you are counting on.
