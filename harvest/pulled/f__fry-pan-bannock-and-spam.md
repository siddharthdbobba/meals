Bannock is a bread built for the constraint set: flour, chemical leavening, water, and a pan. No yeast, no rise time, no oven. The baking powder does its work as soon as the dough gets hot, so the whole thing lives or dies on heat control. A hard fire scorches the crust and leaves raw dough in the middle. Keep the pan over the edge of the coals, not the center.

Cook the meat first for the fat, not for the order of eating. Tinned pork renders enough grease to fry the bread in, and that fat is where most of the calories and all of the browning come from. A cold-handle pan makes this easier over a fire, but any skillet works if you have a glove.
