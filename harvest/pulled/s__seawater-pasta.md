A cup of seawater carries roughly two teaspoons of salt, which is close to what you would throw into a pasta pot anyway. On a boat or a coastal paddle that means every pot of pasta costs you nothing from the fresh tanks except the top three-quarters, and nothing from the salt supply at all. Dilution matters. Straight seawater is too much for pasta that you cook and then drain, and far too much for anything that absorbs its water.

Cut the boil time by about a quarter. Salted water and a pot that keeps working after you kill the stove push pasta past done faster than you expect. Everything else here is shelf-stable on purpose: no fresh dairy, no cheese to sweat, and the fat comes from oil so it survives a hot deck. Skip the beans entirely. Salt early keeps the skins from softening and no amount of extra fuel fixes it.
