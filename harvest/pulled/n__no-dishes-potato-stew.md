This started as an argument about dishwashing, not dinner. The answer that kept coming up on canoe trips was to stop making dishes in the first place: cook one rehydrated thing, eat it out of the pot it was cooked in, then rinse with an ounce of water and drink the rinse. No soap, no sump hole, no gray water to carry away from camp.

That only works if the food is built for it. Potato flakes and gravy powder release from a pot wall with one wet swipe, where anything with cheese, oil-fried meat, or scorched sugar does not. The oil goes in off the heat and last, so it never bakes onto the metal. Add the flakes gradually — dumped all at once they clump, and the dry pockets in the middle are what make you want a scrubber.
