Couscous is the fastest hot dinner there is. It does not cook so much as rehydrate, so one pot of boiling water and five minutes under a lid is the whole method. In cold weather that matters more than taste: less fuel burned, less time standing around with the stove going, less chance the pot goes lukewarm before the grain softens.

The oil and the salami are the calories. Couscous alone is a light, dry pile that will leave you cold by midnight. Dry-cured salami keeps for days without a fridge and brings fat and salt with it. Use dry-packed sun-dried tomatoes, not the ones in oil — the jarred kind are heavy and will soak through a bag.
