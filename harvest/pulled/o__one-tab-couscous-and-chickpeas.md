Couscous is the only grain that cooks itself off the heat, which is the whole point here. One boil, lid on, done. A solid fuel tab burns for about eight minutes and gives you no throttle and no second chance, so a recipe that needs simmering is the wrong recipe for it. This one only asks the fuel to get water hot once.

The chickpea flour is doing two jobs. It turns the water into something closer to a sauce than a soak, and it pushes the protein up past twenty grams without a meat packet. Pre-soaking the vegetable flakes in cold water before lighting the stove matters more than it sounds: dropped into boiling water they stay leathery, and the pot goes cold before they soften.
