Real pho gets its depth from bones simmered for hours. You cannot carry that. What you can carry is the spice profile — star anise, cinnamon, coriander, clove — and a bouillon base strong enough to hold it up. That combination lands close enough that the pot tastes like the thing it is imitating.

The structure matters more than the substitutions. Herbs and lime go in off the heat, because boiling them flattens everything that makes the dish read as pho. Thin rice vermicelli cooks in residual heat alone, so you spend one boil and no fuel on the noodles. The tablespoon of oil is doing calorie work: without it this is a light dinner, around 500, which is thin for a day of walking.
