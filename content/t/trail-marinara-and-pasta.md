The original is a fresh-tomato sauce: dice, sauté, done in the time the pasta boils. None of that survives a pack. Tomato powder does the same job by weight — it is the same tomato with the water removed, and the water is the part you are carrying anyway. Sun-dried tomatoes go in early because they need real soaking time, and they give the sauce texture that powder alone can't.

Cooking the pasta in just enough water is what makes this one pot instead of two. The starch that would normally go down the drain stays behind and thickens the sauce. Add the tomato powder off the heat — boiled hard it scorches on the pot bottom and turns bitter. The oil goes in last and stays whole, which is where about a third of the calories live.
