Home shrimp creole starts with a roux and a long simmer, neither of which fits in a 750 ml pot. The roux is gone here. Flour thickens by scorching against the bottom of a thin titanium pot, and it costs a second dish. Instead the tomato powder and the starch off the instant rice carry the body, and the oil goes in at the end so the fat still reads as fat.

Timing is the whole recipe. Dried vegetables want a real simmer; shrimp want almost none. Cook them together and you get crunchy celery next to rubber. Rehydrating the vegetables cold for ten minutes before the stove goes on saves fuel and shortens the simmer. The bay leaf is worth its weightless self.
