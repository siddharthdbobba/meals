The original is a canning recipe: fresh berries, jars, a gel test, and a pectin set you check on a frozen plate. None of that travels. Dried cranberries do the same job in reverse — instead of cooking water out of fresh fruit, you cook a measured amount of water in and stop before it goes past sauce.

Cranberry pectin still does the thickening, so the sauce firms up noticeably as the pot cools. That is the reason to pull it early. Dried orange peel and apple juice powder carry the acid and background sweetness that the juice and fresh zest provided, and they weigh almost nothing.
