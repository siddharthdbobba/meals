The original is a slow cooker roast: a butter-and-spice paste smeared over a whole boneless breast, four hours on low, onions underneath to keep it off the metal. None of that survives a trip. What does survive is the rub, which is where the flavor was anyway, and the pairing of turkey with soft onion.

So the rub goes in dry and the meat comes freeze-dried. Potato flakes replace the pan juices, giving the spices something to cling to and turning a thin rehydration broth into a dinner with weight. The ghee is not optional. Freeze-dried turkey is nearly fat-free, and without a couple of tablespoons of fat this eats like a bowl of salt.
