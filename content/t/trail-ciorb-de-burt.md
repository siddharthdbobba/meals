Tripe soup is a long simmer, sour cream, and enough garlic to be a statement. Two of those three travel. The tripe does not, so this uses pouch beef and leans harder on the parts that actually define the bowl: the acid and the garlic. Potato flakes stand in for the gelatin a real bone broth would give you, thickening the pot in thirty seconds instead of three hours.

The two things to get right are heat and order. Sour cream powder whisked into boiling broth goes grainy, so kill the flame and wait. Vinegar goes in off the heat as well, since boiling drives the sharpness off and sharpness is the point. Add it in stages and stop when the soup tastes sour rather than salty.
