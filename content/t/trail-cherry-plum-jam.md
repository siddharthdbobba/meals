The original is a Romanian preserve made from cherry plums in the two weeks they are ripe, cooked down with sugar until it sets. None of that survives a pack. What does survive is the shape of it: sour fruit, sugar, and enough boiling to drive off water.

Dried plums are already half of the work done for you, so the pot time is about rehydrating rather than reducing. The citric acid matters more than it looks. Dried fruit loses the sharpness that made the fresh version worth eating, and without it you get sweet mush. Sugar going in late keeps the pot from scorching over a hot stove.
