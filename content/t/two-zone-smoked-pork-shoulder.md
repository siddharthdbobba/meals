Smoking is not a flavor you add, it is a temperature you refuse to exceed. Hold the meat near 250 F for hours and the connective tissue in a cheap shoulder breaks down into gelatin. Run it hotter and the same cut turns into rope. A two-zone fire is how you get that at a campsite: all the coals on one side, the meat on the other, lid on, vent pulling smoke over the top.

The two parts people skip are the thermometer and the rest. Time is a bad proxy here because every fire and every shoulder is different, so cook to 200 F internal and ignore the clock. Then let it sit wrapped for an hour. An empty cooler makes a fine holding box, and the meat comes out wet instead of dry.
