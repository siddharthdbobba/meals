The American version of this dish leans on heavy cream, which is a non-starter in a pack. The original never had cream in it. It was butter, hard cheese, and starchy pasta water whisked into an emulsion, which happens to be exactly what a camp kitchen can produce. Nothing here needs a fridge except the meat, and a pouch solves that.

The sauce lives or dies on two things: heat and water. Add cheese to a pot still on the flame and it seizes into strings. Pull the pot, add cheese in stages, and thin with the water you saved. Dried herbs stand in for fresh; rosemary is the only one worth crushing first, since whole needles never soften.

If you carried out fresh wild turkey breast, cook it the first night. Cut it small, sear it hot, and do not trust it past day one without a cooler.
