The original is a garden recipe: fresh juice cooked down, cream stirred in off the heat. Neither travels. Tomato powder is that reduction already done, and full-fat milk powder plus a little olive oil does what the cream did, which is round off the acid and add fat the soup otherwise has none of.

Order matters more than technique here. Tomato powder clumps if it hits water all at once, and milk powder turns grainy if you boil it. Kill the heat first, hydrate the tomato, then the dairy. Salt last, because dried tomato varies wildly in how much it already has.
