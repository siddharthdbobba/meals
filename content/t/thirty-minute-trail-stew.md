The original is a Scout recipe: brown a pound of hamburger, open two cans, simmer. None of that travels. Cans are heavy and their liquid is the part you are paying to carry. So the beef is freeze-dried, the tomatoes become tomato powder, and the canned green beans and their juice become dried beans and plain water.

The half hour is not padding. Dried potato dices need that long in liquid to go from gravel to something you can chew, and the tomato and bouillon need the time to reduce into a broth with any body. Low heat and a lid, or the bottom burns while the top is still crunchy. The oil at the end is there because freeze-dried beef arrives with the fat stripped out, and stew without fat tastes like salt water.
