Wide-mouth vacuum jugs cook by holding heat, not making it. Boiling water at 350 ml carries enough energy to soften a broken ramen block and rehydrate dried vegetables over a couple of hours, so the stove only runs once, in the morning, when it is already out for coffee. By noon you open a lid instead of setting up in the wind.

The preheat step matters more than the recipe. A cold steel jug steals a real share of the heat before the noodles ever soften, and the difference shows up as chewy noodles and lukewarm broth. Miso and sesame oil go in at the end for the same reason a lot of trail food tastes flat: the salt and fat that carry flavor are the first things a long soak dulls.
