The original is a weeknight skillet dish built on refrigerated teriyaki meatballs and a pile of fresh vegetables. Neither survives a pack. Freeze-dried chicken and dried pineapple do the same job: the pineapple is where the teriyaki sweetness actually lives, and drying only concentrates it.

The cornstarch is the part worth keeping. Lo mein is defined by sauce that coats the noodles, and without a thickener you get salty water at the bottom of the pot and bare noodles on your spoon. One teaspoon, stirred in off the heat, is the difference. Rehydrating the vegetables before the noodles go in matters too, since dried mushrooms take far longer than a ramen block and will otherwise stay like gravel.
