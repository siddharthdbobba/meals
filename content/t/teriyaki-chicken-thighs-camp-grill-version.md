Teriyaki is a glaze, not a marinade, and the difference is sugar. Brush the raw marinade on over a live fire and the sugar burns before the chicken cooks through. Cooking the sauce first, thickening it with cornstarch, and holding back a third of it for basting solves both problems: the meat soaks overnight in the thin version, and the thick version goes on only in the last few minutes.

Thighs over breasts is deliberate. Fire pit heat is uneven and unhurried, and thighs tolerate that. A breast overcooked by four minutes is dry; a thigh overcooked by four minutes is fine. This one stays a cooler recipe — raw chicken has no shelf-stable version — so freeze the marinating bag flat and cook it the first night out.
