The original leans on yogurt and eggs, neither of which survives a day in a pack. Powdered whole milk and powdered egg do the same two jobs: the milk solids brown the outside and the egg binds a batter that has no gluten holding it together. Water goes in cold, which matters, because hot water starts gelling the oats before the leavening has anywhere to go.

Grinding the oats at home is the difference between pancakes and clumpy porridge patties. Whole flakes cook unevenly and tear on the flip. Keep the flame low and give the batter its five-minute rest; both are about letting the oats hydrate rather than sit raw in the middle of a browned cake.
