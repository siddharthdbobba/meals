The original is an eight-hour slow cooker braise, which is the one thing a stove-and-a-pot cannot do. Ten minutes of soak time replaces it. Freeze-dried pork rehydrates all the way through in hot water, so the sauce has to arrive already built rather than being cooked into the meat.

Sauce goes in at the end for a reason. Tomato paste and honey scorch on a thin titanium pot bottom, and soy sauce powder stirred into boiling water clumps. Off the heat, everything dissolves into the rice starch and coats it. The pineapple is the acid that keeps a sweet-salty sauce from going flat.
