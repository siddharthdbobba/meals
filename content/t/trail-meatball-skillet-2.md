The home version binds raw beef with milk-soaked bread and egg. On the trail there is no raw beef, no milk, and no egg, so the binder has to be rebuilt from powder. Rehydrated freeze-dried beef, crushed crackers, powdered egg, and powdered milk do the same job: the starch holds water, the egg sets when it hits heat, and the whole thing stops crumbling in the pan.

The mistake is too much water. A wet mix will not hold a shape and will fall apart the moment you turn it, which is exactly how most first attempts at meatballs go. Add water slowly and stop as soon as a squeezed handful stays together. Fat matters too. Freeze-dried beef is lean, so the three tablespoons of oil are not optional if you want browning instead of steaming.
