Camp cooks fry fresh fish because they have fresh fish. On a trail with no rod and no pan, the honest version of the same idea is a pouch and a mayo packet. Tuna in foil is already cooked and already shelf-stable, so the entire recipe is seasoning and a spoon.

Mayo is the part people distrust. Single-serve packets are shelf-stable at room temperature until you tear them, which is why they turn up in restaurant caddies and not refrigerators. Use the whole packet in one sitting and there is nothing to keep. The fat is also doing real work here: plain tuna is lean enough that it eats like a chore, and a tablespoon of mayo puts about a hundred calories back and makes it stick to the tortilla.
