steps:
  - "Heat 250 ml water in your pot until steaming. Add the dried onion, sage, and peppers. Let it steep for 2 minutes off heat."
  - "In a small cup or mug, whisk the flour with 2 tbsp of the hot water until it's a thin paste. This prevents lumps."
  - "Heat the oil over medium heat. Pour in the flour paste and stir constantly for 2 minutes until it smells nutty (this is your roux)."
  - "Slowly pour in the remaining hot water, stirring constantly to avoid lumps. Add the powdered milk and cream."
  - "Keep stirring until the sauce thickens enough to coat a spoon. Add the bacon bits."

Trail country gravy swaps fresh cream and ham for powders and bacon bits, but keeps the technique: build a roux to thicken a milk base, then season with sage and pepper. The flour-paste keeps it smooth. Powdered cream is essential—without it you get thin and watery instead of rich and coating.

Most trail breakfasts are compromises. This one works because it actually tastes like home and delivers the calories to fuel hours of walking.
