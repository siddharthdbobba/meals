  - "Build a fire and let it burn down. You want a bed of coals, not flame, or the roux scorches."

Gumbo has two thickeners working at once and both matter. The roux is a butter-and-flour paste cooked dark enough to smell like toast, which is where the depth comes from. The okra does the rest: its mucilage, the thing people complain about, dissolves into the broth over a long simmer and never shows up as slime. Cook it hard and fast and you get the slime. Cook it slow and submerged and you get body.

This is a cooler recipe, not a backpacking one. Three kinds of meat and a box of broth are heavy and cold, so it belongs at a car-camp site or a basecamp where the cooler stays packed. The order the meats go in is the whole trick. Chicken and sausage can take the full simmer. Shrimp cannot, and five minutes off the coals at the end is the difference between shrimp and rubber.
