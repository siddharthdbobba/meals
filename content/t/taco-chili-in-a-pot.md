The home version simmers ground beef with fresh peppers and canned beans for half an hour. None of that travels, so the whole thing gets rebuilt from powders: freeze-dried beef, tomato powder instead of sauce and diced tomatoes, and pre-cooked dried beans that rehydrate in the same twelve minutes the meat does. The spice load stays exactly the same, because spices weigh nothing and they are what makes it taste like tacos rather than generic red stew.

Tomato powder is the part worth getting right. It thickens as it hydrates, so a pot that looks thin at minute six will be chili by minute twelve, and it scorches if you walk away. The chips and cheese at the end are not garnish. They are the fat and the crunch, and without them this is a bowl of beans.
