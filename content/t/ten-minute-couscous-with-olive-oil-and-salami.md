Couscous is already cooked before you buy it. All the boiling water does is rehydrate it, which is why this meal costs one pot of fuel instead of ten minutes of simmering. In cold weather that matters more than the recipe does.

The dry ingredients rehydrate at different speeds, so the tomatoes and onion get a two-minute head start in the hot water. Everything after that is fat and salt. Couscous on its own is bland and thin; the oil and cured sausage are what turn it into a dinner that carries you to the next morning.
