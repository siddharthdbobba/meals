Daal is one of the few one-pot camp dinners that gets its flavor from technique rather than from a packet. Split red lentils break down on their own in about twenty-five minutes with no soaking, which is the whole reason this works on a stove instead of at home. The tadka at the end is not garnish. Frying whole spices in hot oil pulls out compounds that plain simmering never touches, so a pot of bland lentils turns into dinner in about thirty seconds.

The changes from a home version are all about what survives a pack. Fresh onion, tomato, garlic, and ginger become flakes, powder, and granules, which rehydrate in the simmering water anyway. The ground spices go into the oil off the heat because turmeric and garlic powder scorch in seconds at frying temperature and turn bitter. Keep the fat: it carries the spice and it is most of the calories here.
