The original runs six hours in a slow cooker on wild turkey legs, which is the correct way to handle dark meat with that much connective tissue and the wrong way to eat at 8 p.m. in a campground. Bouillon does the job the long simmer was doing: it puts gelatin and salt into the water so the broth has body instead of tasting like herb tea. Freeze-dried meat goes in late because it is already cooked and only needs to rehydrate.

Dried mirepoix is the other swap worth explaining. Fresh carrot and celery are heavy and mostly water, and the dried version rehydrates in the broth you are already making, so nothing is wasted. The acid at the end matters more than it looks like it should. Without it the whole pot reads flat and salty, and half a teaspoon of citric acid weighs nothing next to a lemon.
