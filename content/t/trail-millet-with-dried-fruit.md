Millet cooks fast and doesn't paste up like rice or need soaking like some grains. One pot of water handles the whole job. The oil is where the calories actually live—without it, this is thin mush. The dried fruit softens in the steam and adds both texture and sweetness without extra weight.

Millet tastes neutral and toasted if cooked right, which means it's a platform for whatever you add. That's the whole point on the trail. The recipe carries light and cooks in the time it takes to boil water once.
