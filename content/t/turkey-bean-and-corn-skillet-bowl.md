The original was a family skillet dinner built on canned beans, canned corn, a jar of salsa, sour cream, and a pound of ground turkey. Almost none of that carries. The beans and corn are water in metal, the sour cream is a fridge item, and the raw turkey needs real cooking time and a real pan. Freeze-dried meat, bean flakes, and freeze-dried corn get you to the same bowl at a fifth of the weight, and salsa powder replaces a 16 oz jar with two tablespoons.

Order matters more than technique here. The corn and the turkey rehydrate slowest, so they go in first and get a head start while the water is hottest. Bean flakes go in dead last because they thicken on contact and will glue the rice to the bottom of the pot if you add them early. The cheese and oil are not garnish; without them this is a lean, dry bowl that will not hold you through the evening.
