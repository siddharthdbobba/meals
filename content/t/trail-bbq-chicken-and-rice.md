The original is a slow cooker dish: bone-in thighs sitting for hours in ketchup, soy sauce, honey, and mustard until the sauce reduces around them. None of that survives a trail kitchen. Long braises need fuel you are not carrying, and the sauce ingredients are wet weight. So the meat becomes freeze-dried chicken and the sauce becomes its powdered parts, added at the end where they still taste like something.

Order matters more than technique here. Chicken rehydrates slowly and rice rehydrates fast, so the chicken goes in first and gets its own five minutes before the rice ever touches water. The sauce powders go in last because tomato and sugar scorch on a hot pot bottom, and because stirring them into an already-thick pot is how you avoid a lumpy paste. The oil is not optional — it carries the spice flavor and puts the calorie count where a dinner needs it.
