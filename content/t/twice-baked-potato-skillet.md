The original is a casserole: two pounds of potatoes baked for an hour, chopped, layered with sautéed vegetables and yogurt, then baked again. None of that survives contact with a stove that does one thing. So the potatoes become flakes, the yogurt becomes milk powder plus a little sour cream powder, and the layering collapses into one pot.

What carries over is the seasoning logic. Smoked paprika and cumin were doing the bacon's job in the source recipe, and they still do it here. The dried mushrooms matter more than they look: they rehydrate into something with chew, which is the only texture standing between this and plain mashed potatoes. Soak them in the boiling water before anything else goes in, or they stay leathery.
