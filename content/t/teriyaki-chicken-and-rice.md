The original is a meal-prep bowl: pan-fried chicken, sautéed vegetables, rice cooked separately, sauce simmered in the pan. None of that survives a stove with one pot and one burner. So the chicken and vegetables go dried and get rehydrated in the same water that cooks the rice, and the sauce becomes a dry powder you stir in at the end.

Cornstarch is the part worth keeping. Without it the sauce is salty water that sinks to the bottom of the pot; with it the whole thing coats the rice. Add it dry, with the other powders, after the rice has absorbed most of the water — dumped into a full boil it clumps. The sesame oil goes in last, off heat, because its flavor is volatile and simmering it away wastes the calories you carried.
