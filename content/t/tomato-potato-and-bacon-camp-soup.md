The original is a stove-top soup that starts by rendering raw bacon and sweating fresh vegetables in the fat. On the trail you have neither the fat nor the fridge, so the bacon comes precooked from a pouch and the oil does the job the drippings used to. Adding the bacon at the very end keeps it from going soft in twenty minutes of simmering.

Dried potato slices are the part that needs patience. They want real simmer time and more water than most one-pot dinners, which is why this is a soup and not a mash. Tomato and bouillon carry the flavor that fresh vegetables would have built, and the bay leaf earns its weight because there is nothing else in the pot with any depth.
