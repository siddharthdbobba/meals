Carbonara is a sauce made of egg, cheese, and starchy water, held together by heat that is high enough to thicken the egg and low enough not to cook it. That balance is harder on a stove with one flame setting, so the fix here is to take the pot off the heat and wait a minute before the egg goes in. Residual heat in the pasta does the work. Rush it and you get scrambled eggs in noodles.

The trail changes are the perishables. Fresh eggs and a fridge do not travel, so whole egg powder rehydrated with a little cold water stands in, and grated shelf-stable parmesan replaces a wedge that would sweat in a pack. Frozen peas become freeze-dried ones, which soften in the pasta boil for free. Draining is skipped entirely: you cook the pasta down to about half a cup of water and that becomes the sauce base, which saves fuel, water, and a second dish.
