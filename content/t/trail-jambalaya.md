The original is a ninety-minute cast iron dish built on raw sausage, fresh trinity, and long-grain rice that drinks stock slowly. None of that travels. Instant rice hydrates off residual heat, dried onion and pepper rebuild the vegetable base, and tomato powder replaces canned tomato at a fraction of the weight. What survives the translation is the structure: fat rendered first, spices bloomed in that fat, then liquid, then rice.

Cured sausage and country ham are doing double duty as protein and as the salt in the dish. That is why the bouillon is one cube and why you season at the end. Salt it before the ham has steeped and you will overshoot.
