This is a two-burner stove recipe, and the flat griddle plate is the whole point. A pan concentrates heat in the middle; a plate spread across the burner gives you a real temperature gradient, so potatoes can crisp at the center while cooked food holds at the edge without turning to leather. That is why the eggs go on last and directly onto the metal rather than into a separate pan.

Parboiling the potatoes at home is the difference between twenty minutes and forty. Raw cubes on a griddle brown long before their centers give up, and you burn fuel waiting. Everything else here is cooler food, so this stays a car-camp or basecamp meal. The last variation is the version that works out of a pack.
