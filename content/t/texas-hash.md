This is a one-pot rice pilaf pretending to be a hash. The rice cooks directly in the tomato juice and water, so the starch it gives off thickens everything instead of getting rinsed away. That is why the lid matters more than the heat level: the pot has exactly the water it will ever get, and steam that escapes is water the rice does not get.

Browning the beef at home is the real trick. It removes the grease, kills the raw-meat handling problem, and cuts camp time roughly in half. Frozen flat, the bag chills the rest of the cooler on the drive in and is thawed by dinner. Onion and garlic powder stand in for the fresh onion the original called for — they hold flavor without a cutting board and cannot go soft in a pack.
