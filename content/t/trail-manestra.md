The original is a stovetop dish that simmers meat and orzo in a tomato broth until the pasta drinks it. That structure travels well: nothing gets drained, so every calorie stays in the pot. Fresh onion and canned tomato paste are the only things that had to change. Dried minced onion and tomato powder do the same job at a fraction of the weight, and the powder dissolves instead of sitting in a lump.

Cinnamon and mint sound wrong together in a beef dish and are not. A quarter teaspoon of cinnamon is enough to round the tomato without reading as sweet, and crushing the mint as it goes in wakes up the oil in the dried leaves. The olive oil goes in at the end because boiling it for twenty minutes wastes it. Watch the bottom of the pot: orzo starches out fast and will scorch on a hot spot if you stop stirring.
