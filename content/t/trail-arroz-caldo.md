Arroz caldo is normally an hour of simmering: raw rice, bone broth, a whole head of garlic. None of that fits a stove that burns fuel by the minute. The shortcut is instant rice plus a spoonful of glutinous rice flour, which does the thickening that a long simmer would otherwise do. You get the porridge texture in eight minutes instead of forty.

The aromatics are the part not to trim. Dried garlic, onion, and ginger bloomed in oil for thirty seconds taste far more like the real thing than the same powders dumped into water. Fish sauce is the other load-bearing ingredient; leave it out and you have salty rice. The fried garlic on top is not a garnish either, it is the crunch that keeps a bowl of soft starch from getting boring halfway down.
