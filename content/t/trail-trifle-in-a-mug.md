A trifle is layers of soft cake, custard, and fruit, and the only part of that requiring a kitchen is the custard. The original simmers milk, sugar, cornstarch, and an egg on the stove and then chills it for hours. Instant pudding mix does the same job with cold water and five minutes of standing, and powdered whole milk replaces the two and two-thirds cups of fresh milk you cannot carry.

Angel food cake is out. It crushes into powder in a pack and stales in a day. Shortbread holds its shape until the pudding hits it, then softens into the same effect. The whipped cream layer is gone entirely; there is no shelf-stable version of it worth carrying, and the pudding already covers the creamy part. Soak the dried fruit separately so it does not pull water out of the custard.
