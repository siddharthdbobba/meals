Ruth Wakefield's drop cookie was written for a kitchen with an icebox and a wall oven. Two things had to change for camp. Butter and egg both come out, replaced by oil for fat and powdered milk for the browning and body the egg used to supply. Oil-based dough spreads more and stays chewy longer, which is fine here; it also mixes cold, so nothing has to soften.

The heat is the part worth attention. Coals on top and coals underneath is what makes a Dutch oven an oven instead of a skillet, and the ratio matters more than the count. Roughly twice as many on the lid as below keeps the bottoms from burning before the tops set. Pull them early. They finish on the plate.
