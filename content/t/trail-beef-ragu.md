A slow cooker ragu works because eight low hours pull chuck roast apart and reduce the tomatoes down. Neither is available on a stove that burns a canister a week. So the collagen work happens at home instead: use cooked, freeze-dried beef, which is already tender, and let the soak in camp do nothing but put the water back.

The order matters more than the ingredients. Carrot and mushroom are dense and take the longest, so they simmer alone first. Tomato powder goes in last, off the heat, because it scorches on a pot bottom and thickens as it sits anyway. The off-stove rest is where the dish actually finishes, and it costs no fuel.

Oil is not garnish. Without it this is lean and thin, and freeze-dried beef brings almost no fat of its own.
