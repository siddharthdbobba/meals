The original is a four-hour stovetop chili, which is a stove-fuel problem rather than a recipe. Everything wet got dried and everything slow got swapped: bean flakes instead of canned beans, tomato powder instead of crushed tomatoes, bouillon instead of broth. The vegetables were the real weight — zucchini, peppers, and corn are almost entirely water — so they go on the dehydrator tray at home and come back in the pot.

The cocoa is worth keeping. A teaspoon of unsweetened cocoa gives the sauce a darker, rounder edge and some body, which matters more here than it does at home, since a rehydrated chili tends to taste thin. The oil goes in off the heat at the end: it is a third of the calories and does nothing useful while boiling. Fresh toppings are gone, so the salt and the cayenne have to do that work instead.
