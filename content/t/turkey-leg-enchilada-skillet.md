The original is a slow-cooker-and-oven build: roll everything into tortillas, bake, serve. In camp you have neither the eight hours nor the oven, so the work splits. The legs and thighs get slow-cooked at home, pulled off the bone, and frozen flat. Everything after that is a thirty-minute skillet job over coals.

Rolled enchiladas are a bad use of camp time and worse to clean up. Tearing the tortillas and folding them into the sauce gets you the same thing — soft tortilla, chile, meat, melted cheese — in one pan. The torn strips also thicken the sauce, which matters because a can of enchilada sauce is thin and would otherwise run off the plate. Wild turkey leg is dark, sinewy meat that needs long, wet heat; if you skip the home cook step and try to do it at camp, it will be tough.
