The original is a skillet dish: hot oil, fresh vegetables, sauce whisked in a jar, rice on the side. None of that survives a pack. So the vegetables are dried and rehydrate in the same water that cooks the noodles, the rice becomes a ramen block, and the sauce goes in as a powder blend stirred in at the end.

Draining matters. Stir-fry sauce is supposed to cling, and if you leave a full pot of water in there you get soup with soy sauce in it. Pour most of it off, then let the cornstarch and the residual heat do the rest. The oil goes in last, off the flame, because that is where the calories and the sesame smell come from and boiling wastes both.
