The original is a casserole: bake, top with cheese, broil. None of that survives a stove with one pot and one burner, so the sauce gets built in the pasta water instead of a baking dish. Cook the spaghetti in just enough water that you never pour any off, then thicken what is left with the soup mix and milk powder. That is the whole trick, and it is also why the water measurement matters more than usual.

Sour cream and two kinds of fresh cheese all became powder. Powdered milk brings back the body, cheddar powder brings the color and salt, and hard parmesan brings the flavor that survives a pack. The oil is there because dried cheese has less fat than shredded, and without it the bowl reads thin.
