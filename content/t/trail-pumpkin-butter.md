The original is a canned-pumpkin recipe that finishes in a pressure cooker and lives in a fridge for weeks. Neither of those travels. Dehydrated pumpkin powder carries the same solids at a fraction of the weight, and rehydrating it with the sugar and spice already mixed in means the pot does the reducing work the Instant Pot used to do.

Sugar is not decoration here. It pulls water out of the pumpkin and gives the spread body, which is why the mixture stiffens as it cools. Cider powder replaces the apple juice and adds the acid that keeps it from tasting flat. Without a cooler this is a same-day food, so cook the amount you will actually eat.
