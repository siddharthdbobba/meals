The source here was about raising a bird to slaughter weight, not about dinner on a ridge, so what carried over is the shape of the meal: chicken, aromatics, starch, long slow heat. None of that survives a backpack. Freeze-dried chicken replaces the roast, instant rice replaces the two hours of oven time, and bouillon does the work a pan of drippings would do at home.

The order matters more than the ingredients. Chicken and dried vegetables go in first and boil, because freeze-dried meat is slow to take on water and stays rubbery if it only gets the tail end of a soak. Rice goes in after, off a hard boil, so it steams instead of scorching to the pot. The oil at the end is not optional — bouillon and rice are close to fat-free, and this is a dinner, not a side.
