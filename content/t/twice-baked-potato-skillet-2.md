The original is twelve baked potatoes scraped out of their skins and enriched with sour cream, cream cheese, heavy cream, milk, butter, and an egg, then baked again under cheese and bacon. None of that survives a pack. Potato flakes replace the hour in the oven, and the whole dairy list collapses into three powders: milk, sour cream, and cheddar. Butter powder covers the fat. The egg was there to set the casserole in a dish, and nothing in a pot needs setting, so it is gone.

Order matters more than technique here. Dissolve the powders in hot water first, because cheese powder dropped onto already-thickened potato turns into gritty specks that never go away. Hold back some bacon for the top: the source browned its casserole under a broiler, and the only crunch available on a stove is the crunch you save.
