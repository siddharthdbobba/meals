Slow cooker stroganoff runs on three things a stove cannot give you: hours, a fridge, and cubed stew meat. Hours become minutes with freeze-dried beef and dried mushrooms, both of which rehydrate in the same water that cooks the noodles. Stew meat is the one substitution you cannot fake — chunks of beef need a long braise, so the freeze-dried version is doing real work here, not just saving weight.

Greek yogurt becomes sour cream powder, added off the heat. Cultured dairy splits when it boils, which turns gravy into grit, so the pot comes off the flame first and the powder goes in mixed with the cornstarch. The starch keeps the grains apart long enough to dissolve and thickens the sauce as it cools. Mustard powder and dried dill carry the flavor that Dijon and fresh dill would; they weigh nothing and do not leak.
