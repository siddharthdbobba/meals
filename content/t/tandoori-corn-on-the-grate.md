Tandoori cooking is really two things: a fatty, acidic coating and dry heat close enough to blister it. A campfire grate does the second part fine. The first part is where the trail version diverges from the original, which leans on thick yogurt. Yogurt does not travel, so mayo stands in. It is fat and acid in the same proportions, it holds spice the same way, and it browns faster.

The corn goes on naked first for a reason. Coating raw corn means the spices burn long before the kernels give up any sweetness. Char first, coat second, and the mayo only has to be on the fire long enough to set. Chaat masala is added off the heat because its black salt and amchur go flat and bitter over coals.
