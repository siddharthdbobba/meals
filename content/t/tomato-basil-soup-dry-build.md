The original is a pot of fresh vegetables simmered in a quart of stock and then blended. None of that travels. Tomato powder replaces the plum tomatoes and the broth carton at once, and it arrives pre-blended, which removes the only piece of equipment the recipe really needed. Bouillon stands in for the vegetable stock, and dried mirepoix does the work of the onion, carrot, and celery.

Order matters more than technique here. The dried vegetables go in while the water is hot and stay in for the full five minutes; the tomato powder goes in off the heat, or it scorches on the pot bottom and turns bitter. Butter is gone, replaced by olive oil, which carries better and does the same job of rounding out the acid. The parmesan is optional, but it is where most of the protein comes from.
