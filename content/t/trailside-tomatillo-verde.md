Salsa verde is mostly water, so carrying it wet makes no sense. Roasting the tomatillos, chiles, garlic, and onion first does two things: it drives off moisture and it caramelizes enough sugar to survive drying, which raw tomatillos do not. What comes home from the dehydrator is a green powder that costs about an ounce for two servings.

The rehydration is the part people rush. Tomatillo leather takes a few minutes to let go of the water, and if you judge the thickness at minute one you will end up with paste at minute five. Add the oil last; it carries the chile heat and keeps the sauce from setting up stiff in the pot.
