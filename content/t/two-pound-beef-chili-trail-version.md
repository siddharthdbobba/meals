The source was a week of home chili made from two pounds of ground beef. That does not travel, so the beef gets browned, rinsed in hot water to strip the fat, and dried at home. Fat is what turns dried meat rancid in a pack, so the rinse is not optional — you add the fat back as oil at camp, where it keeps.

The bean flakes are the structural piece. Real beans take forty minutes and a lot of fuel; flakes thicken the pot in under a minute off the heat and bring most of the same protein. Add them last, because once they hydrate the pot stops moving heat around and the rice underneath quits cooking.
