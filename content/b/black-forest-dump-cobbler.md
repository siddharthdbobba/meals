The soda is doing the job eggs and oil do at home. Carbonation lifts the batter and the sugar syrup gives it enough moisture to set, so a boxed mix becomes a cobbler topping with nothing that needs a cooler. That is the whole reason this recipe exists in camp form.

Heat placement matters more than time. Two thirds of the coals belong on the lid, because the top has to bake while the fruit below only needs to bubble. Bottom-heavy coals burn the filling to the cast iron before the cake sets. The pie tin on pebbles is insurance against the same problem, and it means the only thing to scrub afterward is a lid.
