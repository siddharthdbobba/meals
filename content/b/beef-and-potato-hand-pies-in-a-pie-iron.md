The original is a two-hour oven project with raw meat and raw potato baking inside the crust. That does not work over a fire, where the outside browns long before the middle is safe. So the filling goes in already cooked: beef browned and cooled at home, potatoes parboiled and diced small. At camp the fire only has to crisp the crust and heat what is inside.

The dough is worth cutting at home too. Flour, salt, and fat combined in advance travel as a single bag and need nothing but cold water in camp, which spares you a pastry blender and a work surface you do not have. Shortening in place of butter means the bag can sit in the pack instead of the cooler. Keep the pies small. Full-size ones will not close in a pie iron and will not cook through if they do.
