This exists because a hydration reservoir holds two liters and most people drink it as plain water. Sugar plus sodium moves water across the gut wall faster than water alone, so the same volume actually gets absorbed instead of sloshing. The ratio matters more than the flavor: roughly 50 grams of carbohydrate and half a teaspoon of salt per two liters lands near what your body will take on without cramping.

The real cost is cleaning. Sugar in a bite valve grows things within a day or two in warm weather, which is why the rinse step is not optional and why some people keep the mix in a bottle and the bladder for water. Citric acid does double duty here — it makes the salt drinkable and it keeps the mix slightly acidic in the line.
