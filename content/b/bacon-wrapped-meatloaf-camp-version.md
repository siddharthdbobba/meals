  - "Build a fire and let it burn down. You want coals, not flame."
  - "Warm the oil in the dutch oven and soften the onion and garlic. Pull them out and let them cool. Hot onion starts cooking the meat before it goes in the pot."
  - "Mix the milk powder into 100 ml of water. In a bowl or a large ziplock, work the pork, beef, salt, and pepper together, then add the cooled onion, breadcrumbs, milk, egg, fennel, rosemary, and mustard."
  - "Lay a double sheet of heavy foil, shingle the bacon across it, and mound the meat down the middle into a loaf. Fold the bacon over the top, then wrap the foil into a sealed packet."
  - "Set the packet in the dutch oven on a few pebbles or a ring of foil so the bottom does not scorch. Lid on. Roughly 8 coals under, 16 on top. Cook 40 to 50 minutes, rotating the pot a quarter turn every 10 minutes."

This is oven food moved onto coals. A dutch oven with more heat on the lid than underneath is a decent oven, and the foil wrap does the rest: it holds the loaf's shape without a bread pan and keeps the bacon fat from running into the coals. Rotating the pot every ten minutes matters more than the exact coal count, since hot spots are what actually burn a loaf.

The breadcrumbs and milk are structural. They hold moisture in a mix that is otherwise just meat and salt, and without them the loaf tightens into something dry by the time the center is safe. Powdered milk rehydrates fine here and saves carrying a carton. Frozen peas are the useful trick of the whole meal: they go in the cooler solid and keep everything else cold on the drive in.
