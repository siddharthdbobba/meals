Coal cooking is a timing problem, not a recipe. The potatoes need forty minutes of steady radiant heat and the steak needs eight minutes of direct heat, so the packet goes in while the fire is still settling and the meat waits until the bed is even. Do it the other way around and you eat rare potatoes with a cold steak.

The foil packet is also the whole cleanup. Nothing touches a pot, and the grate wipes down with a wadded ball of foil while it is still hot. Handle everything with a real glove or long tongs: a grate sitting over open coals holds far more heat than an oven mitt was built for, and cotton gives up fast.
