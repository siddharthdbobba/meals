This is a car-camping recipe and it stays one. A four-pound chuck roast needs four hours of low wet heat to turn its connective tissue soft, and there is no dehydrator trick that gets you there in a backpacking pot. What it does need is a cooler, a dutch oven, and an evening where nobody is in a hurry.

The bacon fat is the whole first half of the recipe. It carries the sear, then the onions, then the fond that the wine lifts off the bottom. The brown sugar in the rub is there for the crust, not for sweetness. Fresh herb sprigs go limp and slimy in a cooler, so dried thyme and rosemary go in instead and they are simmering for three hours anyway. Root vegetables go in late because carrots and turnips fall apart long before the meat gives up.
