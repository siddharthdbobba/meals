The camp version of this is a Dutch oven soup that starts with a ten-minute sauté of onion, carrot, and celery in oil. On a stove with finite fuel, that sauté is the first thing to go. Dried vegetables rehydrate in the same water they will be eaten in, so nothing is lost but the browning, and the oil goes in at the end where it still tastes like oil.

Canned beans and canned tomatoes are mostly water and steel. Bean flakes carry the same protein and thicken the broth on their own, which is what the long simmer was doing before. Add the pasta and flakes together and the pot finishes at one time instead of two.

If you have soaked whole beans instead, they need a full thirty minutes ahead of everything else, and a lot more fuel.
