Elk is lean enough that the usual steak advice works against it. There is almost no intramuscular fat to buffer the heat, so a straight sear over coals dries the middle out before the crust forms. The two-zone fire fixes that: the steaks spend most of their time on the cool side over a pan of water, taking smoke while the interior comes up slowly, and only touch the fire at the end.

The marinade is doing two jobs. Soy sauce and lime bring salt and acid, which help a lean cut hold water. The brown sugar and bourbon are there for the sear — sugar and residual alcohol brown fast, so the crust develops in the two or three minutes the meat can afford over direct heat. This is a cooler recipe, not a pack recipe; the marinating happens before you leave and the meat needs to stay cold until it hits the grate.
