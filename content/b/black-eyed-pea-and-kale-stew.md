A dried black eyed pea takes an hour of hard boiling, which is fuel nobody carries. This version uses precooked dehydrated peas instead, so the stove only has to bring water up once and the pot does the rest of the work off the flame. Ten minutes covered is the whole method.

The rest is substitution. Canned fire roasted tomatoes become tomato powder, fresh mirepoix becomes dried onion, celery, and carrot, and collards or fresh kale become the freeze-dried kind, which rehydrates in about a minute and does not turn to sludge. Smoked paprika carries the flavor the ham bone used to. Keep the oil for the end, off heat, or you lose it to the boil.
