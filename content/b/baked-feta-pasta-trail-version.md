The original bakes a block of feta under tomatoes at 230 C for half an hour, then mashes the two together into a sauce. None of that survives contact with a stove that boils one pot. What does survive is the idea: salty cheese plus concentrated tomato, emulsified with starchy pasta water.

So the tomatoes go in dry and rehydrate in the same water that cooks the pasta, and the feta becomes cheese powder added off the heat. Measuring the water matters more than usual here. Drain it and you have thrown away the sauce; use too much and the powder never thickens. Six hundred milliliters for this much pasta leaves roughly the right amount behind.
