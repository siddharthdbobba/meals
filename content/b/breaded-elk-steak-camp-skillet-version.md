The breading is doing more than adding crunch. Elk is lean enough to dry out fast, and a flour-egg-crumb shell holds the surface moisture in while the crust browns. It also mutes the flavor that makes some people suspicious of wild game, which is useful when you are cooking for a group you did not hunt with.

Two changes from a kitchen version. Fresh eggs are replaced with egg powder and water, so nothing fragile rides in the cooler. And the pan sits on a grate over coals rather than a burner, which means you control heat by moving the skillet, not by turning a dial. Bank the coals to one side so you have a cooler landing spot ready.
