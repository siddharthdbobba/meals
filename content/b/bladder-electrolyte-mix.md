Sugar in a hydration reservoir is a tradeoff, and it is worth naming. Carbohydrate in solution moves water across the gut wall faster than plain water does, and the sodium is what keeps that water in you rather than passing straight through. Roughly 6 percent sugar by weight is the range where absorption works; heavier than that and it sits in your stomach. Three tablespoons in two liters lands close enough.

The cost is cleaning. Any sweetened liquid left in a bite valve overnight grows something, and a reservoir is hard to scrub. Blowing the tube clear and drying the bag open handles most of it. If you will not do that, carry the mix dry and dose a bottle instead.
