This is a car-camping project, not a trail recipe. Two pounds of raw onion is heavy and wet, and there is no way to shortcut the forty-five minutes it takes to drive the water out. What you get in return is a condiment that turns crackers, hard cheese, and cheap sausage into something worth sitting down for.

The order matters. Sugar added too early sets a crust on the pan before the onions have given up their water, and burnt sugar is bitter all the way through. Cook the onions soft first, then sweeten, then acidify. The balsamic goes in last because long heat cooks off the sharpness that makes the jam read as sour rather than just sweet. Keep the flame low; a canister stove on full throttle scorches the bottom of a thin pot in under a minute.
