The original is a slow cooker recipe: a chuck roast, vinegar, and four seasonings, left alone for eight hours while the meat makes its own liquid. None of that survives a pack. So the roast becomes freeze-dried shredded beef, the eight hours become an eight-minute soak, and the balsamic arrives as powder instead of a bottle you would rather not carry.

The potato flakes replace the braising liquid the roast would have made on its own. Without them you have salty vinegar broth with meat in it. Add the balsamic powder after the pot is off the stove — the sharpness is the only thing distinguishing this from generic beef and potatoes, and boiling flattens it.
