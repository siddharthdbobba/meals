The original is a Dutch oven project: cubed brisket seared in bacon fat, then an hour and a half braising in broth, ale, and two cans of tomatoes. None of that survives a pack. What does survive is the spice profile, which is where the recipe actually lives — chili powder and smoked paprika doing the heavy work, brown sugar and a pinch of cinnamon rounding off the edges the long braise used to round off with time.

Tomato powder replaces the cans and drops about two pounds of carried water. Freeze-dried beef will not shred like brisket, so the bacon bits are non-negotiable: they put back the smoke and fat that the searing step used to provide. The bean flakes are the structural piece. Without them you have spiced broth with meat in it, and it will not sit right after a long day.
