The original is a barrel in a cellar: vegetables under salt water for weeks, no vinegar anywhere, sourness coming from fermentation rather than a bottle. None of that fits in a pack, so this keeps the salt and the aromatics and gives up the fermentation. What you get after a day in the jar is a cold, sharp, crunchy vegetable dish, not a true pickle.

Drying the vegetables first is what makes the timing work. Fresh cabbage takes weeks to give up its water to a brine; dried cabbage spends the day pulling brine back in, which is the same exchange running the other direction and much faster. Horseradish, garlic, and the chile carry the flavor because they are strong enough to survive both the dehydrator and the dilution. This is the part of dinner that is not beige.
