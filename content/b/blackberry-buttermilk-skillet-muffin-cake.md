The original is a jumbo muffin recipe built on fresh berries, whole milk buttermilk, and eggs out of a fridge. None of that survives two days in a pack. Powdered buttermilk and powdered egg do the same chemical work: the buttermilk keeps the batter acidic so the baking soda has something to react with, and the egg powder binds it once water goes in. Butter got dropped entirely in favor of oil, which does not need to be kept cold and does not need melting.

Dried berries need the soak. Thrown in dry they pull water out of the crumb and leave hard flecks. Ten minutes in a splash of water is enough to soften them without turning them to jam. Coals mostly on the lid is the other thing that matters — bottom heat alone will scorch the base long before the middle sets.
