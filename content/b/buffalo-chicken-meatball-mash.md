Meatballs need an oven, an egg, and a thermometer, so none of that survives the trip. What actually makes the dish work is the buffalo sauce and the breadcrumb-and-milk panade, and both travel fine. Freeze-dried chicken rehydrates in the same water that softens the crumbs, and the crumbs thicken it into something you eat with a spoon instead of drink.

The fat matters more than it looks. Hot sauce is mostly vinegar and salt; ghee is what keeps it from tasting thin and sharp. Ketchup is doing the same job it does in the original — sugar and body to round off the vinegar — and one packet is enough. Add the breadcrumbs slowly, because you can always thicken further and you cannot un-thicken without another boil.
