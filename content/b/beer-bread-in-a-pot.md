The beer is doing two jobs at once. Its carbonation gives the dough lift alongside the baking powder, and the yeast leftovers give it the sour edge that quick breads usually lack. That is why there is no kneading and no rise time: nothing here needs to ferment, so the dough goes from bag to coals in about five minutes.

Premixing the dry ingredients at home is the whole trick to making this campable. Measuring flour by cup at a picnic table in the wind goes badly. Heat management is the other half — most of the coals belong on the lid, because heat from below scorches the bottom crust long before the middle sets.
