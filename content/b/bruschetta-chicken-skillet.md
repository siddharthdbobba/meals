The original is a baked dish, and baking is the one thing a stove will not do. So the oven work gets split up: the tomatoes rehydrate in the water that would have come out of fresh ones, the chicken arrives already cooked in a pouch, and the cheese melts under a lid instead of under broiler heat. Nothing here needs to reach 165°F, which removes the one real risk of cooking chicken outdoors.

Dry-packed sun-dried tomatoes are the substitution that carries the recipe. They hold the concentrated flavor fresh tomatoes only get after roasting, and they weigh almost nothing. The balsamic goes on at the end, off the heat — cooked into the pan it just turns sour.
