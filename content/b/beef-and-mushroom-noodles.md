This started as a fry-pan stir-fry with fresh steak and a bunch of tender-stem broccoli. On trail it becomes one pot: the mushrooms and broccoli go in the boil, then the noodles and freeze-dried beef finish off-heat in the residual water. Rice vermicelli never needs a rolling boil, which saves fuel and means the pot does soak duty and pan duty in the same ten minutes.

Draining matters more than it sounds. Soy sauce in a wet pot is soup; soy sauce on drained noodles with oil is a coating that sticks to everything. The minute back on the heat at the end is what turns it from rehydrated ingredients into a dish. Skip the salt entirely — a tablespoon of soy is already at the ceiling.
