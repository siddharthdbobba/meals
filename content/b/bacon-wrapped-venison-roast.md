Venison hind quarter has almost no intramuscular fat, which is why steaks off a young deer go grey and tight the moment you overshoot. Leaving the quarter whole and cooking it low fixes the geometry: a big piece heats slowly enough that the connective tissue breaks down before the lean muscle dries. The bacon lattice is the other half. It bastes the surface for three hours and leaves a fat cap where the animal never had one.

This one does not travel. It needs a cooler, a real fire, hours you are not walking, and a knife and board to carve on, so it belongs at a car site or a basecamp after a hunt. Do not chase a pink medium-rare here. The target is fork-tender, which is well past that, and lean venison only gets there by holding it long.
