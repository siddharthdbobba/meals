The original is a stovetop soup built on fresh sausage, cream, and stock. Three swaps make it carryable: home-dehydrated sausage crumbles, bouillon instead of five cups of stock, and powdered whole milk in place of heavy cream. Nothing else about it changes much, because orzo and diced squash both rehydrate in the same pot they cook in.

Order matters more than technique. Squash goes in first because dried cubes take the longest to soften, and orzo goes in second because it releases starch that thickens the broth into something closer to a stew. The dairy and oil go in last, off the heat, where they will not break or stick. Dice the squash small before drying it; large cubes stay leathery in the middle no matter how long you simmer.
