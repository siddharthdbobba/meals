The source was a family baking day: bread, cinnamon rolls, muffins, all of it meant to be eaten within the week at a kitchen table. Only one thing from that list travels, so this takes the honey-sweetened whole grain idea and turns it into something that survives four days in a pack. No eggs, no butter, no leavening. Oil and honey are the whole binder.

Honey is what makes it keep. It holds moisture without going wet, and it does not go rancid the way a butter-based bar does. Pressing the mix down firmly matters more than the bake time: an under-pressed pan comes out as granola, which is fine but not something you can eat one-handed on a switchback.
