An oven does two things to a sprout: browns the cut face and drives off the water that makes it taste bitter. A pan does the same job if you let the halves sit still long enough to color before you start stirring. That first undisturbed four minutes is the whole recipe.

Fresh apple is mostly water and bruises in a pack, so dried slices go in instead. They rehydrate in the covered steam and end up close to roasted apple. The nuts get toasted first and added last, because anything that spends the steam phase in the pan comes out soft.

Sprouts keep about three days in a cooler, which puts this in car-camping and basecamp territory rather than a long carry.
