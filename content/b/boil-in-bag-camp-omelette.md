This is a Scout-camp trick, and the appeal is entirely about cleanup. The bag is the pan. Water in the pot never touches food, so the same pot boils coffee water immediately after and nobody washes a skillet with egg welded to it. Cooking in water also caps the temperature at boiling, which is why the eggs come out as a soft curd instead of a brown rubber disc.

Two things matter. Use a freezer-grade bag, not a thin sandwich bag, and keep it off the pot wall where the metal runs far hotter than the water. A paperclip or a stick across the rim does that. Note that the BSA has since discouraged this method over plastic-in-boiling-water concerns, so if that bothers you, run the last variation and scrub the pot. Freezing the egg bags at home is the other half of the trick: eggs are day-one food otherwise, and a frozen bag doubles as the cold pack that keeps everything else in the cooler alive.
