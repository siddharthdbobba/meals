The traditional version simmers fresh blackcurrants with roughly their own weight in sugar until the syrup sets. Fresh berries do not travel, so this uses dried ones and puts the water back at camp. You lose some of the fresh pectin, which is why the citric acid earns its place — it firms up the set and restores the sharpness that all that sugar buries.

The real risk on a stove is scorching. A pot of dissolving sugar has almost no water buffer once the fruit has taken its share, and a canister burner puts all its heat in one small circle. Keep the flame low and the spoon moving. Made hot and eaten within a day or two it needs no jar; if you want it to last the trip, it has to be sealed and it has to be sugary.
