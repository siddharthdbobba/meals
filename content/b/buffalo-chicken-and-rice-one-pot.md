The source cooks wings low and slow in a crockpot, then broils them under a butter-hot sauce-ketchup glaze. None of that survives a trail kitchen: no slow cooker, no broiler, no raw poultry, no cold butter. What survives is the flavor combination, and that is really just cayenne heat, vinegar tang, fat, and a little tomato sweetness. All four exist in powder form.

Rehydrating the chicken before the rice matters. Dropped in together, the rice finishes while the chicken is still leathery. Ghee powder or a hard fat is doing what the butter did in the sauce — it rounds off the vinegar edge and carries the heat. Cut it and the whole pot reads as sour rather than hot.
