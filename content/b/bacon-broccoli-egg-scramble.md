A quiche is eggs, cream, and fat set slowly in an oven. On trail you have none of that: no oven, no crust, no fridge. What survives the translation is the filling, and the filling was always the point. Egg powder plus milk powder rebuilds the custard closely enough, and scrambling it in a pot gets you there in seven minutes instead of fifty.

The order matters more than the ingredients. Broccoli goes in first because freeze-dried florets need real hot water to soften, and if you add them to eggs already setting they stay squeaky. Everything else goes in off the heat so the powder actually dissolves. Bacon goes in last and unheated — it is already cooked, and stirring it through hot eggs is all the warming it needs.
