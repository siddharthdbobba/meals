steps:
  - "Stir the salt and sugar into 4 cups of cold water in a heavy zip bag until you cannot feel grit. Add the chicken, press the air out, seal."
  - "Lay the bag in the cooler for 30 to 60 minutes. Longer is not better. Past an hour the meat goes properly salty."
  - "Build the fire early and let it burn down to coals. You want steady heat under the grate, not flames."
  - "Pull the chicken, pour the brine out well away from camp, and pat each piece dry with a paper towel. Wet meat steams instead of searing."

Chicken breast has almost no fat in it, which is why campfire chicken so often ends up dry and stringy. The brine is insurance. Salt loosens the muscle proteins so they hold water through the heat, and the sugar gives the surface something to brown with over coals that are never quite as even as a stove.

The source recipe finished in a 350F oven, which no one has at a campsite. Ice cubes in a pan are the same trick by another name. Here the indirect side of the fire does that job: sear over the hot coals, then move the meat to the edge under a foil tent so it coasts to done instead of scorching. This one needs a cooler, so it stays a car-camping and paddle dinner, not a backpacking one.
