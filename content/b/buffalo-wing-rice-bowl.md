The source is a tray of oven wings. Bones, an hour of dry heat, and a saucepan of butter sauce are all things a stove in the dirt cannot give you, so what carries over is the flavor logic, not the method. Buffalo sauce is three things: cayenne vinegar heat, fat to round it off, and a little tomato for sweetness. All three are available shelf-stable.

The cornstarch matters more than it looks. Without it the hot sauce runs to the bottom of the pot and the rice on top stays plain. One teaspoon and a minute of heat turns it into something that coats. Add the sauce after the rice has rehydrated, not before, because acid slows the water going in.
