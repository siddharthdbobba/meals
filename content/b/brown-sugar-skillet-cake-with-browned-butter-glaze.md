This started as a home cupcake recipe, which does not survive a campsite: no muffin tins, no oven thermostat, no cold buttermilk. The dutch oven takes the whole batter as one cake instead, and the eggs and buttermilk arrive as powder, which is the only part that actually needed changing. Brown sugar is doing double duty here, since it is both the sweetener and most of the moisture retention. That is why the cake stays edible on day two.

The coal split matters more than the timing. Most of the heat belongs on the lid, because a dutch oven bottom sitting in embers will burn a cake long before the center sets. Browning the butter for the glaze is the one step worth not rushing. Take it off the heat when it smells toasted, and pour it out of the pan immediately, or the residual heat carries it past brown into bitter.
