The dried roots are the point. They spend two hours pulling broth in, and by the end they eat closer to meat than to vegetable. Hominy takes about the same time to bloom open, which is why both go in at the start and why both need an overnight soak first.

Soak the two separately. Hominy water goes into the pot as part of the broth; the root water is milder and gets held back as top-up liquid. Coals, not flame, for the whole cook. The cornflour at the end is there because a long, low simmer thins out more than it reduces, and the stew wants body.
