The original is a baked casserole with three separate cooking steps and a fridge full of dairy. None of that survives a trail. What actually matters is the structure: starch, seasoned beef, tomato, and something creamy over the top. All four keep in powder or dried form, so the whole thing collapses into one pot and one boil.

The order is the only fussy part. Beef rehydrates slower than pasta cooks, so it goes in first with a soak. Tomato powder and sour cream powder go in after the heat is off, because tomato scorches on a hot pot bottom and cultured dairy powder curdles in boiling water. Cook the pasta in exactly the water you plan to eat, since draining a starchy pot in camp wastes both fuel and calories.
