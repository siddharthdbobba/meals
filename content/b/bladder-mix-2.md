This is the drink mix, unbundled. Commercial sports powders are mostly sugar with a few cents of salt in them, and they cost by the sachet. Mixing your own means you set the concentration, and concentration is the whole game: roughly 60 grams of carbohydrate and 500 milligrams of sodium per liter is what a working gut clears without cramping or sloshing.

The citric acid is not decoration. Acid makes a salty drink taste like a drink instead of like broth, which matters when you have to finish four liters in a day. Sugar plus sodium also moves water across the gut wall faster than plain water does, so a hot afternoon goes better on this than on a bladder of tap water.
