Blackening is a dry-heat trick: oil goes on the meat, not the pan, and the spices scorch against hot iron into a crust. Coals matter more than the recipe. An open flame runs too hot and turns paprika bitter before the inside is safe to eat, so wait for the fire to settle.

Thin cutlets are the other half of it. A thick breast forces you to choose between a burnt crust and raw center. Pounded or split thin, the whole thing cooks in about twelve minutes. The lime at the end is not decoration — the acid cuts the fat and the salt load, and it goes on after the pan leaves the heat so it doesn't just steam off.
