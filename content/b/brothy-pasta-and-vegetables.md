Brothy pasta sits in the gap between soup and a noodle bowl. It is mostly water by weight once it is cooked, and that is the point: on a cold night you need to rehydrate as much as you need to eat, and this does both in one pot.

The soak is the part people skip. Dried vegetables take longer to come back than pasta takes to cook, so fifteen minutes in cold water before the flame goes on is the difference between soft carrots and gravel. The oil is not optional either. It carries the broth flavor and adds most of the fat in the bowl. The lemon at the end cuts through the salt.
