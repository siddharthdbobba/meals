Baked oatmeal is a casserole dish that spends an hour in an oven. Everything that makes it worth eating survives the move to a pot: oats, fat, sugar, and a lot of dried fruit and nuts, cooked past the point of porridge until it holds a shape on the spoon. The oven is doing texture work, not flavor work, and a low flame with steady stirring gets you close enough.

Milk powder replaces the half cup of fresh milk and does it better on trail, since it carries fat and protein without weight or a cooler. Eggs are the only real loss. A quarter teaspoon of baking powder makes up a little of the body they gave it. The walnuts matter more than they look like they should: they are most of the fat and most of the reason this holds you until lunch.
