The oven version bakes a russet for an hour and builds a roux on the side. Neither happens on a stove with one pot and a fuel budget, so the potato becomes flakes and the roux becomes powder. Cheese powder and milk powder do the same job the flour and milk were doing: body and dairy fat, without a fridge or a second pan.

Order matters more than technique. The powders go into hot water before the flakes, because once the starch sets you cannot stir a lump out. Buffalo sauce goes in last and off heat; it is mostly vinegar and it thins the whole pot if it boils.
