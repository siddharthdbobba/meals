The source material provided only basil and parmesan romano. These are bare-bones Italian flavors that need a starch base to work as a meal. The dried basil isn't subtle—it does heavy lifting here because there's nothing else to contribute aroma or taste. Romano is sharper and drier than standard Parmesan, which makes it better suited to shelf-stable trail cooking.

This is strategy more than recipe: you're using the starch to carry fat and make the cheese particles coat everything evenly. The oil matters as much as the cheese. Without it the pasta is just seasoned dry carbs.
