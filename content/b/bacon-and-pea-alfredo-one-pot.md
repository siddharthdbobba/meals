Draining pasta on the trail usually means throwing away the one thing that makes a powdered sauce behave. Here you keep a cup and a half of it. The starch suspended in that water gives the alfredo mix and milk powder something to grab, so the sauce clings instead of pooling at the bottom of the pot.

The milk powder and butter powder do most of the heavy lifting. A packet of alfredo mix on its own is thin and salty; the added fat and dairy solids build it back into something with body. Freeze-dried peas rehydrate in the same boil as the pasta, which is the only reason they are worth carrying. Take the pot off the heat before the parmesan goes in, or it will string and separate.
