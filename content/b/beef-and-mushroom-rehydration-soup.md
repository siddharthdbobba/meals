This soup works because three separate things happen during the fifteen-minute rest. The orzo rehydrates into its normal starchy state. The jerky softens and dissolves its salt and savory notes into the broth. Dried mushrooms bloom and release umami. The milk powder dissolves into a creamy suspension. You are not cooking; you are waiting for the broth to absorb what it contains.

The butter and flour are the calories. The jerky and mushrooms are the flavor and substance. Everything else—milk powder, beef base, onion—is infrastructure. One sandwich bag carries dinner for two. One minute of heat brings it to a boil. The rest happens on its own.
