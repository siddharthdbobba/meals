Mealie bread is a braai side from South Africa, and the version that works over a fire is the one built around a tin of creamed corn. The corn is the liquid, the starch, and the sweetness all at once, which is why this holds together without milk or buttermilk. That matters in camp: nothing here needs a cooler.

The two ways to ruin it are overmixing and direct heat. Stir past the point where the flour vanishes and the gluten tightens into something rubbery. Set the pot over live flame and the bottom is charcoal while the middle is still batter. Coals under, more coals on the lid, quarter turns, and a skewer test at 45 minutes.

Dried whole egg replaces the two fresh eggs and oil replaces the butter. Both are straight swaps and neither changes the crumb much.
