A real banh mi runs on a crackly baguette, fresh cucumber, and pickles made days ahead. None of that rides well. Tortillas take the place of the roll because they pack flat and do not crumble, and dehydrated slaw rehydrated in vinegar and sugar does the job of the quick pickle in about half an hour of soaking time you were spending anyway.

The drain step is the one that matters. Rehydrated cabbage holds far more water than it looks like, and a wrap built on wet slaw comes apart in your hands. Mayo is doing double duty here: fat for calories, and a barrier between the tortilla and everything damp. Shelf-stable single packets survive heat that a jar would not.
