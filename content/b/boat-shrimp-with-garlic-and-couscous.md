This started as a five-minute skillet dish off a boat in the Pacific Northwest: shrimp, garlic, heat, nothing else. The point was never the technique. It was leaving the seafood alone so it still tasted like seafood.

On the trail there is no fresh shrimp and no skillet, so the work moves to rehydration. Freeze-dried shrimp go rubbery if you rush them, hence the long covered soak before anything else happens. The couscous then cooks in that same shrimp water, which is where most of the flavor ended up. Garlic and oil go in at the end, off the heat — garlic granules scorch fast, and heating good olive oil wastes it.
