This is a preserving recipe carried into camp, and the whole trick is restraint. Sugar equal to the weight of the fruit pulls out enough juice on its own, so no water goes in the pot and nothing has to boil off. Stirring is what breaks the berries down into ordinary jam, so you swirl the pot and skim instead. What you get is fruit suspended in syrup, still recognizably berries.

Call this a day-one recipe. Fresh berries do not travel past the first night, and the finished jam sits in an open pot rather than a sealed jar, so eat it within a day or two. If you want it to keep, do the canning at home and carry the jar.
