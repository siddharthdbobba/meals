Chicken breast dries out over a fire faster than anything else you might cook on one. The bacon is the fix. It bastes each chunk from the outside while the fat renders, and it renders into the pot rather than into a fire pan, so the sauce that follows has something to hold onto.

The two-stage heat matters. Frying with all the coals underneath renders bacon fat and browns the outside. Only then does the sauce go in, because teriyaki is mostly sugar and sugar over direct heat turns black before the chicken is done. Moving coals to the lid finishes the inside with gentler heat from above. This one needs a cooler, so it is a first-night or car-camping dinner, not a backpacking one.
