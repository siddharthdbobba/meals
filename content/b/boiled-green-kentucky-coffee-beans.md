Green Kentucky coffee beans are one of the few wild legumes big enough to be worth the labor. The mature seeds are famously armored and are usually roasted; the unripe ones are soft enough to shell with a pocket knife, which is what makes them a camp food instead of a project. Late August is the window. Once the pods go brown and hard, the recipe is over for the year.

The long hard boil is the whole recipe, not a suggestion. Raw and undercooked seeds from this tree make people sick, and forty-five minutes at a genuine rolling boil is what fixes that. Drain the water and do not drink it. After that they behave like any cooked bean: bland, starchy, and improved almost entirely by fat, salt, and heat, which is why the oil and garlic go in at the end rather than the beginning. This one is day-one only, because you are eating something you picked that afternoon.
