The original bakes in a hot oven, which is not something a stove gives you. A covered pot on a low flame does the same job slower: the bottom crisps by direct contact and the trapped steam finishes the top. Deep dish helps here, since a thick crust tolerates uneven heat far better than a thin one.

Fresh onion and bell pepper turn into dried versions that rehydrate in the same pot before the dough goes in, and the canned chickpeas become dried ones. The gouda stays as is because waxed hard cheese does not need a fridge. Use a barbecue sauce with some heat in it. Dried onions and peppers both go sweet when rehydrated, and a sweet sauce on top of that is too much.
