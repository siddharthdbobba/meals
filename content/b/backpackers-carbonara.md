Real carbonara is an emulsion: egg and cheese held together by starchy water and fat, not cream. That works on a stove as long as you respect the temperature. Egg proteins set around 150°F, so the pot comes off the flame before the egg goes in, and the leftover starchy water does the emulsifying.

The trail adaptation is mostly about fat and food safety. Fat blocks dehydration and goes rancid, so it travels separately in a bottle instead of being dried into the meal. Raw egg should never be dried at home — commercial pasteurized egg powder is the only version worth carrying. Parcooking the pasta in stock at home and drying it cuts camp boil time to a couple of minutes, which matters more than it sounds when fuel is finite. Parmesan is the one perishable, and a vacuum-sealed wedge holds about a week without a cooler.
