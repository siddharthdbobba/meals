Layering is the whole method. The bacon goes in first and renders into a slick of fat on the bottom of the oven, which is what keeps the potatoes from welding themselves to the cast iron. The onions sit in that fat and go soft and sweet; the potatoes sit on the onions and steam in their own moisture under the lid. No water is added and none is needed.

Six servings is the ceiling in a 12-inch oven. Past that the pile gets deep enough that the top layer is still raw when the bottom is browning. In a 10-inch oven, cut the amounts to about two thirds and leave the heat and the clock alone. This one stays a car-camping recipe — raw potatoes are heavy and bacon needs a cooler — but the variations get you close without either.
