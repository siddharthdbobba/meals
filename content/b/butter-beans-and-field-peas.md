The original is a cast iron dish built on fresh shell beans and homemade sausage, neither of which survives a pack. Dried beans do the same job if you soak and dehydrate them at home. That step is the whole recipe, really: it turns a two-hour simmer into fifteen minutes on a canister stove, which is the difference between possible and not.

The butter and lemon go in off the heat and they matter. Beans cooked in plain water taste like plain water. Fat gives the liquid body, and the acid cuts through it so the pot does not read as flat and starchy. Salt at the end too, since salting early keeps bean skins tough.
