The original is a college-apartment skillet: two pounds of ground elk, frozen stir fry vegetables, four packs of ramen. None of that travels. Home-dried ground game and dried vegetables get the same dish down to about six ounces a person, and the water comes from the creek instead of the freezer.

Order matters more than technique. Meat and vegetables need the full simmer to come back; ramen needs five minutes and no more. Run them together and you get soft noodles floating over hard carrot. The oil goes in last because drying strips fat out of the meat, and without it this reads as salty broth rather than dinner.
