The original is a skillet dish that finishes under a broiler with ricotta, fresh mozzarella, and a jar of sauce. None of that travels. Eggplant and zucchini dry well, though, and they rehydrate into something close to sauteed rather than mush, because both are mostly water to begin with. The jar of sauce becomes tomato powder or a sauce leather dried on a tray at home.

Cooking the pasta in the same water that rehydrates the vegetables is what keeps this to one pot. There is no draining, so the starch stays in and thickens the sauce the way the sauce jar used to. The cheese lid at the end is the broiler step: low-moisture mozzarella under a closed lid on a hot pot melts in about three minutes. Fresh ricotta and mozzarella balls are gone and not missed much.
