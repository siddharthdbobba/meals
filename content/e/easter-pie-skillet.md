The original is a baked pie: cured meats and ricotta suspended in egg under a crust, cut cold. Nothing about that survives a backpack. What does survive is the ratio — a lot of salty cured meat, a modest amount of egg, enough dairy to soften it. Cook that in a pot and you get the same thing without the pastry.

Dry-cured meat is the reason this works on trail: soppressata and pepperoni are shelf-stable by design, and they render enough fat to cook the eggs in. Powdered whole milk replaces the ricotta. It will not be creamy the same way, so the parmesan at the end does the work of putting salt and body back. Low heat matters more than anything else here — powdered eggs go rubbery fast.
