Espresso in a rub is not a gimmick. Ground coffee is bitter and dark-roasted, which is the same register as char, so it reads as more fire than the fire actually gave you. The brown sugar is there to caramelize on contact with the grate, and the two teaspoons is a ceiling, not a suggestion. More than that and the rub burns black before the pork is done.

The original is a sear-then-oven recipe, which nobody has at camp. A two-zone fire does the same job: hot coals for the crust, the cooler edge of the grate to finish the middle without scorching the outside. Bone-in chops are the right cut for this because the bone slows the center down and gives you room to be imprecise. Rest them. Cutting early dumps everything you just cooked into the dirt.
