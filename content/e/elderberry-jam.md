This one is only worth doing where the elderberries are, which means a basecamp or a car site in late summer with time to burn. It is a two-ingredient recipe pretending to be more: berries and sugar, near equal by volume, boiled until enough water leaves that the sugar concentration sets. The lemon juice is not for flavor. Elderberries are low in acid, and without it the pectin never gels and you get syrup.

The long boil is the whole job. Elderberries are also low in pectin, so the set comes from evaporation rather than chemistry, and that takes half an hour of stirring over a flame you have to keep steady. The cold-plate test is the only honest way to know when to stop. A campfire works if you cook over coals at the edge rather than flames, since the sugar burns fast once it thickens. Strip the stems carefully. They are not food.

The finished jars are shelf-stable until opened, which makes this a rare camp recipe you carry home instead of eating on the spot.
