This is a leftovers recipe, which means it only exists on day two of a car camp — the night you grilled backstrap, then the morning after. Cooked wild game reheats badly. Elk is lean enough that a second pass over real heat turns it to shoe leather, so the chiles go in the pan first and the sliced meat goes in last, just long enough to warm through.

Roasted Hatch chiles carry the whole thing. They bring moisture and heat that the lean meat does not have on its own, and cotija adds salt without melting into grease the way cheddar would. This one needs a cooler and real eggs, so it is a basecamp or truck-camp breakfast, not something to carry on your back.
