Fresh eggplant is mostly water, which is why the original recipe blanches it before baking. Dehydrating does the same job in reverse: the cell structure is already broken down, so rehydrating in salted boiling water gets you soft cubes without a pot of grey liquid to pour off later. That also cuts the weight by about eighty percent.

The rest is a Dutch oven doing what an oven does. Coals on the lid brown the crumb topping from above while the tomato reduces underneath. Toast the panko at home in oil, because a lid full of coals browns unevenly and pre-toasted crumbs give you a margin. Parmesan is the one dairy item that survives out of a cooler; nothing here needs a fridge except the butter, and only in heat.
