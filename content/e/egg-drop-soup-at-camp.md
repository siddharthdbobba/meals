Restaurant egg drop soup depends on fresh eggs and a fridge, so this version runs on whole egg powder rehydrated in a mug before it ever touches the pot. That step is not optional. Dry powder dumped into hot broth clumps into yellow gravel instead of ribbons.

The other two rules carry over from a home kitchen unchanged. Cornstarch thickens the broth just enough to suspend the egg rather than let it sink, and the pot has to come off the heat before the egg goes in. Rolling water tears the strands apart. Pour slow, stir once, then leave it alone.
