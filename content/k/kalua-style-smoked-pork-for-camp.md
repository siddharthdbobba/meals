Kalua pork is an imu pit compressed into a slow cooker: smoke from a bottle, salt with real minerals in it, and enough time that the collagen gives up. None of that happens on trail, so the cooking is home work. The pot at camp is only doing rehydration.

The rinse step matters more than it sounds. Pork butt is a fatty cut, and fat does not dehydrate, it just sits there and goes off in your pack. Washing the shreds and adding oil back at dinner separates the two problems: keeping the meat stable and getting the calories in.
