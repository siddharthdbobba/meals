This started as a power-outage plan: a kettle, boiling water, and shelf-stable food that asks nothing more of you. On the trail that describes every dinner already, so the only real change was cutting the water down to what one pot holds and swapping canned chicken for a foil pouch.

The potato flakes do the structural work. A soup packet alone is thin and gets cold fast; flakes turn the same water into something with body that holds heat while you eat it. Fat and protein come from the oil and the chicken, and both go in last so nothing sits over heat and turns stringy.
