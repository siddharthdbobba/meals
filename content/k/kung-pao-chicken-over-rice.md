The original is a wok dish built on high heat and fresh aromatics, neither of which a canister stove offers. What survives the translation is the sauce: soy, vinegar, sugar, and cornstarch in the right ratio, which thickens the same on a backpacking stove as it does in a wok. Garlic powder and ground ginger stand in for grated fresh, and dried pepper and celery flakes replace the vegetables that would rot by day two.

Bloom the chilies and peppercorns in oil first. That step is not optional flavor-wise, since it is the only heat you get and it takes half a minute. The shrimp from the source does not travel without refrigeration, so this is chicken only. Peanuts go on last so they stay dry, and the rice steams off-heat while everything else cooks, which keeps it to one pot.
