  - "At home, two to three days out: grind the dried koji fine and roll the trimmed backstrap in it until the whole surface is coated."
  - "Set the meat on a wire rack over a tray, uncovered, in the fridge for about 50 hours. The rack matters. On a flat surface the meat sits in its own drawn-out moisture and the coating goes slimy."
  - "Brush and wipe the koji crust off. Wrap the loin tight and pack it cold. Cook it the first night out."
  - "In camp, get a cast iron skillet over the fire or on the grate until it barely smokes. Add the oil."

Koji is a mold grown on rice, and it makes enzymes that break down protein the same way a dry-aging locker does — just faster, because you are putting the enzymes directly on the meat instead of waiting for the meat's own to work. Two days of a koji crust gets you somewhere near six weeks of hanging, without needing a controlled chamber or a cut thick enough to survive the trim loss. Venison is lean and short on the fat cap that protects a real dry-aged ribeye, so the fast version is the only version that makes sense here.

All the work happens at home. What you carry is a finished piece of meat and a plan for one pan. Keep the seasoning to salt: the aging is the flavor, and a marinade buries it. The butter goes in late on purpose — it burns if it goes in with the oil, and its job is basting at lower heat, not searing. Pull it earlier than feels right. Lean meat carries over hard and there is no fat to hide overcooking.
