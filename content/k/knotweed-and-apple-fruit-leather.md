Knotweed tastes like rhubarb and behaves like it too: sour, stringy, and full of water. Cooking it down with an equal weight of apples solves both problems at once. The apples supply the sugar and pectin that turn a watery puree into a sheet that holds together, and they hide the stringy texture that puts people off cooked knotweed.

This is all home-kitchen work, which is the point. What you carry is a dry strip with no water weight, no cooler, and no packaging to speak of. Get it fully dry before it goes in a bag — leather that is still tacky in the middle will mold weeks into a season, and you will not notice until you unroll it in the field.
