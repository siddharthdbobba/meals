Home versions of this sauté fresh kale in broth until the pan goes dry. On trail that step does double duty: the broth is the rehydration water, so dried kale and dried peppers come back to life and the bouillon replaces the flavor that drying takes away. Six minutes of simmer is enough for kale, which is why it beats spinach here.

The eggs go in second, in a wiped pot, over the lowest heat you can manage. Powdered egg has no fresh water buffering it and will stick and brown if you rush. Mixing it cold in a bottle first is the difference between scrambled eggs and yellow gravel. The avocado is the one thing here with a clock on it, so treat it as a day-one luxury rather than part of the recipe.
