Kasha is roasted buckwheat, and the roasting is what makes it a trail grain. The groats hold their shape in a 2:1 water ratio and finish in about ten minutes, so a full dinner costs one boil and a short simmer. Raw buckwheat does not do this. It collapses.

The dried mushrooms are doing two jobs. They rehydrate into something with texture, and their soak water becomes the cooking liquid, which is where the flavor actually lives. The original uses fresh mushrooms, fresh onion, and butter; dried mushrooms, onion flakes, and ghee cover all three without a cooler. Fat at the end matters more than it looks — buckwheat is dry and the ghee is most of what makes it eat like a meal.
