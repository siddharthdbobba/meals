Foil packets work because the sealed pouch steams and roasts at the same time. The potato and onion release water, the fat from the beef renders down into it, and none of it escapes. That is why the crimp matters more than the fire does. A loose seam leaks the liquid onto the coals and you get a scorched packet with raw potato in the middle.

Thin potato slices are the other half of it. Potato takes longer than ground beef, so cutting it to about an eighth of an inch is what makes thirty minutes enough for both. The double foil layer is insurance: coals are uneven and a single sheet tears when you flip it. This one needs a cooler, so it belongs at the truck. The corned beef version below carries without one.
