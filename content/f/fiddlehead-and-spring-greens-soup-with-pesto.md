This is a spring soup that happens to work in a camp pot, which is most of the reason to make it. Everything except the wild greens can be carried dry: bouillon instead of stock, freeze-dried beef or a stick of summer sausage instead of fresh meat, parsnips prepped at home. What you pick up on the walk in is the part that matters.

The method is doing the work, not the ingredient list. Browning the meat hard and deglazing with wine builds a broth out of nothing but water and bouillon. The pesto goes on at the table because heat kills it. Skip the cream that shows up in most fiddlehead soups; fat from the oil and the pesto is enough, and cream is a bad idea without a fridge anyway.
