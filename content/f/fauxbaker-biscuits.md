This is steam baking, not baking. The dough never touches the pot: it sits in an open bag on a perforated disc above an inch of simmering water, and steam does the work an oven would. That means you need a fraction of the water and a fraction of the fuel a boil-and-drain method would burn, and the pot comes off the stove dry and clean.

Two things decide whether it rises. The dough has to be stiff enough to hold its shape, so add water slowly and stop early. And the lid stays on the whole twenty minutes, because a rush of cold air will flatten a raised batter and it will not come back. The five-minute rest off the heat lets the center finish on residual steam.
