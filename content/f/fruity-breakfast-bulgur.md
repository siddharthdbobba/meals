Bulgur is already parboiled, so it does not need a real simmer — only hot water and time. That is the whole trick. One boil, then fifteen minutes of sitting while you pack up, and the grain finishes on stored heat instead of fuel. It stays chewy rather than going to porridge, which is either the point or the dealbreaker depending on how you feel about oatmeal.

The dried fruit does double duty: it rehydrates alongside the grain and pulls the sugar through the whole pot instead of leaving a sweet layer on top. Powdered milk covers the dairy without a cooler. Granulated honey is the easier carry — liquid honey goes in at the end, since stirring it into boiling water just glues it to the pot.
