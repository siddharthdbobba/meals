steps:
  - "Pour 400 ml of boiling water over the dried mushrooms in a mug or bowl. Leave them 20 minutes. Keep the soaking liquid."
  - "Simmer the daikon rounds in salted water until a knife slides in with slight resistance, about 10 minutes. Drain and pat them as dry as you can. Wet daikon will not brown."
  - "Wipe the pot, heat the oil, and dredge each round in flour. Fry in batches until both sides are brown. Set them aside on a plate."
  - "Chop the soaked mushrooms. Add the butter and onion flakes to the pot, then the mushrooms. Cook until they stop releasing water."

Daikon is mostly water, which is why it needs two cooks. The simmer softens the middle; the fry drives off surface moisture and gives the flour something to crisp against. Skip the drying step and you get pale, steamed rounds.

The original is a restaurant plate with a made-from-scratch ragu. Here the sauce comes from an ounce of dried mushrooms and their soaking water, which is the whole reason this works in camp: it is stock you carry dry. The flour left in the dredging bag thickens it, so nothing is wasted and there is one pot to wash.
