The original is a pressure-cooker dish built on white wine, clam juice, fresh potatoes, and lemon. None of that carries. Bouillon and tomato powder rebuild the broth, potato flakes do the thickening and the starch in one move, and citric acid gives back the acid that the wine and lemon were providing. The dill is the one thing worth carrying as-is; it is what makes this read as fish stew and not generic soup.

The fish goes in last and barely cooks, same as in the kitchen version. Freeze-dried fish rehydrates in about five minutes of low simmer and turns to mush past that, so pull the pot early and let the lid finish it. Add the potato flakes off the heat too, a little at a time. Dumped in all at once they seize into a lump you cannot stir out.
