This is a fire-coal dessert, so the peel is the cookware. It holds the banana's shape while the inside goes soft and the fillings melt, and it means the only thing to clean up is a ball of foil. The hinge matters: leave the flap attached at one end and it folds back over the filling like a lid, which keeps chocolate off the foil.

Coals, not flames. Direct flame scorches the peel before the middle gets warm, and four minutes over open fire will give you a black banana with cold candy in it. Bananas are the one perishable here, so this is a first-night item — they bruise fast and won't survive day three in a pack.
