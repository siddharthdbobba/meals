This started as an oven recipe and the oven was doing two jobs: gentle heat first, then a hot blast to set the glaze. A fire does both, just in the wrong order unless you manage it. The foil packet is the fix. Sealed, it traps steam and cooks the chicken through at a temperature the coals do not control precisely. Opened, the same fire caramelizes sauce in five-minute passes.

Saucing three times matters more than the sauce you pick. One heavy coat slides off and drips into the coals. Three thin ones dry down between passes and build a layer that stays put. Bone-in dark meat is here because it tolerates the ten-minute window where you are guessing about the fire. This one needs a cooler, so it is a first- or second-night meal, not a backpacking dinner.
