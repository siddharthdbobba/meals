The original is a slow cooker dish: onions cooked down in butter and sugar, then eight hours in beef broth, then a bowl broiled under cheese. None of that survives contact with a trail. What does survive is the caramelization, and that is the only part that actually takes time. Do it at home in a pan, spread the onions on dehydrator trays, and dry them hard. A large onion cooks down to almost nothing and weighs almost nothing.

Everything after that is a ten-minute pot. Bouillon replaces the stock, Worcestershire powder replaces the bottle, and dried thyme was already the source's choice. The bread and cheese are the point of French onion soup, so they stay: sourdough dried into croutons instead of a broiled slice, and real gruyere laid on the surface under a lid. Noodles turn it from soup into dinner, because soup does not carry enough calories to justify the fuel.
