This is a car-camping or basecamp job, not something to carry up a hill. Deep frying wants a real pan, an inch of oil, and a stove you can hold at a low simmer for the sauce and a steady 340F for the fry. Halloumi is the one cheese that earns the trouble: it holds its shape at heat instead of running, so the outside crisps while the inside goes soft.

The double breading is not fussiness. Flour, egg, flour, egg, then panko builds a shell thick enough to contain melting cheese. One thin coat splits and you end up frying a puddle. The sauce takes 45 minutes because canned tomatoes need that long to lose their tinny edge, and the whole onion and unpeeled garlic flavor it without leaving you chopping by headlamp. Halloumi is vacuum sealed and stable until you open it, but once cut it wants the cooler, so plan it for the first night or two.
