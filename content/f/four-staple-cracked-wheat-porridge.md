This comes out of the standard long-term food storage list, which is mostly four things: whole wheat, dry milk, sugar, and salt. Those four keep for decades in a bucket, which is a strange thing to have in common with a backpacking meal, but the reasons overlap. No refrigeration, no fragile fats, high calories per ounce.

Whole wheat berries will not cook in a reasonable amount of stove fuel. Cracking them at home is the whole trick — coarse pieces hydrate in about twelve minutes instead of an hour. The milk powder and butter powder go in after the flame is off, because both scorch and clump if they hit a hot pot bottom.
