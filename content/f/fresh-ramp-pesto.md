Ramp pesto trades pine nuts for toasted sunflower seeds. The sunflower oil carries the fat that makes pesto work as a condiment—it binds the whole thing and makes the brightness of the ramps stick around. Seeds and oil are where most of the calories come from.

The key to fresh pesto is minimal processing. Chop the leaves by hand first, then pound or pulse gently, and stop while it's still chunky. Over-blending damages the cell structure and turns the whole thing brown. Most of what you're tasting should be the ramp itself—green, sharp, herbaceous—not a uniform sauce.
