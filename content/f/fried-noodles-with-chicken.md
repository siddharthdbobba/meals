This is a two-stage noodle dish and the second stage is the whole point. Boiling ramen and eating the broth gives you soup. Draining it and putting the noodles back on heat with oil gives you browned edges and a sauce that clings instead of pooling. The seasoning packet becomes a dry rub rather than a broth base, which is why one packet does the work of two.

The original called for a cooked chicken breast, which means a cooler and a cutting board. A foil pouch gets you the same protein with no fridge and no knife. Oyster sauce is the other carry: it is shelf-stable, it is sugar and salt and glutamate, and two tablespoons will season a whole pot. Drain step matters more than it sounds. Wet noodles steam instead of frying, and you end up back at soup with extra steps.
