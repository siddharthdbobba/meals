Freeze-dried stew is one of the few commercial meals worth its price on a cold night, because the potatoes and carrots rehydrate into something with actual texture instead of mush. The beef is the reason: freeze-drying keeps diced meat intact in a way home dehydrating rarely does.

The honest problem is calories. A two-serving pouch runs light for a full dinner after a long day, so the oil is not optional if this is your only hot meal. Potato flakes do the same job and thicken the broth, which matters when you are eating out of a bag with a long spoon.

Water goes in boiling, not warm. Below freezing the pouch loses heat fast, and lukewarm water leaves you chewing dry carrot.
