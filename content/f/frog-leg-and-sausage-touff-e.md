An étouffée is a roux with vegetables in it and something tender cooked on top. Everything here happens in one Dutch oven, which is the only real reason this belongs at a camp kitchen instead of a home stove. The roux is the part that demands attention: butter and flour over steady heat, stirred without pause, for a quarter hour. Coals give you more even heat than a burner does, but they also keep giving heat after you want them to stop, so pull the oven off the fire the moment the color is right.

Frog legs cook fast and go rubbery faster. They go in last, on top of an already-finished sauce, and they only need long enough for the meat to loosen from the bone. The sausage is the opposite: it is already cooked and cured, so it can simmer the full half hour and spend its fat into the sauce. That fat plus the roux is what makes this hold together on a plate rather than running off the rice.
