This is a method more than a recipe. A whole russet is mostly water, and the foil traps that water so the potato steams itself from the inside while the coals work on the outside. An hour is not a suggestion — a big potato has a lot of mass to heat through, and pulling it early gets you a raw core with a soft edge.

The fork holes matter more than the butter. They let the steam out gradually instead of all at once. The butter is there because the inside is bland starch with nothing in it, and fat carried into hot potato is most of what makes it worth an hour of firewood. Whole potatoes are heavy, so this stays a car-camping and basecamp dish; nobody should carry these up a hill.
