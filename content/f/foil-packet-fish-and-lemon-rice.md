This is a two-fire dinner that only needs one. The rice cooks by absorption on a stove or a corner of the coals, and while it steams off-heat for its final ten minutes, the fish goes on the grate. Both are ready at the same time and neither needs watching.

The foil packet is doing the real work: it traps steam so a lean fillet cooks through without drying out, and the liquid it releases becomes the sauce for the rice. The original called for fresh parsley, fresh zest, and white wine — none of which survive a cooler well. Dried parsley and lemon peel rehydrate in the packet's own steam, and lemon slices or bottled juice cover the acid. The fish is the only thing here that needs cold, so this is a car-camping or paddle-in dinner, night one.
