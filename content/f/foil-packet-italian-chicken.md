The original is a home dish that leans on fresh mozzarella and ripe tomatoes, neither of which survives a weekend outdoors. Sun-dried tomatoes in oil do the same job with no refrigeration and more concentrated flavor, and vacuum-packed aged cheese holds in a cooler far longer than a fresh ball does. Dried basil replaces the leaves; giving it a few minutes in the oil is what keeps it from tasting like dust.

Cooking the chicken in its own sealed foil packet is the point. The meat steams in its own juices instead of drying out over open flame, and there is no pan to scrub. Wait for coals, not flames — a foil packet over live fire chars the outside while the center stays underdone, which with chicken is a real problem rather than a texture complaint.
