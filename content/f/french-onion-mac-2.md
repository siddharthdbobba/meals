The original is a bechamel baked under breadcrumbs, which is three pans and an oven. Neither travels. What survives the translation is the part that actually matters: onions cooked down until they are sweet and dark, sitting in something cheesy and starchy. Everything else was structure holding that up.

The roux is gone. Cornstarch plus the pasta water does the thickening, and the milk powder replaces the two and a half cups of milk without carrying any of the weight. Cheese powder gives you a sauce that will not break; the wedge of real cheese gives you flavor the powder cannot. Use both. Dehydrating the onions is the only step that takes real time, and it happens at home, weeks ahead.
