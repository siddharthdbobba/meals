Baked haddock is a fish-house standard: butter, crumbs, heat, nothing else. That is a casserole dish in a home oven, so the change here is foil. A sealed packet traps the fish's own moisture and steams it while the coals brown the top, which gets you the same result without an oven or a pan to scrub.

The butter is not a garnish. Lean white fish dries out fast over live fire, and the fat in the crumb layer is what keeps the top from turning to sand. Use less than six tablespoons and you will notice. This one needs a cooler and there is no way around it, so it is a first-or-second-night meal, not something you carry for a week.
