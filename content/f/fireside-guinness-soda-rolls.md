Yeast bread is a bad fit for camp. It wants hours, a warm room, and attention you do not have after a long day. Soda bread trades all of that for a chemical rise: the bicarb hits the acid in the buttermilk and the lift happens in the pan, so the dough goes from bowl to coals with nothing in between.

The one rule is to barely touch it. Knead soda dough and you build gluten, which traps the gas and gives you hard little bricks. Pat it together and stop. The stout is not just flavor, though it does bring a dark malty edge: it keeps the crumb moist for a second day, which matters when these are meant to mop out a stew bowl.

This one needs a cooler and a real fire, so it is a car-camping or basecamp recipe, not something to carry up a hill. Powdered buttermilk makes it shelf-stable if that is what you need.
