The potatoes are insulation, not a side dish. Coals are hotter than any burner and they do not spread heat evenly, so a chop sitting on bare foil scorches on one face while the other stays raw. Sandwiched between two layers of potato, it cooks by steam and conducted heat instead, and the potatoes take the abuse.

Honey and mint are doing two jobs. The honey caramelizes against the meat and gives the packet enough sugar to brown something in an otherwise wet environment; dried mint holds up to that heat far better than fresh, which turns black and tastes like nothing. This one needs a cooler, so it is a car-camping and basecamp recipe, not a backpacking one — there is no honest shelf-stable version of a raw pork chop.
