Frying quail is fine at a stove and miserable at a fire ring for a crowd. Hanging the birds over coals solves the problem: they cook from all sides at once, drip into the fire instead of into a pan, and you can run eight of them at a time with nothing but twine and a frame.

The glaze is the part that needs discipline. Sugar over a coal bed burns fast, so keep the birds high, keep the flames down, and quit brushing before the last ten minutes. The twine also has a job beyond hanging: a trussed bird holds a compact shape and cooks evenly instead of drying out its wings while the breast is still raw.
