This started as an air fryer recipe, which does not help anyone in a campsite. The method transfers cleanly, though: a whole russet only needs dry, steady, all-around heat, and a coal bed gives you that for free once the flames die down. Foil replaces the fryer basket. The oil and salt on the skin still do the same job, drawing moisture out of the surface so it firms up instead of steaming soft.

Coals are the part people get wrong. Flames scorch the foil side facing them and leave the potato raw in the middle, so wait for the fire to settle and use the edge of the bed. Turning every ten minutes is not fussiness, it is the only way to get even heat without a fan. Russets specifically, because the starch is dry and mealy and falls apart when you mash it; waxy potatoes stay dense and disappointing.
