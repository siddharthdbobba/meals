steps:
  - "Burn a fire down to coals, or run a grill at a steady medium. You want heat you can hold a hand over for three seconds, not flames."
  - "Pat the drumsticks dry with a paper towel and season them hard with salt and pepper. Dry skin is the whole game."
  - "Brush on about 3 tablespoons of sauce, then wrap the legs in doubled foil in packets of three or four."
  - "Set the packets on the grate over the coals for 20 minutes, turning once."
  - "Open the foil, flip each leg, and brush on more sauce. Leave the packets open and cook another 10 to 15 minutes so the sauce sets and the skin tightens."

Home ovens hold 400°F without being asked. A fire does not, so the foil does that job instead: it traps steam and cooks the meat through at whatever heat the coals happen to be giving. Opening the packet for the last stretch is what turns it from steamed chicken into something with a crust, because the sauce cannot caramelize while it is sitting in trapped moisture.

This is a cooler recipe, not a backcountry one. Raw chicken needs to stay cold until it hits the fire, so freeze it solid at home and cook it in the first day or two. No water goes in the pot at any point, and the foil comes off as trash, which means nothing to scrub in the dark.
