This is car-camping food, not pack food. A two-pound bone-in cut needs a cooler, a real fire, and a thermometer, and none of that fits in a backpack. What makes it work in camp is that all the fussy parts happen at home: the overnight salt cure and the compound butter are both done days ahead and travel wrapped.

The dry brine matters more than the fire does. Uncovered overnight, the surface dries out and the salt works inward, so the steak browns fast instead of steaming. Coals over flame for the same reason. The butter goes on after slicing, off the heat, where the herbs stay green instead of scorching.
