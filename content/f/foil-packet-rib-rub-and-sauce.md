  - "Lay out a double layer of heavy foil per half-slab. Spread the onion slices down the middle as a bed."

Ribs are a time problem, not a technique problem. The connective tissue needs a couple of hours of low moist heat before it gives up, and a sealed foil packet does that job over coals the same way a slow cooker does it on a counter. The onion bed matters: it lifts the meat off the hot foil so the bottom braises instead of scorching, and the half cup of liquid turns to steam and stays trapped.

This is a cooler recipe, not a pack recipe — raw pork rides on ice and gets cooked the first night. The rub is the part worth doing at home. Five spices measured into one jar means you are shaking a jar at camp instead of opening five bags in the wind. Sauce goes on at the very end only, over direct heat; put it on early and the sugar burns black long before the meat is done.
