This is a car-camping dinner, not a trail meal, and it stays one. Raw ground beef needs a cooler, so the recipe is built for the first night out when the ice is still solid. The condensed soup is doing the work of a stock, a spice cabinet, and a vegetable bag all at once, which is why two cans and a can of water turn a pound of beef into four bowls.

Draining the fat matters. A pound of 80/20 sheds a few tablespoons, and if you leave it in, the soup breaks into a greasy layer that coats the pot and every bowl. Pour it off into the dirt well away from camp, not into the fire, where it flares. The ten-minute simmer at the end is not optional either: straight from the can the vegetables are firm and the broth tastes like a can. Give it the time.
