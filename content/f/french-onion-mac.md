A holiday casserole is really just onions cooked down until they taste like more than onions, plus starch and fat to carry them. None of that requires an oven or a refrigerator. Dried minced onion gets most of the way there if you give it a head start in hot water before the pasta goes in; drop it in cold with everything else and it stays chewy.

The cheese and milk powders go in after the flame is out. Powdered dairy on a live burner turns grainy and sticks to the pot, and scrubbing a pot is the actual cost here. The fried onions on top are not decoration. They are the only crunch in the bowl, and after four days of soft food that matters more than it sounds.
