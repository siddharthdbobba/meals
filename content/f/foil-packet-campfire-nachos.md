Nachos fall apart when they get wet, so the order of operations matters more than the ingredient list. The beef goes in already cooked and drained, and the wet toppings stay out of the packet entirely. Salsa and sour cream go on after the foil is open, once the chips have stopped being exposed to steam.

Two shallow layers beat one tall stack. Heat comes from the bottom of the packet, and a deep pile leaves the top cheese cold while the bottom chips scorch. A grate keeps the foil off the coals, which is the difference between melted and burned. This is a cooler recipe, not a backcountry one: the beef, cheese, and sour cream all need to stay cold until they hit the fire.
