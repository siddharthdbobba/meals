This is a make-ahead dish pretending to be a camp dish. The meatloaf is cooked at home and frozen, so the fire is only reheating meat and actually cooking vegetables. That is why 45 minutes works at all; a raw two-pound loaf buried in coals would still be pink in the middle when the potatoes turned to paste.

The triple foil wrap matters more than the seasoning. Coals run hot and uneven, and a single layer will burn through on one side and leak fat into the fire. Three layers spread the heat and hold the steam, which is what cooks the potatoes. Frozen solid, the loaf also does cooler duty on the drive in. This one needs a cooler and a real fire, so it is a car-camping recipe, not a trail one.
