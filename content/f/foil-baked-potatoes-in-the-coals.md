The original of this is a pressure cooker recipe, which is not a thing you carry. The method that survives the trip is the older one: whole potato, oiled skin, foil, coals. A pressure cooker steams the potato in an inch of water. Coals do it with the potato's own moisture, which is why the skin dries out and crisps instead of going slack.

The oil and salt on the outside are not seasoning so much as construction. Oil conducts heat into the skin and salt pulls the last water out of it, and together they give you the crust the source got from a broiler. Coal heat is uneven, so turn them once and test with a knife rather than trusting the clock. No toppings here that need a fridge — sour cream and butter are the first things to go bad in a cooler, and a potato out of the fire does fine with oil and salt alone.
