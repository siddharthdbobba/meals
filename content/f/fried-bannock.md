Bannock is the workhorse of camp cooking, and frying it in oil makes it stretch far. The dough is forgiving—no rising time, no special equipment, just flour and water that turns crisp and golden. Oil does the heavy lifting here; a little goes far and adds calories without weight.

The oil you choose matters less than the amount. Olive oil is traditional, coconut oil works but needs watching for smoking, and duck fat is genuinely superior if you can carry it. Pick whichever you have; all work fine. The bread itself is shelf-stable dry goods; everything lives in your pack indefinitely.
