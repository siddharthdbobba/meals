Couscous is not really cooked so much as soaked. That is why it belongs in a pack: one boil, no simmer, no fuel burned watching a pot. The bouillon does all the seasoning work, so nothing else has to be measured in the dark.

The fried onions are the reason this beats plain couscous. Half go in with the water and turn soft and sweet through the grain; half go on at the end and stay crisp. They are also mostly fat, which is where the calories come from — a cup of couscous alone is a light meal, not a dinner.
