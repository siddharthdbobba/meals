The original was an oven recipe, which most camps do not have. A lidded cast iron skillet with coals piled on top is the same thing: heat from below, heat from above, eggs that set instead of scramble. That is the only real trick here.

Two shelf-stable swaps. Evaporated milk replaces fresh milk and holds indefinitely until the can is opened, and it is richer, so the custard sets firmer. Cook the potatoes fully before the eggs go in, because once the eggs are in the pan the heat is set by what the eggs need, and undercooked potato stays undercooked.
