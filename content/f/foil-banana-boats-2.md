Bananas are mostly water and starch, and heat converts a lot of that starch to sugar. That is why a ripe banana goes soft and almost caramel-flavored in the coals while an underripe one just goes warm and chalky. The peel does the real work: it holds the shape, keeps the sugar from scorching, and doubles as the bowl you eat out of.

The foil is not optional. Direct coals will burn the peel through and dump the whole thing into the fire. Wrapped, the banana steams in its own moisture, which is also why this needs no added water. Twelve minutes is the window; much longer and it collapses into pudding.
