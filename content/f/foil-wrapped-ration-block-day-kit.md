These are lifeboat rations, not a recipe in the usual sense. Compressed flour, fat, sugar, and added vitamins, pressed into 200-calorie blocks and sealed under foil. They exist because a stove is not always an option and a soft granola bar turns into paste in a hot car. Two of the common brands were held at 200 F for eight hours and both stayed edible; one held its shape considerably better than the other, and both came out darker than they went in. Nothing leaked. That is the whole appeal.

The packaging does the work, so treat it as part of the food. The thin plastic around each block keeps it in one piece under crushing, and the outer foil keeps light, air, and moisture off everything inside. Breaking bars out into a loose snack bag defeats both. What the blocks lack is protein and sodium, which is why the fruit and an electrolyte tab ride along. Use this as a day-one bail-out ration or a pack-bottom reserve, not as your dinner plan.
