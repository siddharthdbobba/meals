Foil packets work because two teaspoons of water is enough to steam a sliced potato. The sealed foil traps that steam and cooks the vegetables through before the outside chars. Cut everything thin and about the same thickness; a thick carrot coin is the piece that leaves you resealing the packet for a third round.

Coals, not flames. Flames scorch the foil at one spot and leave the rest raw, which is why the packet gets flipped halfway. The cheese goes on at the end and off the heat — it only needs residual warmth, and cheddar left over coals separates into grease.
