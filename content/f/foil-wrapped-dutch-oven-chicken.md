This is car-camp cooking and it does not pretend otherwise: a whole raw chicken means a cooler and a long fire. The foil is what makes it work over coals. Coal heat swings, and a bare bird in a hot dutch oven scorches on the bottom long before the thighs are done. Sealed in foil, the chicken cooks in its own steam and the foil takes the abuse instead of the skin.

The seasoning goes in the cavity rather than on the skin because wrapped chicken does not crisp, so anything rubbed outside mostly rinses off in the juices. Inside, it works through the meat as it cooks. Keep the foil seams facing up. Every bit of liquid that escapes is flavor going into the bottom of the pot, and the pot is what you have to scrub.
