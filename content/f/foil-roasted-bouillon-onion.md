An onion is mostly water and sugar, and neither one goes anywhere inside sealed foil. It steams in its own juice, the sugars caramelize against the hot skin, and the bouillon dissolves into the liquid that collects at the center. What comes out is French onion soup without the pot, the stock, or the forty minutes of stirring.

This is not a lightweight meal. A large onion is close to half a pound of water you are carrying up the trail, which is why it belongs at a car campsite or a paddle-in site rather than in a thru-hiker's food bag. Coals matter more than time here: open flame chars the outside long before the core softens. Double-wrap the foil, because a leak drains the broth into the fire and leaves you with a dry roasted onion.
