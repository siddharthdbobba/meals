This started as a Scout patrol exercise: build an oven out of foil and bake biscuits over a tea candle. That constraint is the useful part. A candle or a small alcohol burner puts out very little heat, so the recipe has to survive slow, uneven cooking. Doubled foil with air between the layers does the work of an oven wall — it spreads the heat and stops one hot spot from burning a hole through the bottom before the center sets.

The dough is stiffer than what you would make at home, closer to a shortcrust than a drop biscuit. That is deliberate. A wet dough slumps in foil and steams into paste. Powdered milk stands in for the fresh milk the original called for, and it goes into the mix at home so camp is just water and hands.
