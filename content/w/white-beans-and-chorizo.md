The original is an oven dish: raw sausage seared in a skillet, then roasted over beans and turnip until everything softens. Nothing about that survives a trip, so this version keeps the flavor logic and throws out the oven. Dry-cured chorizo replaces the fresh sausage, since it is already cooked and safe unrefrigerated. Precooked dehydrated beans replace the can, dropping about a pound of water weight per meal.

Rendering the sausage first is the step that matters. That paprika-red fat coats the beans and does the work the oven's long roast used to do. The lemon powder at the end is not decoration either; a pot of beans and cured pork goes flat and heavy without acid, and the tarragon needs something bright to sit against.
