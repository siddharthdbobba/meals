The original is a home recipe: a butter-and-flour roux, milk and heavy cream, pasta boiled and drained in a second pot. None of that travels. Milk powder plus a spoonful of flour does the same job dry, and the pasta water becomes the sauce base instead of going down the drain — which saves a pot, a strainer, and about half a liter of water.

Cheese is the one thing worth carrying wet. A hard aged cheddar keeps for days out of a fridge and melts into a real sauce; powdered cheese does not. Cube it before it goes in, and add it off the heat — boiling a sharp cheddar breaks it into grease and grit.
