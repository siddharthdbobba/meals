The original simmers canned white beans in ale for forty minutes. None of that travels. Dehydrated cooked beans rehydrate in ten and carry a fraction of the weight, and the beer goes away entirely — there is no shelf-stable version of it worth the ounces. Cumin, garlic, and green chile were doing most of the flavor work anyway.

Drying the beans is the step that matters. Rinse the canned beans well, spread them one layer deep, and dry them until they are hard; half-dried beans go rancid in a food bag. Some will break down into starch in the pot, which is what thickens this into chili instead of soup. The avocado is gone for obvious reasons — the oil replaces the fat it was there for.
