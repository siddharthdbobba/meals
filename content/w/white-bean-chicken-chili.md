The original is a stovetop simmer built on canned beans, canned tomatoes, and a cup of broth. On trail all three of those are water you would be carrying, so they come out: bean flakes for the cans, tomato powder for the tomatoes, bouillon for the broth. The result rehydrates in one pot with a single boil and lands somewhere near the same place.

Order matters more than technique. Freeze-dried chicken needs open water and a few quiet minutes to come back; once the bean flakes go in, the pot is thick enough that nothing else absorbs properly. Smoked paprika covers for the fire-roasted tomatoes, and the oil at the end is where most of the calories live, since dried beans and lean chicken are almost none.
