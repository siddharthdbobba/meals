The original wants a cup of sour cream, a cup of chopped onion, and four cups of boxed broth. All three are volume and refrigeration you do not want in a food box, so they become sour cream powder, dried minced onion, and bouillon plus water. The cans of chicken and beans stay. They are heavy, but they are also the entire point, and nothing shelf-stable rehydrates into chicken chunks.

The undrained can of northern beans matters. That liquid is bean starch, and it is what keeps the chili from separating into broth and solids while it sits. The other rule is that the dairy goes in off the heat. Powdered sour cream and jack cheese both curdle if you boil them, and once a pot of chili goes grainy there is no fixing it in the field.
