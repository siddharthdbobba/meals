The kitchen version of this soup leans on a can of tomatoes, a can of beans, frozen vegetables, and a second pot of water for the pasta. None of that travels. Tomato powder, instant bean flakes, and dried mirepoix carry the same flavors at a fraction of the weight, and cooking the pasta in the soup instead of draining it separately means one pot and no wasted starch water.

Order matters more than technique. Vegetables need a head start to rehydrate, pasta needs a real boil, and the bean flakes need to go in last with the stove off, or they glue to the bottom and scorch. The oil and parmesan at the end are doing the calorie work; the soup is thin and forgettable without them.
