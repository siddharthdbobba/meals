This one is Scout-camp cooking, and it works because the apple is its own pot. Foil plus a wet fruit means the inside steams rather than burns, so the coals can be hotter than you would risk with anything else. The sausage is already cooked — the fire is only heating it and letting the fat run down into the softening apple.

Coals matter more than time. Open flame scorches the foil and leaves the core raw; a bed of glowing coals cooks evenly from all sides. Firm apples are worth the trouble too, because a soft variety collapses into sauce around the sausage and there is nothing left to hold.
