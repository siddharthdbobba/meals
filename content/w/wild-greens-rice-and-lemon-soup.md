Dock is one of the easier wild greens to find and one of the easier ones to ruin. It goes soft fast and holds a lot of grit, so the blanch does two jobs: it cleans the leaves and it stops them before they turn to pudding in the soup. Toss the blanch water. It carries the sourness and the sand.

Everything else here is a swap for what a kitchen would give you. Dried onion and garlic rehydrate in the oil, bouillon stands in for stock, and powdered egg does the same thickening work as a fresh one as long as you mix it with cool water first and add it off the boil. Lemon powder and the last glug of oil go in at the end because heat flattens both.
