The original is a long-simmer soup: soak beans overnight, brown sausage, sweat a mirepoix, deglaze with wine, blanch the greens separately. None of that survives a stove that burns fuel by the minute. Instant bean flakes replace the overnight soak and thicken the pot on their own, dried mirepoix stands in for fresh, and hard salami carries the same rendered pork fat without a cooler.

Browning the salami first is the one step worth the extra minutes. Dry-cured meat has little water in it, so it darkens fast and leaves a fond that a bouillon cube alone will not fake. The bitter greens are the point of the dish, not a garnish, so give them the full simmer or they stay leathery.
