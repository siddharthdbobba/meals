Pancakes are a car-camp breakfast because they need a flat pan, a controlled flame, and a spatula. This version moves the whole thing into powders so nothing has to ride in a cooler. Milk, eggs, and pumpkin all come back from dry with water and time.

The pumpkin soak is the only part that takes patience. Half an hour with warm water turns the powder into real puree; rush it and you get sandy batter. Whole wheat pastry flour matters for the same reason it does at home. It has less gluten, so the pancakes stay soft even when a camp cook stirs too long.
