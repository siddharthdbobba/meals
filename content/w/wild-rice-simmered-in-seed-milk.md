Cooking grain in a weak nut milk instead of plain water is old practice, and the reason is nutritional rather than culinary. The fat and protein suspended in the water get absorbed into the grain as it swells. The rice does not come out tasting of nuts. It comes out sitting heavier, which is what you want at the end of a long day.

Use a thin milk, not a thick one. Grinding the whole seeds into the pot would give you a sticky, gluey rice that scorches on a stove with one heat setting. Two tablespoons of ground seed to 600 ml of water is enough. The original calls for fresh hickory nuts, syrup, and butter; toasted seeds, granulated maple sugar, and oil do the same work and survive a week in a pack. Parched wild rice matters more than any of that: paddy rice needs 45 minutes and far more fuel.
