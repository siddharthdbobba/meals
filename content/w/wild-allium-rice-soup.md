The original leans on butter, cream, white wine, and a blender. None of those travel. Rice does the thickening either way, so that part survived the trip intact: it simmers down and clouds the broth without turning it into paste, which is why the source used it instead of flour. Milk powder and olive oil, stirred in off the heat, cover what the cream was doing.

The leaves are the whole point and they go in last. Wild alliums lose their color and their sharpness in under a minute of boiling, so treat them like a garnish you happen to stir under. Fibrous species — field garlic, wild onion — are stringy in a pot you cannot strain, so chop them fine or pick something broad-leafed. Know your plant before you pick it.
