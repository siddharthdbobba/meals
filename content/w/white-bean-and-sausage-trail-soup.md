The original is a pressure-cooker recipe, and pressure is the whole reason it works: dried navy beans go tender in one sealed cycle. A backpacking stove cannot do that. Whole beans want an hour of simmering and most of a fuel canister, so the beans here are instant flakes, which rehydrate off the heat and thicken the pot at the same time.

The rest is substitution. Summer sausage replaces raw links because it is already cured and needs no cooking, only warming so its fat melts into the broth. Tomato powder does what a spoonful of paste did, dried kale does what fresh kale did, and the butter is gone because there is no fridge. Olive oil at the end covers the lost fat and pushes the calories up where a dinner needs them.
