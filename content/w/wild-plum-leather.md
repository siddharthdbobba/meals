  - "At home: pit the plums. Skins stay in — drying is what tames them."

  - "Spread the puree onto silicone mats or lined dehydrator trays, roughly an eighth of an inch thick and even edge to edge."
  - "Dry at 135F until the sheet is leathery and no longer tacky in the middle, 6 to 10 hours. An oven on its lowest setting with the door cracked also works."

Wild plums have good flesh and bad skins — tough, and tannic enough to dry out your mouth. Cooking and pureeing breaks the skins down instead of throwing them away, and something about the drying knocks the tannin back further. You end up carrying the whole fruit at a fraction of the weight.

This is sour, not sweet, so treat it as two things. It is a snack if you like sour, and it is an acid you can carry at zero risk of leaking. A strip stirred into a stew does what a lemon would, which is the traditional use in Georgian cooking. Sugar is optional and pushes it toward the snack end; leave it out if you mostly want the sour.
