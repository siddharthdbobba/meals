This starts as a Romanian preserve — watermelon rind cooked in syrup and jarred — which is the wrong format for a pack. Wet preserves are heavy, and glass is worse. So the syrup step stays and the jar goes: after the rind turns translucent, the pieces come out and get dried down into candy. What is left is sugar and fiber with almost no water weight.

The rind works because it holds its shape. Pectin and cell wall in that pale layer survive an hour of boiling, so the cubes end up chewy instead of collapsing into jam. Equal weight sugar to rind is not a suggestion; below that the pieces stay damp and go moldy in the bag. The lemon juice inverts some of the sugar and keeps the syrup from grainy crystallization, which would leave you with sandy candy.
