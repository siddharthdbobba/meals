The original is a foraged soup built on wapato tubers, duck stock, and cream. None of those travel. Potato flakes do the job the tubers did, since they were acting as starch and body rather than flavor, and milk powder with a spoon of flour rebuilds the cream without a cooler. Parched wild rice keeps its flavor after drying, which is why it is worth cooking at home rather than swapping in something faster.

The morels carry the whole pot. A third of an ounce of dried mushroom weighs nothing and puts more into the broth than the stock base does, so give them the full simmer in plain water before anything dairy goes in. Add the thickener off the heat and the flour disperses instead of seizing into lumps.
