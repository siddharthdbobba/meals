This is a wartime rationing trick, and it works because butter is an emulsion already. Around 16 percent of a block is water held in suspension by milk solids. You can push that fraction higher and the mix will hold, as long as the water goes in slowly and the butter is soft enough to accept it. Add it fast, or add it hot, and the emulsion breaks into a greasy puddle.

The use case on a trip is stretching one block across a long weekend of toast, pancakes, and corn. It is not a backpacking item: the water you added has to stay cold or the whole thing separates, and it halves the calories per spoon, which is the opposite of what you want in a pack. It also behaves badly in baking, so keep a plain stick aside for the dutch oven.
