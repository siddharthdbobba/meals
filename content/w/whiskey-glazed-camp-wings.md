This is car-camping food, not pack food. The wings need a cooler and the glaze needs a second pan, so it earns its place only where weight does not matter. What travels well is the prep: brining, drying, and dredging all happen at home, which means camp is thirty minutes of turning wings and five minutes of stirring sugar.

The cornstarch is the part people skip and regret. It pulls surface moisture and crisps the skin over a grate that never gets as hot or as even as an oven. The glaze goes on at the end for the same reason — brown sugar over live coals burns long before the meat is done.
