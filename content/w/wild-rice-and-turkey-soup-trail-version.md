The home version leans on a butter-and-flour roux, a carcass stock, and half a cup of cream. None of that travels. Potato flakes do the roux's job in ten seconds without a fat to cook, powdered whole milk carries the richness, and bouillon plus dried mushrooms build a stock that tastes like it simmered. The mushroom soak from the original happens on its own here, since the mushrooms rehydrate in the pot and their liquid stays in the soup instead of getting strained off.

Wild rice is the part to get right. Raw wild rice takes forty-five minutes and will empty a canister. Buy a parboiled or quick-cooking blend and check the package time before you leave home. Add the milk powder and potato flakes only after the heat is off, or the milk scorches on the pot bottom and the flakes turn gluey.
