This is the campfire s'more taken apart and rebuilt so it works without a fire. Melting the chocolate first means every marshmallow gets coated evenly, instead of the usual half-melted square sliding off a hot marshmallow onto the dirt.

The low heat matters more than anything else. Chocolate seizes into a grainy paste somewhere around 120°F, and a canister stove blows past that in seconds. Pull the pot on and off the flame and let residual heat in the metal do most of the work. The biscuit crumbs are not decoration: they soak up the melt and give the whole thing enough structure to eat with fingers.
