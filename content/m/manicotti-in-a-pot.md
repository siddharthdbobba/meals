Baked manicotti needs an oven, a piping bag, and an hour. None of that is happening at camp. What actually makes the dish work is the ratio: salty tomato, soft pasta, and enough cheese and fat to bind them. That survives the trip to one pot.

Ricotta and cottage cheese are the parts that cannot travel, so parmesan and a block of low-moisture mozzarella do their job instead. Cooking the pasta in a measured amount of water rather than draining it is the whole trick. The starch left behind is what turns tomato powder into sauce, so do not dump the water.
