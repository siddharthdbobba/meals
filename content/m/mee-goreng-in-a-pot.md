The original is a wok dish: high heat, fresh greens, everything drained and tossed. None of that survives a stove that puts out one flame at one temperature. The fix is to rehydrate first and never drain. The vegetables and meat go in the water that becomes part of the sauce, so nothing is thrown away and nothing is wasted on a second boil.

The sauce carries this. Soy, ketchup, brown sugar, and sambal give you salt, acid, sweet, and heat in four shelf-stable things, and the ramen seasoning packets add the glutamate that would otherwise come from a wok's browning. Mix it at home. Measuring six liquids in the dark is how you end up with a pot of soy sauce.
