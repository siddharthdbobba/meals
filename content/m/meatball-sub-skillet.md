The original is a tray of assembled sliders baked in an oven. Camp does not have an oven, so the sandwich gets deconstructed: same meatballs, same sauce, same cheese and butter and parmesan on top, but the buns go into the pan instead of under it. Torn bread soaks up sauce that would otherwise sit in the bottom of the skillet, and the lid does the work the oven used to.

Halving the meatballs matters more than it sounds. Whole ones roll around and stay cool in the middle; cut open, they take on sauce and heat through in the time you have. The butter and parmesan are not garnish either — they are what keeps the top from tasting like plain wet bread.
