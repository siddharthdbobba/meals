The original is a fridge salad built on fresh tomato, cucumber, and feta, none of which survive a pack. What actually carries the dish is the chickpeas, the olives, and the lemon-oil dressing, so those stay. The vegetables come back as dried pieces, which rehydrate in the same water the chickpeas need anyway.

Feta is gone with no substitute. Nothing shelf-stable tastes like it, and the olives already supply the salt and brine the cheese was there for. Home-dehydrating cooked chickpeas is the one step that matters: raw dried chickpeas will not soften in cold water, no matter how long you wait.
