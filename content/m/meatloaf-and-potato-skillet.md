A meatloaf needs an oven, a pressure vessel, or both, and none of that fits in a pack. The flavors survive the trip anyway. Everything that made the loaf worth eating — sausage fat, the pepper-onion-celery base, the BBQ glaze — works just as well loose in a pot, and the potatoes go in the same pot rather than steaming alongside.

The potato flakes replace the binding job the eggs and the loaf shape were doing. They pull the fat and pan juices into something you can eat with a spoon. Fresh milk becomes powdered milk stirred into the boil, and the diced vegetables get cut at home so camp is browning meat and stirring, nothing else. Meat in a cooler means this is a first-night dinner.
