The original is a home kitchen job: simmered stock, soft-boiled eggs, a chicken breast browned in a pan. None of that survives a trail. The stock becomes bouillon plus a short spice steep, the eggs are gone, and the chicken comes out of a foil pouch already cooked. What is left is the thing that made the dish work in the first place, which is a broth with layers in it.

The spice steep is the step worth keeping. Three minutes of anise, ginger, garlic, and lemongrass in hot water gives you something with depth instead of salt water with noodles in it. Miso goes in last because heat kills it, and the oil goes in last because it is the only fat here and you want to taste it.
