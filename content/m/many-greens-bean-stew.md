The original is a Tuscan poverty dish: thirty-odd wild plants picked in spring, simmered with beans, eaten with a flat corn cake. The point was never a specific green. It was volume and variety — a lot of different plants doing together what none of them does alone. That principle carries to a dehydrator perfectly, because dried greens weigh almost nothing and rehydrate in minutes.

The beans are the structural piece. Bean flakes save you an hour of fuel over dried beans and thicken the pot into stew instead of leaving a bowl of green water. Fresh vegetables, stock, and cured pork all became shelf-stable stand-ins here: dried mirepoix, a bouillon cube, and a splash of oil at the end that does the job the pork fat used to.
