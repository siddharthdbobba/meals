The original is a pan sauce: chicken browned in butter, deglazed with stock, finished with cream and parmesan. None of that survives a week in a pack, so the sauce gets rebuilt from powders. Cream powder and bouillon do the work of stock and heavy cream, and the potato flakes replace the flour dredge as the thickener. You lose the browned bits. You keep the fat, the salt, and the tomatoes, which is most of what the dish actually is.

Sun-dried tomatoes are the one ingredient worth carrying as-is. Buy the dry-packed kind, not oil-packed, and chop them at home; they rehydrate in the hot water while the rest sits. Add the parmesan off heat or it goes stringy and stays that way.
