The original is a restaurant plate: fresh pasta, a hot pan, a splash of wine, cold butter. Almost none of that survives a pack. Dried ravioli replaces the fresh kind, dried mushrooms replace the creminis, butter powder replaces the butter, and the wine becomes mushroom soaking water. The soaking water is the important swap, because it carries most of what wine was there to do, which is deglaze the pan and add acid and depth.

The sear is the only step that costs real effort, and it is worth it. Rehydrated mushrooms hold a lot of water, and if you dump them straight in with the pasta they stay grey and rubbery. Dry pot, hot oil, and one minute of not stirring gets you browning instead. Cook the ravioli first and set them aside so the pot is free for that.
