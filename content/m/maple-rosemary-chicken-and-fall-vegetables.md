The original cooks raw thighs and raw vegetables together in a hot oven for the better part of an hour, basting three times. None of that survives a camp kitchen. What survives is the glaze: maple, dijon, oil, rosemary. Mustard is the part doing the work — it emulsifies the oil and syrup so the mixture clings instead of running off into the foil.

So the cooking is split. Water and heat rehydrate everything first, which is the slow part and needs no precision. Then the foil packet over coals does the fast part: drives off the last moisture and lets the sugar in the syrup catch. Drain hard before the glaze goes on. Leftover water dilutes it and you get a soup instead of a coating.
