The original is hut food: potatoes, butter, sugar, a pan, and a long walk behind you. Real potatoes need twenty minutes of boiling and a pot of water you have to carry or filter, so this version starts from flakes. They rehydrate into a dough stiff enough to hold a cake shape, which boiled potatoes only do after you mash and cool them.

The butter is not a garnish. It is the frying medium and most of the calories, and it is what puts a crust on the outside so the cake doesn't fall apart on the spatula. Low heat matters — sugar in the dough browns fast, and a hot pot gives you a black skin over a raw middle.
