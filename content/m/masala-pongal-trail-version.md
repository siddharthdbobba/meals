Pongal is rice and mung dal cooked past the point where either one holds its shape. That texture is the whole point, and it is why this works on a stove with one pot: everything goes in the same water and the grains do the thickening themselves. Roasting the dal at home matters more than it sounds. Untoasted mung tastes flat and takes longer to soften, which costs fuel.

The masala powder is the part to grind before you leave. Whole spices carried loose take a real pan and a grinder to be useful; two teaspoons of premade powder does the same job at a fraction of the weight. Ghee is the one dairy product that belongs in a pack — the milk solids are cooked out, so it sits at ambient temperature for weeks without turning. It also carries the spice, which broth cannot do.
