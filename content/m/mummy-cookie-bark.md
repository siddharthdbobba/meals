This is a party treat, not backcountry food, so the adaptation is mostly about heat management. There is no microwave and no double boiler, so the pot becomes the water bath: boil, cut the flame, and let residual heat do the melting. White chocolate scorches above about 110 F, and a stove left running under it will turn it grainy in under a minute.

Bark rather than chips matters here. Melting wafers carry more stabilizer, so they set firm at camp temperatures instead of staying tacky and gluing the container shut. The cookies are structural — they hold the chocolate and the eyes, and they are the reason this survives a cooler-free drive in the trunk.
