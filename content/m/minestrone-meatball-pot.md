The original is a fry pan job: brown fresh meatballs, pour in a carton of soup, wilt fresh spinach, toast bread on the side. None of that survives a pack. Fresh meat and a litre of liquid soup are the two heaviest things you could carry, so both get dried. The tradeoff is time — dried meatballs need a real simmer, not a soak, and that is the fifteen minutes.

The browning step is gone and it costs you something. Fat and oil bring back part of what searing would have. Add the parmesan off the heat so it melts into the broth instead of clumping on the pot floor, and add the spinach last so it rehydrates without turning to sludge.
