Millet behaves like risotto rice without the labor. It swells, breaks down at the edges, and turns starchy on its own, so you get the creamy texture from a covered pot rather than twenty minutes of stirring in broth. That matters when fuel is finite and your arm is tired.

The source version was a home kitchen dish with fresh mushrooms, stock, and butter. Dried mushrooms replace all three: they rehydrate in the cooking water and leave behind the dark savory liquid that stock was providing. Hard cheese and olive oil go in off the heat, which is where the fat and most of the calories come from. Millet is naturally gluten-free, which is the one real advantage it has over couscous here.
