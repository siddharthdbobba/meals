This is a five-can dinner with a pound of protein stirred through it, and the canned spaghetti is the part people argue about. It earns its place: the noodles are already soft, the sauce is already thickened, and together they turn beans and tomatoes into something that coats a spoon instead of sloshing around a bowl. No pasta water, no second pot, no draining boiling water into the dirt.

It is not backpacking food. Six cans and two pounds of meat is a cooler and a car. What it is good at is feeding a crowded picnic table cheaply with one pot and one spoon, which is most of what a basecamp kitchen is asked to do. Draining the beef fat is the only step you cannot skip.
