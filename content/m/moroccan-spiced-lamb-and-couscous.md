The original is a fry-pan dish: raw mince rolled into meatballs, seared alongside sliced capsicum, spooned over couscous. None of that survives a trail kitchen. Raw lamb needs a cooler, meatballs need a pan you are not carrying, and fresh mint wilts by day two. So the meat becomes freeze-dried ground lamb, the capsicum becomes dried bell pepper, and both rehydrate in the same water the couscous will use.

Couscous is the reason this fits in one pot. It does not need a boil, only water hot enough and five minutes of standing. That means fuel goes entirely to rehydrating the meat and vegetables, and the grain rides the leftover heat. Add the dried mint early, with the water, not at the end — dried herb needs the steam to open up. The oil is not optional; couscous and lean freeze-dried meat are both nearly fat-free, and without it this is a lot of chewing for not many calories.
