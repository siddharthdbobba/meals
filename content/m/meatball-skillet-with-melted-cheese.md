The original is an oven dish: raw meatballs broiled on a sheet pan, dumped in a casserole, buried in a jar of marinara and two cups of shredded cheese. None of that travels. The rebuild swaps freeze-dried beef for ground, powdered egg and milk for the fridge versions, and tomato powder for the jar, which is where most of the weight went. Everything happens in one pot over a single burner.

The binder matters more here than at home. Rehydrated beef has no fat holding it together, so the panko, egg, and parmesan are structural, not seasoning. Brown the balls before the sauce goes in or they will fall apart in the simmer. Low-moisture mozzarella melts without releasing the water that fresh mozzarella would, so the sauce stays a sauce.
