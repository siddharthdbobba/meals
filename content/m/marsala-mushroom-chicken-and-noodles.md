The original is a skillet dish built on wine reduction and heavy cream, neither of which travels. Cornstarch and powdered whole milk do the same structural job: the starch gives the sauce body once it hits a simmer, and the milk powder carries fat and rounds off the salt. Dijon survives fine in a packet and provides most of the acidity the wine used to.

Soak the chicken and mushrooms first and reuse that water. It is dark, it smells like mushrooms, and dumping it wastes the only real depth in the pot. Add the mustard and oil off the heat, since boiling mustard turns it flat and bitter.

The dish is not vegetarian or gluten-free, so those tags are off. Everything here is nut-free.
