The original poaches eggs and sears pork neck, which needs three pans and a fridge. On a stove in the dirt you get one pot, so the eggs are gone and the pork is a foil packet. What survives is the part that actually carries the dish: peanut butter and miso emulsified into stock, thick enough to coat the noodles instead of pooling under them.

Order matters more than technique here. Peanut butter dropped into boiling water seizes into strings; taken off the heat and beaten into hot stock it goes smooth. Vinegar and chili crisp are there to cut the fat, and without them 900 calories of peanut and sesame oil sits heavy after a long day.
