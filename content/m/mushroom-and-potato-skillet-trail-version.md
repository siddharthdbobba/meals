The original is an oven casserole: blanched potato slices layered over sautéed mushrooms and baked until the top browns. None of that survives a pack. What survives is the flavor logic, which is mushroom, garlic, butter, parsley, and salt on top of starch, and that transfers cleanly to a pot.

Rehydrating the mushrooms first is the whole method. Their soaking liquid becomes the broth the potato flakes drink, so the mushroom flavor ends up through the mash instead of sitting on it. Season the water before the flakes go in; once it thickens, salt stops distributing and just sits in pockets. Fat is added last so it does not get boiled off, and it is what turns a starchy mash into a dinner.
