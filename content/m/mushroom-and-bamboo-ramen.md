The original is a home kitchen soup with fresh aromatics and a long simmer. On trail the whole thing collapses into one pot, because dried mushrooms are already the ideal backpacking ingredient: near-weightless, indefinitely shelf-stable, and they rebuild their own broth as they rehydrate. Wood ear brings almost no flavor but all the chew, which is what keeps this from being noodles in salt water. Shiitake handles the savory side.

The five-minute head start matters. Wood ear is stubborn and will not soften in the two minutes a noodle block takes. Bamboo shoots are the other reason to bother — they stay crisp when everything else has gone soft, and that contrast is the point of the dish. Sesame oil goes in off the heat so it still smells like something.
