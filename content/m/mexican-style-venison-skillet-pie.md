This is a taco filling with a crust around it, cooked in the one vessel that can do both jobs over coals. The crust uses lard because lard is shelf-stable, melts on the fire, and gives a short, flaky texture that butter cannot match once you are working without refrigeration. Beer instead of water is not a flourish: the carbonation and the cold both keep the dough from turning tough while you handle it in the open air.

The part that matters most is drainage. Venison renders almost no fat, so the moisture in the filling comes entirely from the tomatoes, and any liquid you leave in there soaks straight into the bottom crust. Cook the filling until the pan looks dry before it goes anywhere near the dough. The coal count is the other lever — most of the heat belongs on the lid, or the bottom burns long before the top browns.
