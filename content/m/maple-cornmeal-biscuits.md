This is a home biscuit rebuilt for a fire. The cornmeal soak is the part worth keeping: dry cornmeal in a biscuit stays sandy, and giving it liquid twenty minutes ahead softens the grain so the crumb comes out even. Milk powder plus water does that job as well as cold milk does, which removes the only refrigerated ingredient.

The fat has to be solid when it hits the flour. Cold pieces of shortening leave pockets that turn to steam and lift the biscuit; melted fat just makes it dense. On a warm day, keep the shortening in the shade until the last minute. Coals on top do most of the browning, so bank them there rather than underneath, or the bottoms burn before the middle sets.
