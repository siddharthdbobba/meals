This is a real yeasted dough, so the clock matters more than the technique. Two rises and a bake put it around three hours from the first stir, which makes it a layover-morning project rather than a break-camp one. Start the dough the night before and hold it somewhere cool if you want to shorten the morning.

The potato flakes are in the dough on purpose. They hold moisture and keep the crumb soft through an uneven coal bake, which forgives the hot spots a Dutch oven always has. Fresh milk and a shell egg are the two things that will not survive a camp box, so both come as powder rehydrated at the same ratio. The topping goes in the bottom of the pot and ends up on top after the flip, which is the whole trick: sugar that would burn against direct heat instead cooks under the buns and turns to sauce.
