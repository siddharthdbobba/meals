Raccoon runs lean and stringy, and the fat is where the flavor people complain about actually sits. Trim it off, then cook low and long. Ninety minutes over coals breaks down the connective tissue and the texture lands somewhere near pulled pork.

The glaze is doing two jobs. Sugar browns and gives the outside something to hold onto, and the acid in the bourbon cuts the richness. Apply it at the end, not the start, or the syrup burns black before the meat is anywhere near done. Foil for the last stretch traps steam and finishes the tough parts without drying the rest.

This is a car-camp or basecamp meal. It needs a cooler, a real fire, and time you would not have on a hiking day.
