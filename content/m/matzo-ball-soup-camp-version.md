Matzo ball soup normally starts with an hour of roasting vegetables for broth. On a stove with one pot and finite fuel, that is not happening. Dried parsnip and carrot, onion flakes, bouillon, and a scrap of kombu get most of the way there: the kombu adds the savory backbone that a long simmer would otherwise build, and turmeric supplies the gold color.

The balls are the part that can actually fail. Powdered egg stands in for fresh, and the dough gets rested at ambient temperature rather than chilled, which keeps them light. Stir until the mix is just hydrated, roll them small, and leave the lid on for the full twenty minutes. Every time you check, you drop the steam and they come out tight.
