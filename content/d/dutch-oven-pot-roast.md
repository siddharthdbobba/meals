This is coal cooking, not stove cooking. The browning step is where the flavor is made, so it happens dry and hot before anything else goes in. After that, the half cup of water is all the liquid you need: the onions and the roast give up plenty on their own, and a flooded oven boils the meat instead of braising it.

The long low hours are what turn a cheap cut into something you can pull apart with a spoon. Rump roast is full of connective tissue, and three hours at 300 is roughly how long it takes to melt. Keep the lid shut and rotate the oven instead of peeking. Every lift dumps heat you then have to rebuild with more coals.
