The original is a cast iron cake built on butter, eggs, sour cream, and fresh peaches, none of which survive a weekend without a cooler. Powdered egg and powdered sour cream carry the same job in a bag that weighs nothing, and oil takes over for the creamed butter. You lose the aeration from beating butter and sugar, so the baking powder and soda are doing all the lifting. That means mixing fast and getting it on the coals.

Dried peaches are the real substitution. Rehydrated, they hold their shape better than fresh ones, which tend to collapse into wet spots in a cake. Soak them long enough or they pull moisture out of the batter and leave you with a dry crumb. Coal count matters more than time: most of the heat belongs on the lid, or the bottom burns before the middle sets.
