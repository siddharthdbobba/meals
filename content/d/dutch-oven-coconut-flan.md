Flan is normally a cold-kitchen dessert: fresh eggs, half and half, a water bath, an oven you can trust. Two of those are gone at a campsite. Egg powder and evaporated milk cover the dairy and the eggs without a cooler, and the dutch oven under coals is close enough to a low oven that the custard sets without curdling. The condensed milk and cream of coconut are already shelf-stable, so they come along unchanged.

The caramel is the part that goes wrong. Melted sugar sets hard the instant it touches cool cast iron, and swirling it into a smooth coat rarely works even at home. Do it in the oven itself while the iron is already warm, and stop caring if it cracks. An hour of baking turns those shards into syrup at the bottom of the pan, which is where they were headed anyway.
