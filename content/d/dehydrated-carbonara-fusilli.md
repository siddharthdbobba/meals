Real carbonara is egg, cream, and cheese, none of which survive a dehydrator or a pack. The workaround is a boxed carbonara powder doing the emulsion job, propped up with milk powder for body and a little flour so the sauce still sets when you give it more water than the box asks for. What you get is not carbonara. It is a creamy pasta that behaves in a pot at altitude, which is the useful version.

Pre-cooking and drying the pasta is the part that makes it a one-pot meal. Dry pasta needs eight to ten minutes of hard boil and a drain; dried cooked pasta just soaks the water back up, so nothing gets poured out and no fuel goes into a rolling boil. The vegetables are sautéed before drying for the same reason: they rehydrate on the same clock as the pasta instead of staying crunchy. Broccoli stems never come back soft, so they stay home.
