This is a low-and-slow pot meal, which makes it a camp recipe rather than a trail recipe. The point of the Dutch oven is thermal mass: once the cast iron is hot, coals hold it at a steady simmer for an hour without anyone watching. That is what breaks down the connective tissue in cheap stew beef. Rush it on a high flame and you get gray cubes that fight back.

The gravy granules do the thickening. Slurry them in cold water first or you will spend the next ten minutes chasing lumps around the pot. Everything else is standard: brown the meat for the fond, add the hard vegetables early, and pull it when the potatoes give. It reheats well the next morning, which is the real argument for making six servings.
