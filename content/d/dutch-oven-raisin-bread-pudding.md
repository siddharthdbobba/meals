Bread pudding is a custard held up by bread, which makes it forgiving in a dutch oven. The egg-and-milk mixture sets slowly and evenly under lid coals, so a few hot spots in the coal bed will not ruin it. Stale bread soaks better than fresh — day-old slices hold their shape instead of dissolving into paste.

Coal placement matters more than exact temperature. Most of the heat goes on the lid so the top browns and the bottom does not scorch. If you have no cooler, milk powder does the job: the custard only needs the protein and sugar, not cold milk. Eggs are the one thing without a good shelf-stable swap here, so this stays a car-camping dessert.
