steps:
  - "At home: brown the beef, breaking it fine. Drain every bit of fat you can, then pour boiling water over the meat in a sieve and drain again."
  - "Add onion, carrot, garlic, tomato paste, tomatoes, and herbs. Simmer 30 minutes until it is thick and no longer loose. Season it slightly harder than you would at the table."
  - "Spread the sauce thin on lined dehydrator trays and run at 135F for 8 to 10 hours, until it is dry and brittle with no soft spots. Break it up and portion it into two bags."
  - "In camp: bring 600 ml of water to a boil. Add the sauce crumbles and the pasta at the same time."
  - "Boil 6 to 8 minutes, stirring so nothing welds to the pot bottom. Add splashes of water if it tightens up before the pasta is done."

This is the whole-meal method of dehydrating: cook the dish the way you cook it at home, then dry the finished thing. Drying components separately and mixing them in camp is faster, but the sauce never tastes like it simmered. Half an hour on the stove at home does work that no amount of hot water in a pot at 9,000 feet will do.

Fat is the one thing that does not travel. It will not dry, and what stays behind goes rancid in the bag over a few weeks. So use lean meat, rinse it, and carry the calories as oil you add at the end instead. Dairy stays out for the same reason plus a bacterial one. Skip the parmesan; the tomato and herbs carry it.
