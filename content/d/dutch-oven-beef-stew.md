This is old-fashioned camp-oven cooking and it does not shortcut. The two hours are not optional: chuck is full of connective tissue that only turns soft after a long, low, wet cook. Rush it and you get chewy cubes in thin broth. The vegetables go in at the end because ninety minutes past that point they would be paste.

The flour does two jobs. Dredging the meat gives the browning something to grab, which is where most of the color and flavor comes from. The second spoonful, slurried in cold water so it does not lump, thickens the cooking liquid at the end. Pouring off the browning fat matters — leave it and the gravy separates.

This one needs a cooler and stays in car camp. Four pounds of raw beef and a bag of potatoes are not backpacking weight, and none of it is shelf-stable.
