This is a long-simmer stew built entirely from dried food, which makes it a good fit for cold weather and a fire you are sitting next to anyway. The original leans on a rich trotter broth for body; on the trail that is not happening, so bouillon carries the salt and the hominy carries the thickening. Dried meat gives you protein but almost no viscosity, so the starch has to do that work.

The timing matters more than the technique. Hominy and prairie turnips are the slowest things in the pot, so they get a full day of cold soak and a head start on the simmer. Squash, onion, and jerky go in late — they only need to soften, and jerky held at a boil for ninety minutes turns to thread.
