Three hours over coals is the whole point. Rump and chuck are full of connective tissue that only turns to gelatin with long, low heat, and that gelatin is what gives the broth body without any flour. Rush it at high heat and the meat tightens into cubes of rope.

The timing of the vegetables matters more than the recipe suggests. Onion, carrot, and celery root go in early because they are there for flavor and can afford to fall apart. Potatoes go in near the end so they stay potatoes. Peas go in off the heat, because anything more than a few minutes turns them grey. This is a cooler meal, not a pack meal: the raw beef and the weight of a cast iron pot both keep it at the trailhead.
