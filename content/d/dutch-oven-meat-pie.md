  - "Build a fire and let it burn down to coals. You want a bed you can pull from for the next hour."
  - "Set the 12-inch dutch oven over coals and fry the bacon pieces until crisp. Lift them out with a slotted spoon and leave the fat behind."
  - "Brown the beef and pork in the bacon fat, breaking it up as it cooks. Pour off the excess grease."
  - "Return the bacon and add the onion, celery, garlic, sage, oregano, salt, pepper, and 1 cup of water. Bring it to a boil."
  - "Lid on, pull most of the coals, and simmer 15 minutes. Stir often so the bottom does not catch."
  - "Sprinkle the cornstarch over the meat, pour in the last 1/4 cup of water, and stir until the liquid turns glossy and thick. Take it off the heat."

This is a Scout dutch oven recipe and it stays one, because a pie needs an oven and coals are the only oven out here. Everything before the crust is one-pot work: bacon fat becomes the cooking medium, the meat browns in it, and the same pot holds the simmer. The prep that would slow you down at camp — chopping onion and celery, measuring four spices — happens at home in bags.

The cornstarch slurry is the part worth respecting. A wet filling turns the bottom crust to paste and floods the tin when you cut it. Thicken until the liquid coats the spoon, then rest the baked pie a full ten minutes so it sets. Heat placement matters too: three quarters of the coals on the lid browns the top instead of scorching the bottom.
