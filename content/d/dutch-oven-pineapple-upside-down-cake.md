The original is a home oven cake with heavy cream caramel and fresh dairy in the batter. Neither survives a weekend in a bin, so the cream sauce is gone and the milk and egg are powdered. What is left still works because the brown sugar and butter under the fruit make their own caramel as the pot heats. That layer is the whole point of an upside down cake, and it does not need a recipe of its own.

Coal placement matters more than timing. Most heat goes on the lid so the top sets before the sugar below burns, and turning the pot evens out the hot spots every fire has. The batter is deliberately thin over the fruit. A thick one steams instead of baking and comes out gummy in the middle.
