This is a fudgy brownie, not a cakey one. There is only a third of a cup of flour against three quarters of a cup of cocoa, so almost all the structure comes from fat and egg rather than gluten. That is what lets it survive uneven coal heat: a low-flour batter has less to overwork and less to dry out, and it stays edible whether you pull it at 25 minutes or 35.

The fridge ingredients are the part that changed. Powdered whole egg and powdered whole milk rehydrate in the half cup of cold water and behave close enough to the real thing here, since the batter is a wet slurry either way. Butter is the one thing worth carrying real, because it is most of the calories and the flavor; ghee is the safer choice if the cooler is not coming. Coals on the lid do the browning, coals underneath do the baking, and going heavier on the bottom is how you get a burnt floor.
