This is a thick baked pancake, and the eggs are the whole structure. Six of them in a cup and a half of flour means the batter puffs against the lid and sets into something closer to custard than cake. There is no leavening in it, so nothing goes stale in the pack and nothing needs measuring by teaspoon in the cold.

The apples go in first for a reason. Sitting in the butter on the bottom, they cook down and caramelize with the cinnamon sugar while the batter above them steams under the lid. Granny Smiths stay in slices instead of turning to sauce. Heat placement matters more than time here: most of the coals belong on the lid, or the bottom scorches before the center firms up.
