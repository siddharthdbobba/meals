The original is a three-hour stockpot job with a poultry carcass. What actually carries the flavor is the mushroom, so that is what got carried up the hill. Tough, mature dryad saddles are useless to chew but dry well and rehydrate into broth in about ten minutes, which is the one part of this you should not shortcut. The oil goes in at the end because sesame turns flat and bitter when it boils.

The bay leaf and thyme stand in for the bouquet garni, and the bouillon replaces the meat stock without a cooler. Dryad saddle has a faint cucumber smell that the dried cilantro or mint plays against, so keep both in the bag even though they weigh nothing and look like an afterthought.
