Fresh milk does not survive a camp trip, so this uses powdered whole milk mixed straight into the dry ingredients and cold water added at the fire. The dough behaves the same, and there is nothing to keep cold. Shortening replaces butter for the same reason: it sits in a jar at ambient temperature for weeks.

The two things that decide whether these come out right are fat temperature and handling. Cold, distinct lumps of shortening turn into steam pockets in the oven, which is what makes a biscuit flaky instead of dense. Kneading past thirty seconds develops gluten and undoes that. Heat management matters just as much: most coals go on the lid, or you scorch the bottoms before the tops set.
