This is a dump cobbler, which means the cake mix never gets mixed. Left dry on top of the fruit, the bottom of that layer hydrates from the steam coming off the cherries while the top stays powdery and takes the butter. You end up with three textures out of three ingredients: syrupy fruit, wet cake in the middle, crisp crust on top. Stirring it destroys all of that and gives you a flat, gluey cake instead.

Heat management is the only real skill involved. Coals under the oven bake the fruit and coals on the lid brown the top, so you want roughly twice as many on top as below. Rotating the pot and lid in opposite directions keeps a hot spot in the fire from cooking one quadrant twice as fast as the rest. If the top browns early, pull coals off the lid and give it the rest of the time from below.
