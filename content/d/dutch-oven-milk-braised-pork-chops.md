Braising in milk sounds unlikely until you see what an hour does to it. The milk proteins break down and brown, and the sauce ends up dark and thick without any flour. The original does this with fresh milk and heavy cream, which is not a thing you carry. Whole milk powder and sweet cream powder reconstitute close enough and live in a bag on the shelf.

The baking soda is not optional. It nudges the pH up so the milk browns instead of breaking into curds, which is what plain simmered milk does. The other rule is heat: a hard boil will split the sauce no matter what you add, so pull coals until it barely moves. Fresh garlic and thyme sprigs became granulated garlic and dried thyme, going in with the liquid rather than sitting on top.
