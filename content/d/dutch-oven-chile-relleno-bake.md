This is a crustless cheese casserole wearing a chile relleno's name. No frying, no batter-dipping one pepper at a time — the chiles get laid flat in layers and the egg batter goes over the whole thing at once. That is the only way this dish works over coals, where you get one temperature and no ability to fuss.

The flour and evaporated milk are structure. Flour keeps the eggs from weeping into a puddle under the cheese, and evaporated milk is thick enough to set without a fridge in the picture. Whipping the whites separately is worth the extra bowl: it lifts the top into something like a soufflé instead of a wet omelet. Lid coals do the browning, so keep more heat on top than underneath.
