The original is a church-basement casserole built on frozen potatoes, canned soup, and a pint of sour cream. None of that survives a weekend without a cooler, so all three get swapped for their dried versions: boxed hashbrowns, powdered condensed soup, and sour cream powder. The powders rehydrate off the same water the potatoes did not absorb, which is why draining matters. Drain too well and it goes dry; skip the drain and it stays soup.

The cornflakes are fried in butter first and added last for a reason. Stirred in early they turn to paste. Sitting on top under a lidful of coals, they brown and stay crisp, which is the only textural contrast in an otherwise soft pan. Cheese and butter carry the calories here, and there is no shame in the amount of either.
