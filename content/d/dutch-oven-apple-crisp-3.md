The crust is laid down twice, bottom and top, which is what separates this from a plain crumble. The bottom layer takes the liquid the apples throw off and turns it into something you can lift out with a spoon. Without it you get syrup pooling in the bottom of the oven.

Coal placement is the whole game. Two thirds on the lid, one third underneath: that browns the top crumb while the apples steam through slowly. Load the bottom heavy and the sugar on the floor of the oven burns before the fruit is soft. The original called for 2 tsp of salt, which reads like a typo against 2 cups of sugar; 1 tsp is enough to keep it from tasting flat.
