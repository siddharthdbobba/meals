Store-bought dried hash browns exist, but a bag of them costs more than five pounds of russets and comes with a longer ingredient list. Doing it yourself is mostly waiting. The blanch is the part you cannot skip: raw dried potato stays hard and chalky no matter how long you soak it, because the starch was never gelatinized.

Citric acid is insurance against browning, not flavor. A quarter teaspoon across ten cups of water is enough. In camp, the only real technique is draining hard before the oil goes in. Potatoes that are still holding water will never form a crust; they just sit there and steam.
