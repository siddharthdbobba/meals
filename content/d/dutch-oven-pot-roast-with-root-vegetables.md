This is a coals recipe, not a stove recipe. The whole thing depends on holding a low simmer for two and a half hours, and coals do that better than a burner because the heat comes from all sides and fades slowly. A boil at this stage tightens the muscle fiber and you end up with grey, stringy meat. Barely-moving water breaks the connective tissue down into gelatin instead.

The vegetables go in near the end for a reason. Carrots and turnips turn to mush in three hours, and cooking them alongside the meat from the start gives you soft meat and nothing to chew with it. The last forty minutes is enough to cook them through in broth that is already full of fat and salt. Strain the broth before serving or you will bite a clove.
