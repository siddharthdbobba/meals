The original of this is an oven cake with fresh peaches, a fresh egg, and a splash of milk. None of that travels. Canned fruit is already cooked and already shelf-stable, and it releases less liquid than fresh, which matters when the bottom of the pan is supposed to turn into caramel rather than syrup. Powdered egg and powdered milk cover the two refrigerated items; the batter is stiff enough that neither is doing structural work you would notice.

The coal split is the part worth getting right. Two-thirds of the heat on the lid and one-third underneath makes it bake instead of fry, and rotating both oven and lid keeps hot spots from burning a stripe into the cake. Self-rising flour saves carrying baking powder and salt separately, but check the date on the bag before the trip, since old leavening gives you a dense, wet layer under the fruit.
