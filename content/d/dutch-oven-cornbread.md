Baking at camp fails in two places: the heat and the wet ingredients. The pie tin solves the first. It lifts the batter off the cast iron floor, so the bottom sets instead of scorching while the top is still raw, and the tin comes out with the bread in it and leaves the oven nearly clean.

The milk and egg are powdered here because neither survives a warm day in a pack. Rehydrated, they behave close enough — the baking powder does the lifting, not the egg. Shortening instead of butter is the same logic: it sits on a shelf at 90°F and it coats the flour, which is what keeps the crumb short rather than bready.
