  - "Burn a fire down to coals well before you want dessert. You need about 20 briquette-sized embers, not flames."
  - "Melt the butter in the dutch oven, then pull it off the heat. Dump in the gingersnap crumbs and stir until every crumb is dark. Press the mix up the sides and across the bottom with the back of a spoon."
  - "In a separate pot or a gallon bag, mix the pumpkin, condensed milk, brown sugar, cornstarch, vanilla, and salt until it is one color with no dry streaks."

The crust is the reason this works in a dutch oven. There is no dough to roll, chill, transfer, or crimp, which are all things that go badly on a picnic table. Crumbs and melted butter pressed into the pot with a spoon is the whole technique, and cast iron holds heat evenly enough that the edges set before the middle burns.

The original filling leans on a separated egg, whipped white folded in for lift. Eggs are a poor bet away from a fridge and whipping one by hand over a fire is not worth the arm, so cornstarch does the setting instead. You lose some airiness and gain a denser, custard-like slice that holds up to being cut with a camp knife. Give it the full hour of cooling. Cut it hot and you are serving pumpkin soup in a bowl of crumbs.
