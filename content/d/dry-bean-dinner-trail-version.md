Dry beans are the cheapest protein in a grocery store, but a pound of them wants an hour of hard boiling and a lot of fuel you are not carrying. Dehydrated bean flakes are the same bean, already cooked and dried, and they rehydrate in the time it takes water to stop steaming. That is the whole trade: you pay a little more per ounce and get the two hours back.

The rice is there for bulk and to keep the beans from turning into a single dense paste. Fat is the other fix — bean flakes on their own are lean and mealy, so the oil and cheese are load-bearing, not garnish. Add water in stages if you are unsure; flakes will keep thickening for a minute after you stop stirring, and thin is easier to correct than cement.
