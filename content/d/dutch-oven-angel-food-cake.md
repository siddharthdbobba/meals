  - "Build a fire and burn it down to coals well before you start. You want about 25 briquette-sized coals or their equivalent, and no flame."
  - "Whisk the flour, the 3 dl of powdered sugar, and the vanilla sugar together in a bowl. Sift it through a strainer if you brought one. Lumps do not disappear later."
  - "In a clean, completely dry pot, stir the powdered egg whites into 350 ml of cold water and let it sit five minutes. Add the salt and beat until it holds soft peaks."
  - "Keep beating and add the granulated sugar one spoonful at a time. Stop when the foam is stiff and glossy. This takes a while by hand. It is the whole recipe."
  - "Sprinkle in the citric acid, then fold the flour mixture in a third at a time. Fold, do not stir. Get every streak of flour in without knocking the air out."
  - "Scrape the batter into an ungreased cake pan and level the top. Do not oil the pan, and do not bake this directly in the dutch oven unless it is bare cast iron with no seasoning to speak of. The batter has to climb the walls."

Angel food cake is flour, sugar, and a large volume of trapped air. The original calls for twelve fresh egg whites, which is not a thing you carry down a dirt road, so this uses powdered whites rehydrated in the pot. They whip nearly as well and they keep in a bag for a year. Citric acid replaces the lemon juice for the same reason: it does the acid's job of stabilizing the foam without a piece of fruit going soft in a duffel.

Two rules decide whether this works. Nothing that touches the batter can carry grease, because fat collapses egg foam on contact, and the pan stays ungreased so the cake has something to grip while it rises. Cooling it upside down is not optional either. The structure is still soft when it comes off the coals and it will sink into itself if it sits right side up. The butter-and-milk glaze from the original became a plain sugar and water pour, which needs no cooler and sets up fine in camp.
