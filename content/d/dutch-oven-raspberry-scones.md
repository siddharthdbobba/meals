steps:
  - "At home, cut the shortening into the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt until the pieces are pea sized. Add the milk powder and egg powder, then bag it."
  - "At camp, build a fire and let it burn down. You want a bed of coals, not flame."
  - "Pour about 1/2 cup of cold water and the vanilla into the bag of mix. Stir, then work it with your hands until it just holds together. Stop there."
  - "Press the dough flat on a sheet of parchment, scatter the raspberries and apricots over half, fold the other half on top, and press it back into a round about an inch thick. Cut into 8 wedges."
  - "Line a 10-inch dutch oven with the parchment, set the wedges in with a little space between them, and sprinkle the coarse sugar over the tops."

Scones are one of the few baked goods that survive the trip to camp intact, because everything that makes them work happens at home. Cutting cold fat into flour is the fussy part, and once it is done the mix sits in a bag for weeks. At the fire you are adding water and shaping dough, nothing more.

The substitutions matter. Shortening replaces butter because it holds its texture in a hot car, milk and egg powder replace the wet versions, and freeze-dried raspberries replace fresh ones — fresh berries bleed and turn the dough pink and slack, while the dried ones rehydrate from the dough itself and hold their shape. Coals over and under is what gives you crisp edges instead of a pale, steamed biscuit, so keep more heat on the lid than the bottom.
