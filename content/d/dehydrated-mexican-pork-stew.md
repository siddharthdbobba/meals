  - "Cook the rice with the extra salt, spread it over two lined trays, and dry at 63C for 4 to 6 hours."
  - "Rinse and drain the chickpeas and lentils. Soften the onion, garlic, and chili in the rest of the coconut oil, then move it to a pot with the water, stock cube, bell pepper, and crushed tomatoes. Simmer 10 minutes, add the beans and lentils, simmer 5 more, then take it off the heat and stir in the cilantro."

  - "Split meat, rice, and sauce into four equal portions and seal each set in one bag."

This is a home-dehydrator meal, not a camp-cooking one. All the real cooking happens before the trip, which is why the trail side is one boil and a wait. Drying the meat, rice, and sauce on separate trays matters: each one needs a different temperature and time, and pork fat dries slowest of the three. Lean pork is not a preference here. Fat is what turns dried meat stale, so the leaner the grind, the longer the bag keeps.

Use canned chickpeas and lentils rather than dry ones you soaked. Dry legumes that were only soaked overnight tend to come back gritty in cold conditions, while canned ones have already been cooked soft and rehydrate on schedule. The olive oil goes in at the end because dried food absorbs water better without fat coating it, and because the sauce lost most of its calories in the dehydrator. Insulating the pot while it sits is doing half the work: 325 ml of boiled water cools fast, and rice needs the heat to hold.
