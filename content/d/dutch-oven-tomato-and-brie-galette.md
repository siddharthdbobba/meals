The original is a summer tart built on garden tomatoes and a fridge. Two things had to change for camp. Fresh tomatoes release too much water for a crust baked in a pot you cannot see into, so sun-dried ones in oil do the job with none of the flooding. And butter in a crust means cold butter; shortening cuts in just as well and does not care how warm the car got.

The parmesan is in the dough rather than sprinkled on the filling, which keeps it from scorching under a lid full of coals. Caramelizing the onion first is the only slow part, and it is the part that matters — raw onion in a 35-minute bake stays raw and sharp.
