This is a coal-cooked chili, so the whole recipe is a heat management problem. You need frying heat at the start to brown the beef and build fond, then a long low stretch that never boils. That means moving coals twice, not adjusting a dial.

The fat from the beef is doing the flavor work. Chili powder, cumin, and oregano are all fat-soluble, so browning first and adding them to a greasy pan gets more out of them than dumping everything in cold. The hour is not optional either. It is what turns the canned tomato from acidic and thin into something that coats a spoon.

This one stays as written rather than getting a trail conversion. Ground beef and a 20 oz can are cooler-and-car food. If you want it in a pack, use the bean variation and dehydrate the finished pot.
