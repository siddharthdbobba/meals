Dried tomatoes are the whole point here. Water leaves, sugar and acid stay, and what you carry is a quarter of the original weight with more flavor than the fresh fruit had. Soaking them cold before the boil matters: dropping hard tomato pieces straight into boiling water leaves you chewing leather in an otherwise finished pot.

The tomato powder does the thickening that a simmered sauce would normally do over half an hour. Couscous takes only steeping heat, so the stove runs once and then goes off, which is the difference between this and a real pasta dinner. Skip the oil and it eats thin — the fat is carrying both calories and the herbs.
