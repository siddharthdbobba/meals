This started as a home baking recipe, which means an oven and cold butter. Neither travels. Oil replaces the butter and works better here anyway, since it stays workable at any camp temperature, and powdered egg carries the binding without a cooler. The dough is deliberately stiff. A wet dough spreads into one sheet in a dutch oven and burns at the rim.

The coal ratio is the whole recipe. Roughly two-thirds of the heat on the lid and one-third underneath bakes the tops before the bottoms scorch. Heat-stable chips matter for the same reason: regular chocolate is liquid long before the cookies are done, and you end up with a smear instead of pockets. Hazelnuts hold their crunch through the bake and give the cocoa something to work against.
