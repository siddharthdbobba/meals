steps:
  - "Build a fire and let it burn down. You want coals, not flame."
  - "Whisk the coconut milk powder and bouillon into 300 ml of cold water plus another 2 cups. Pour it into the dutch oven with the rice, butter, and salt. Stir once so the rice sits flat."
  - "Lid on. Set the oven on a bed of coals and shovel more coals onto the lid. Bake about 15 minutes."
  - "Lift the lid, scatter the cabbage over the rice, and lay the frozen gyoza on top in one layer. Do not stir them in. Lid back on, coals back on, another 25 minutes."
  - "While that cooks, shake the soy sauce, gochujang, oil, garlic, and ginger together in a jar until it stops separating."

The whole thing works because rice and frozen dumplings want roughly the same amount of time and the same wet heat. The rice absorbs the coconut milk from below; the dumplings steam on top of it and never touch the pot. Nothing gets fried, so nothing sticks, and there is one pot to scrape at the end.

Coconut milk powder replaces the can. It weighs a fraction as much and there is no metal to pack out. The last uncovered five minutes matters more than it sounds: the dumpling wrappers go from slick to slightly tacky, which is the closest you get to a pan-fried bottom without a pan.
