Bread on a trip sounds ambitious until you realize this one asks for four ingredients and no technique. There is no kneading and no second rise. Time does the work gluten development would otherwise need, which is why the two-hour wait is not optional and why you start it long before you are hungry.

The dutch oven is the reason it comes out crusty. A closed cast iron pot traps the steam coming off the dough, which keeps the surface soft long enough to expand, then dries and browns it once the steam escapes. Coals on the lid matter more than coals underneath; heat from below alone will scorch the bottom while the top stays raw. Nothing here is perishable, so the dry mix can ride in your bin for a week without complaint.
