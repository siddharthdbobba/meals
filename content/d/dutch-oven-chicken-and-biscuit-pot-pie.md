The original is a home kitchen dish: butter, fresh mushrooms, half-and-half, whole milk. None of that rides in a car for three days without a cooler. Shortening replaces the butter and the chilled fat in the biscuits, whole milk powder replaces both dairies, and dried vegetables replace the fresh dice. The flavor is a little flatter than the real thing, which is what the bouillon and the mushroom soaking liquid are there to fix. Do not throw that liquid out.

The roux is the part worth doing carefully. Flour cooked in fat before the liquid goes in gives a gravy that holds the filling together under a wet biscuit lid; skip it and the biscuits sink into soup. Heat placement matters too, roughly two thirds of the coals on top, because biscuits brown from above and the bottom is already hot from the gravy. Rotate once so the seam in your coal bed does not scorch one side.
