Butter swim biscuits work because the fat never has to be cut into the flour. It sits in the pan and the dough falls into it, so you get a fried bottom edge and a soft middle without cold butter, a pastry cutter, or a countertop. That makes it one of the few real biscuit methods that survives a picnic table.

The only change from the home version is the buttermilk. Powdered buttermilk and water rehydrate close enough that the baking powder still gets its acid, and nothing has to stay cold. Coal placement matters more than time here: most of the heat belongs on the lid, or the bottoms burn before the tops set.
