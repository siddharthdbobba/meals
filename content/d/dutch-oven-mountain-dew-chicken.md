The soda is doing two jobs. Its sugar reduces down over 45 minutes into a glaze that coats the vegetables, and its acid keeps the whole braise from tasting flat, which is what happens when you cook chicken and potatoes in plain water. The flour on the chicken is what thickens it: the coating loosens into the liquid as it cooks and pulls the sauce together.

This one stays a car-camping recipe. Fresh chicken and a cooler are the point, and there is no shelf-stable version worth eating. What you can control is the heat. Three quarters of the coals belong on the lid so the top browns and the bottom does not scorch, and coals burn down faster than the recipe implies, so plan on feeding the pile once.
