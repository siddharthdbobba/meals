The whole recipe is built around the drying, not the cooking. Lean beef and a nonstick pan keep fat out of the sauce, and fat is what goes rancid in a sealed bag three weeks later. Fine crumbles and small dice matter for the same reason: thin pieces give up their water in a single session, thick ones hold a wet core that spoils quietly.

Pasta and sauce dry at very different rates, so they go on separate trays and come back together only in the bag. At camp there is no cooking left, just 300 ml of boiling water and twenty minutes of insulation. The pot goes in your quilt because a mug on cold ground loses heat faster than the pasta can absorb it, and half-rehydrated pasta is crunchy in the middle.
