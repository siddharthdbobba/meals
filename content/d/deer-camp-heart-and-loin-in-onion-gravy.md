This is a first-kill camp meal, so it is built around the two cuts that will not keep: the heart and the inside loins. Both go bad faster than the rest of the animal and both are lean enough that fast, hard cooking turns them to leather. The answer is a coat of seasoned flour, a hard sear, and a long low simmer in liquid.

The onions are the actual recipe. Three of them cooked down for 20-plus minutes give up enough sugar to carry the gravy, and the flour that fell off the meat thickens it without any other help. Dark beer adds bitterness that cuts the fat from the butter; stock makes it rounder and sweeter. Cook it over coals, not open flame, or the bottom scorches before the heart is tender.
