The original is a buttermilk cake baked in a wreath pan in a kitchen oven. Almost none of that survives a trip, so the perishables were swapped out: powdered buttermilk and powdered egg for the fresh ones, oil for the creamed butter, powdered milk for the glaze. Oil does the job here because there is no butter to cream anyway, and it keeps the crumb moist for a second day.

Heat placement is the whole recipe. Most of the coals go on the lid, because a cake wants to bake from above; too many underneath and the bottom is carbon while the middle is raw. Rotating the oven and the lid in opposite directions evens out the hot spots your fire will inevitably have. The glaze is not decoration. It soaks into a camp cake that has spent thirty minutes losing water to a fire.
