This is a bread pudding pretending to be French toast, which is why it works in camp. There is no flipping and no standing over a griddle for nine people. The bread does its own work overnight, and in the morning you are only managing coals.

The layer order matters. Butter and brown sugar go down first so they caramelize against the hot iron instead of scorching on top, and the apples sit in that syrup as a buffer between the bread and the metal. Tart apples stay in slices through an hour of heat. Rotating the oven and lid is the only real technique here — coals burn unevenly, and a quarter turn every fifteen minutes is what keeps one side from setting up black.
