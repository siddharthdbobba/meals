This is a car-camping recipe and it stays one. The braise needs almost two hours of steady low heat and a heavy lidded pot, which is exactly what a dutch oven over coals does well and exactly what a canister stove does badly. Raw chicken also means a cooler, so there is no backpacking version of this worth writing.

The wine is not decoration. It is most of the liquid, and it and the bacon fat are what turn a plain braise into something with depth. The butter-and-flour paste at the end is the only real technique here: stirred in off the heat, it thickens the pot without lumping, which matters more when you are eating out of a bowl next to a fire than at a table.
