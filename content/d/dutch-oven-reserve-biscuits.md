Biscuits are a fat-and-flour problem, not a baking problem. The lard has to stay in solid flecks until it hits heat, because those flecks turn to steam and push the layers apart. Warm fat smears into the flour and you get a scone. Everything else here is negotiable; cold fat is not.

The original leans on fresh buttermilk and heavy cream, which is a non-starter without refrigeration. Buttermilk powder and whole milk powder rehydrated together get close enough: the acid still reacts with the baking powder, and the milk fat covers what the cream was doing. Lard replaces the butter entirely because it keeps at ambient temperature and takes a hard chill better. Dough this wet is correct. Rolling each scoop through dry flour is what makes it handleable.
