Dried vegetables are the cheapest weight-to-volume trade in a food bag. A quart pouch of mixed carrot, celery, onion, and cabbage weighs a few ounces and turns into several dinners once water goes back in. The catch is time: air-dried vegetables need ten to fifteen minutes of actual simmering, not a two-minute steep, so plan the fuel for it.

The potato flakes and the oil are what make this a meal instead of hot vegetable water. Flakes give it enough body to coat a spoon, and the oil carries most of the calories. Without both you have a nice broth and an empty stomach an hour later.
