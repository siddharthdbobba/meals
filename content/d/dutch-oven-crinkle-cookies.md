  - "Build a fire and let it burn down. You want coals, not flame."

  - "In a bag or bowl, whisk the flour, cocoa, baking powder, and salt together."
  - "In the pot, beat the rehydrated egg with the sugar, oil, and vanilla until it looks glossy."

Crinkle cookies are a coals-and-lid recipe more than a baking recipe. The cracks are mechanical, not magic: the dough rises and splits, and the dry powdered sugar coating tears open along the seams. Coat them thinly and you get brown cookies with no pattern, so be heavy-handed with the sugar bowl.

Fresh eggs do not belong in a food bag, so this uses powdered whole egg rehydrated in cold water. It behaves the same here because the eggs are doing structure and moisture, not flavor. The rest of the recipe was already shelf-stable. The rest at step five replaces the hour in a refrigerator; twenty minutes in shade is enough to stiffen the oil so the cookies hold their shape.
