Sauce-based food dries and comes back better than anything else. The tomato and bean puree traps the meat, so when the water goes back in, everything rehydrates at once instead of leaving you with soft sauce and gravel-hard beef. Thin, brothy dishes dry into flakes that lose their body permanently; this is why stews, pasta bakes, and casseroles are the standard candidates.

Fat is the constraint. It does not dehydrate, it just sits there and turns rancid over weeks in a pack, so the beef gets drained hard at home and the calories come back at camp as olive oil. Drying at a quarter inch deep matters too: thicker than that and the center stays damp, which is how a bag of dried chili spoils quietly between the freezer and the trailhead. Test a cooled piece by folding it. It should snap, not bend.
