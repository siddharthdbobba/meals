The original is an oven recipe with cold butter, buttermilk, and an egg, none of which travel. Powdered buttermilk and powdered egg go into the dry mix and rehydrate with the water you add, and shortening replaces the butter because it holds its shape in a hot pack without going rancid. The chemistry is the same: solid fat in pea-sized lumps steams apart in the heat and leaves layers.

Sesame seeds are the part worth keeping. Six tablespoons is more than it sounds, and they toast during the bake into something with actual crunch against a soft crumb. Coal placement matters more than timing here. Most of the heat belongs on the lid, or you get a burnt bottom and raw middle.
