The original is a home kitchen pie: fresh apples, cold butter, liquid buttermilk. None of that survives a weekend in a car. Dried apples rehydrated in boiling water give you the same volume at a fraction of the weight, and their soaking liquid is already concentrated apple flavor, so it does the job the boiled cider was doing. Powdered buttermilk keeps the crust tender without a cooler, and shortening replaces butter because it does not go soft at ninety degrees in a trunk.

Cornstarch is what keeps the bottom crust from turning to paste. Rehydrated apples release a lot of water and it all has to go somewhere. Coal placement matters more than timing here: most of the heat belongs on the lid, or the bottom scorches long before the top browns.
