This is the standard no-knead loaf, moved outdoors. The long, cold, wet rise does the work that kneading normally does: with 12 to 18 hours the gluten organizes itself and the yeast has time to build flavor from a quarter teaspoon. That suits camp perfectly, since the only active labor is 30 seconds of stirring and a fire you were building anyway.

The preheated Dutch oven is not optional. A cold pot lets the dough spread before it sets, and you get a flat disc. Hot cast iron plus a sealed lid traps the dough's own steam, which is what gives the crust its shell. Coals go mostly on top — heat from below scorches the bottom long before the middle is done.
