This is a two-part breakfast run through one pot, which is the whole reason it works in camp. The biscuits bake first while the coals are at their hottest, and the gravy gets made in the fond they leave behind. Nothing extra to wash.

The fresh-dairy problem is solved with powdered whole milk on both sides: a third of a cup in the dough for tenderness, the rest reconstituted for the gravy. Shortening replaces cold butter because it holds its shape at 80 degrees and does not need a cooler. Coal placement is the part that actually decides the outcome — most of the heat belongs on the lid, or you get burnt bottoms and raw middles.
