Venison is lean enough that it will go dry and grainy if you treat it like beef. The tablespoon of oil and the long low simmer are both there to fix that, and the tomato sauce and bean liquid keep the meat sitting in something wet for two hours instead of drying out at the bottom of the pot.

The spices go in twice on purpose. The first half toasts in the hot fat and turns deep and slightly bitter, which is the backbone. The second half goes in near the end still raw and sharp, so you get the top notes back that two hours of simmering would otherwise cook flat. This one needs a cooler and real heat, so it is a car camp or basecamp dinner, not something to carry.
