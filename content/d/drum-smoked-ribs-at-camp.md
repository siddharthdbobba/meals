steps:
  - "Build a small charcoal bed on one side of the fire ring or grill. You want 225-250F at the grate, not flames. A cheap probe thermometer is worth carrying."
  - "Add a wood chunk. Lay the racks bone-side down on the far side, away from the coals. Lid or foil tent on."
  - "Hold the temperature for two hours. Feed one or two coals at a time rather than a fresh handful, and add a wood chunk when the smoke thins out."
  - "Wrap each rack tight in foil and put it back on for another hour. This is where they get tender."
  - "Unwrap and return them to the grate for a final hour to firm the outside back up. Pull earlier if you want them softer."
  - "They are done when the meat has shrunk back off the bone ends by a finger width. Tent in foil and leave them alone for fifteen minutes before cutting."
packing: "Ribs travel in the cooler, trimmed and rubbed at home in a zip bag so there is no prep at camp. Rub, foil, and wood chunks ride in the dry box. Bring more charcoal than you think."

Ribs are the one camp meal where the cooking time is the point. Nothing here is hard, but you are committing an afternoon to holding a fire at a temperature it does not naturally want to sit at. Small additions of fuel, often. A big handful of coals spikes the grate and you spend the next half hour recovering.

The three-stage timing — open, wrapped, open again — splits the difference between smoke and tenderness. Open smoke builds the crust, the foil wrap steams the connective tissue down, and the last open hour dries the outside back out. Shift the hours around to taste: more wrap, softer ribs. The pullback at the bone ends is a more reliable finish signal than the clock, since every fire runs differently. This one stays in the cooler and needs real heat, so it is a car-camping meal, not a pack-in.
