Coffee cake is a butter-and-sour-cream recipe, which is exactly what a camp kitchen does not have. Oil replaces the butter one for one by volume and behaves better in the cold. Powdered sour cream and milk powder go into the dry premix and rehydrate with the water, which keeps the acid that the baking soda needs to lift the crumb.

The streusel is layered through the middle as well as on top for a reason: it gives the cake two seams of sugar and fat that stay damp while the edges set against the hot cast iron. Coal count matters more than time here. Too many under the pot and you get a burnt bottom with raw center, which is the standard dutch oven failure.
