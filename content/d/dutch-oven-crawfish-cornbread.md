This is a cornbread that has to carry a pound of shellfish, so the batter gets help. The extra baking powder and soda push against the weight of the crawfish, cheese, and onion, which would otherwise settle into a dense wet layer on the bottom. Cooking the tails and onion first matters for the same reason — raw onion sheds water into the batter as it bakes, and wet batter around firm crawfish is the usual failure here.

The frying step and the baking step want different coal setups, so plan for the switch. Frying puts nearly everything underneath; baking moves two thirds of the coals to the lid so the top browns instead of the bottom burning. This is car camping food — it needs a cooler for the eggs, cheese, and crawfish, and it will not scale down to a backpack.
