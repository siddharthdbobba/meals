The whole point is that the pasta water never leaves the pot. Drained pasta water takes the starch with it, and starch is what turns tomato paste into sauce instead of a red slick sitting on top of noodles. Leaving two cups behind gives you enough liquid to reconstitute the mix and enough body to make it cling.

The beef gets browned at home. Cooking raw ground meat over coals in the same pot you are about to boil pasta in means grease, guesswork, and a food safety problem you do not need at a campsite. Freeze-dried beef works the same way and drops the cooler entirely.

Coal management matters more than timing here. A hard boil under the lid will scorch the bottom once the paste goes in, so pull coals off after the noodles soften.
