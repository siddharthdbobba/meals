Chuck is a working muscle, full of connective tissue, and that is the whole point. Cook it fast and it is leather. Cook it three hours under a lid and the tissue turns to gelatin, which is what makes the liquid in the pot worth eating.

The coal split matters more than the recipe does. Most of the heat belongs on the lid, because a dutch oven bottom sitting in embers scorches long before the middle of a three-pound roast gets anywhere. Rotating the pot evens out the hot spots in your fire ring. Vegetables go in late so they finish with the meat instead of dissolving into it.
