This is a car-camping recipe and it stays one. The cans and the pound of beef are heavy, and the dish depends on twenty minutes of even, lidded heat, which is not something a canister stove does. If you have a cooler and a fire ring, it feeds six out of one pot.

The canning liquid is the sauce. Drain the beans, corn, and tomatoes and you have dry macaroni casserole, so leave them alone — or add a quarter cup of water back if you already poured. The bake matters too: it is what melts the cheese through the pasta instead of leaving it in stringy pockets on top.
