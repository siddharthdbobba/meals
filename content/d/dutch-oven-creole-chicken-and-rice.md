This is a layered bake, not a stew, and the order matters. Rice and tomatoes go on the bottom where the liquid pools, the chicken sits on top and steams while its juices run down into the grain. Nothing gets stirred, which is the whole point of a dutch oven meal: you set it and then go do something else for an hour.

The honey is doing more than sweetening. Cajun blends are mostly salt and cayenne, and a cup of honey over four breasts is what keeps the heat from flattening the dish. It also caramelizes on the exposed tops of the chicken. Season twice, once on the rice and once over the honey, or the bottom layer comes out bland. A 10-inch oven takes about two thirds of everything at the same temperature and time.
