The source version is a lattice-crust pie with four pounds of fresh berries, an egg wash, and an oven. None of that travels. Freeze-dried strawberries carry the same flavor at a tenth of the weight and rehydrate in the pot, which is also where the cornstarch does its thickening. Fresh fruit needs a 45-minute sit and a drain to shed water; freeze-dried needs the opposite, so the water goes in on purpose and in a measured amount.

A cobbler top replaces the crust because rolled pastry and cold butter are not camp materials. Shortening stays solid without a cooler and cuts into flour the same way, and powdered milk plus water gets you the liquid without the egg wash. Coals matter more than the recipe: heavy on the lid, light underneath, or the fruit scorches before the biscuits set.
