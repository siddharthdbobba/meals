Duck is a car-camping protein, not a backpacking one. It needs a cooler and it needs a real fire, but it rewards both: the skin renders its own fat, so the breast fries in a pool it makes itself and you carry no cooking oil for it.

The original called for fresh-squeezed juice twice, once in the marinade and once in the sauce. Powdered concentrate does both jobs without a cooler slot or a citrus that turns to pulp by day two. Cornstarch is what makes the sauce cling instead of running off; skip it and the glaze pools under the meat. Cook over coals, not flame, since rendering fat drips and flares.
