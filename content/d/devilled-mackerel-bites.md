The original is a restaurant plate made from fish caught that morning. Tinned mackerel is the trail version and it holds up better than you would expect, because the fish is already cooked and oily enough to take a hard sear without falling apart. The point of the pan time is crust, not doneness.

Blooming the spices in oil before the fish goes in matters. Mustard seed, cumin, and cardamom are fat-soluble, so dumping them on at the end just leaves grit. Thirty seconds in hot oil pulls the flavor out and into everything that follows. The Worcestershire goes in off the heat so it reduces to a glaze instead of burning.
