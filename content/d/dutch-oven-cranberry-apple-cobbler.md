The original is a lard-crust pie with three pounds of fresh apples, which is a lot of weight and a food processor you do not have. Dried fruit does the same job: it rehydrates in the pot it bakes in, so the water you add becomes the filling instead of something you drain off. Cranberries stay tart even dried, which is the whole point of putting them in with apples.

Shortening replaces the butter because it does not need a cooler and it still makes a short, crumbly topping. The vinegar is not a flavor, it slows gluten from forming so the dough stays tender after you handle it with cold hands. The three tablespoons of flour stirred into the fruit is what keeps the bottom from turning into syrup.
