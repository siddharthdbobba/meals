Browning two pounds of beef over a campfire is a grease-spattered chore that ties up your only pot for twenty minutes. Do it in your kitchen the day before, drain it well, and camp cooking becomes opening cans. The drained fat is also why this keeps a couple of days in a cooler instead of turning.

The condensed tomato soup is doing more than it looks like. It is thickener, sugar, and salt in one can, and it smooths the raw edge off tomato paste without a long reduction. The baked beans bring their own sweet sauce, so the whole thing arrives at chili consistency in thirty minutes rather than the hour a from-scratch pot needs. Cook on coals, not flame: cast iron over open fire scorches the bottom before the middle is hot.
