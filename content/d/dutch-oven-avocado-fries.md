steps:
  - "Build a fire and burn it down to coals. You want steady heat, not flame."
  - "Halve and pit the avocados. Cut each half into four or five thick wedges and lift them out of the skin with a spoon."
  - "Set up three bowls: flour in one, egg beaten with the tablespoon of water in the second, panko mixed with chili powder, garlic powder, salt, and pepper in the third."
  - "Roll each wedge in flour, then egg, then press it into the panko. Press hard. Loose breading burns off in a dutch oven."
  - "Grease the dutch oven or line it with foil, lay the wedges in a single layer, and spray the tops with oil."
  - "Lid on. Set the oven over about 8 coals and put 14 or so on the lid. You are aiming for a moderate oven, roughly 350F."

This is a home-oven appetizer dragged out to a fire ring, so it only makes sense at a site where you already have a cooler and coals. Avocados are the reason: they bruise, they will not keep, and they go mealy if you chill them. Cook them the first night or leave them home.

The breading is doing structural work, not just adding crunch. Avocado flesh has nothing holding it together once heated, so the flour-egg-panko shell is what keeps a wedge a wedge. Press the panko on hard and use firm-ripe fruit. A dutch oven with coals split roughly one-third under and two-thirds on the lid gives you top-down heat, which browns the crust instead of steaming it.
