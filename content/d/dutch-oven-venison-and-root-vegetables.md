The long braise at low heat is the entire point of this recipe. It transforms a whole tenderloin into meat so tender that a fork shreds it, and the canned soups provide salt, fat, and body without any advance work. The vegetables absorb the braising liquid as the potatoes soften and thicken everything into a stew. The reason this works over a campfire is that the Dutch oven, with coals underneath and on the lid, gives you the low, even heat of an oven. You are not trying to boil or brown—you are gently braising. Rotate the pot every couple of hours so nothing scorches on one side.

After eight hours of patient tending, you have a complete one-pot dinner that tastes like it required real skill when it actually required only time, coals, and the will to not mess with it. The crew eats well and asks for this recipe again next year.
