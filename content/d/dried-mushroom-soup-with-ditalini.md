The original is a long-simmered soup built on a food-processed base of onion, leek, shallot, and celery. None of that survives a pack, so the aromatics come dried and the potatoes come as flakes. What matters is preserved: the mushroom soak. Half an ounce of dried mushrooms in hot water makes a stock stronger than anything a bouillon cube produces on its own, and pouring that soaking liquid into the pot is the whole point.

Potato flakes replace the diced potatoes that traditionally thicken this. They do it in thirty seconds instead of twenty minutes of simmering, and they carry the oil and the parmesan fat instead of letting it slick the surface. Salami stands in for pancetta because it does not need a cooler. Add the tomato paste with the aromatics and let it hit hot oil for a moment before the water goes in, or it just tastes like tomato.
