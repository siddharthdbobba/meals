This is diner food, so it needs a cooler and a real pan — it does not compress into a freezer-bag meal. Par-boiling is what makes it work at camp: raw potatoes take twenty-plus minutes in a pan and burn on the outside before the middle gives up, while par-boiled ones only need to brown. One pot does both jobs if you dry it between.

The eggs are the part that fails. A camp stove throws heat at one spot, so the whites near the edge stay raw while the bottom sets. Covering the pan turns the residual moisture into steam and cooks the tops down. Drain the mushrooms well and do not crowd the pan; too much water and the potatoes steam grey instead of crisping.
