steps:
  - "Build a fire early and let it burn down. You want 8 to 10 coals under a 12-inch Dutch oven and 16 to 18 on the lid."
  - "Grease the bottom and the lower half of the pot with butter. Tear the doughnuts into rough chunks and pile them in with the raisins."
  - "In a separate pot or a large bag, whisk the egg powder and milk powder into 600 ml of cold water until no dry lumps remain. Add the cinnamon, vanilla, melted butter, and 1/4 cup of bourbon."
  - "Pour the custard over the doughnuts. Press down with a spoon so everything is wet, then leave it ten minutes to soak."
  - "Lid on, coals under and on top. Bake about 60 minutes. Rotate the pot a quarter turn every 15 minutes and the lid the other way, or one side burns."
  - "It is done when the center is set and a knife comes out clean. Pull the coals off the lid and let it sit ten minutes."
  - "For the glaze, stir the powdered sugar with 3 tbsp milk powder, the rest of the bourbon, and cold water a splash at a time until it pours. Drizzle over the pot."

The original recipe leans on half-and-half and cream cheese icing, neither of which survives a weekend in a cooler. Whole milk powder and powdered eggs rebuild the custard from a bag, and the glaze becomes powdered sugar, more milk powder, and bourbon. That trade costs a little tang and saves you a fridge.

Stale doughnuts are the point. Fresh ones dissolve into paste when the custard hits them, while a day-old cake doughnut holds enough structure to stay recognizable after an hour over coals. Coal placement does the rest: too many underneath and the bottom sets into a crust before the middle cooks.
