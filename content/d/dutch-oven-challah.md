steps:
  - "Stir the yeast and 1 tbsp of the honey into the warm water in your largest pot. Leave it five minutes, until it foams. No foam means dead yeast and no bread."
  - "Beat in four eggs, the oil, the rest of the honey, and the salt. Add the flour a cup at a time, stirring until it stops being a batter."
  - "Turn it out onto a clean board or a floured lid and knead about ten minutes, until it is smooth and stops sticking to your hands."
  - "Oil the pot, return the dough, cover it, and set it somewhere warm. An hour, until it has doubled. Cool weather makes this longer, not shorter."
  - "Punch it down and let it rise a second time, about 45 minutes."
  - "Divide into three ropes, braid them, and coil the braid to fit the dutch oven. Oil the oven well. Let the loaf rest 20 minutes while you build the coals."
  - "Beat the last egg with 1 tbsp water and brush it over the loaf."
  - "Bake covered with about 8 coals under the oven and 16 on the lid, roughly 375F, for 35 to 40 minutes. Rotate the oven and the lid a quarter turn every ten minutes so it does not scorch on one side."
  - "It is done when the bottom sounds hollow. Pull it out of the pot to cool or the crust goes soft."

This is a car-camping project, not a trail recipe. Nothing about it is fast: two rises, a braid, and forty minutes of managing coals. The reason to do it anyway is that fresh bread out of a dutch oven is a thing you cannot resupply, and the dough tolerates a lot of neglect while you do other camp chores.

The honey and oil are structural, not decorative. Both keep the crumb soft for a second day, which matters when there is no fridge and no toaster. Coal placement is the part people get wrong: twice as many on the lid as underneath, or the bottom burns before the middle sets. Eggs are the weak link on shelf life, so bake this on day one.
