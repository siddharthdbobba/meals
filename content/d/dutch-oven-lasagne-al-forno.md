The original of this is a fridge-and-oven dish, so most of the work here was replacing things that spoil. Freeze-dried beef rehydrates in the ragu and weighs nothing in the food box. The bechamel is built from milk powder and water, which behaves the same as milk once the roux has it. No-boil sheets matter: there is no second pot of water at camp, and they take their moisture from the sauce.

The thing that ruins a camp lasagne is a loose ragu. Any liquid that has not cooked off before assembly comes out of the layers in the dutch oven and pools at the bottom. Simmer it until it is genuinely thick. Coal placement is the other half. Most of the heat belongs on the lid, or the base scorches while the top stays pale.
