The original is a stovetop white sauce with fresh milk, fresh potatoes, and a chunk of refrigerated ham. None of that travels. Powdered whole milk makes an honest roux sauce, dehydrated potatoes rehydrate in the same boil you were making anyway, and canned or pouched ham keeps at ambient temperature until you open it.

The roux is the part worth doing carefully. Flour cooked in butter for a minute before liquid goes in is what keeps the sauce from breaking into grit when the potatoes hit it. Coals on the lid do the rest: the crumb layer needs top heat to brown, which is why the count is heavier up top than underneath.
