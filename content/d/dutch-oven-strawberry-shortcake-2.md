The original is a sheet cake that leans on cold butter, eggs, and four cups of heavy cream. None of that survives a weekend in a car. Shortening is the whole trick: it is shelf-stable at any temperature, it cuts into flour the same way cold butter does, and it carries enough fat to make up for dropping the eggs. The dough is stiffer than the original and bakes closer to a biscuit than a cake, which is what shortcake was to begin with.

Dried strawberries with sugar and a little water do the same job as macerating fresh ones, just from the other direction. Give them the full twenty minutes or you get chewy fruit and dry sugar. The powdered topping mix is the only fussy part: it needs cold water and a hard whisk, so mix it last, after the cake is off the coals and your hands are free.
