Molasses is doing the structural work. It is thick enough to glaze without reducing, so the sauce clings to chicken instead of pooling under it, and the vinegar and mustard cut it back from candy. Half the sauce goes on early so it soaks in during the first bake, half goes on late so it stays glossy.

The pineapple waits until the second half. An hour under coals turns rings to mush, and the acid in the juice is already in the sauce. Canned chicken and canned sweet potatoes replace the fresh version, which drops the cooler requirement entirely and shortens the browning step, since the chicken is already cooked and only needs color.
