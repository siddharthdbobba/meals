The bread crumbs are load-bearing. Twelve eggs alone in a dutch oven give you a wet curd that never sets in the middle; the crumbs absorb liquid and hold the whole thing together as a sliceable casserole. Skip them and you are stirring scrambled eggs for forty minutes instead.

Coal placement is the other half. Most of the heat goes on the lid, not underneath, or the bottom scorches before the center cooks. Dry mustard and Worcestershire are doing what a fresh onion would do at home, without anything that spoils.
