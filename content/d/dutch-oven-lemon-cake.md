The original was a pressure cooker cake, which is really just a steamed cake. Coals under a dutch oven do the same job by a different route, so the substitution holds: an enclosed pot, gentle heat, and a batter thin enough to set evenly. Orange juice in place of half the water is the one thing worth keeping from the source. It adds sugar and acid, which is why the crumb stays moist without the fridge the box assumes.

Coal placement is the whole recipe. Heavy on the lid, light underneath, or you get a burnt bottom and raw center. Powdered egg replaces the real thing so nothing has to survive a warm day, and the glaze is mixed at camp because it sets hard in a bottle.
