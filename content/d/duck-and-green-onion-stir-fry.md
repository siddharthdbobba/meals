Duck is fatty enough that most of the flavor survives freeze-drying, which is why it holds up here where lean chicken would not. The rehydrating water is worth keeping: it carries the fat and salt, and it makes a better sauce base than plain water.

The fry step matters. Rehydrated meat comes out wet, and if you pour sauce straight onto it you get soup. Three minutes in hot oil first drives that off and gives the cornstarch something to cling to. Fresh aromatics were replaced with dried, which is the only real change: powder in a bag beats a bruised knob of ginger four days in.
