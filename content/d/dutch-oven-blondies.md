Blondies are a brownie without the cocoa, which means brown sugar and butter carry the whole thing. That works in a dutch oven because there is no chocolate to scorch against the cast iron, and the batter is thick enough that it will not run into the seam of the lid when the pot sits crooked on uneven coals.

Most of the failure here is heat, not batter. Two thirds of your briquettes go on the lid so the top sets before the bottom burns, and you rotate pot and lid in opposite directions so no one spot cooks twice as long as the rest. The nuts and chips go on top rather than mixed in for the same reason: chips resting on the surface stay chips instead of melting into a scorched layer at the bottom. Powdered egg turns this into a no-cooler recipe if you need it to be one.
