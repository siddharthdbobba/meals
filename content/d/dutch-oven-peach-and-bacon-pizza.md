Home pizza dough leans on a long cold rise for flavor. In camp there is no fridge and no overnight, so the yeast goes up to a full teaspoon and the dough rises once, warm and fast. You lose some depth. The semolina makes up for it — it gives the crust body and a coarse chew that survives being baked on coals rather than a stone.

The rest is substitution. Caramelized onions become dried onion flakes soaked back to life, fresh peaches become dried slices, and the drained yogurt cheese is gone entirely because it will not keep. Blue cheese and bacon carry the salt, the peaches carry the sweet, and that contrast was the whole point of the original. Heat control is the real skill here: more coals on the lid than under it, or the bottom burns before the top sets.
