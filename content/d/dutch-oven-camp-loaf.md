steps:
  - "Warm 1 1/2 cups of water on the fire until it is barely above body temperature. Too hot kills the yeast, so pull it early and let it sit."
  - "Stir the yeast into the warm water and leave it ten minutes. It should foam. If it does not, the yeast is dead and you are making pan bread instead."
  - "Add the yeast water and 4 tbsp of butter to the bag of flour, sugar, salt, and milk powder. Mix with a spoon until it clumps, then turn it onto a clean surface and knead five minutes."
  - "Grease the dutch oven, put the dough in, put the lid on, and set it somewhere warm but not hot. An hour, until it has doubled."
  - "Punch it down, shape it into a log, and let it rise a second time in the greased oven, about 45 minutes."
  - "Bake with 8 coals under the oven and 16 on the lid, roughly 35 to 40 minutes. Rotate the oven and the lid a quarter turn every ten minutes so it does not scorch on one side."
  - "It is done when the top is brown and a knock on the loaf sounds hollow. Brush the last tablespoon of butter over it and get it out of the pot before the residual heat dries it out."

This is a basecamp recipe, not a backpacking one. Two rises and a coal bake take about three hours, and you need a real dutch oven. The tradeoff is a full loaf of bread for a weekend, which beats carrying squashed sandwich bread from a store.

The original calls for fresh milk. Milk powder does the same job, since the milk is there for softness and browning rather than moisture. Coal placement is the part that actually decides the outcome: more on the lid than underneath, or the bottom burns before the middle sets. Turning the oven and the lid in opposite directions evens out the hot spots any fire has.
