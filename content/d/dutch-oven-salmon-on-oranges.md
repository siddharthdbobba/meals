The orange slices are a rack, not a garnish. They lift the fish off the cast iron so the bottom steams instead of scorching, and the quarter cup of water plus the juice they release makes enough steam to cook a full layer of fillets under a closed lid. That is also why nothing gets flipped.

This one keeps its fresh ingredients because a dutch oven meal is already a cooler-and-vehicle affair. Dill is the exception: dried holds its flavor for months and there is no reason to carry the fresh bunch. Heat control matters more than timing, so build a coal bed rather than setting the oven straight in flames, and check doneness with a fork instead of the clock.
