Hoppin' John is a one-pot dish by nature, which is most of why it belongs in camp. The original calls for frozen peas and a can of broth, and neither survives a car trunk in August. Dried peas soaked in a water bottle on the way in do the same job at a fraction of the weight, and bouillon cubes replace fifteen ounces of liquid you would otherwise carry.

The timing is the part that goes wrong. Rice needs about twenty minutes and soaked black-eyed peas need closer to forty, so the peas go in with a head start on nothing — they cook together, and the soak is what closes the gap. Skip the soak and you get crunchy peas in mushy rice. Coals underneath, only a few on the lid: too much bottom heat scorches the rice before the peas give up.
