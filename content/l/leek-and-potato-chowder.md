The original is a stockpot recipe built on a stick of butter, a quart of half and half, and three-quarters of a cup of sour cream. None of that travels. Milk powder, butter powder, and sour cream powder do the same three jobs and weigh a few ounces. The tang of the sour cream matters more than you would expect, so do not skip it and expect the milk powder to cover.

Two kinds of potato are doing two different things. The flakes are the thickener, which is why they go in last and off the heat. The freeze-dried dice give it texture so it does not read as gravy. Add the flakes slowly. You can always thicken a thin soup, but you cannot thin one out without a second liter of water you probably hauled uphill.
