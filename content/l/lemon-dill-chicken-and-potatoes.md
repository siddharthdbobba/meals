The original version of this is a skillet dish: flour-dredged thighs browned in butter, then a cream sauce built in the same pan. None of that survives a stove that only boils. So the flour comes out, the potato flakes do the thickening, and the chicken arrives already cooked in a pouch.

The acid matters more than it looks. Powdered milk and butter powder alone read flat and chalky. A quarter teaspoon of citric acid cuts through the fat and makes the dill taste like an ingredient instead of a garnish. Add it before you taste for salt, or you will oversalt trying to fix the wrong problem.
