The original is a slow cooker recipe: cubed russets, celery, and onion sitting in broth for eight hours, then mashed and finished with milk and butter. None of that travels. The fix is to start from the mashed end. Potato flakes already are cooked potato, so the only thing that needs real cooking time is the dried vegetables, and four minutes at a simmer handles them.

Milk, butter, and cheese all become powders, which is what makes this shelf-stable at all. The dried diced potatoes are worth the extra bag space — flakes alone give you a smooth soup with no texture, and the cubes put back what the masher would have left behind. Add the flakes slowly. It thickens fast and there is no going back once it seizes.
