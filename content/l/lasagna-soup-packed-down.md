The original is a slow cooker recipe: brown beef, dump in a jar of sauce and a quart of broth, walk away for six hours. None of that travels. Jarred sauce is mostly water in glass, broth is water, ricotta is a cold-chain problem, and fresh spinach turns to slime by day two. So everything wet became a powder and the six hours became twelve minutes over a stove.

The milk powder and parmesan are doing the ricotta's job — fat and body stirred in off the heat so it goes creamy instead of grainy. Add them while the pot is still boiling and the proteins seize into curds floating in red broth. Tomato powder is worth sourcing over more bouillon; it is what makes this read as lasagna and not as beef noodle soup.
