This is a density trick wearing a drink costume. Sugar makes water heavier, so a mix with 40 g of sugar sinks under one with 20 g, and a zero-sugar mix floats over both. The colors just make the physics visible. If the layers blend, one of your three drinks had sugar closer to its neighbor than you thought.

It stays on the car-camping and day-hike end of the library because it needs a clear cup, three containers, and a steady hand. Cold water matters more than ice: heat drives convection and mixes the layers on its own. Nothing here needs a fridge or a stove, and the powders keep indefinitely in a bin.
