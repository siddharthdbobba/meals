Home versions of this soup sauté fresh vegetables in butter, then simmer eight cups of broth for the better part of an hour. Neither the fridge nor the fuel budget survives the trip. Dehydrated vegetables and bouillon do the same job: the vegetables rehydrate in the same pot they cook in, and the water goes from eight cups to two and a half because you are eating the soup, not swimming in it.

The squash is the slow part. Dried cubes stay hard well past the point where the rice is done, so the off-heat rest matters more than the simmer does. Oil goes in at the end because a soup this lean is thin without it, and boiling it just drives the flavor off.
