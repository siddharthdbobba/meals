Lu rou fan is a braise, which means it wants a long simmer and a sauce that reduces. Neither is a problem on a camp stove as long as the pot has a lid and the stove will hold a low flame. The whole spices are the part that goes wrong outdoors, so they ride in a tea ball. You pull one object out at the end instead of hunting star anise pods by headlamp.

The swaps are dried onion for the fresh yellow onion, jarred garlic and ginger for the knob and cloves, and instant rice for a second long boil. The mushroom soaking water does double duty as the braising liquid, so nothing is carried in just to be poured away. Pork is the one cooler item; cook it the first night out, or use the chicken pouch version and skip the cooler entirely.
