Lampascioni are the bulbs of wild hyacinth, dug in southern Italy before the plants flower. Raw they are bitter and not safe to eat: the bitterness comes from saponin-type compounds that boiling pulls out. Traditional practice is to boil them, then cure them in olive oil with salt and herbs and serve them as antipasto. Buy them that way. Cured bulbs in a jar are a pantry item; raw bulbs from a garden center are not food and may carry pesticide.

Everything else here is a shelf-stable carrier for them. Canned beans give the plate bulk and protein, the olives push the salt up, and the oil does the same job it does in the jar. There is no cooking step, so this works at a picnic table, in a boat, or at any camp where you can open two containers and stir.
