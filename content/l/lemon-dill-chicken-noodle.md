The original poaches raw chicken breast in stock and browns fresh mirepoix in butter. Neither travels. Freeze-dried chicken and dried vegetables get you to the same place, and the vegetables go in first with a few minutes of hard boiling so they rehydrate before the noodles start dumping starch into the water.

The acid at the end is the part worth keeping. Dried soup tastes flat and salty on its own; lemon or citric acid cuts through it and makes the dill read as dill instead of dust. Add it off the heat, or the brightness cooks off. Butter powder stands in for the fat that used to come from browning, which is what keeps this from tasting like a bouillon cube.
