steps:
  - "At home: get a heavy pot hot and sear the lamb hard on every side. Pull it out and set it aside."
  - "Drop the heat to medium. Cook the onion, celery, carrot, and garlic until soft and just colored."
  - "Stir in the tomato paste and give it a minute, then add the tomatoes, stock cube, rosemary, and bay leaf."
  - "Move everything to a slow cooker with the lamb on the bottom. Season, add water to barely cover, lid on, 6-8 hours on low. Or keep it in the pot, lid on, 3-4 hours in a low oven."
  - "Lift the lamb out. Boil the sauce down by about a third. Shred the meat off the bone and stir it back in. Taste and fix the salt."

This is a home recipe that travels because you do all the work in your kitchen. Shoulder is the whole point: it has enough collagen and fat that six hours on low turns it silky instead of dry. Reducing the sauce after the meat comes out is what keeps it from being soup, and chilling it overnight lets you lift off the set fat, which is both the greasy part and the part most likely to go off in a cooler.

Frozen flat, a bag of this is its own ice pack for the first day out. In camp there is no cooking left, only reheating, so one pot and one boil covers pasta and sauce both. Nothing here is shelf-stable, so eat it the first night and plan dry food after that.
