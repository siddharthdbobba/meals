This is the meal that eats the cooler. Half an onion, a stub of pepper, the last of the shredded cheese, the eggs you overbought on day one. Nothing here needs to be measured precisely, which is the point on a morning when you are also breaking down tents.

The order matters more than the ingredients. Browning the potatoes and vegetables first drives off water and builds a crust; pour the eggs in too early and you get a wet scramble instead of a sliceable round. The lid does the rest, trapping enough heat to set the top without a broiler. Keep the flame low once the eggs go in, since a camp stove runs hot in one spot and will scorch the bottom long before the middle firms up.
