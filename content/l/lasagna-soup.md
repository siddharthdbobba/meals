Lasagna is a layering problem, and layering is not something a pot on a rock does well. Boiled in one vessel, the same ingredients still work, because the starch coming off the noodles is what gives lasagna sauce its body in the first place. Break the noodles small at home. Long shards are hard to eat with a spoon and they expand more than you expect.

The fresh parts had to go. Crushed tomatoes become tomato powder, which weighs almost nothing and rehydrates fine as long as you add it late so it does not stick and burn. Ricotta and fresh mozzarella have no trail future, so grated parmesan and olive oil carry the fat instead. Summer sausage replaces raw Italian sausage: already cured, already cooked, and it releases enough seasoned fat into the broth to do most of the same job.
