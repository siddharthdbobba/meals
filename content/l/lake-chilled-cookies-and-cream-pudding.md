Instant pudding sets by hydrating starch and thickeners, not by chilling, so it works cold in a bag with no stove and no fridge. Nido whole milk powder stands in for the milk on the box. The fat matters: nonfat powders make a thin, chalky pudding, and the whole-milk version sets closer to the real thing.

The short pour is the point. Three quarters of a cup of water for a whole box, rather than the two cups the package wants, gives you something firm enough to eat out of a bag without wearing it. Fifteen minutes in cold water finishes the set and drops the temperature; in warm water it still sets, just softer. Split the box into two servings at home so you are not measuring powder on a rock.
