The original is a cast iron omelet built on fresh eggs, cream cheese, heavy cream, and cold-smoked salmon. Every one of those is a refrigerator problem. Powdered whole egg and cream cheese powder rehydrate into something close enough, olive oil replaces the butter without melting in your pack, and shelf-stable pouched salmon carries the smoke and salt that made the dish worth eating. Dried onion soaked for a few minutes gets most of the way to raw red onion.

Scrambled instead of folded, because a proper omelet needs a wide pan and a steady flame and you have neither. Keep the heat low. Reconstituted egg turns rubbery faster than the fresh kind, and once it squeaks against the spoon there is no fixing it.
