The dry mix does two jobs at once. The bottom layer drinks up the peach syrup and turns into batter; the top stays powdery until the melting butter soaks down through it and browns. That is why nothing gets stirred. Stir it and you get one wet layer that steams instead of baking.

Heat placement matters more than time. Most of the coals go on the lid, because a cobbler needs to brown from above and the fruit on the bottom only has to bubble. Too many underneath and the syrup scorches before the top ever sets. This is car-camping food — canned fruit and a cast iron pot are heavy, and there is no lighter version of it worth eating.
