This is a three-hour braise, so it belongs at a car camp or a basecamp where the fire is going anyway. Neck and shoulder are the cheap, tough, collagen-heavy cuts, and collagen is exactly what makes stew feel like stew. Rush it and you get chewy meat in thin liquid.

Two changes from a kitchen version. Fresh mushrooms are 90 percent water and bruise in a pack, so dried ones go in dry and rehydrate in the braise, which also concentrates the liquid. Pearl onions get peeled at home or bought already peeled, because peeling eight ounces of them by headlamp is a bad night. The flour-and-oil paste at the end replaces butter, and it goes in last so you can stop when the thickness is right.
