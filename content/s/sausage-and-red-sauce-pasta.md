The original is a slow cooker recipe: sausage simmered in jarred sauce all afternoon, poured over pasta. None of that survives a pack. Jarred sauce is mostly water and glass, fresh sausage needs a fridge, and draining pasta wastes both fuel and a liter of hot water. So the sauce becomes tomato powder, the sausage becomes a cured stick that keeps for weeks, and the pasta cooks in exactly the water the sauce will need.

The measured water is the part to get right. Too much and you get soup, too little and the pasta stays chalky. Six hundred milliliters for four ounces of small shapes lands close, and the tomato powder absorbs whatever is left over. Cured sausage brings its own fat and salt, which is why the added salt stays modest.
