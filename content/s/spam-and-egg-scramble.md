The original is a cooler recipe: fresh eggs, a fresh onion, a can of meat. Only one of those three survives a warm car for three days, so the eggs became powder and the onion became flakes. The canned meat was already the shelf-stable part and needed no help.

Crisping the meat before the egg goes in matters more than it sounds. Rendered fat coats the pan, which keeps powdered egg from welding itself to the metal, and the browned edges give the scramble the only texture contrast it has. Reconstituted egg sets faster than fresh, so pull it while it still looks underdone.
