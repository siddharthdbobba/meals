Baked oatmeal is a casserole, and a casserole is what a dutch oven is for. The only real conversions are the wet ingredients: milk becomes milk powder, eggs become egg powder, and both rehydrate in the same water the oats need anyway. Nothing in the pot needs a cooler.

The eggs and baking powder are structure, not richness. They are why this cuts into squares and travels in a bag for a second breakfast, instead of collapsing into porridge. Coal placement matters more than time: most of the heat goes on the lid, because a dutch oven sitting in a pile of coals burns the bottom long before the middle sets.
