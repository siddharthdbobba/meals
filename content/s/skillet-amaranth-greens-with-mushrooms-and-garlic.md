Amaranth greens dry well and rehydrate fast, which is what makes this work away from a kitchen. Fresh, this is a garden-thinning dish; on trail it becomes a bag of green flakes weighing almost nothing. The mushroom soaking water is the important part — it carries most of the flavor, so it goes into the pan, not the dirt.

The garlic sets the tone. Browning it slowly in fat before anything else hits the pan builds the base the greens cook into. Rush it over a roaring canister flame and you get scorched garlic and a bitter pan. This is a side, not a meal: about 200 calories, so pair it with rice, couscous, or a foil packet of something.
