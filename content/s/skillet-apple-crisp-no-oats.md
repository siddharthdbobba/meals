The home version bakes sliced fresh apples under cold-butter crumb for nearly an hour. Neither of those survives a trip. Dried apples do the same job at a fifth of the weight, and they bring their own sugar back when you rehydrate them. Butter powder replaces the stick, and oil gives the crumb something to hold onto so it clumps instead of staying dust.

The part that matters is heat from above. A crisp gets crisp because dry topping sits in dry air over wet fruit. In a lidded pot on a stove, the topping mostly steams and comes out soft but still good. Coals on a dutch oven lid are what turn it brown, so use one if you brought it.
