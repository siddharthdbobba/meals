steps:
  - "Build a fire and let it burn down to coals. You want steady heat under the grate, not flames licking the meat."
  - "Pat the thighs dry with a paper towel. Wet skin steams instead of crisping."
  - "Mix the salt, pepper, brown sugar, and paprika, then rub it over every surface, skin side hardest."
  - "Lay the thighs skin side up on the grate, off to the side of the hottest coals. Cover with a foil tent or an inverted pot if you have one. Cook 25 to 30 minutes."

This started as an oven recipe, which is useless in camp. The method transfers cleanly because the oven was only ever doing two jobs: steady moderate heat to cook the meat through, then a short blast to crisp the skin. Coals under a grate do the first, and moving the thighs directly over them does the second.

The rub is equal parts salt, pepper, brown sugar, and paprika, which is why it survives being measured by eye at a picnic table. The sugar is what browns and blackens, so it is also the reason those last few minutes need your full attention. Bone-in thighs are forgiving over uneven coals in a way breasts are not — a few extra minutes costs you nothing.
