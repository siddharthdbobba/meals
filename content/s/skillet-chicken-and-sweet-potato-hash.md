The source was a spatchcocked bird roasted over sweet potatoes and brussels sprouts in a 425F oven. None of that survives a camp kitchen: no oven, no five-pound raw chicken, no cooler space for it. What does survive is the structure, which is fat, salt, garlic, and hard herbs on starchy vegetables with the meat added at the end.

Dehydrated sweet potato does the work here. It rehydrates in about twelve minutes and then browns properly once the pot goes dry, which is the closest you get to roasting without an oven. Coconut oil is the right fat for this because it stays solid in a jar and takes the heat without smoking. Pouch chicken goes in last and only to warm up, since it is already cooked and will shred to nothing if you cook it twice.
