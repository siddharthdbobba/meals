The original is a stuffed pork roast that bakes an hour in a covered pan. None of that survives a stove with one pot, so what carried over is the flavor logic: fruit sugar, soy salt, and vinegar acid against fatty meat. Dried cranberries and orange do the same job as the drained cans, and they weigh nothing.

The cornstarch matters more than it looks. Without it you get pork and rice sitting in a thin brown puddle. One teaspoon turns that liquid into a glaze that coats the rice instead of pooling under it. Add the vinegar off the heat so the sharpness is still there when you eat.
