Rhubarb is mostly water, which makes it a good candidate for drying and a bad one for carrying fresh. Dried, it rehydrates back into fruit in about fifteen minutes, and simmering it in sugar syrup rather than plain water does two things: the sugar keeps the slices intact instead of collapsing, and it gives you a real syrup at the bottom of the pot. Star anise and vanilla carry the sour end of it.

The original serves this with fresh raspberries and vanilla quark. Neither travels, so the berries are freeze-dried and added at the end where they will not turn to sludge, and the dairy is gone. The caramelized walnuts are the one step that needs attention — sugar goes from melted to burnt fast on a canister stove, and there is no second chance once it does.
