The original simmers brown lentils in broth, then cooks a canned tomato sauce down for another quarter hour. That is two boils and a lot of fuel. Red lentils solve it: they break down in about twelve minutes and thicken the pot themselves, so the sauce arrives already at the right consistency.

Everything wet in the original has a powder that does the same job. Tomato powder replaces both the can and the paste, and it hydrates in the residual heat rather than needing its own simmer. The molasses is what keeps this from tasting like plain tomato lentils, so do not drop it without putting something dark and sweet in its place. Add the acid and the soy at the end; boiling them long only flattens them out.
