This is a condiment, not a meal, and it earns its weight by making plain camp food taste like it was planned. Ketchup is already cooked tomato, sugar, and vinegar, so the simmer is not about cooking raw ingredients down. It is about driving off water, letting the sugar deepen, and giving the mustard, garlic, and chipotle time to stop tasting like separate powders.

Heat control is the only real skill here. Sugar and honey sitting on a hot spot will catch and turn bitter before you notice, so keep the pot off direct flame and keep the spoon moving. Once it cools it holds about two weeks in a cooler, or a few days in a jar on a cool trip. Left undated in a hot car, it will ferment, so make it the night you plan to use it.

One note on the tags: this is dairy-free and gluten-free only if your Worcestershire is, since some brands are cut with malt vinegar. It is not vegetarian either way, as Worcestershire is anchovy-based.
