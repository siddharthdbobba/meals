The original is a baked dish: tomatoes hollowed out, stuffed with rice and peas, run under a breadcrumb crust in an oven. None of that survives a trip. What does survive is the combination — sweet cooked-down tomato, soft rice, onion and pepper underneath, and something crunchy on top — so the recipe keeps the parts and drops the shell.

Tomato paste from a tube does the work the tomato pulp and sauce did, at a fraction of the weight, and it thickens as the rice absorbs the water. Toasting the breadcrumbs first is the step worth not skipping; dumped in raw they turn to paste. Butter is out, so olive oil carries the fat, and hard cheese travels a couple of days without a cooler.
