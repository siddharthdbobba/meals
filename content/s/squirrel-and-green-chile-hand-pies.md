steps:
  - "Put the squirrel, tomatoes, chiles, cumin, garlic powder, cayenne, salt, and pepper in the dutch oven with about 500 ml of water. Lid on."
  - "Bury it in coals, low heat, and let it simmer two hours or until the meat pulls off the bone with a fork. Add coals as they die."
  - "Pull the meat out, let it cool enough to handle, and pick it clean. Shred it and return it to the pot with just enough of the cooking liquid to hold together. Cook the rest off. Stir in the cilantro and cheese."
  - "Make the dough while the pot works: flour, baking powder, and a pinch of salt in a bowl, then cut in the cold butter and lard until it looks like gravel. Add cold water a little at a time until it comes together. Rest it 15 minutes."

Old squirrels are tough. That is the whole reason this recipe exists. A long wet simmer with tomatoes and chile breaks down connective tissue that a skillet never will, and the braising liquid ends up doing double duty as the sauce. Young squirrels do not need any of this and are better fried.

The crust uses both butter and lard on purpose. Butter gives flakiness, lard gives structure that holds up when you carry a pie away from the fire in your hand. Keep both cold until they hit the flour. This is car camping food, not pack food: you need a dutch oven, a cooler, and a couple hours of coals, and the payoff is a dinner you can eat standing up.
