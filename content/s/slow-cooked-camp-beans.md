This started as a slow cooker recipe, which is a device you do not have in the woods. The translation is a lidded pot on the lowest flame your stove will hold, or set at the edge of a fire's coals rather than over the flames. Same principle: gentle, steady heat for a long time, so the beans hydrate evenly instead of blowing their skins off in a hard boil.

The overnight soak is the part that saves fuel, and fuel is the real cost here. Boiling briefly and then letting the beans sit off the heat does the same work as a cold soak in a third of the time, and it cuts the cook down to an hour or so. Salt goes in at the end, not the start, because salted water slows the skins from softening. Dump the soak water rather than cooking in it, which takes the edge off the digestive fallout.
