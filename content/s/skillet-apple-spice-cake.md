This is a cake that works because it has no eggs and no butter in the crumb. Applesauce and oil do both jobs — the sauce brings moisture and acid to react with the baking soda, the oil brings fat that does not need to stay cold. That means the whole dry side travels premixed in a bag and the wet side travels in pouches and a bottle. Nothing here cares about a cooler.

The apples go on the bottom because that is the hottest surface, and the sugar in them caramelizes against the cast iron. Flip the pot at the end and they are the top of the cake. The cocoa is a small amount on purpose: it darkens the crumb and deepens the spice without reading as chocolate. Coals on the lid matter more than coals underneath — too many below and the apple layer scorches before the center sets.
