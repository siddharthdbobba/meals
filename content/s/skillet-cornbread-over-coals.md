The original is three ingredients and a hot oven. Camp has neither an oven nor a refrigerator, so the egg and the buttermilk become powders and the oven becomes a bed of coals under a lid. The chemistry is unchanged: self-rising cornmeal already carries its leavening, and buttermilk powder brings back the acid that sets it off.

The two things that decide whether this works are a preheated, oiled pan and a batter thinner than you think it should be. A cold pan means the bottom steams instead of crusting. Thick batter in a covered pot cooks to a dense center that never sets. Pour it, cover it, and leave it alone for twenty minutes.
