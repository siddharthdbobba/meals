The original bakes this in an oven for an hour. A skillet with a lid over low coals does the same job: the crust forms where the rice touches hot metal, and the covered top steams itself. Patience is the whole technique. Move the pan around too much or run the heat too hot and you get a scorched layer instead of a crust.

Yogurt and fresh eggs do not travel, so powdered egg and milk powder stand in. They set the same way when heated, which is what holds the wedge together on the flip. The vinegar or lemon powder is not decoration; without an acid the milk powder tastes flat where the yogurt used to be.
