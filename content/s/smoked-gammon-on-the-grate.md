Gammon is already cured and mostly cooked, so the fire is not doing food-safety work here. It is doing two other things: rendering the fat cap so the outside is not rubbery, and pushing smoke into the scored cuts. That is why the coals stay banked to one side and the meat sits on the cool half. Direct heat over four hours would just dry it out.

The glaze goes on last for a reason. Honey and sugar burn well below the temperature the coals put out, so brushing early gives you a black bitter shell instead of a lacquer. Hold it until the last half hour, then paint every ten minutes and let each coat set. This is a car-camping or basecamp cook, not a backpacking one. The joint needs a cooler and the fire needs four hours of tending.
