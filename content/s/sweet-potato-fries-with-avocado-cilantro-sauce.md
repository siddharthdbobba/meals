steps:
  - "Build a fire and let it burn down to coals. Flames scorch the outside before the middle cooks."
  - "Cut the sweet potatoes into wedges about a finger thick. Toss them in a bag with the corn starch, chili powder, garlic powder, salt, and pepper until every piece is dusty."
  - "Add 1.5 tbsp oil to the bag and work it around. Oil after starch, not before."
  - "Spread the wedges in a single layer on a doubled sheet of heavy foil, fold a loose tent over the top, and set it on a grate above the coals."
  - "Cook 20 minutes, then open the tent, turn the wedges, and leave it open another 10 to 15 so steam escapes and the edges dry out."
  - "While they cook, halve the avocado, mash it in its own shell with a spoon, and stir in the lime juice, cilantro, remaining oil, and a pinch of salt."

Sweet potatoes go limp when they cook in their own steam, which is exactly what a sealed foil packet does to them. The fix is the corn starch and an open tent for the back half of the cook. The starch soaks up surface moisture and sets into a thin dry shell, and venting the foil lets the rest of the water leave instead of pooling under the wedges.

This is a car-camping recipe and it does not pretend otherwise: it wants 35 minutes, a bed of coals, and a cooler for the avocado. The sauce is a thin guacamole, and the lime is not just flavor. Enough acid keeps the cut avocado green long enough to finish the meal and use the rest tomorrow. Mash it in the shell and there is nothing to wash.
