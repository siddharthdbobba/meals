This is the one recipe here that asks for a cooler, a table, and half an hour of hands. It belongs to car camping, the first night of a basecamp, or a hunt camp working through a fresh deer. Nothing about it packs down to a freezer bag.

Venison is lean enough to go dry and crumbly on its own, so the liquid does the heavy lifting: stock, soy, vinegar, and two oils beaten into the meat until it turns tacky. That emulsion is the fat and moisture the animal does not carry. Steaming keeps it there, which is why this method is more forgiving over a camp stove than frying. Fold the filling at home and freeze it flat and the only camp work left is pinching wrappers.
