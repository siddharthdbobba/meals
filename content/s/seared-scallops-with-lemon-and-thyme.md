  - "Build the fire early and let it burn to coals. Set the cast iron on the grate and give it four or five minutes to come up to heat."

  - "Add the olive oil. When it shimmers, lay the scallops down with space between them and leave them alone for two to three minutes."
  - "Flip once and give them another minute or two. Pull them onto a plate and let them sit."

This is a home recipe that survives one night out and no further. Scallops are the whole cost of the dish and they go off fast, so they get cooked the first evening, straight from the coldest part of the cooler. Everything else on the list keeps indefinitely.

Two changes from a kitchen version. Bottled lemon juice replaces a fresh lemon, because a lemon rolling around a pack comes out as a bruised mess and the juice is what you actually need. Dried thyme replaces sprigs for the same reason. The rest is unchanged, and the technique is the point: a dry surface and a pan that is genuinely hot, or you get pale rubber instead of a crust. Coals give steadier heat than flame here, so wait for them.
