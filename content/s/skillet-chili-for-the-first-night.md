This is first-night food. The beef and onion have to be cooked before the cooler warms up, and the whole thing runs on shelf-stable cans you can throw in a bin weeks ahead. Nothing here needs a lid, a thermometer, or attention beyond the occasional stir.

The three tomato products are doing different jobs. Whole tomatoes give you pieces, the sauce gives you body, and the Rotel brings the heat and the acid. Cut any one of them and the pot goes flat or watery. The half hour of simmer is not optional either — that is where the grease, the tomato, and the seasoning stop being three things.

The source cooked this in an electric skillet on shore power. Over a fire or a two-burner it works the same, but heat runs hotter and less even, so keep the flame low once the simmer starts or the bottom scorches.
