steps:
  - "Warm 250 ml of water at the fire until it is barely above body temperature. Too hot kills the yeast."
  - "Stir the water into the dry premix along with the starter and the softened butter. Work it in the pot with a spoon until it holds together, then knead on a clean cutting board for eight minutes. It should stop sticking to your hands."
  - "Cover the pot and leave it somewhere warm for an hour. Near the fire, not in it. It should roughly double."
  - "Punch it down, cut it into 12 pieces, and roll each into a ball: pull the edges to the center, pinch, then roll under a cupped palm until smooth."
  - "Line a 12-inch dutch oven with parchment and set the balls in touching each other. Lid on, rest 30 minutes."
  - "Mix the reserved egg powder with a splash of water and brush the tops. Scatter sesame seeds."
  - "Bake with 8 coals under the oven and 16 on the lid, about 25 minutes. Rotate the oven and the lid a quarter turn each in opposite directions every 10 minutes or the bottoms burn."

This started as a kitchen recipe with a stand mixer, fresh milk, and an oven thermostat. All three are gone. Milk powder and water replace the milk, powdered egg replaces the two yolks and the wash, and a dutch oven with counted coals replaces the 350°F setting. The kneading is by hand, which takes about the same eight minutes the dough hook did.

The instant yeast is what makes this a camp recipe rather than a two-day project. Unfed starter brings sourness and a little softness, but it is slow and unreliable at whatever temperature your campsite happens to be. The yeast handles the rise on a schedule you can plan dinner around. Crowding the rolls in the pan matters too: shoulder to shoulder they push up instead of out, and the sides stay soft.
