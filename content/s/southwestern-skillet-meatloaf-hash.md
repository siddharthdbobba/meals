A meatloaf needs an oven, ninety minutes, and a wire rack. None of that travels. What does travel is the reason meatloaf works: fat, starch, and vegetables held together in one hot mass. Cooked flat in a skillet, it gets there in twenty-five minutes and gains a crust across the whole bottom instead of just the outside.

The vegetables are dehydrated at home rather than roasted at camp, which solves the same problem roasting does. Fresh sweet potato and pepper dump water into the pan and leave you with a stew. Dried ones take up only the water you give them. Cornmeal replaces the egg and breadcrumbs, soaking up the last of the liquid and binding the hash. Add it at the end, a little at a time, or it goes pasty.
