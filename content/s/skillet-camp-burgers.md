The binder is the part that matters when you are cooking over a fire. Egg and bread crumbs hold the patty together through a flip on uneven heat, where a plain ground-beef puck tends to crack and drop half of itself into the coals. The diced onion adds moisture that lean beef otherwise does not have.

Mix and press at home. A camp kitchen has no running hot water, and raw beef on your hands with a two-gallon jug is a bad trade. Patties keep two days on ice; past that, freeze them and let them thaw in the cooler on the drive in.
