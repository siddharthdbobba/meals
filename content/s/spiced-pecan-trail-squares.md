steps:
  - "Heat the oven to 350F. Line a shallow tray, roughly 9x13, with parchment."
  - "Melt the butter in a pan, then take it off the heat and stir in the brown sugar and syrup until it looks smooth."
  - "Let it cool a few minutes, then beat in the eggs. Hot butter plus cold eggs is how you get scrambled batter."
  - "Fold in the flour, ginger, cinnamon, nutmeg, salt, and pecans. Fold, do not beat. Air is the enemy here."
  - "Spread it thin and flat across the whole tray. Bake 22 to 25 minutes, until the edges pull in and the middle is set but still soft."
  - "Cool completely in the tray before cutting. Warm, it tears. Cold, it cuts clean into 12 squares."

This is a bake done at home, not at camp, and the whole point is the shape. Baked flat in a shallow tray with melted butter and no whipping, it comes out dense and low with no air pockets. Air pockets are what turn cake into crumbs at the bottom of a pack. Squares cut from a flat bake hold their edges for days.

The calories come from pecans and butter, which is fat rather than sugar, so it burns slower than a gel or a candy bar. The syrup and brown sugar do double duty: sweetness and moisture retention, which is why it stays chewy for a week instead of drying into a brick. Keep it under a week or the butter starts to taste tired.
