The original is a stuffed chicken breast roasted at 475 alongside boiled potatoes. Neither part survives a trip without a cooler and an oven, so the structure moves rather than the technique: cured pork gets rendered first and its fat becomes the cooking medium for everything after it, which is what the stuffing was doing in the first place. Freeze-dried chicken replaces the raw breast, and it browns once the surface water is gone.

Potato flakes take the place of the boiled rainbow potatoes and cut the water you carry or filter down to half a liter. Add them off the heat and hold back some water — flakes thicken on standing, and a stiff pan is easier to fix than a soupy one. The lemon matters more than it looks; without acid the rendered fat and salt flatten out.
