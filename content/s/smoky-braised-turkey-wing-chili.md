The original is a two-stage kitchen project: beer-braise the wings low and slow to break down the connective tissue, then fry them for crisp skin. Neither stage happens on a stove in the dirt. What does travel is the braise. Cook the wings at home in beer with chili powder, mustard, garlic, onion, brown sugar, salt, and pepper until the meat pulls off the bone, pick it clean, and dry it. The seasoning goes into the meat during the braise, so the dried shreds carry the whole flavor of the pot at a fraction of the weight.

Wing meat is heavy muscle, and dried heavy muscle rehydrates slowly. That is why the meat goes in with cold water and gets a real boil before anything else joins it. The bean flakes at the end do the thickening; skip them and you have a thin soup with rice in it. The vinegar goes in off the heat because a long simmer flattens it.
