The original bakes breaded tofu on a wire rack in a 400-degree oven, which is not a thing you have. A skillet does the same job: the crust sets against hot metal instead of hot air, and you only brown two sides rather than six. Egg is the other casualty. A thin flour-and-water paste glues the crumbs on just as well and does not require carrying something that cracks and spoils.

Pressing matters more than it looks like it should. Aseptic tofu comes packed in liquid, and a wet slab steams in the pan instead of browning, which means the breading slides off in one sad sheet. Ten minutes under a loaded pot is enough. Add the sauce at the very end and keep it shallow, or you undo all of it.
