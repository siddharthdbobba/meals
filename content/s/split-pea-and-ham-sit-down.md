Split peas need an hour of real heat to break down, which is not a thing you do on a canister at 9,000 feet. So the cooking happens at home and the drying does the rest. Peas rehydrate well because they were already pureed to mush before they hit the trays — there is no cell structure left to soften.

Ham hocks and broth do not travel, so bouillon and diced shelf-stable ham carry the same job at a fraction of the weight. The oil goes in at camp, not before drying, because fat in a dehydrated meal turns rancid faster than anything else in the bag.
