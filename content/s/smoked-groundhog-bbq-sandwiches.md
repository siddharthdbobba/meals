This is a long cook, so it belongs at a camp you are not leaving. Five and a half hours of tending a fire is the whole point of the day, not an inconvenience. Groundhog is lean, dense, and full of connective tissue, which is why it goes in two stages: dry smoke first for flavor, then sealed in foil with beer so the collagen breaks down instead of the meat drying out.

The glands matter more than the rub. Miss them and no amount of sauce fixes it. Everything else here is forgiving, including the temperature, as long as you stay under 300F and give it the time. Sauce goes on at the end because sugar burns long before the meat is ready.
