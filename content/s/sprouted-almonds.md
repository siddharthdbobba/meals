Raw almonds carry phytic acid and enzyme inhibitors, which is why a big handful can sit heavy. A long salted soak neutralizes a good share of that, and the almond starts the germination it was holding back. The salt also seasons the nut from the inside, which is why nothing gets added at the end.

The drying step is the part that matters for the trail. A soaked almond is a wet almond, and wet food in a pack is mold in three days. Dry it below 115°F so the fats stay intact and the texture goes crisp rather than leathery, then treat it like any other shelf-stable nut. Done right it keeps for months in a sealed jar.
