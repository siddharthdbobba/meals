The original is a cast iron dish: bone-in thighs seared skin down, then finished in an oven with the orzo drinking the pan drippings. Neither the fridge nor the oven travels, so the chicken becomes foil packets and the drippings become bouillon and olive oil. The orzo still cooks in flavored liquid rather than plain water, which is the part that actually matters.

The orzo absorbs almost all of the 800 ml, so there is no draining and no strainer. Dried lemon peel goes in early because it needs heat and time to give anything up. The bottled juice goes in at the end, off the heat, where the brightness survives.
