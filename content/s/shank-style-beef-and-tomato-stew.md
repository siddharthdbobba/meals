Osso buco is a four-hour braise built on bone marrow and collagen, and neither of those travels. What does travel is the flavor structure underneath: browned beef, tomato reduced hard, a soffritto of onion and celery and carrot, and enough rosemary to cut the sweetness. This rebuilds that with dried parts and fifteen minutes.

Tomato powder is the piece that makes it work. Canned tomatoes are mostly water and glass, and paste is a wet ingredient you cannot carry; the powder gives you the same acid and body at a fraction of the weight, and it thickens as it hydrates. The polenta at the end stands in for the traditional bed of the same, so it is one pot instead of two.
