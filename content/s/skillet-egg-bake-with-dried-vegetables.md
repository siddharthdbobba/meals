The original is an oven casserole in a cast-iron skillet. Nothing about that survives a stove with one burner, so this is a scramble instead of a bake: the same eggs, vegetables, herbs, and cheese, set in a pan over low heat rather than in dry oven air. Fresh milk becomes milk powder, fresh eggs become egg powder, and the whole produce list becomes dried.

Order matters more than technique. The vegetables have to be simmered and drained before the eggs go in, because dried chard and zucchini hold a lot of water and will leave you with egg soup otherwise. Low heat is the other half. Powdered eggs turn rubbery faster than fresh ones, so pull the pan the moment the last of the liquid sets and let the residual heat and the cheese finish it.
