Cast iron over a campfire is mostly a heat management problem. The pan holds a lot of energy and passes it straight into whatever touches the bottom, so direct flame gives you a burnt underside and raw dough half an inch above it. Coals off to the side, a grate, and a few rotations solve that. Low and slow is the whole technique.

Tube dough is the shortcut that makes this a camp recipe instead of a baking project. It is a cooler item and it stays soft, so nothing gets measured or mixed at the site. Covering the skillet with foil traps heat above the dough and helps the top catch up to the bottom, which matters more when your coal bed runs hot.
