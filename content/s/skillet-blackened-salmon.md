  - "Build the fire early and let it burn down. You want a bed of coals, not flames, and a grate that sits steady."

  - "Coat both sides of each piece with the seasoning while the butter is still liquid. Once it cools it stops sticking."
  - "Put the reserved butter in the skillet and set it over the coals for three to five minutes, until it sizzles. Sprinkle half a teaspoon of seasoning into the fat."

Blackening is a crust, not a burn. The butter carries the spice against the fish and the dry heat of a cast iron pan toasts the paprika and pepper in it until they go dark. That is the whole mechanism, and it only works if the pan is genuinely hot before the fish goes down and if you leave the fish still once it does. Moving it early tears the crust off and leaves it in the pan.

This one stays in the cooler category. Salmon is not shelf-stable and there is no way to make it so at camp, so pack it against ice at the bottom and cook it the first night. Butter is the other cooler item; ghee in a jar solves that if you are out for more than a couple of days. Fire management matters more than the recipe here. Cook over coals, because flames will char the butter before the fish is done.
