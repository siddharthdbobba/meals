steps:
  - "Warm 300 ml of water until it is barely above skin temperature. Too hot kills the yeast. Stir in the sugar and yeast and leave it five minutes, until the surface goes foamy."
  - "Add the flour, kosher salt, and 2 tbsp of the oil. Stir with a spoon until there is no dry flour left. The dough should be wet and sticky. Do not knead it and do not add flour to fix the stickiness."
  - "Cover the pot and set it somewhere warm for 45 minutes. In cold weather, put it inside a jacket or near the fire, not on it."
  - "Oil the skillet or dutch oven heavily with the rest of the oil. Pour the dough in, wet your fingers, and press it out to the edges. Dimple it all over. Scatter the flaky salt and herbs."

Focaccia is the easiest bread to make outdoors because it does not need kneading, shaping, or a second rise. It is a wet dough poured into an oiled pan. The oil is not a garnish. It fries the bottom crust while the top steams under the lid, which is how you get bread out of a covered pan with no oven.

The sugar is there to shorten the rise to something you can wait out in camp. The original counts on a warm kitchen; outdoors the dough goes slower the colder it gets, so judge by size rather than the clock. Everything dry travels premixed in one bag, and the only fresh thing involved is water.
