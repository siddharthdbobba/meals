The oven version bakes because it needs the top layer to brown. You cannot brown anything on a canister stove, so the browning is gone and the rest stays. What is left is a pasta cooked in exactly the water it needs, thickened by its own starch, with tomato powder doing the job a jar of marinara did at home.

Measuring the water matters more than usual. Too much and you have soup with cheese floating in it; there is no colander out here to fix that. Undercook the pasta by a minute before you add the powder — it keeps absorbing while the sauce sets up, and mushy is the failure mode. Low-moisture mozzarella and dry parmesan are the only cheeses worth carrying; fresh mozzarella is mostly water and will not survive a warm afternoon in a pack.
