  - "At home: pull the seeds out of the pulp and rinse them under cold water until the strings let go. Fingers work better than a colander."

  - "Drain and dry them on a towel. Wet seeds steam and go leathery."

This is a use-what-you-have snack. A single squash yields roughly a cup of seeds that most people throw away, and roasted they turn into a dense, salty, crush-proof handful that costs nothing and weighs almost nothing.

The brine step is the part worth not skipping. Ten minutes in salted water gets salt into the seed rather than leaving it sitting on a dry hull where it falls off in the bag. Drying them thoroughly afterward is what makes them snap instead of bend, and a seed that snaps keeps for weeks without a fridge.
