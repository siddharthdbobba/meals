This is fish-camp cooking, so the fish is not something you carry. Everything else is: breading, seasoning, and a pound of fat that stays solid in a tub at any temperature you will meet on the water. The lard matters more than the recipe. It holds heat through a batch of cold fillets in a way a thin puddle of oil does not, and cast iron over coals is a slow instrument to correct once the temperature drops.

The home swap is the egg. Buttermilk and fresh eggs are what a lodge kitchen uses; powdered whole egg with cold water does the same job of gluing crumbs to fish and rides in a pack for a week. Flour first, then egg, then crumbs, in that order. Skip the flour and the coating slides off in the pan.
