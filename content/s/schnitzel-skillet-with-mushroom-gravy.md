This is a two-pan recipe pushed into one skillet, in order. Gravy first, then wipe and fry, because frying oil in a pan you then want to make a roux in is a mess. The gravy sits fine off heat for the ten minutes the chops take.

The camp changes are all about the fridge you don't have. Eggs become egg powder, heavy cream and sour cream become their powdered versions, and the pork goes into the cooler frozen so it doubles as ice. Everything else was already shelf-stable. Pound the chops thin at home: quarter-inch meat cooks through before the crust burns, which is the whole trick over a fire you can't turn down.
