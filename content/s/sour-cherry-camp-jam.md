This is preserving stripped to the two things that matter: fruit and its own weight in sugar. No pectin, no water, no jars boiled in a wash basin. The sugar sits on the cherries and draws their juice out overnight, so the pot starts with liquid already in it and you never have to thin the mix and then boil the thin back out.

The long low simmer is doing the work. Water leaves, sugar concentrates, and the syrup thickens on its own once it passes the right point. That point is easier to catch by eye than by clock — the drop test on a cold spoon is the whole technique. Camp jam without a proper canning setup is cooler food, not shelf-stable food, so make a batch you will finish in a few days or carry the jars home to process.
