The original is a kitchen burger built on boiled sweet potato and canned butter beans, which is a lot of water to carry. Dehydrated flakes of both do the same job at a fraction of the weight, and they rehydrate stiffer than the fresh versions, which is what you want in a patty that has to survive a flip on an uneven stove.

Binding is the whole game. Powdered egg and flour set the outside, breadcrumbs soak up whatever moisture is left, and the ten-minute rest before frying is not optional if your pan runs hot. Feta packed in oil is the one shelf-stable soft cheese worth carrying; if the jar worries you, dried parmesan gets you the salt without the risk.
