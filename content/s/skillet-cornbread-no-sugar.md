Preheating the oil in the pan is the whole technique. Batter hitting hot fat sets an immediate crust, which is what keeps a lidded cast iron bake from turning into steamed cornmeal. Pouring that same hot oil back into the batter is not a step to skip either. It warms the mix so the leavening starts working before the pan cools.

There is no sugar in this. The sweetness comes from the creamed corn, which is also most of the liquid. Between the corn and the sour cream the crumb stays moist well past the first night, so leftovers wrapped in foil are breakfast. The powdered egg and sour cream are the trail swap for the home version; both rehydrate close enough that nobody will ask.
