This is the lunch that works because nothing in it needs a stove, a cooler, or your attention. Dry-cured salami and aged hard cheese are both preserved by design, so they sit in a pack at eighty degrees for several days without turning. The bread is the weak link, which is why it should be dense: a soft sandwich loaf turns to compressed crumb by day two, while rye or pumpernickel arrives intact.

Fat and salt are the point. A sandwich like this runs most of its calories through the salami and the cheese rather than the bread, which is what you want at midday when you still have miles left. Cut both on the trail rather than at home. Exposed surfaces are where the oil bleeds and the flavor goes flat.
