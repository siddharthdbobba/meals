The original is a fridge-clearing soup: fresh onion, homemade stock, a bag of spinach on its last day. None of that travels. Bouillon replaces the stock, dried onion and garlic powder replace the aromatics, and freeze-dried spinach replaces a pound of leaves that would turn to slime in a pack by noon. Tomato paste from a tube is the one thing worth carrying as-is — it gives the broth color and a backbone that bouillon alone does not.

Orzo is the reason this works on a stove. It is small enough to cook through in under ten minutes on one boil, and it thickens the broth as its starch comes out, so you get a stew rather than pasta sitting in water. Add the meatballs late; they are already cooked and long simmering only makes them tough.
