Cornbread is one of the few baked goods worth doing over a fire, because it forgives uneven heat. The original calls for fresh buttermilk and whole eggs. Both become powder here, which changes nothing about the crumb and removes the cooler from the equation entirely.

The soda needs acid to work, and that is what the buttermilk powder is for once it hits water. Mix only until the dry streaks disappear. Overworked batter goes tough, and a fire gives you no way to hide it. Coals on the lid are what browns the top; heat from below alone will burn the bottom before the center sets.
