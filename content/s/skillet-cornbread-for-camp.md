This is home cornbread with the fridge taken out of it. Buttermilk and eggs are the two ingredients that normally chain the recipe to a cooler, so both go powdered — dried buttermilk keeps the acid that reacts with the baking soda, and egg powder binds well enough that the loaf slices instead of crumbling. Oil replaces the melted butter because it pours in cold weather and does not go rancid in a food bag.

The hot pan matters more than the exact temperature. Oiling the skillet and warming it before the batter goes in sets the crust immediately, which is what keeps the bread from welding itself to the iron over an uneven coal bed. Coals on the lid give you heat from above so the top sets instead of staying wet while the bottom scorches.
