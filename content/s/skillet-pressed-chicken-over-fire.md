Weight is the whole trick. A chicken left to its own shape touches hot iron in maybe four places; pinned flat under a second pan, most of the skin is pressed into the metal at once. That contact is what browns it, and it also cuts the cook time enough that the breast is done around the same moment the thighs are.

The brine is not optional and it is not seasoning. Salt and sugar in water hold moisture in the meat through forty minutes of hard heat, which is what keeps a flattened bird from going dry at the edges. Camp version: dried herbs instead of fresh sprigs, since fresh ones turn to slime in a cooler by day two, and the cooler does the refrigerating overnight. Everything here is a car-camping or basecamp move. A four-pound bird and two cast iron pans are not going in a pack.
