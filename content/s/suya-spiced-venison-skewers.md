Suya is street food from Nigeria and Cameroon: thin meat, a dry peanut-and-chile rub, high heat, a few minutes a side. It suits camp cooking because the rub is dry. Grind it at home and it keeps for months in a bag, no marinade, no leaking container, no cooler space beyond the meat itself.

Venison is the reason to watch the clock. There is no intramuscular fat to buy you slack, so a minute past done is jerky. The peanut oil and the ground peanuts in the rub cover for that a little, adding back the fat the animal never had. Cook over coals rather than flame; flames will scorch the peanut solids before the meat is through.
