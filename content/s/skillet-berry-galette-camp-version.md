The original wants a food processor, fresh berries by the pint, and a refrigerator. None of that travels. Dried berries plus a soak get you the same volume of fruit at a fraction of the weight, and the soaking liquid becomes the syrup once the cornstarch goes in. Shortening replaces the cold butter because it holds its shape at 80 degrees in a pack; you lose some flavor and gain the ability to make this in August.

Coals on the lid are the whole technique. Heat only from below burns the bottom crust before the top sets, so you split it: a thin bed under the pan, a heavier scatter on top. Rotating the skillet evens out the hot spots every fire has. The rest is patience — cut it hot and you get fruit soup with pastry floating in it.
