steps:
  - "At home, combine flour, cornmeal, oats, powdered milk, sugar, and baking powder in a quart freezer bag. Seal and shake to mix thoroughly."
  - "At camp, pour half the water into the bag and start kneading from the outside, working the bag with your hands."
  - "Add water in small amounts until the dough is stiff—you want bread dough, not pancake batter."
  - "Heat oil in your skillet over medium heat until shimmering."
  - "Flour your hands lightly with extra flour if you have it, or use a little dry mix from the top of the bag."
  - "Pull off a piece of dough about the size of a golf ball, flatten it with your palms into a thin disk roughly 1/8 inch thick."
  - "Fry in the hot oil until golden brown on the first side, about 3 to 4 minutes."
  - "Flip and fry the other side until golden, another 2 to 3 minutes."

Bannock works because cornmeal and oats do what flour can't do alone: they add structure and weight without requiring yeast or long rising. Powdered milk helps bind the dough, and frying in oil is faster than baking while producing an exterior that actually crisps instead of steaming.

All the work happens at home. The dry mix keeps for months in a sealed bag and weighs almost nothing per serving. At camp, you add cold water, knead through the bag, and fry. No bowls, no precision, no time. Slightly thick dough yields a flatbread that holds together; too-thin dough becomes more pancake-like, which is still edible but different.
