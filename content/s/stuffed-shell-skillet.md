Stuffed shells are a casserole: pasta, sauce, and a ricotta filling, baked under foil. None of that survives a pack. So the shells go in empty and the filling gets built in the pot at the end, out of milk powder, parmesan, and oil. It lands in the same place — creamy, tomato-heavy, cheese on top.

The part that matters is not draining. The starchy water left in the pot is what carries the tomato powder and milk powder into a sauce instead of a paste, and it means one pot and no strainer. Add the dairy off the heat. Milk powder held at a boil goes grainy and sticks.
