This started as a home oven cake and needed three changes to work outside. Fresh apples get replaced by dried slices rehydrated in a cup, which cuts the carried weight by most of their volume. The egg and the milk both come as powder, reconstituted with the same half cup of water the original recipe called for. Nothing here needs a cooler.

The coal count is the part people get wrong. A Dutch oven bakes from the lid, not the bottom, so a heavy bed of coals underneath just burns the base while the middle stays raw. Two-thirds up top, one-third below, and turn it regularly. The topping sugar melts into the apples and forms a crust that tells you when it is done.
