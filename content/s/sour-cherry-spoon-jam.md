Fresh sour cherries are a two-week season and they weigh a lot for what you get. Dried ones carry the same acid in a quarter of the weight, and rehydrating them in the pot is just the first ten minutes of the cook. Sugar and heat do the rest: water leaves, the syrup concentrates, and the fruit falls apart into it.

The acid is not optional. Sour cherries bring their own, but drying dulls it, so the lemon juice at the end puts the edge back and helps the jam set. Add it early and you cook off the brightness. Watch the bottom of the pot on a stove flame — this is mostly sugar, and sugar scorches faster than fruit does.
