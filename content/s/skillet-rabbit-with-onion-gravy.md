This is a fry-and-braise, not a fry. The flour dredge does two jobs: it browns on the meat, and what falls off thickens the pan liquid later. Rabbit is very lean, so straight frying dries it out. The twenty minutes in gravy at the end is what keeps it edible.

Age matters more than technique. A young rabbit is tender enough to go from raw to browned to gravy in one pass; an older one has worked its legs for years and needs a wet simmer first or nothing you do in the skillet will fix it. This is a day-one meal off the cooler or straight from the field, and it needs a real skillet, so it stays in camp rather than in a pack.
