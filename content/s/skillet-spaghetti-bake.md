This is a home skillet bake pushed onto a fire ring. The method barely changes, because cast iron does not care where the heat comes from: a bed of coals under the pan and a few on the lid gives you the same top-browning an oven would. The dried herbs and garlic powder are what make it travel. Nothing in the seasoning needs a fridge, and premixing them into one jar means you are adding a single spoonful in the dark instead of opening six containers.

The sugar and vinegar are not decoration. Canned tomatoes run sharp and thin, and those two pull the sauce toward something closer to a long-simmered one in fifteen minutes. Pull the pasta early, since it keeps cooking in the sauce under the lid. Beef and mozzarella both need a cooler, so this is a first- or second-night meal, not a backpacking dinner.
