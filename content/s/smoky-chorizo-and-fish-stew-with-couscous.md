The original is a fresh-fish dish built around a can of tomatoes, which is two pounds of water you would be carrying up a hill. Tomato powder does the same job at a fraction of the weight, and bean flakes stand in for the tin of cannellini. Fish is the one thing worth buying wet: a foil pouch is shelf-stable, needs no cooler, and stays in recognizable pieces.

The chorizo is the whole reason this works. Cured sausage keeps for weeks without refrigeration and renders enough paprika-red fat to season everything that follows, which is how you get depth out of a pantry that is mostly powder. The vinegar at the end is not a garnish. Dried tomato reads flat and sweet, and the acid is what makes it taste like a stew instead of sauce.
