Neck is the muscle a deer uses all day, every day, which is why most people bone it out for burger. It is also why it is worth cooking whole. Ten hours of low, damp heat breaks all that connective tissue down into gelatin, and the meat ends up richer than a backstrap ever will.

This is a car-camp or basecamp cook, not a backpacking one. The roast rides frozen in a cooler and the pot holds the fire's work all day. The only real skill is coal management: low and steady, lid closed, patience. Opening it to check costs you heat every time. Cheese and buns are optional and cooler-dependent; the meat and its own liquid are the dish.
