Couscous never actually cooks. It rehydrates, which means one boil and a lid instead of a burning stove and a stirred pot. That is the whole reason it belongs in a pack: fuel spent per calorie delivered is about as low as grains get.

The dried vegetables need a head start in the water or they stay leathery while the grain goes soft. Fat is the other half of the job. Couscous alone is a thin dinner, so the oil, the cured meat, and the cheese are doing the calorie work, and all three sit at ambient temperature for days without complaint.
