Everything happens in the skillet, which is the point. The butter and sugar are cooked in the same pan the cookie bakes in, so there is no bowl to wash and no creaming to do by hand in the cold. Melted butter also gives a denser, chewier cookie than creamed butter would, which survives being cut and handed around better.

The one step that matters is cooling the butter-sugar mix before the yolk goes in. Hot pan plus raw yolk equals sweet scrambled egg. Coals rather than flame matter too: a cast iron pan sitting on live fire will have a black bottom and raw center in about eight minutes.
