The original is a baked casserole: roux on the stove, breadcrumbs browned in butter, twenty minutes in an 8x8 dish. None of that survives a stove with one burner and no oven, so the structure changed. The roux becomes flour and cornstarch dusted straight onto the drained pasta. The milk, half and half, and shredded cheddar become powders, which is the only version of cheese sauce that travels without a cooler. The bake becomes a stir.

The crumbs are worth the extra step. Toast them first, in the dry pot, before any water goes in; add them at the end and they stay crisp for the length of one meal. Stir them in early and they turn to paste. Low heat matters once the powders go in, because cheese powder scorches on the pot bottom faster than you would expect.
