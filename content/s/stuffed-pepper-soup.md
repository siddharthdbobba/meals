The original is a stovetop soup built on a long sauté: peppers and onions cooked down until they brown, then beef, wine, broth, tomatoes, rice. On trail there is no time and no fuel for fifteen minutes of browning, so the sauté is gone and the wine with it. What is left is the part that carries — the tomato-oregano-mint frame, beef, and rice.

Dried peppers go in with the boil rather than at the end. They are the slowest thing in the pot and the instant rice is the fastest, which is why the rice waits until the heat is off. The olive oil at the end is not garnish. It is a third of the calories and it also mutes the tinny edge of tomato powder.
