The original runs six hours in a slow cooker, which is a machine for making tough sausage tender. Cured sausage arrives already tender, so the long cook has nothing left to do and the whole thing collapses into one boil. The tradeoff is fat: a hard salami stick renders less into the sauce than raw links, hence the tablespoon of oil.

Tomato powder replaces the 24 oz jar and cuts roughly a pound and a half of water out of your pack. It thickens fast, so add it off the heat and off the pasta water you kept — that starch is what gives the sauce body. The sugar is not a garnish. Jarred sauce almost always has some, and without it the reconstituted tomato reads sharp.
