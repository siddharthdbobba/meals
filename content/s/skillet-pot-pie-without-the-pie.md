The original is a baked pie, which is a nonstarter without an oven. What survives the translation is the part that actually matters: a roux-thickened gravy carrying meat and vegetables, with a biscuit lid steamed on top instead of baked. A lidded pan on a low flame is an oven for anything under an inch thick.

The fresh stuff all has a dry stand-in. Onion, celery, and mushrooms go in as flakes, milk goes in as powder, stock goes in as bouillon. The flour and butter are non-negotiable — they are what turns hot water into gravy, and without the roux you get soup with biscuits floating in it. Keep the flame low once the lid is on. The gravy scorches long before the biscuits are done.
