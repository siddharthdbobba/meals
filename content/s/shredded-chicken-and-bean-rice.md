Pouch chicken comes packed as one wet slab, and a slab does not distribute through rice. Breaking it into threads first is the whole difference between a bite of meat and a bite of everything. Your spoon against the pot wall does the job in seconds.

Bean flakes are the structure. They absorb the leftover water the rice does not take, so you end up with a thick pot instead of a soupy one, and they add protein for almost no weight. The oil goes in last, off heat, because the fat is what carries the seasoning and gets it onto every grain.

Adapted for the trail: no fridge, no fresh meat, no draining. This is a one-boil version of a home dish that normally starts with a whole roasted bird.
