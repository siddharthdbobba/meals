A real frittata finishes in a 375°F oven. Over a fire you do not have one, so the lid does that job instead: trapped steam sets the top while the bottom cooks on the coals. Low heat is the whole trick. Too much flame and the underside is leather before the middle moves.

Powdered egg rehydrates thinner than shell eggs, so the milk powder and the parmesan are structural, not garnish. They give the batter enough solids to set into a slice you can lift. The goat cheese goes on at the end because it does not want heat, and it is the only thing here that needs a cooler.
