Serviceberries do something most fruit leather fruit does not: the seeds set the sheet. They are small and soft, closer to sesame than raspberry, and they hold the puree together as it dries. Straining them out gives you a thinner, floppier leather that tears in the pack. Leave them.

The maple is not only sweetener. Sugar pulls water out and keeps the finished strips stable at ambient temperature for months, which is the reason this travels at all. The syrup's own water also loosens the puree enough to spread thin and evenly, and thickness is what decides whether it dries in six hours or stays gummy in the middle. Under-dry slightly rather than over-dry; a brittle leather shatters into crumbs at the bottom of a food bag.
