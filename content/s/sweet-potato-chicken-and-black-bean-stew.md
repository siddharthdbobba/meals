The original is a fresh-ingredient pot on a home stove. What survives the trip to a pack is the seasoning logic: smoked paprika plus sweet potato does most of the work, so there is no long spice list to carry. Bouillon replaces stock, tomato powder replaces the can, and dried diced sweet potato replaces the two pounds of fresh root you are not hauling.

Thickening comes from the starch, not from flour. Bean flakes dissolve on contact and the sweet potato breaks down when you press it, which is why the mashing step matters. Skip it and you get seasoned water with cubes floating in it. The oil goes in at the end because fat helps the beta-carotene actually land, and because a stew this lean eats thin without it.
