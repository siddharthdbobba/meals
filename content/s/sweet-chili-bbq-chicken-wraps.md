The original is a pan of oven-toasted slider rolls, which needs a baking dish and an oven. On the trail the same three parts still work: sweet-hot sauced chicken, something crunchy, something to hold it. Pouch chicken replaces leftover shredded breast, dried minced onion replaces the raw Spanish onion, and tortillas replace the rolls because they survive a pack and a hatch.

The simmer is the part that matters. Ten minutes of low heat drives off most of the added water and thickens the barbecue and chili sauces enough to stay in a wrap instead of running out the end. The slaw goes on dry and dressed at the last second — bagged cabbage keeps a few days without a cooler, but it goes limp the moment it hits hot sauce.
