The original wants roasted sweet potatoes, four eggs, buttermilk, and a cream cheese frosting whipped with butter. None of that survives a night without a fridge. Dehydrated sweet potato flakes rehydrate to about the right texture and skip the hour of roasting; egg powder and buttermilk powder do their jobs at a fraction of the weight. The cream cheese frosting becomes a sugar glaze, which is the part of frosting that actually travels.

The raisins matter more than they look. Soaking them in liquor for a few hours puts moisture back into the crumb as the cake bakes, which covers for a dutch oven that runs hotter than an oven ever would. Coals on the lid, not under it, is the whole technique: heat from below burns the bottom long before the middle sets.
