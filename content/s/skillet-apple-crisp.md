Fresh apples are heavy and bruise in a pack, so this uses dried slices and puts the water back in at camp. That step matters: dried apples baked straight into a crisp pull moisture out of the topping and leave you with leathery fruit under wet crumble. Boil them first, drain, and the ratio comes out right.

The brown sugar and butter smeared on the pot bottom before the filling goes in is doing two jobs. It keeps the fruit from welding itself to cast iron, and it caramelizes into a thin syrup at the base. Coals go mostly on the lid, since a crisp browns from the top and the bottom only needs enough heat to bubble.
