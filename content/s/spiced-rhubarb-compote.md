Fresh rhubarb is 95 percent water and bruises in a pack, so this version starts dried. The 15-minute soak before the heat goes on matters: dry rhubarb thrown straight into hot syrup stays leathery, because sugar pulls water out faster than the fruit can take it in.

The vinegar is not a garnish. Rhubarb loses acid when you cook it this long with this much sugar, and without the vinegar you end up with sweet mush. Sugar at this ratio is also what makes the leftovers safe in a warm pack for a couple of days — it is a preserve, not a fruit sauce.
