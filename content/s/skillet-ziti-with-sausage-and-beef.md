Pasta cooked in the sauce instead of in a separate pot of water is the whole point in camp. You skip the second boil, you skip the colander, and you skip dumping a gallon of starchy water somewhere it shouldn't go. The starch stays in the pan and thickens the sauce, so no roux or extra paste is needed at the end.

The ratio matters more than the timing: four cups of water to a pound of dry ziti. Too much and it stays soupy, too little and the bottom scorches before the pasta softens. Cook it over coals rather than flame and stir often. This one is a cooler recipe by design, so make it the first night out.
