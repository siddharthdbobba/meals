The original is an oven casserole built on frozen ravioli, which is two things a camp kitchen does not have. Dried tortellini solves both: it cooks in the sauce the same way the frozen kind does, just on a burner instead of in a pan at 350. The water ratio matters more than the timing. Too little and the pasta scorches to the bottom before it softens; too much and you get soup with cheese on it.

The cheese goes on at the end, off the heat, because mozzarella in a boiling pot turns into rubber threads. Low-moisture block cheese holds up for a few days out of a cooler, which is why this is a car-camping or first-night-at-basecamp dinner rather than a week-deep one. Freeze-dried beef is the honest swap for a pound of ground chuck; if you are cooking from a cooler on day one, brown a half pound of real beef first and skip the soak.
