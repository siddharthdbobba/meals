A shepherd's pie is normally an oven dish: filling below, mashed potatoes above, browned under heat. The skillet version skips the oven by building both layers in the same pan and letting a lid plus a few coals do the browning. The order matters more than the technique. Brown the beef first so the fond is there for the vegetables, and get the filling thick before the potatoes go on, or the topping sinks.

The camp changes are in the topping and the liquid. Instant potato flakes with powdered milk mash up stiffer than boiled russets and take five minutes instead of twenty, which also means no draining a pot of hot water into the dirt. Bouillon cubes replace a carton of broth at a fraction of the weight, and freeze-dried peas rehydrate in the simmering filling without needing a freezer. The beef and cheese still want a cooler; the variations note covers you if you do not have one.
