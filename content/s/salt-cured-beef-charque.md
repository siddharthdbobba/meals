Charque is the Brazilian answer to a problem every long trip has: meat is the heaviest useful food you can carry, and it spoils. Salt fixes both. Burying lean beef in its own weight of salt drives out most of the water in two days, and sun or moving air takes the rest. What is left is roughly a third of the original weight and keeps for weeks without a cooler.

The camp step people skip is the first boil. Charque comes out of the cure aggressively salty, and if you cook it straight into your dinner the whole pot is inedible. Simmer it once, throw that water out, and start over. The meat is also fully preserved, not cooked, so the simmer is doing double duty as the actual cooking. Use lean cuts only — fat does not cure, it goes rancid.
