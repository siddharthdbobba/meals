Popover batter is mostly water and it climbs by steam alone, which is why the ratios matter more than the technique. Eggs and milk are the problem on a trip, so both come as powder here and get reconstituted with cold water in camp. Powdered egg behaves the same way in a thin batter; it just needs a harder whisk to lose its lumps.

The browning step is not optional. Raw skin sitting in wet batter never renders, and the fond left in the pan is what keeps the underside of the popover from tasting like plain flour. Coal placement does the rest: heat mostly on the lid, so the batter sets and rises from the top down while the chicken finishes underneath. This one needs a cooler and real coals, so it stays a car camping dinner.
