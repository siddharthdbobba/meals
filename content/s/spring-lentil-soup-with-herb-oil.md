The original is a spring produce dish, which means it is mostly water and refrigeration. What survives the swap is the structure: whole lentils for body, quick-cooking green vegetables added at the very end so they do not turn to paste, and a raw herb sauce spooned in off the heat. Freeze-dried peas and spinach rehydrate in the residual heat alone, so they stay separate from the lentils instead of dissolving into them.

The pistou becomes a herb oil. No food processor, no fresh basil, no cheese to go rancid — dried herbs bloom in olive oil while the lentils simmer, and the acid goes in at the end where heat cannot flatten it. That oil is doing two jobs: it is most of the fat in the meal, and it is the only reason a pot of lentils and bouillon tastes like anything.
