Split peas are one of the few legumes worth carrying whole. They cook without soaking, they break down into their own thickener, and half a cup dry turns into a full pot. The cost is fuel: thirty-plus minutes of simmer is real gas. Soaking them in cold water while you hike cuts that roughly in half and costs nothing but planning.

The home version leans on fresh onion, celery, and lemon. Dried onion and celery flakes cover the aromatics well enough once they rehydrate in the broth. The acid matters more than it looks like it should — peas and potato go flat and heavy without it, and the dill has nothing to push against. Add both at the end, off the heat, or the flavors cook out and the acid stalls the peas.
