The source for this was a solar oven pot: a black-walled, ceramic-lined 2.5 L pot with a heavy lid, meant to sit in the sun for hours. That works at a basecamp with clear sky and nothing else to do. It does not work at 6 p.m. under trees, so this version moves the same slow, lidded, low-water cook onto a stove or fire and cuts it to 45 minutes by using parboiled rice and bean flakes instead of dried beans.

The lid is the part that matters either way. Steam trapped in the pot does most of the cooking, which is why 700 ml is enough water for two servings that would normally take a liter and a half. Bean flakes go in off the heat because they thicken on contact and will weld to a hot pot bottom. The oil is not optional; without it this is a bowl of seasoned starch.
