This is a car camp sauce, not a pack sauce. It needs a real fire, a pot, and twenty-five minutes of attention, and the reward is that the smoke goes into the fruit rather than being poured out of a bottle. Blackberries at the low end of the grill pick up wood smoke the way meat does, and the bourbon in the pan carries it deeper because alcohol pulls the smoke compounds in and then boils off later in the pot.

The rest is a standard sweet and sour build. Ketchup and brown sugar give it body and the sweetness that reads as barbecue, vinegar and mustard keep it from going cloying, and the honey holds it together once it reduces. Everything except the butter and the berries is shelf stable and travels in squeeze bottles. Watch the pot at the end — sugar this concentrated scorches on a hot spot in under a minute, and burnt sauce cannot be fixed.
