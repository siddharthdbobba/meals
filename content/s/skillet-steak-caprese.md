This started as a cast iron kitchen recipe and it survives the trip out mostly intact, because a skillet over coals is what cast iron was built for. The one real change is the cheese. Fresh mozzarella lives in water, weeps into everything, and turns to slime after a day in a cooler. A low-moisture block holds its shape, melts on residual heat, and does not flood the pan.

Order matters more than technique. The steaks go first because they need a dry, screaming-hot surface; the tomatoes go second because they want the fond left behind. Resting the steak off-heat while the tomatoes blister is not a courtesy, it is the only way both come out right in one pan.

Coals, not flame. A live fire scorches the outside before the middle moves, and cast iron holds enough heat that you want it steady, not climbing.
