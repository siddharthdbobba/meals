Camp gravy fails in one of two places: the roux or the milk. Adding flour a spoonful at a time until the rendered fat is fully absorbed is what keeps it from going grainy, and reconstituted powdered whole milk behaves close enough to the real thing that no cooler space gets spent on a carton. Mix the powder into water in a bottle first. Dumping dry powder into hot fat gives you lumps you cannot whisk out on a camp stove.

Biscuits go first because they need the oven hot and the gravy does not wait. A self-rising mix that takes only water skips the butter-cutting a home biscuit needs, and drop biscuits skip the rolling. The sausage is the one thing here that has to stay cold, which caps this at the first morning or two out of the cooler.
