The original is a kitchen soup: fresh sausage, refrigerated tortellini, heavy cream, two cans of tomato. All four have shelf-stable stand-ins. Dried salami browns and renders like raw sausage does, tomato powder rebuilds both the tomatoes and the sauce from a third of a cup, and milk powder plus cornstarch does what cream does without a cooler. Dried boxed tortellini takes about ten minutes in boiling broth, which is the whole reason this fits one pot.

Kale goes in off the heat, at the end. It is sturdy enough to rehydrate in a covered pot without turning to slime, and it holds its shape in leftovers if you carry a second serving. The cornstarch matters more than it looks: without it the milk powder sits in a watery broth instead of coating the pasta.
