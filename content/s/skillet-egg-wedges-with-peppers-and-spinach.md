The original is an oven dish built on fresh eggs, cream, and ricotta. None of that travels, so the whole thing moves onto dry goods: whole egg powder for the eggs, milk powder for the cream, and a hard aged cheese instead of ricotta. Ricotta is the one real loss. It is mostly water and it cannot be replaced, so the cheese goes on at the end where it melts into pockets rather than disappearing into the custard.

Coals matter more than the recipe does. Eggs over open flame set on the bottom and stay raw in the middle, so build the fire down first and cook covered, rotating the pan. Rehydrating the spinach separately and wringing it out keeps that water out of the eggs, which is the difference between a set frittata and a weeping one.
