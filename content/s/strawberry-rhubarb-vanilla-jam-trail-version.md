Real jam is a long boil that drives water out of fresh fruit until sugar and pectin set it. On trail you cannot afford either the fuel or the fresh fruit, so this runs the process backward: start from dried fruit and add back only the water you need. Ninety milliliters is enough to soften everything without leaving syrup you have to boil away.

The sugar is not only for sweetness. At roughly half the dried fruit weight it pulls moisture and keeps the mix from tasting flat, and the lemon powder does the acid job the fresh rhubarb would have handled. Cornstarch stands in for pectin, which needs a longer, hotter cook than a canister stove is worth. Add it as a slurry or you get lumps.
