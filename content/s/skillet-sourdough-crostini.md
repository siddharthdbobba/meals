The original bakes these in an oven, which nobody has in camp. A pan does the same job because the bread is thin and mostly dry already, so heat only needs to drive off the last of the moisture and brown the starch. Toasting the first side dry matters: fat added too early fries the crumb soft instead of crisping it.

Butter is out. It leaks, it goes rancid in a warm pack, and it burns fast in a thin camp pan. Ghee has the milk solids removed, so it sits at ambient temperature for a week and takes more heat before it smokes. Olive oil works too if you already carry it. Stale bread is preferred here, not tolerated, which makes this the day-three use for a loaf that has stopped being good on its own.
