Fresh heirloom tomatoes are mostly water, which is why the original calls for salting and draining them. On a trip you skip that problem entirely: sun-dried tomatoes in oil are already concentrated, already shelf-stable, and they will not turn the crust to paste. The oil they sit in replaces the fresh olive oil, so nothing is wasted.

The rest of the substitutions are about the cooler you do not have. Powdered shortening and powdered egg make the dough and the wash without refrigeration, and hard grated parmesan keeps for weeks in a bag. Heat control is the real skill here — coals under the skillet cook the bottom, coals on the lid brown the crust, and without the second half you get a pale, floppy fold.
