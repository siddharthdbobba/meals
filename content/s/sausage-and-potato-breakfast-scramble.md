The original is a baked casserole: raw eggs poured over sautéed potatoes and sausage, then an hour in the oven or a night in a slow cooker. Neither is available on a stove that runs one burner off a canister. So this is the same set of ingredients handled as a scramble, cooked in the order each thing actually needs.

Potatoes take the longest and eggs take the shortest, which is why the potatoes rehydrate first and the eggs go in last, off a low flame. Reconstituting the egg powder in cold water in a separate bottle is the step people skip, and it is the one that decides whether you get eggs or lumps. Freeze-dried cheddar goes in after the heat is off so it melts instead of turning to oil in the pot.
