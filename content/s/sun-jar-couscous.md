Couscous is already cooked and dried, so it needs moisture and time rather than heat. A dark bag strapped to your pack supplies the rest. Nothing here gets hot enough to be called cooking, but the difference between a jar in the sun and a jar in the shade is real: warmer water pulls the vegetable flakes back to soft instead of leaving them leathery.

The crushed chickpeas are the protein and most of the texture. They go in dry and stay a little firm, which keeps the whole jar from reading as one soft mass. The oil goes in at the end because fat coating the grain early slows down how much water it takes on.
