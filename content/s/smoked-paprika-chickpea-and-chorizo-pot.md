The original wanted a cooler, fresh peppers, and an onion to chop. None of that is necessary. Dry-cured Spanish chorizo is already shelf-stable, and dehydrated onion and bell pepper rehydrate fine in a tomato base that simmers for twenty minutes anyway. That turns a cooler dinner into a bin dinner.

The order matters more than the ingredients. Browning the chorizo first pulls the paprika-heavy fat out of the sausage and into the pot, and blooming the dried vegetables in that fat is what makes them taste like vegetables instead of like flakes. Chickpeas go in last because they only need to warm through; simmer them from the start and they turn to paste.
