  - "Salt the roast on all sides and let it sit while the fire settles. Do not cut it up yet."
  - "Melt the fat in the dutch oven over the coals and sear the whole roast hard on every face. Whole meat browns; cubed meat steams. Pull it out and set it on a plate."
  - "Drop the onion into the fat and cook until it goes soft and picks up the brown off the bottom. Add the garlic, cumin, paprika, and cocoa and stir for a minute."

This is a fire-and-time recipe, not a stove recipe. It exists because a basecamp afternoon is one of the few places where three hours of low heat is free. Venison neck is the cut worth hauling out for it — connective tissue that turns to body in the pot, which lean leg meat will never do.

Two details matter. Sear the roast whole and cut it after, because meat in pieces gives off water and steams instead of browning. And the beans go in soaked, from home, or they will still be gravel when the fire dies. The cocoa is not for sweetness; it darkens the chilis and rounds off the tomato.

The cheese and crema ride in the cooler, so this is a car or boat trip meal. Nothing here scales down to a pack.
