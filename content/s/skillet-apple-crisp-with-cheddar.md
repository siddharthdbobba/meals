Pie dough on a fire grate is a losing bet. The crust needs steady, even heat from below and above, and coals give you neither. A crumble made from the same flour, butter, and cheddar solves that: it sits on top, it browns from radiant heat, and nothing has to hold a shape. Same flavors, none of the two-hour chill.

The cheddar is not a novelty. Cheese and apple share the same sharp-sweet axis, and the salt in it keeps the topping from reading as candy. Cold butter is the one thing you cannot cheat here. If it softens into the flour you get paste, not crumble, so keep it in the cooler until the pan is loaded and the coals are ready. The vinegar goes in the filling instead of the dough and does the same job it always did, cutting the sugar.
