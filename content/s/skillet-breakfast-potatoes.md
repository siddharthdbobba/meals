The oven version relies on dry heat and forty minutes of it. On a stove you have one small pot and a flame that only heats the middle, so the browning has to come from oil and patience instead. Fresh potatoes are also two pounds of water you carried up a hill, which is why this uses the dehydrated kind and the fresh onion and pepper became dried flakes.

The drain step is the one that decides how this turns out. Rehydrated potatoes hold far more water than they look like they do, and anything left in the pot boils off against the oil instead of crisping. Press them dry, then resist stirring. Contact time with hot metal is the only thing making a crust.
