The original is a broiler recipe, and camp does not have a broiler. A dry cast iron skillet gets you the same thing from the other direction: direct contact heat on the cut faces instead of radiant heat from above. That means the sprouts go in cut side down and stay put, and the pan has to be hot before anything touches it. A crowded or lukewarm pan steams them instead, and steamed sprouts are the reason people say they hate sprouts.

The cheese goes in off the heat. Parmesan on a hot pan sticks and burns before it coats anything, so kill the flame first and let residual heat do the melting. The nuts go in last for the same reason: toasted once at home is enough, and a second round in the pan turns them bitter. Sprouts and grated parmesan are cooler food, not backpacking food, so this is a first- or second-night car camping dinner.
