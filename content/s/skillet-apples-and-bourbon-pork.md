Cast iron and an open fire want something that browns, and shelf-stable pork loin browns fine as long as you dry it off and leave it alone in the pan. The original version of this leans on fresh apples, which bruise in a pack and weigh four times what dried rings do. Soaking the rings first gives you both the fruit and the liquid you need, so no separate stock is required.

The glaze is the point. Sugar, bourbon, and bouillon reduce down onto the meat instead of sitting under it as sauce, which matters when you are eating out of the skillet. Watch it near the end — once the liquid goes, the sugar burns fast.
