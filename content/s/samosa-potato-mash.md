A samosa is mostly a delivery vehicle for spiced potato, and the shell is the part you cannot make on a stove with one pot. So this is the filling, eaten straight. Instant flakes replace the baked russet, freeze-dried peas replace cooked ones, and dried onion rehydrates in the same boil.

The mustard seeds are worth the extra thirty seconds. Popped in hot oil they go nutty; stirred in dry they taste like gravel. Butter and yogurt are gone because neither travels, and the oil goes in at the start instead, where it also carries the spice. Salt harder than feels right — potato flakes absorb it and go flat otherwise.
