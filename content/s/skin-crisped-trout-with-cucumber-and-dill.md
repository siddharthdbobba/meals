The whole recipe lives in one decision: cook the fish almost entirely from the skin side and never flip it. Heat moves up through the fillet slowly, so the flesh stays wet while the skin dries out and crisps. Every time you lift or shove the fish, you break the contact and the skin steams instead. Medium heat, not high. High heat scorches the skin long before the thick end of the fillet is cooked.

The butter goes in last, once the fish is nearly done, because it browns fast and burns faster. Spooning the hot foam over the top finishes the last raw layer without turning the fillet over. The cucumber salad is there to cut the fat, which means the salting-and-squeezing step matters more than it looks; skip it and the water in the cucumber thins the skyr into something that runs off the plate.
