The original of this is a four-part restaurant project: a 24-hour brine, a second marinade, a fourteen-ingredient chopped salad, and a yogurt sauce. In camp, most of that is water weight and knife work you do not want. The brine goes first — it exists to insure against overcooked breast, and cutting the meat into cubes does the same job for free. The salad shrinks to five things that survive a cooler, with cabbage in place of iceberg because iceberg turns to slush by day two.

The yogurt sauce is the part worth protecting. Yogurt powder plus cold water rebuilds a thick, sour sauce with no dairy in the cooler, and freeze-dried cucumber gives back the crunch that fresh tzatziki has. Go light on the garlic. Camp garlic reads stronger than kitchen garlic, and there is nowhere to brush your teeth afterward.
