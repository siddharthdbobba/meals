The original was a refrigerator recipe: fresh eggs, milk, half-and-half, yogurt, and a four-hour cold soak. None of that travels. Powdered eggs and whole milk powder rebuild the custard from a dry bag, and freeze-dried strawberries deliver the fruit without the water weight or the mush. The soak drops from four hours to twenty minutes because stale bread absorbs faster than fresh, and because nobody is starting breakfast at 4 a.m.

Coals, not flames, are the whole game. A custard set over direct fire scorches on the bottom and stays raw in the middle. Bank the coals underneath, pile more on the lid, and the skillet bakes instead of fries. The rest time at the end is not optional either — pull it early and you are serving sweet soup.
