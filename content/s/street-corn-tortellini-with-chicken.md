Slow-cooker casseroles do not travel, but this one comes apart cleanly into things that do. Chicken, corn, cream cheese, and yogurt all have dry equivalents, and the tortellini already exists in a shelf-stable form. The one real change is method: instead of building a sauce separately and folding pasta into it, everything cooks in the same pot and the pasta water does the thickening.

Hold onto that water. Cheese powder and milk powder need starch and heat to turn into sauce rather than grit, and draining the pot leaves you with dry pasta and a lump of powder. The mayo goes in last and off the heat — it is the fat that pulls the whole thing together, and it splits if you boil it.
