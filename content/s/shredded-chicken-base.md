This starts as a slow-cooker meal-prep chicken and ends as trail protein. The slow cooker is doing the tenderizing, which matters more than it looks: meat pulled apart into loose strands rehydrates in minutes, where a diced cube stays leathery in the middle no matter how long you wait.

Fat is the whole problem with carrying meat. It does not dry, and it turns on you within days at pack temperature. So the skin comes off, the fat gets blotted, and the cooking liquid gets chilled so you can lift the cap before dehydrating what is left. What you carry is nearly pure protein — about 42 grams a portion at two ounces, which no other trail food matches by weight. Bring your own calories in the form of oil and starch.
