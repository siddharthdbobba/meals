Real arancini are deep-fried risotto balls, which is not a thing you do at camp. What actually makes them work is the binder: starchy grain, cheese, and egg holding a shape long enough to get a crust. All three of those exist in shelf-stable form, so the dish survives the trip intact.

The changes are the mozzarella, the fresh spinach, and the shape. Fresh mozzarella needs a fridge and a molten center needs an oven, so the parmesan does all the cheese work and the patties go flat to cook through in a pan. Let the barley cool before the egg goes in, or you get scrambled bits instead of glue.
