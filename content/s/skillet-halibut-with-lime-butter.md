Fish is unforgiving on a fire because the window between raw and dry is about ninety seconds wide. Two things fix that. Cut the fillets to a half inch so heat gets to the center before the outside overcooks, and cook over coals rather than flame so the pan holds a steady temperature you can actually judge.

The butter is browned first, not added at the end. That gives you a fat already hot enough to sear the surface on contact, which keeps the fillet from sticking to cast iron and welding itself down. Lime goes in off-heat at the last second: acid on a hot pan cooks off, and the point of it is the raw brightness against the fat. This one stays in the cooler category on purpose. There is no shelf-stable version of fresh halibut worth eating.
