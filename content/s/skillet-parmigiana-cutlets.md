This started as a kitchen recipe with fresh parmesan and a carton of milk, neither of which survives a weekend outdoors. Powdered whole milk mixed thin does the same job as a dredging liquid, and the shaker parmesan is already dry, which is what you want in a breading anyway. The tomato sauce comes from a pouch or a paste tube rather than a jar.

The double dip is the part worth doing carefully. One pass of milk and flour slides off in the pan; two builds a crust thick enough to stay put over uneven fire heat. Pounding the meat thin matters too, since a quarter-inch cutlet cooks through in the same two minutes the crust needs to brown, and lean wild game turns to leather if you leave it in longer chasing color.
