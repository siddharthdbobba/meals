A dutch baby is flour, egg, and milk baked until it sets around whatever is already in the pan. That structure survives the trip to camp better than it has any right to. Powdered egg and powdered milk rehydrate into a batter that behaves close enough to the fresh version, and the pan does the rest.

The two things that go wrong are heat and water. Coals underneath and coals on the lid give you top and bottom heat, which is what makes the batter set instead of steaming into scrambled paste. And rehydrated vegetables carry a lot of water, so cook them down in the sausage fat before the batter goes in. Skip that and the middle never firms up.
