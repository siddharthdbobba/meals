The source bakes this and eats it hot over rice, which is a fridge-and-oven meal. The fix is to keep drying past the point where it is dinner and stop when it is jerky. Pressing matters more than the marinade: water left in the slab means hours of extra drying time and a piece that never sets up firm.

Sugar and salt are doing preservation work here, not just flavor. Honey holds a little moisture and keeps the pieces from turning to gravel, and the soy sauce keeps them shelf-stable at the water content you can reach in a home dehydrator. This is one of the few plant sources that gives you real protein per ounce without oil or nuts carrying the weight.
