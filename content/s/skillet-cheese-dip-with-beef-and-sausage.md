Two pounds of cheese and two pounds of meat is a lot of skillet, which is the point: this feeds a full site and holds up while people wander over. Velveeta is doing the structural work. It emulsifies instead of breaking, so the dip stays pourable over uneven campfire heat where a pan of pure jack would split into oil and curds. The jack is there for flavor the processed cheese does not have.

Brown the meat at home if you can. It is the messy, greasy, fat-disposal part of the job, and doing it in a kitchen means camp is fifteen minutes of stirring instead of forty of scrubbing. Everything here needs a cooler, so this is a car-camping recipe and not a pack-it-in one. Coals, not flames — the bottom of a cast iron pan full of cheese scorches faster than you expect.
