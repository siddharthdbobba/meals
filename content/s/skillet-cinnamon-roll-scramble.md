The original is a yeasted overnight dough baked in a pan, which is three things the trail does not have: a proof, an oven, and refrigeration. What survives the trip is the part that matters — dough, butter, brown sugar, cinnamon, and a cream cheese glaze on top. Chemically leavened baking mix replaces the yeast dough, and tearing it into pieces means every piece cooks through in a skillet instead of relying on oven heat reaching the middle of a coil.

The order of operations is the whole recipe. Sugar goes in after the dough is cooked and the pan is off the heat, because brown sugar in a hot dry pan scorches long before dough is done. The glaze is powdered sugar, cream cheese powder, and milk powder mixed cold in a mug, so nothing perishable rides along and there is one pan to clean.
