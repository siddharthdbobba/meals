The original wants cold butter, cold buttermilk, and two cups of fresh milk. None of that survives a weekend in a pack, so the fat becomes powdered shortening and the milk becomes powder plus cold water. Powdered whole milk has enough fat to make a gravy that coats a spoon, which skim powder will not.

Biscuits in a skillet are a heat-control problem, not a baking problem. A lid traps enough steam to cook the middle while the bottom crisps, and low heat is the only way both finish at once. Cook the biscuits first and build the gravy in the same fat-slicked pan: one pot, and the browned bits go into the roux.
