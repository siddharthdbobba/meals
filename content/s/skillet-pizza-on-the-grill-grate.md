Cast iron solves the problem that makes campfire pizza fail: a grate puts open flame directly under thin dough, and it burns before the cheese does anything. The skillet spreads that heat into an even floor, and the preheat gives the crust its bottom in the first two minutes instead of the last two.

Covering the pan is the other half. Radiant heat from below cooks the base fine, but the top needs trapped hot air. A lid or foil tent turns the skillet into a small oven. Alpine cheddar is used here over mozzarella for a practical reason: it is a firm aged cheese, so it survives a cooler without weeping, and it melts without releasing the water that makes a soft crust.
