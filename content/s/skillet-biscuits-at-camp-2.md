The original is an oven recipe with chilled lard and fresh buttermilk. Neither survives a cooler-free trip, so the buttermilk becomes powder mixed into the dry blend and the lard becomes any shelf-stable solid fat. Lard is not sentimentality here: it stays solid longer than butter in warm hands and leaves the crust crisp rather than soft. Butter is mostly water, and that water steams the outside soft.

The dough is supposed to feel wrong. It should be crumbly and barely held together, and the biscuits get torn and shaped by hand rather than cut, which is where the ragged crust comes from. Coals go on the lid as well as underneath, because a dutch oven heated only from below burns the bottoms before the tops set.
