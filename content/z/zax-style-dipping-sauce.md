This is a diner-counter sauce adapted for a pack. The original leans on a jar of refrigerated mayonnaise and a two-hour rest in the fridge, neither of which exists on trail. Single-serve mayo packets solve both problems: they are shelf-stable until you tear them, they portion themselves, and the sugar and acid in the ketchup keep the mixed sauce stable for the length of a meal.

The pepper is the whole point. It goes in twice rather than all at once because coarse pepper floats and clumps if you dump it, and mixing in two passes gets it distributed. The rest matters more than it looks like it should: garlic powder is dry starch until it takes on moisture, and a sauce eaten straight after mixing tastes dusty. An hour in a warm pocket does what the fridge overnight was doing.

No protein worth counting here. It is a condiment for tortillas, foil-packet chicken, or fried potatoes at a car camp.
