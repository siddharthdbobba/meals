This is a Zaanstreek soup stripped down to what a stove can do. At home it starts with butter and finishes with cream. Both are dead weight on trail, so the fat comes from oil instead and the body comes from the roux alone. It still thickens, because thickening was always the flour's job.

The order matters more than the ingredients. Cook the flour before the water goes in or the soup tastes like paste. Add the mustard after the heat is off — boiling it flattens the sharpness that the whole recipe exists for. Around 75 g of dry ingredients for a hot bowl is a fair trade in cold weather.
