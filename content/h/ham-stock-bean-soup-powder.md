The trick here is the stock. A ham hock is too fatty to dehydrate safely, but the liquid it gives up is not — simmer the hock with vegetables and wine, chill the stock overnight, lift the solid fat off the top, and what remains is gelatin and flavor that dries into a clean powder. The meat stays home and gets eaten in sandwiches. You carry the taste of it at a fraction of the weight.

Beans instead of split peas is a protein decision, not a flavor one. Dried cooked beans rehydrate in minutes and land near 20 g per serving, where peas leave you short. Powdered, the same mix becomes a lunch you drink standing up; left chunky, it is dinner. Carry olive oil separately and stir it in at the end — the fat you took out at home has to go back in somewhere, and it is the difference between broth and a meal.
