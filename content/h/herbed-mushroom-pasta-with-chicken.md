Dried mushrooms are the reason this works. They go into the water before the pasta does, so by the time the noodles are done the mushrooms have rehydrated in what became a mushroom broth, and the pasta picked that flavor up on the way. Angel hair earns its place too: three or four minutes of boil time is a lot less fuel than penne.

The cheese, garlic, and herbs go in after the heat is off. Herbes de Provence is mostly leaves and flowers, and boiling them flattens the whole thing out. Draining is the one fussy step. Leave a little water behind so the parmesan and oil have something to cling to, or you get dry noodles with powder on them.
