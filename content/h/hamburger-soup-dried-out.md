The original is a stove-top pot of ground beef, broth, and fresh vegetables. On the trail the beef and the vegetables are the same recipe, only dried, and the timing is what changes. Potato dices are the slowest thing in the bag, so everything else is sized to survive fifteen minutes without turning to mush. Carrot and green bean hold up. Celery mostly disappears and is there for the broth, not for texture.

The fat matters more than it seems. Draining the beef is right at home and wrong here, because a soup this wet needs calorie density from somewhere. Drying the meat lean and adding oil at the end gets you both: a bag that stores for months and a dinner that is actually worth carrying. Tomato powder does the work of the can without the weight of the liquid, and the paprika covers for the fire roasting you gave up.
