  - "At home: brown the meat, drain every bit of fat off, then simmer it with the beans, tomatoes, vegetables, and spices until thick. Cool it."
  - "Spread the chili thin on lined dehydrator trays. Dry at 135°F for 8 to 10 hours, until nothing is tacky and the beans rattle."
  - "Break it up and bag one meal per quart bag with the instant rice in its own bag."
  - "In camp: bring 600 ml of water to a boil. Stir the dried chili in, cover, and pull it off the heat."

Commercial freeze-dried dinners are mostly air and money. A home dehydrator turns a pot of real chili into flakes that weigh a fifth of what they did and keep for a year in a sealed bag. The limiting factor is fat: it does not dry, and it will go rancid in the bag. So the meat gets drained hard at home and the calories come back as oil poured on at camp.

Run the whole thing once in your kitchen before the trip, using only the pot, stove, and water you plan to carry. That is where you find out that the beans need twelve minutes instead of eight, or that you needed a second container to soak the chili while the water boiled. Rehydration time depends on how thin you spread the trays, and nobody else's number will match yours.
