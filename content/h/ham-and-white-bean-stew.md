The original is a slow pot: soaked beans, a ham hock, and hours of simmering to pull collagen out of the bone. None of that fits a fuel canister. The workaround is moving the long cook home — beans get cooked and dehydrated in the kitchen, so at camp they only need to rehydrate. The hock's job, salt and pork fat and body, gets split between bouillon paste, diced ham, and a slug of olive oil.

The herbs matter more than they look. Sage and thyme are what make this read as ham and beans instead of generic bean soup, and dried versions of both hold their character fine. Add them at the start so they have time in the water. The ten-minute off-heat rest is not optional: bean flakes swell slowly, and stew judged before the rest is always thinner than what you end up with.
