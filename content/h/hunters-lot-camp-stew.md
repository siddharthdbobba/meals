This is a clearing-out stew. Whatever odd packages are in the bottom of the freezer — a rabbit, a couple squirrels, a pound of venison tidbits — all cook to the same place given an hour of low heat. Mixing meats does not hurt anything; the flour, the browning, and the thyme give the whole pot one flavor.

Two steps decide whether it works. The flour dredge and hard sear build a fond in the bottom of the pot that water alone will never produce, and the simmer has to stay a simmer. Boiling seizes lean game and no amount of extra time will walk that back. The camp adaptations are the vegetables and the thickener: dried onion and carrot rehydrate in the broth, and instant potato flakes stand in for a roux at the end, so nothing needs a fridge or a second pan.
