Round steak is a cheap, lean cut that turns to leather over fast heat. The two-hour simmer is not optional — it is the whole point. Cutting it thin across the grain before the trip means it browns quickly and breaks down evenly once the gravy goes in.

Stirring during the brown matters more than it sounds. A dutch oven holds heat unevenly and the strips will weld to the bottom in the first four minutes if left alone. After that the gravy protects them and you can leave it mostly alone. The water chestnuts go in late because they only add crunch, and boiling them for two hours removes the reason they are there. Everything except the steak is shelf-stable; the steak needs a cooler and should be cooked the first night out.
