This is less a recipe than a use for the heavy thing in your pack. A vacuum flask costs you eight or ten ounces of dead weight, and it only earns that back if you fill it at the one moment the stove is already running. Breakfast is that moment. You boil once, drink half, and carry the other half hot to a ridge where you would not want to set up a stove.

The preheat step matters more than the recipe. Cold steel pulls heat out of the first cup and you never get it back. Straining also matters: tea left sitting on its leaves in a sealed flask is undrinkable by noon. Sugar and salt are there for the walking, not the flavor.
