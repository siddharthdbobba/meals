The original is a roux soup: butter, flour, broth, then milk and cream at the end. None of that travels. Potato flakes do the same job as flour without needing fat and heat to cook out the raw taste, and powdered whole milk covers both the milk and the cream. Russets in the original break down to thicken the pot, which is exactly what dehydrated potatoes do anyway.

The order matters more than the ingredients. Milk powder added to boiling water separates and turns chalky, so it goes in off the heat. The flakes go in last because once they hydrate you cannot judge how much more water the potatoes still want.
