This is a braise that turns into a glaze. The cup of water keeps the sugar from burning for the first half hour while the onions release their own liquid, and then the whole thing reduces down to something thick enough to cling. Stirring every fifteen minutes is not optional. Sugar on a bare cast iron bottom over coals will scorch, and once it does the whole pot tastes like it.

The sausage is already cooked, so the hour is about the sauce, not the meat. That means it holds well and reheats fine over the same coals the next morning. It stays in the car-camp column because four pounds of sausage needs a cooler. Nothing else here does — the sugar, mustard, garlic, and cayenne premix is shelf-stable and can sit in the food box all season.
