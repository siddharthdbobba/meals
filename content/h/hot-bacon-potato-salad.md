The original is a skillet dish built on boiled red potatoes and rendered bacon fat, neither of which travels. Dehydrated potato slices rehydrate in the same water they cook in, and shelf-stable bacon crumbles carry the smoke and salt without a cooler. The fat you lose gets replaced with a spoon of oil, which the dressing needs to cling.

The vinegar-and-sugar ratio is the whole point. It is sharper than most people expect, and it should be: dried potatoes are bland and thirsty, and they soak up a dressing that would be too aggressive on fresh ones. Add the vinegar at the end, off a hard boil, so it does not just steam away.
