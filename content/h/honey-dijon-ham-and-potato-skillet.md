The original is a bone-in ham under a slow cooker lid for eight hours. None of that survives a pack, so what carries over is the glaze: sugar, honey, Dijon, and apple. Those four are the reason that dish tastes like anything, and all four exist in shelf-stable form that weighs almost nothing.

Potato flakes replace the roast as the bulk. Mixing the glaze separately in a mug matters more than it sounds. Stirred straight into the pot it goes flat and uniform; poured over at the end you get sweet and sharp in the same bite, which is the point of a mustard glaze. Cured ham is already salty, so no salt goes in.
