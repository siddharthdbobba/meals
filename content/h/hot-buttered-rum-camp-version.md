The paste is the whole recipe. Butter, sugar, and ground spice creamed together at home dissolve into hot water in seconds; the same ingredients added loose in camp leave you with gritty sugar on the bottom and a slick of plain butter on top. Make it once, carry it in a jar, and each mug is a spoonful and a pour.

The fat is not decoration. It floats and holds heat in the mug longer than water alone, and it gives the drink enough weight to count for something after a cold day. Add the water in two stages so the first splash can do the work of melting the paste before the mug is full and hard to stir. Butter travels fine in winter but not in a hot car, so this one stays a cold-weather recipe.
