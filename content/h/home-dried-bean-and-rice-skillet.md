Dried beans do not belong on a stove above 8,000 feet. They take an hour at sea level and longer up high, and no amount of soaking fixes the fuel math. The workaround is to do the cooking at home: drain canned beans, spread them on trays, and dry them until they snap rather than bend. Ground to a coarse powder in a blender, they rehydrate in the time instant rice needs anyway.

The powder is also what gives this body. Whole dried beans rehydrate unevenly and leave you chewing gravel; powdered beans dissolve into the water and thicken the pot into something closer to a stew. The sausage and oil carry the calories, and the spices do the rest, since bean powder on its own tastes like not much.
