The original is a stovetop soup built on a can of tomatoes, four cups of broth, and raw potatoes. None of that travels. The fix is to move the slow, wet work home: brown and dehydrate the beef, use tomato powder instead of a can, and cut the vegetables small enough that they rehydrate in the same twelve minutes the potatoes need. Bouillon replaces the broth so you carry seasoning instead of water.

Rinsing the fat off the beef before drying it is the step people skip, and it is the one that decides whether the bag is edible in week two. Fat left on dried meat goes rancid fast. You add the calories back at camp with the oil, which also gives the soup the body it lost when the beef stopped being greasy.
