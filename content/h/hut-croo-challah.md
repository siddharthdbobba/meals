Hut kitchens bake bread because it turns cheap flour into the thing everyone remembers about dinner. A car camp can do the same, and challah is a good candidate: the oil and egg make a dough that stays soft for two days instead of going to rock overnight.

The trail changes are all about the fridge. Powdered whole egg rehydrates into something close enough to shell eggs for an enriched dough, and it keeps in a pack all summer. The yeast is cut well below the hut quantity, since a small loaf rising slowly in cool air gets better structure than one blown up fast. The braid is not decoration. Three strands bake more evenly in a dutch oven than one thick log, which otherwise stays raw in the middle.
