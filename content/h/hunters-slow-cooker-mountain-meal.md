The original is a slow cooker recipe: cubed game meat browned in oil, two cans of condensed soup, a ninety-minute simmer. None of that travels. Canned soup is water and steel, raw rice wants twenty minutes of real fuel, and fresh onion and celery rot. So the cans become soup powder, the raw rice becomes instant, and the meat gets dehydrated at home after it is cooked. What survives is the thing that made the dish work: a thick, salty cream base holding starch and meat together.

The two soup powders are doing separate jobs. Chicken carries the salt, mushroom carries the body. Use one and it reads thin. Rehydrate the meat and vegetables first, before the rice goes in, or the starch thickens the pot while the venison is still leather. Twenty minutes of stove time is a lot for backcountry cooking, but most of it is off-heat soaking under a lid.
