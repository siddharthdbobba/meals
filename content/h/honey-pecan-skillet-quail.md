This is a cooler-and-cast-iron dinner, not a pack meal. Quail are small and lean, which means they go from raw to dry in about a minute of inattention. The two-stage method is the whole point: hard sear in butter for color, then covered low heat off the direct flame to finish them through without driving the moisture out.

The honey goes on last for the same reason. Sugar over a hot fire turns black fast, so it hits the pan only after the birds are cooked and the skillet has been pulled to the cooler edge. The pecans are there for texture against soft meat, and they toast a little in the residual butter.
