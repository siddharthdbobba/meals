The long first cook is not about flavor, it is about collagen. Chuck is a working muscle and it stays tough until the connective tissue breaks down into gelatin, which takes hours of wet heat and cannot be rushed with a hotter fire. Water, not sauce, does that work. Sugar and tomato in the pot for three hours would scorch against the cast iron long before the meat gave up.

So the sauce goes in second, after the shredding, and the hour that follows is just reduction. The cooking liquid is already loaded with beef and vegetables, and boiling it down with the ketchup concentrates both. Do the chopping and the sauce mixing at home. In camp this becomes two bags dumped into a pot, which matters when you are managing coals at the same time.
