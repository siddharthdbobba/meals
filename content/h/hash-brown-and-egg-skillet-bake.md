The original is a casserole built on frozen potato patties, a carton of milk, and nine cracked eggs, none of which survive a night in a pack. Dehydrated shreds, whole egg powder, and powdered milk do the same job at a quarter of the weight and no refrigeration. The mustard powder is worth keeping: it sharpens egg powder, which otherwise tastes flat and faintly sulfurous.

Water discipline is the whole trick. Rehydrate the potatoes first and drain them hard, because leftover water in the shreds ends up in the custard and the center never sets. Low heat and a lid matter for the same reason a casserole gets covered in the oven: eggs set from the outside in, and hurrying the bottom just gives you a scorched layer under raw middle.
