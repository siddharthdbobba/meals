Crackers solve the bread problem. Real bread crushes, molds, and stales in two days; a flat, low-moisture cracker has almost nothing left in it for mold to work with, so it rides in a pack for a week without complaint. The whole recipe is home prep — nothing happens at camp except opening the bag.

Thinness and dryness are the two things to get right. A wet dough steams rather than bakes and comes out leathery, so hold back water and roll harder than feels reasonable. The olive oil is what makes them crisp instead of hard, and it is also the ingredient with a clock on it: fat goes rancid long before flour does, which is why old batches get a smell test.
