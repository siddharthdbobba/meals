steps:
  - "Whisk the buttermilk powder into 2 cups of cold water. Let it sit while you build the fire."
  - "Get a bed of coals going and set a deep cast iron pot on the grate with two inches of oil. You want 325-350F. A pinch of flour should sizzle, not scorch."
  - "Salt and pepper the rabbit. Roll each piece in flour, drop it in the buttermilk, then back into the second bag of flour."
  - "Set the coated pieces on a plate and leave them ten minutes. The crust needs that time to grip or it slides off in the oil."

Nashville hot is a technique, not a bird. The heat lives in fat, not in a batter, so it goes on after frying and works on anything with a crust. Rabbit is leaner than chicken, which means the fat baste is doing more than flavor work here.

Two changes for camp. Buttermilk becomes powder and water, since a jug of it will not survive a cooler for long. And the ten-minute rest after the second dredge is not optional over a fire, where your oil temperature swings more than a stove ever will.
