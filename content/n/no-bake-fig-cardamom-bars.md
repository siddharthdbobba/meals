Most homemade granola bar recipes come out of the pan as granola. The fix is mechanical, not culinary: enough liquid sugar and fat to coat every dry piece, and real pressure when you press the pan. Honey sets firm as it cools and coconut oil goes solid below about 76F, so the two together act as the binder. Skipping the pressing step is what produces crumbs.

Nothing here needs a fridge or a stove once it leaves the kitchen, which is the point. The chia and the pressed shape are doing the same job, holding the slab together so it survives a week of being sat on. In heat above the coconut oil's melting point the bars soften, so wrap them and keep them out of direct sun.
