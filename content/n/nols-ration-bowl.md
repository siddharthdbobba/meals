NOLS teaches a formula, not a recipe: a carb, a protein, a fat, and something to make it taste like food. This is that formula run through one pot. Oats are the carb because they cook in two minutes and cost nothing; the potato flakes are the second carb, and they turn a loose porridge into something you can eat with a spoon in wind.

Savory oatmeal reads wrong until you try it. The bouillon and dried alliums go in before the oats so they have time to bloom in the water, and the fat goes in last, off the heat, where it stays fat instead of cooking off. Skip the oil and you lose about a fifth of the calories in the bowl. The trail change from home cooking is the dairy: powdered milk instead of a splash from the fridge, and drained packet meat instead of anything that needs cold.
