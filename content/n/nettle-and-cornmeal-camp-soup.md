The original is a Spanish soup built on fresh nettles, a ham bone, and butter. None of that travels. What survives the swap is the actual structure: salty cured pork against a green that tastes like dirt and iron, held together with cornmeal instead of a puree.

Cornmeal is the part worth understanding. Two tablespoons turn 600 ml of broth into something you eat with a spoon rather than drink, and it does it without dairy, which is why this version does not miss the butter. Add it slowly. Dumped in at once it clumps, and the lumps never break up in a camp pot.

The ham is doing double duty as protein and as the entire salt-and-fat backbone. Cured Serrano or prosciutto sits fine in a pack for a few days in cool weather. In heat, treat it as a day-one meal.
