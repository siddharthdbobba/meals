The restaurant version is fried chicken tossed in a reduced sauce. Neither half survives a trail kitchen: deep frying costs a liter of oil, and fresh juice and zest are heavy and perishable. What actually matters is the sauce, and a sauce is just sugar, acid, salt, aromatics, and a thickener. Drink mix supplies the orange and the acid, dried peel puts the bitter oil back, and cornstarch does the same job it does at home.

The slurry step is the only place this can fail. Cornstarch dumped dry into hot liquid seizes into pellets that never dissolve, so mix it cold in a separate container first. Rehydrating the chicken before the rice goes in matters too — freeze-dried meat needs several minutes of standing time, and rice cooked alongside it will be gluey by then.
