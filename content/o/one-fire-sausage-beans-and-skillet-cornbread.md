steps:
  - "Burn seasoned hardwood down until you have a deep bed of coals. A flaming fire will scorch the sausage and leave the beans cold."
  - "Rake a shallow layer of coals to one side and set the grate over it. Lay the sausage on the grate whole, turning every few minutes until it is blistered and hot through, about 12 minutes. Pull it off and cut into coins."
  - "Set a pot directly on the coal bed. Dump in both cans of beans with their liquid, the tomato paste, brown sugar, mustard powder, onion flakes, and half the salt. Stir and let it bubble down for 15 minutes, moving it off the hottest coals if it starts to catch."

This is basecamp cooking, not stove cooking. One fire pit does three jobs at once if you manage the coals instead of the flames: the grate takes direct radiant heat, the pot sits down in the coal bed where the heat is steady, and the cornbread bakes off reflected heat at the edge. Nothing here needs to happen at the same time, which is the point. Stagger the three and one person can run all of it.

Everything on the list keeps without a cooler. Vacuum-packed cured sausage and canned beans are the shelf-stable stand-ins for fresh meat and a long soak, and the cornbread uses water and oil instead of milk and butter so there is nothing to spoil in the bin. The bean liquid is doing real work — drain it and you lose the starch that thickens the pot.
