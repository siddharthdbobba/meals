This is a car camp dinner, not a pack meal. It needs a cooler, a cast iron skillet, and about forty minutes of attention, and it repays all three. The order matters more than the technique: sear the venison first while the pan is at its hottest, then let the potatoes cook in the fond and butter the meat left behind.

Venison is lean enough that it goes from rare to leather in about ninety seconds of inattention. That is why it comes out of the pan early and goes back in only at the end, on top of the potatoes rather than against the iron. The wine is doing two jobs — it lifts the browned bits off the pan and it steams the potatoes under the lid so you are not fighting scorch.
