The powdered milk and butter transform into a thin sauce that carries the noodles and chicken. It's not real cream, but it stops this from tasting like noodles in salty water. Cheese powder adds enough depth to make it taste intentional. The freeze-dried vegetables soften in the broth and add small flecks of color.

Watch the pot—it will boil over if you look away. Eight minutes of simmering cooks the pasta through if your stove runs normal; adjust down a minute or two for a hot flame. The five-minute rest is crucial. That's when the powders finish absorbing liquid and thicken the whole thing from a thin soup into something that actually coats your spoon.
