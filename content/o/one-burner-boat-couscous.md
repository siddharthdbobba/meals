Couscous is the only grain that cooks with the stove already off. That matters when the burner is a single alcohol unit on a gimbal and the boat is moving — you want one boil, a lid, and no minutes spent hovering over an open flame. Steeping off-heat also means nothing scorches to the bottom of the pot, which is the difference between rinsing it and scrubbing it.

The fat and salt do the flavor work. Dry couscous alone is bland and drinks up whatever you give it, so the oil goes in at the end where it coats rather than boils off, and the bouillon seasons the water before the grain swells. Lemon powder replaces the fresh lemon this dish normally wants; it is sharper and less bright, but it keeps without a fridge.
