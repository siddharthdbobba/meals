French onion soup is an hour and a half of standing over a pan, which is not a thing anyone does at a campsite. So the long part happens at home: cook five to seven sliced onions down slowly in oil and butter until they are dark and jammy, deglaze with a splash of brandy, then spread the jam thin on trays and dry it. A cup of dried jam weighs almost nothing and rebuilds into the same soup with boiling water.

The flour and oil at the start are what give the broth body without cream. Everything else is shelf-stable by design: bouillon instead of stock, tube tomato paste, hard cheese with a rind. The toast goes on top of the pot rather than under a broiler, and a lid for two minutes does the same job.
