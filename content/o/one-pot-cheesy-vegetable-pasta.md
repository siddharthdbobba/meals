The original bakes under a broiler, which is not happening on a stove that has one setting. The fix is to leave the water in. Cooking the pasta in just enough water and never draining it means the starch stays in the pot, and tomato powder stirred into that starchy liquid comes out closer to a sauce than to soup. No colander, no second dish, no drained-off calories.

Dried mushrooms and vegetable flakes rehydrate in the same boil as the pasta, which is why they go in at the start rather than at the end like the fresh ones would. Mozzarella off the shelf melts poorly once it has been dried, so grated hard cheese does the actual melting and the parmesan handles salt and smell. Two minutes with the lid on and the stove off does more for the cheese than any amount of stirring over heat.
