The original is a van-kitchen stew with fresh onion, celery, tomatoes, and spinach, and eight cups of broth. That is four pounds of produce and a lot of water to carry. Dried vegetables and bouillon do the same job at a fraction of the weight, and cutting the liquid to 900 ml gives you a stew instead of a soup you have to drink.

Cauliflower gnocchi is the part worth keeping. It thickens the pot and gives it something to chew on without the starch load of pasta. Buy the vacuum-packed shelf-stable kind, not frozen, or it will be a puddle by dinner. The tomato paste and sun-dried tomatoes replace fresh tomatoes and carry more flavor per ounce than canned ever did.
