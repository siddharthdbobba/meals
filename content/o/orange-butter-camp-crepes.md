The original is a tableside flambé with fresh eggs, whole milk, and liqueur set alight. None of that travels. Powdered egg and powdered milk rebuild the batter with no cooler and no cracked shells in a pack, and the resting time matters more than it does at home because the powders need a few minutes to hydrate before the batter will spread thin.

Crepes fail on pan temperature, not on batter. Cook them over coals rather than open flame and give the cast iron a full five minutes to even out, otherwise the center sets before the edges do and the flip tears. The orange butter is doing the work of the flambé: sugar, fat, and dried peel melting into a hot crepe gets most of the way there without the alcohol.
