Venison ground for jerky has essentially no fat in it, which is why this leans on soup mix, breadcrumbs, and water instead of relying on rendered grease to keep things moist. The breadcrumbs and eggs hold that added liquid in place during the cook. Without them you get a dry brick.

The camp change is the pan. A foil packet on a grate does the same job as a loaf pan in a 350°F oven, so long as you keep the loaf thin and the packet off direct flame. Shaping it wide also cuts the cook time down from an hour to something a fire will hold steady through. Venison is a cooler item, not a shelf-stable one, so this is a first-night dinner.
