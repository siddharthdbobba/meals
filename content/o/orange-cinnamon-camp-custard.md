The original is baked custard: yolks, milk, cream, an hour in a low oven. None of that survives a pack. Cornstarch does the same job as the yolks, thickening a hot liquid into something that holds a spoon, and it does it in three minutes over a burner instead of an hour in an oven you don't have. Whole milk powder covers both the milk and the cream, since the fat is what keeps it from tasting like paste.

The cinnamon stick steeping in the water first is the one step worth the wait. Ground cinnamon dumped in with everything else reads as dusty; steeped, it comes through as background warmth. Dried orange peel carries the citrus that the fresh zest gave the original. The sugar on top is honest about what it is — texture, not a caramel crust.
