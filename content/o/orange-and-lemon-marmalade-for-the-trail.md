This is home preserving turned into trail food. The whole fruit goes in, peel and pith and all, which is the point: the peel carries the bitterness that keeps two pounds of sugar from tasting like nothing, and the seeds and pith carry the pectin that sets it. Skip the seed bundle and you get syrup.

Sugar at roughly one-to-one with fruit is what makes it shelf-stable. That is a preservation ratio, not a sweetness preference, so cutting it costs you keeping time. Cooked at home and packed in squeeze tubes, it needs no stove, no water, and no cold in camp, which is why it earns its two ounces on a morning when everything else is dry.
