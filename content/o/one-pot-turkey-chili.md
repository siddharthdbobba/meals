This is straight camp cooking, not backpacking food. Cans are heavy and the meat needs a cooler, so it belongs on night one at a drive-in site or in a boat. What it buys you is a real dinner with one pot and no cutting board.

The simmer is not optional. Twenty minutes is what moves the chili powder and oregano out of the spice pile and into the tomato, and it thickens the diced-tomato juice enough that the whole thing stops being soup. Over a fire, cook it off to the side rather than in the flames — direct heat scorches the tomato sauce on the bottom of the pot long before the top is done. No cheese or sour cream here, which is why the dairy-free tag stands; add them at the site if you brought a cooler that runs cold.
