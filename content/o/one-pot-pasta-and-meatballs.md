The original is a pressure cooker dump meal, which is the wrong tool at 9,000 feet. The method survives the trip anyway: cook the pasta in exactly the water it needs, then build the sauce in that starchy leftover water instead of draining it. No colander, no second pot, no wasted fuel.

Tomato powder is what makes this packable. A can of crushed tomatoes plus a can of sauce is about three pounds of water you would be carrying up a hill. A third of a cup of powder does the same job and rehydrates in the pot. The sugar and the vinegar are not optional garnish — powdered tomato runs flat and sharp at the same time, and those two pull it back into something that tastes cooked.
