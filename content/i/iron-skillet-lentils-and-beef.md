This one is built around iron, not flavor theory. Lentils, jerky, and dried greens are three of the densest shelf-stable sources you can carry, and they happen to cook in the same pot on the same timer. The jerky rehydrates into something closer to stew meat than it has any right to.

The acid at the end is the part people skip. Plant iron absorbs poorly on its own, and a sour hit at the finish helps it along — which is why the lemon powder goes in off heat, where a long boil will not flatten it. Tomato powder is doing double duty here as body and as more acid. Cook the lentils in plain water first; acid early makes them stubborn.
