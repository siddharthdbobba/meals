A real lamb stew leans on a long braise and a puree of its own vegetables for body. Neither of those survives a 20-minute window on a canister stove, so this version splits the difference: the meat and vegetables are cooked and dried at home, where the braising already happened, and the thickening comes from instant potato flakes instead of blending half the pot.

The stout is the other compromise. Carrying a bottle is absurd, and beer boiled down loses the bitterness that made it worth adding. Vinegar powder gives back the sharp edge that keeps the sweet potato from turning the whole pot into dessert. Soak the vegetables in cold water before lighting the stove and you cut real fuel off the simmer.
