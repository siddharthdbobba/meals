The original is a one-hour braise: tough shoulder, hard root vegetables, and enough time for both to give in. Neither part of that is available on a stove burning fuel you carried. So the braise happens at home. Cook the lamb until it is tender, dry it, and camp becomes a rehydration problem instead of a cooking one.

Dried potato and cabbage do most of the work of thickening, so no flour or roux is needed and the recipe stays gluten-free without trying. The oil matters more than it looks. Lamb shoulder carries a lot of fat, and drying strips most of it out, which costs both calories and the mouthfeel that made this a stew and not a soup.
