This is a car-camping recipe and it stays one. The chicken has to ride cold, and there is no dehydrated version of a cup of ranch dressing that behaves the same way. What it buys you is a dinner for six that needs no chopping, no measuring at camp, and one pot.

The four liquids each do a different job. Both dressings are oil and acid, so they tenderize and keep the meat from drying out over two hours of dry heat. The cola contributes sugar that reduces into a glaze and a little acid alongside it. The barbecue sauce is the only thing here with real spice and smoke, so it carries the flavor. Coals on the lid matter more than coals underneath — heat from the bottom alone will scorch the sugars long before the thighs are done.
