Bannock is bread stripped down to what a fire can actually do. There is no yeast and no proofing, so nothing waits on temperature or time. Baking powder does the lift the moment water hits it, which is why the dough goes to the coals immediately rather than sitting while you find your gloves.

The fat matters more than it looks. It coats the flour and keeps the gluten short, so the result is a tender crumb instead of a hot brick. Powdered milk replaces the fresh milk a home recipe would use and adds a little sugar for browning. Angled heat from a coal bed, rather than direct flame, is what gets the middle done before the outside turns to charcoal.
