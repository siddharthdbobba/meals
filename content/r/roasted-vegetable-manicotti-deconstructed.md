The original is an oven casserole: shells stuffed with ricotta and roasted vegetables, baked under sauce for over an hour. None of that survives a trail kitchen. So the parts get separated and rebuilt in a pot. The roasted vegetables become dried vegetables, which rehydrate into something closer to roasted than steamed because the drying already drove the water out. The ricotta and egg binder is gone entirely; milk powder and parmesan do the same job of turning thin sauce into something that clings.

Order matters more than technique here. Vegetables go in first because dried mushroom takes longer to soften than pasta takes to cook. Dairy goes in last, off the heat, because milk powder scorches on a stove that only has one setting. Tomato powder is the item worth buying ahead; jarred sauce is mostly water and you are not carrying water.
