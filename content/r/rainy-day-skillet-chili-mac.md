Cooking the pasta directly in the tomato liquid is what makes this a one-skillet meal. The starch that would normally go down the drain stays in the pan and thickens the sauce, so you never need a strainer, a second pot, or a place to dump hot water in the rain.

Watch the liquid rather than the clock. Camp stoves run hot and uneven, and the difference between chili mac and a scorched skillet is a splash of water added thirty seconds earlier. Add it in small amounts; too much at once and you are back to soup with no way to reduce it before dinner gets cold.
