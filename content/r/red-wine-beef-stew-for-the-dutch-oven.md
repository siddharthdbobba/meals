The original of this is a three-hour stew built on raw chuck, which needs the time to break down. Freeze-dried beef is already broken down, so the long braise is gone and what is left is the part that actually builds flavor: browning the floured meat, deglazing with wine, and giving the dried vegetables enough simmering time to rehydrate through instead of staying chalky in the middle.

The flour does two jobs and is worth splitting deliberately. The dredge browns and sticks to the pot, which is where the dark flavor comes from. The reserved half goes in cold at the end as a slurry, because dumping dry flour into hot broth gives you lumps. Wine is the one heavy item here; a cup weighs half a pound, so this is a basecamp dinner and not something to carry uphill.
