Real tomato soup gets its depth from roasting, which you cannot do on the trail. Sun-dried tomatoes are the shortcut: they are already concentrated and caramelized, and fifteen minutes in simmering broth brings them most of the way back. The canned tomatoes do the other half of the job, keeping the pot bright and acidic instead of flat and jammy. The whole head of garlic in the coals is the one step worth the wait, since roasting turns it sweet and mild enough to eat by the spoonful.

There is no blender out here, so the texture comes from mashing against the pot wall. It stays rustic. Croutons crisped in butter are not a garnish here, they are the calories and the reason the bowl counts as dinner. Salt at the end, because bouillon is salt with a tomato-colored opinion attached.
