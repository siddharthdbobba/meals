Red lentils are the only legume that fits a stove schedule. They cook in about twenty minutes from dry with no soak, and they fall apart into the broth instead of staying separate, so the soup thickens itself. That is why this one works on trail and a black bean version would not.

The original is a home kitchen soup with fresh onion, canned tomatoes, and a squeezed lemon. Dried onion and tomato powder do the same job at a fraction of the weight, and citric acid replaces the lemon. Acid and oil go in off the heat on purpose: cooking the acid in from the start toughens the lentil skins, and the oil carries flavor better when it is not boiled. Dried herbs are weaker than fresh, so use more than feels right.
