The original is a casserole: frozen ravioli, jarred sauce, browned beef, a fistful of mozzarella, forty minutes in an oven. None of that travels. Dry ravioli replaces the frozen kind and cooks straight in its own sauce water, powdered tomato replaces the jar, and freeze-dried beef replaces the pound of ground chuck. One pot, no draining, no oven.

The order matters more than it looks. Sauce powder added before the pasta is done will scorch on the pot bottom and thicken too early, leaving the ravioli crunchy inside. Cook the pasta in plain water, then build the sauce off heat with what is already in the pot. The oil is there because freeze-dried beef carries almost no fat, and a tomato sauce with no fat in it tastes flat.
