Ricotta gnocchi is potato gnocchi without the potatoes, the boiling, or the ricing, which is what makes it worth doing away from a kitchen. The whole dish is cheese, egg, and flour, and two of those three ship as powder. Rehydrated ricotta powder is drier than the tub kind, which is the direction you want anyway — wet ricotta is the usual reason gnocchi dissolve in the pot.

The original recipe is a bulk freezer batch with four eggs and a 32 oz tub. This is cut down to two dinners so you are not rolling dough by headlamp for an hour. Add flour slowly and stop early. Every extra spoonful buys insurance against a pot of grey mush and costs you texture, and past a point you are eating boiled paste.
