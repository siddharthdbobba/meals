Rain in camp means you want one pot, a lid, and twenty minutes of standing over a stove instead of an hour. Cooking the pasta in the water it will finish in does most of the work here: the starch that would go down the drain stays behind and thickens the whole thing into a sauce, so there is nothing to strain and nothing to wash but the one pot.

The tomato paste is not optional. Canned chili beans on their own are thin and taste like the can; two tablespoons of paste give it enough body and acid to read as a real meal. Oil goes in off the heat so it stays as fat rather than frying, and the cheese melts on residual heat under the lid.
