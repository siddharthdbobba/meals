The original is a Norwegian Constitution Day dessert: stewed rhubarb and prunes under an egg-yolk vanilla cream, set with gelatin and finished with whipped cream. None of that survives a pack. Six yolks and a pint of cream are a refrigeration problem, and gelatin needs hours of cold to set. So the custard here is thickened with cornstarch and built on whole milk powder, which does the same job on a stove in three minutes and carries dry.

What matters is keeping the two halves distinct. Rhubarb is sour and prunes are sweet, and the compote is the sharp part; the custard is the flat, fatty part that makes it a dessert instead of stewed fruit. Cook them separately even though it means an extra scrape of the pot. The starches also need different handling: potato starch sets the fruit off the heat and breaks if it boils, cornstarch needs heat to do anything at all.
