This is a car-camping bread, not a trail bread. It needs two hours, a heavy pot, and a coal bed you tend. What it does not need is a fridge or a mixer: the dough is wet enough to stir with a spoon, and no kneading means no floured surface to clean up.

Roasting the garlic first is the whole point. Raw cloves would stay sharp and would fight the yeast; roasted, they collapse into soft sweet lumps that stay whole in the crumb. Premeasuring the dry mix at home turns camp work into pouring one bag, which matters when you are measuring by headlamp. Watch the lid coals more than the bottom ones. Bread scorches from below long before it browns on top.
