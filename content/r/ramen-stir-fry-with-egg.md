The original is a skillet dish: veggies in one pan, noodles in a pot, eggs scrambled in a cleared corner. None of that survives a stove that boils one pot. So the vegetables and noodles cook in the same water in sequence, and the egg goes in as powder after the heat is off, where it sets into the noodles instead of scrambling separately.

The important move is draining. Ramen with the water left in is soup, and this sauce is too concentrated to be soup. Pour off most of the liquid and the soy, hoisin, and sugar reduce onto the noodles in the residual heat. Fresh garlic, ginger, and scallion become dried, which costs some sharpness; the vinegar and chili crisp put the edge back.

Note on tags: hoisin usually contains wheat and some brands contain no animal products but many do, so only dairy-free and nut-free are claimed here. Check your chili crisp for peanuts if that matters.
