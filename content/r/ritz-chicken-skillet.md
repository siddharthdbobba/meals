The original is a slow cooker dish: raw chicken breasts under a sour cream and condensed soup blanket, buttered Ritz on top, four hours on low. None of that survives a trailhead. Refrigerated sour cream, a can of soup, and a four-hour cook are all non-starters, so the whole thing is rebuilt as a fifteen-minute pot dish. Freeze-dried chicken replaces the breasts, powdered sour cream and dry soup mix replace the wet dairy, and butter powder carries the fat.

The crackers are doing two jobs, which is why they go in twice. Half get stirred in and dissolve, which is what actually thickens the gravy into something that clings instead of running. The other half go on last and stay crunchy for about as long as it takes to eat. Ranch seasoning is most of the flavor here, so measure it rather than eyeballing it.
