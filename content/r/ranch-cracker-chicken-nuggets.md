The original is an oven recipe: raw chicken, bottled ranch dressing, baked twenty minutes. None of that travels. Freeze-dried chicken replaces the raw breast and removes the food-safety problem of hauling meat that has to hit 165 degrees. Powdered ranch plus powdered milk replaces the bottled dressing and does the same job, which is glue — it is only there to hold crumbs to meat.

The rehydration water is worth keeping. It carries chicken flavor and it hydrates the ranch powder into a paste that actually sticks; plain water makes a thin slurry that slides off. Frying replaces baking because a pot over a low flame is what you have. Keep the flame low or the cracker crumbs scorch before the outside sets.
