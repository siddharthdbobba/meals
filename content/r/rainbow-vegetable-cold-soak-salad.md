The original is a bowl of raw vegetables, which is a fridge dish and a heavy one. Dried, the same vegetables weigh a few ounces and rehydrate on their own while you hike. Cabbage and carrot are the ones that make this work: they hold structure after soaking instead of collapsing, so you still get something to chew. Cucumber and tomato go soft either way, so they act as flavor rather than crunch.

The dressing goes on last, not with the soak water. Salt and acid slow rehydration and pull water back out of the vegetables, so a jar dressed at the start ends up soupy and still hard in the middle. Draining before dressing also means the oil coats the vegetables instead of floating on a puddle.
