The original is a Dutch oven recipe with fresh roots, a can of beer, and a bottle of vegetable juice. None of that travels. Dried root vegetables carry the same body at a fraction of the weight, tomato powder replaces both the canned tomatoes and the juice, and the beer goes away entirely. What survives is the spice profile, which is where the recipe was actually doing its work: chile, cumin, coriander, smoked paprika, and enough cinnamon to round the edges without announcing itself.

Order matters more than usual here. Carrots and parsnips need real simmer time, and dumping the beans in early gives you a thick pot that scorches before the roots soften. Spices go in late for the same reason: twenty minutes of boiling flattens paprika. The rest period at the end is not optional — the source says to make it a day ahead, and ten minutes under a lid is the closest you get on a trail.
