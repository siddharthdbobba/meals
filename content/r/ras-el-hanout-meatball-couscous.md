The original is meatballs browned in a skillet and simmered in sauce, which needs an egg, breadcrumbs, milk, and a pan you do not want to wash. What actually carries the dish is the spice, the sweet tomato base, and the raisins, so this keeps those and drops the meatball. Freeze-dried ground meat rehydrated in a spiced tomato broth lands in the same place with one pot and one boil.

Raisins matter more than they look. Ras el hanout is heavy on cinnamon and ginger, and without something sweet the whole pot reads as dusty. The acid at the end does the job lime juice did: it lifts everything after a long day when your palate is flat. Add it off the heat or it cooks away.
