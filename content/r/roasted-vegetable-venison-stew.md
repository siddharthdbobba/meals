The original is a pressure-cooker stew with a tray of vegetables roasted separately, so they go in near the end with some texture left. You cannot roast anything on a canister stove, so the trail version fakes it: dried vegetables that never fully collapse, plus a thirty-second toast of onion and garlic in oil if you want the browned edge back.

The venison has to be cooked before it is dried. Raw dried meat will not soften in fifteen minutes at any altitude, and a hard simmer is the one thing that turns this into shoe leather. Keep it barely bubbling. The thickener goes in off the heat because flour dumped into boiling liquid seizes into lumps you will be chewing around all night.
