The original of this is a supermarket rotisserie chicken stretched across three weeknight dinners. None of that survives contact with a trail: no fridge, no oven, no carrying two pounds of bone. What does survive is the shape of the meal, which is shredded cooked chicken folded into a starch that has already absorbed a seasoned broth. Foil-pouch chicken does that job at about a third of the weight, and it is already cooked, so nothing here is a food-safety question.

The pouch liquid goes into the pot rather than onto the ground. It is salted chicken broth and it is most of the flavor you paid for. The potato flakes at the end are the difference between a dinner and a bowl of wet rice; they bind the loose broth and hold the oil in suspension instead of letting it slick the top.
