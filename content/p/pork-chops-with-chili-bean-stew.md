This started as a Norwegian home dinner: pork neck chops seared in a pan, beans and vegetables stewed alongside. The bones of it travel well because the stew is canned beans, which need no fridge and bring their own sauce. Fresh onion, pepper, and celery become dried ones — rehydrate them in hot water first or they stay crunchy in the beans and never catch up.

The notches in the fat rim matter more than they sound. Pork shoulder has a thick band of fat along one edge that shrinks faster than the meat, so an unnotched chop domes up and only touches the pan in the middle. Cut it and the chop stays flat. Brown hard, then move it off the direct heat; over a campfire that means the edge of the grate, where it finishes without burning.
