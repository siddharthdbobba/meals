The original is a wok dish: seared ground pork, soft-boiled eggs, fresh scallions, sauce built in a bowl. None of that travels. Freeze-dried pork replaces the ground meat and rehydrates in the same time the noodles take, the eggs are gone entirely, and the scallions become flakes stirred in at the end. What survives is the sauce, which is the only part that mattered.

Powdered peanut butter and soy sauce powder keep the weight down and dissolve into hot noodles without a whisk. Add them off the heat — peanut sauce scorches on the bottom of a titanium pot faster than you expect. The chili paste carries the garlic and the heat, so it is worth the small container. Chopped peanuts go on last for texture, not flavor; stirred in early they just go soft.
