The original is a two-hour roast and a stock pot of chicken wings and pork hock reduced by half. None of that fits in a pack, but the reason it works does: five-spice, star anise, ginger, and lemongrass build a broth that tastes long-cooked in far less time. Whole aromatics are what carry the load here. Powdered five-spice alone goes flat and slightly dusty; one real star anise pod simmering in the pot is the difference.

Bouillon and soy powder stand in for the reduced stock, and dried pork replaces the roast. Order matters more than usual. The aromatics need water and time, the meat needs a head start on the noodles, and the noodles need the least of all. Add everything at once and you get soft pork in gluey noodles with no broth worth drinking. The oil goes in at the end because fat carries the spice and boiling it just scatters it.
