The coffee shop version depends on a syrup, ice, and a fridge. None of that travels. What does travel is the cold soak itself, which is just what cold brew already is: grounds sitting in cold water for hours. A wide-mouth bottle in your pack does the same job as the jug on the counter.

The syrup gets rebuilt as dry brown sugar plus pumpkin pie spice, and the milk becomes milk powder. Powder dissolves better in cold liquid if you shake it rather than stir, and the pinch of salt cuts the sourness cold brew picks up from a long soak. Straining matters more than it sounds like it should. Grounds left in the bottle keep extracting all morning and turn bitter.
