Pollen is a fat-friendly ingredient, and shortbread is mostly fat, so the two get along. A quarter cup of pine or cattail pollen gives the crumb a dense, faintly waxy chew you cannot get from flour alone. Honey is the only sweetener here, which keeps the dough soft and slows it drying out in a pack.

The overnight chill does the real work. It firms the butter for clean slicing and lets the flour hydrate, which is the difference between a cookie and a pile of crumbs at the bottom of your food bag. Nothing happens at camp except opening the bag, so this counts as no-cook once you are walking. Butter and egg are baked through, so the cookies hold at ambient temperature for about a week.
