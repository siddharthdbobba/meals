The original wanted fresh potatoes and two cups of hot milk, neither of which travels. Dehydrated potato dices rehydrate in the same water you were going to boil anyway, and whole milk powder mixed into cool water gives you the fat and body that skim or shelf milk cannot. The cans stay because cream-style corn is doing structural work here, not just adding sweetness.

The flour slurry is the part people skip and then wonder why the pot is corn floating in cloudy water. Mix it with the milk powder in cold water first. Dumped dry into a hot pot it seizes into lumps you will be chasing with a spoon for the rest of dinner. If you are counting ounces, potato flakes replace the dices and cut ten minutes of simmering, at the cost of a smoother texture.
