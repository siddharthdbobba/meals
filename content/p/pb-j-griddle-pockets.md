The original was a pan of refrigerated biscuits layered with peanut butter and jam and baked in an oven. Neither of those travels. Dry biscuit mix and water make the same dough at camp, and sealed pockets in a covered skillet do what the oven did, as long as the heat stays low.

Low heat is the whole trick. Biscuit dough browns fast and cooks through slowly, so a hot pan gives you a dark crust over raw flour. Keep the flame down, keep the lid on, and move the pan around. Sealing the edges properly matters too: an open seam dumps hot jam into the pan and burns it.
