This is a soak, not a simmer. Instant rice is already cooked and dried, so hot water and a covered fifteen minutes do all the work while the stove sits cold. Cutting the heat early is what keeps the pot bottom clean and the fuel use to a single short boil.

The milk powder is the structural piece. It thickens as the rice swells and turns the water into something that coats the grains instead of pooling under them. Cranberries pull in a little of that liquid too, which is why 3/4 cup of water is enough. Nuts go on at the end because anything crunchy stirred in at the start comes out of a cozy soft.
