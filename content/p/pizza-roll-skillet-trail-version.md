The original is rolled, sliced, and baked, which needs an oven and an hour you probably do not have. Cooking the dough flat in a covered pot gets the same three things — bread, meat sauce, melted cheese — with a stove and a lid. The lid is what makes it work: it traps enough steam to cook the top of the dough while the bottom browns, so nothing needs turning until the halfway flip.

Canned tomatoes and 400 g of raw ground beef are mostly water, so both come out. Freeze-dried beef plus tomato paste and sun-dried tomatoes rebuild the sauce from about a pound of dry weight instead of three. Mayonnaise packets keep at ambient temperature and turn into the chili sauce with two squeezes, which is the only cold thing on the menu and the reason the pot food is worth eating.
