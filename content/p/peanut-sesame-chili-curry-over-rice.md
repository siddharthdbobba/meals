The original is a stuffed-chilli curry that runs an hour and a half with a blender and a frying pan. What survives the trip is the gravy, and the gravy is really just toasted peanut, sesame, and coconut ground fine, loosened with water and turned sour with tamarind. All of that grinding happens at home, so camp is one pot and one boil.

The substitutions worth naming: browned onion paste becomes dried onion flakes, fresh ginger and garlic become powders, and the fresh green chillies become dried ones or a drained jar of rings. Deseed the dried chillies or the heat runs away from you. Do not skip the tempering step at the start — the mustard seeds and curry leaves in hot oil are most of what makes this taste like anything other than peanut butter soup.
