Hot water is not a preference here. The mix has powdered egg and buttermilk powder in it, and cold water leaves both of them grainy and the batter thin. Boiled water, rested a minute so it does not cook the egg outright, hydrates everything inside five minutes and gives you a batter that holds its shape in the pan.

The source version is a home griddle recipe, so a few things changed. Butter became oil, which does not need a cooler and pours from a bottle. The batch is scaled to two servings so you are not carrying three cups of flour for one breakfast. Pumpkin powder is doing real work beyond flavor: it is starch and fiber, and it makes a pancake that sits heavier than a white-flour one, which is the point on a cold morning.
