The original is a pressure-cooker chili built on a can of pumpkin, a can of beans, a can of tomatoes, and a cup of apple cider. That is roughly four pounds of water carried up a hill. Every wet component here is the powdered version of itself, and the water goes in at camp instead.

The pumpkin is not a gimmick. Its starch is what thickens the pot, which matters more on trail than at home because there is no simmering-down step to lose liquid. Cocoa and pumpkin pie spice sound odd together in a chili and are the reason it tastes like something other than dumped-out powder. The cider packet replaces the cup of cider: sugar and apple, no volume.
