steps:
  - "At home: dust the steak with flour, pound it hard with a mallet, flip, flour, and pound again. Cut into serving pieces, bag, and chill in the cooler."
  - "In camp, heat the oil in a heavy skillet and add the garlic. Keep the heat moderate so it goes gold, not black."
  - "Lay in the steak. About a minute and a half a side. Pull it while it is still soft. Crisp means overcooked."
  - "Set the meat on a plate and pour off all but a spoonful of oil."
  - "Return the pan to heat, add 300 ml of water and the bouillon, and scrape the browned bits loose."
  - "Whisk 2 tbsp of the remaining flour into a few spoonfuls of cold water, stir it into the pan, and simmer until it coats a spoon. Salt and pepper it."
  - "Make the potato flakes in your mug with hot water. Steak over potatoes, gravy over both."

Round steak is lean and stringy, and the mallet is the whole point. Pounding shortens the muscle fibers and drives flour into the surface at the same time, so the meat tenderizes and gets its crust in one step. Store-bought cubed steak is machine-tenderized and never comes out the same.

This one needs a cooler and a real skillet, so it is a car-camping or basecamp dinner, not a pack dinner. The changes from a kitchen version are small: the pounding and chopping happen at home, bouillon and water stand in for carried stock, and instant potato flakes replace boiled reds. That keeps camp down to one pan and one mug.
