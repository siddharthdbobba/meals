The original bakes raw chicken breasts for 35 minutes in an oven, which no camp has. Foil-pouch chicken is already cooked and shelf-stable, so the fire is only reheating and driving the pesto oil into the meat. Twelve minutes on coals does that. Longer just dries it out.

Pesto is mostly oil, which is what makes it work here: it does not need refrigeration for the hour it spends out of the packet, and it carries the seasoning without any liquid to boil off. Salt and pepper still matter. Pouch chicken is bland on its own, and the pesto alone will not cover it.

Note on tags: most jarred pesto contains pine nuts and parmesan, so this is neither dairy-free nor vegetarian. Check your label if pine nuts are a problem for you, since some brands use cashews or walnuts instead.
