This started as an oven casserole, which is not a thing you have. The rebuild is a skillet: the pierogies brown in the sausage fat instead of steaming in a dish, and the eggs go in last so they set around everything rather than baking into a slab.

The two real substitutions are raw breakfast sausage for a cured one, and fresh eggs and milk for powder. Raw sausage is a food safety problem after day one and eggs crack in a pack. Powdered eggs need slightly cooler water than boiling or they scramble in the cup before they reach the pan. Pierogies come out of the freezer at home and hold in a cold cooler for a day or two; past that, use the potato flake version.
