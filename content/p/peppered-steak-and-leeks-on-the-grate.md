This is a car-camping dinner because skirt steak needs a cooler and real heat. Nothing about it is dehydratable. What makes it work on a fire rather than a griddle is timing: the leeks cook long and slow on the edge, the steak cooks fast and hot in the middle, and they never want the fire at the same moment.

The rub is cracked, not ground. Whole peppercorns broken under a pan bottom form a crust that survives searing; ground pepper burns into bitterness in the same three minutes. Skipping the blanching step the home version uses is deliberate — a pot of salted water is a second pot and a second cleanup, and coals do the softening if you give the leeks the ten minutes.
