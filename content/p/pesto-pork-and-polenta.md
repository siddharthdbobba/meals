The original is a butterflied pork loin rolled around fresh pesto and roasted for twenty minutes. None of that survives a pack, so the roulade gets unrolled. Same four flavors, different order: pork from a foil pouch, and a pesto built dry from basil, pine nuts, parmesan, garlic, and oil, stirred in at the end instead of spread on raw meat.

Polenta replaces the plate. It gives the pesto something to cling to and turns a pouch of meat into a real dinner for about two ounces of extra weight. Add the cheese and oil after the heat is off — parmesan goes stringy and grainy if you boil it, and the oil carries more of the basil when it is not cooking off.
