The oven version bakes fruit under a butter-and-oat topping until it browns. No oven on trail, so this splits the job: simmer the fruit first, then steam the topping on top of it with the lid on and the burner off. Residual heat does the cooking, which saves fuel and keeps the bottom from scorching.

Dried peaches replace fresh ones because fresh fruit is water you are carrying up a hill. They rehydrate into something jammier than canned peaches ever were, and their sugar concentrates, so you need less sweetener. Butter powder stands in for the stick of butter — it will not crisp the oats, but it carries the fat that makes this read as dessert instead of porridge.
