The original is a cast iron skillet dish that finishes in a 350-degree oven, which does not exist at a campsite. Freeze-dried pork replaces the tenderloin, so the whole thing stays on one burner and the meat is already cooked before it hits the pan. Rehydrating it separately matters: dropped in dry it will pull the liquid out of the sauce and go stringy.

Dried apple rings behave better here than fresh ones. They hold their shape in the sugar and fat instead of collapsing, and the soaking water goes back into the pan so nothing is thrown away. The bourbon is not decoration. It lifts the browned bits off the bottom of the skillet, which is where the flavor is, and the alcohol cooks off in under a minute. Add it off the heat over a campfire or it will flare.
