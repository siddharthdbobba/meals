Squirrel is lean, tough, and stuck to a lot of small bones, which is why frying dominates. Two stages of heat fix all three problems. The first hour uncovered over coals puts smoke on the meat while it is still dry enough to take it. The next two hours sealed over onions and liquid is a braise, and braising is the only thing that turns that connective tissue soft enough to pull.

The onions are not garnish. They hold the meat off the hot bottom of the pot and give off enough moisture to keep the braise going if the beer cooks down. This one needs a cooler and a real fire, so it belongs at a drive-in site, not in a pack. Pick the meat carefully when you get there. Squirrel bones are thin and easy to miss.
