A pie iron is a small cast-iron press on a long handle, and it does one thing well: it seals two slices of bread into a closed pocket so the filling steams while the outside toasts. That seal is the whole point. Anything loose at the edge leaks into the fire and welds itself to the iron.

Coals, not flames. Bread goes from pale to burnt in under a minute over open flame, and the cheese in the middle will still be cold when it does. Preheating the empty iron first matters too, because bread dropped into a cold iron sticks. This one needs a cooler for the cheese, so it stays a car camping meal unless you switch to the shelf-stable toppings in the variations.
