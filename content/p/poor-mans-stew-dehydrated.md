The original is a slow cooker recipe: six hours on low turning raw potatoes and carrots tender. Nothing on the trail runs six hours, so the softening moves home. Dehydrated potatoes and carrots have already given up their water and take it back in about ten minutes off the heat. The ground beef is cooked, drained, rinsed, and dried at home too, which is the only way meat like this rides safely without a fridge.

Rinsing the beef matters more than it sounds. Fat left on the crumbles goes rancid in the bag and blocks the meat from rehydrating. Dry it lean, then put the calories back at camp with the oil, which you control. Tomato powder replaces the paste for the same reason everything else changed: a can of paste is water and steel you would be carrying uphill.
