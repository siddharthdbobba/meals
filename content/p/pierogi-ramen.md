The home version uses frozen pierogi and chicken stock, neither of which survives a pack. Freeze-dried filled pasta is the substitution that keeps the point of the dish: a dumpling with a starchy, cheesy middle floating in ramen broth. Bouillon powder replaces the stock at a fraction of the weight, and garlic powder and ground ginger do the same job as the fresh aromatics because the broth is boiling anyway.

The pierogi go in first. They need four minutes of real simmering to rehydrate all the way through, and the ramen only needs two, so adding them together leaves you with mush and gravel in the same pot. Egg powder stirred in at the end thickens the broth slightly and replaces the soft-boiled egg you cannot carry.
