This started as an argument about cans. If you are paddling water in anyway, a can looks appealing: no rehydration, no fuel, no critter-proofing. The math still favors dried. A dinner like this takes about a cup and a half of water, which weighs less than the liquid already sealed in two cans of chili, and at the end of the week the trash is a fistful of flat bags instead of a bag of sharp metal.

The beef is the part that goes wrong. Ground beef dried straight out of the pan holds fat, and fat blocks water from getting back in, which is how you end up chewing gravel. Rinse the cooked meat with hot water before it hits the trays and it rehydrates in minutes. Bean flakes do the thickening so the pot never has to simmer, and the oil goes in last, once the heat is off, where it adds calories instead of interfering.
