The original is a skillet stew: pork cubes browned in margarine, then canned tomatoes, canned beans, and two fresh peppers simmered down. None of that carries. The rebuild swaps every wet ingredient for its dry equivalent — tomato powder for the can, dried pepper for fresh, flaked beans for canned — so the whole dinner weighs about five ounces and needs one boil.

Dehydrating your own pork is the part that takes planning. Cook it lean, cube it small, and dry it hard; fat left in the meat goes rancid in a few weeks. The oil goes in at the end instead, which puts the calories back without risking the shelf life. Lime at the end matters more than it sounds — the powdered tomato is flat, and acid is what makes it read as chili rather than bean mush.
