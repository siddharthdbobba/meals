The original was a home skillet dinner for eight, built on fresh chicken, frozen peas, and a bell pepper. Everything perishable got swapped: freeze-dried chicken, dried vegetables, garlic powder for cloves. The proportions of the sauce stayed, because that is what the dish actually is.

The sauce goes in off the heat. Peanut butter dropped into boiling water breaks and the oil separates out, and the noodle starch is what pulls it back together into a coating. Whisking it cold first also means you are not chasing lumps in a dark pot. Cut the water down and you get sauce; leave it up and you get soup, which is fine on a cold night.
