Wild turkey breast is lean to the point of being unforgiving. Two minutes past done and it turns to sawdust. The brine is insurance: salt in the pickle juice pulls moisture into the muscle and holds it there through the heat, and the acid softens the surface enough that a bird that ran wild all spring eats like one that did not.

The rest of the marinade is balance. Honey offsets the sourness and helps the outside color over coals, Worcestershire adds depth the pickle juice has none of, and the garlic and flakes cover the seasoning. This is a car camping recipe because it needs a cooler and 8 hours of lead time, so do the brining at home and let the drive count as marinating time. Slicing across the grain at the end matters as much as the brine does.
