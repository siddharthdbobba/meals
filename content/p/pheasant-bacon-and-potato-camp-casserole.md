This is hunt camp cooking, which means one heavy pot, a long cook, and no attention required in the middle. The bacon lining is the reason it works. Pheasant breast is very lean and dries out fast on a grill, so wrapping it in bacon and burying it under potatoes means it steams in rendered fat instead of losing water to the air.

The layering order matters. Meat on the bottom sits in the fat, potatoes above take on the drippings, and the ranch and cheese seal the top so nothing dries out. Two hours at a moderate temperature is not a suggestion — the potato slices need that long to go soft, and the bacon on the bottom needs it to fully render. This one needs a cooler and a real fire or grill, so it belongs at a road-accessible camp, not in a pack.
