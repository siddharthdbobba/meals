Cooking noodles off-heat saves fuel and keeps the pot from scorching. Steaming water softens a ramen block fine, and the whole thing is done before your stove would have finished a hard boil. Draining into a bag rather than the pot means the cheese hits hot noodles, not hot water, so it melts into a coating instead of settling out as grit at the bottom.

Parmesan is salty and dry enough to keep for days out of a cooler, which is why it is the cheese here. The oil is not optional. It carries the herbs, adds most of the fat, and turns a bag of noodles into a dinner that holds until morning. Skip the ramen seasoning packet; between the parmesan and the added salt there is plenty already.
