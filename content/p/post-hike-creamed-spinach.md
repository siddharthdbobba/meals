The original is a skillet dish built on fresh cream, cream cheese, and a fridge. None of that travels. Dried spinach, milk powder, and a teaspoon of cornstarch get to the same place: the starch does the thickening a cream reduction would have done, and the fat comes from oil and butter powder instead of double cream.

Water is the thing to watch. Fresh spinach has to be wrung out or the sauce splits; dried spinach has the opposite problem and will keep pulling water out of the sauce for a minute or two after you think it is done. Add the powders off the heat, stir them in slowly, and let the pot rest before you judge the thickness. The nutmeg is not decoration — a pinch is what makes it read as steakhouse rather than as cheese sauce.
