The whole point here is that one pan of water does two jobs in sequence. Asparagus goes in first and comes out crisp, then the same water gets pulled down to a simmer for the eggs. Nothing gets rinsed between steps, which matters when your dish sink is a bottle and a scrub pad.

The home version leans on toast to carry everything, so this one leans on the mustard instead. Wholegrain mustard is acidic and coarse enough to cut a soft yolk, and it keeps at ambient temperature, which the original's butter-and-bread setup does not. Hold the water at a lazy simmer, not a boil, or the whites will come apart into rags.
