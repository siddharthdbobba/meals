This is a foraging recipe, not a camp recipe, and it works best where you have a full-size pot and a burner you can hold steady for an hour. Prickly pear fruit is nearly all water and seeds. Boiling breaks the pulp down, straining takes the seeds out, and the long second boil drives off enough water that sugar and fruit thicken into syrup instead of juice.

The citric acid is not optional. Prickly pear is low in acid, so without it the syrup tastes flat and keeps badly. A teaspoon brightens the fruit and lowers the pH enough that a sealed jar holds up unrefrigerated. Once you have it, it goes on pancakes, into a mug of hot water, or over anything at camp that needs sugar.
