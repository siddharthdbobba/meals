The original is a fridge-clearing soup: leftover roast pork, a cutting board of raw vegetables, two quarts of stock. None of that travels. Freeze-dried pork and dried vegetables carry the same flavors at a tenth of the weight, and bouillon replaces the stock so you are not hauling water you could scoop from a creek.

The potato flakes do two jobs. They stand in for the diced potatoes that would otherwise need twenty minutes of fuel, and they turn a thin broth into something closer to stew. Add them off the heat and slowly, because there is no undoing a pot of cement. The oil goes in last for calories and to keep the whole thing from tasting like salt water.
