The original is a canning recipe: pepper rings packed in jars with garlic, oregano, basil, oil, and a hot vinegar brine, flipped daily for a few days and eaten after a week. None of that travels. Brine is water and glass, and neither belongs in a pack.

So the brine gets taken apart and carried as parts. Dried pepper rings, the same herbs, salt, and vinegar powder go in with the couscous; water at camp puts the pickle back together while the grain swells. The oil goes in last and off the heat, because it coats the couscous and stops the whole thing from setting into a block. Vinegar powder is the piece worth sourcing — without acid this is just spicy starch.
