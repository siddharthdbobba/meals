This started as a summer grilling dinner and it moves to a fire ring almost unchanged. The packet does the work of a covered pan: the food steams in its own moisture while the side against the grate browns. That is why the oil and the tight crimp are not optional. Loose foil vents the steam and you end up with raw potatoes and dry chicken.

Coals, not flames. A live fire runs far hotter than a gas grill and will blacken the outside long before a potato half cooks through. Doubling the foil buys you margin against both scorching and tearing, and it means the only thing to clean afterward is your hands.
