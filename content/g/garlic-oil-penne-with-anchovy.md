Aglio e olio is already a trail recipe pretending to be Italian dinner. Oil, garlic, salt, dried pasta, all shelf-stable, all cheap, one pot. The only change from a home kitchen is the parmesan: use the dry grated shaker kind instead of a wedge, since it survives a warm pack.

The part people get wrong is the water. Reserve a cup before draining. Starchy pasta water is what turns oil into sauce instead of leaving a slick at the bottom of the pot. Anchovies do the same job as salt but with more behind it, and they dissolve completely, so nobody has to look at a fish.

The original used a handful of fresh yarrow, which is bitter and loses its aroma the second it hits real heat. Dried herbs off the flame get you closer than a wilted forage does.
