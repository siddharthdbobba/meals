Guajillos are the whole point. Toasting wakes up the oils, the hot soak softens them, and the soaking water becomes the braising liquid, so nothing gets thrown out and no stock has to be carried. The sauce is mild and fruity rather than hot, which means you can eat a bowl of it after a long day instead of picking at it.

Two changes from a kitchen version. Tenderloin is wrong here: it goes gray and stringy over coals, so use chuck or sirloin and let the long simmer do the work. And 1 1/2 cups of chicken stock is a pound and a half of water to haul, so it becomes powdered bouillon plus the chile soaking liquid. Cook it over coals rather than flames, since the sauce scorches on the bottom fast if the pot runs hot.
