The narrow end of a turkey breast is a problem when you are cutting steaks: the slices get thin, and thin slices are dry before the thick ones are done. Rolling that end solves it. A pounded, rolled, tied breast is one uniform cylinder, and a cylinder cooks evenly over coals.

Goat cheese and dried cherries carry the whole thing because wild turkey is lean and has no fat of its own to work with. The cheese melts into the seam and keeps the inside from drying out; the cherries push back against the salt. Both are cooler food, not pack food, so this is a first- or second-night meal off the tailgate. Tie it at home and the camp work is coals and a thermometer.
