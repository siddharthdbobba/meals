Stir fry moves fast, which makes it a bad time to be hunting for the garlic. Everything gets sliced and measured before the stove is lit, and the sauce gets mixed at home in a bottle so it is one pour instead of five little containers on a picnic table.

The cornstarch does two jobs. On the meat it makes a thin crust that browns quickly, which matters because venison has almost no fat to protect it and turns to leather if you cook it past the sear. In the sauce it pulls everything into a glaze that clings to the meat and beans instead of pooling under the rice. A flat skillet beats a wok on a camp stove: the burner is a small circle of heat, and a flat bottom actually touches it.
