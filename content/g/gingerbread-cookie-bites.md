steps:
  - "At home: melt the butter in a saucepan over medium heat. It foams, then quiets down and turns gold with a nutty smell. Pull it before it goes past brown."
  - "Pour the hot butter over both sugars and stir. Beat in the eggs one at a time."
  - "Whisk the flour, baking powder, baking soda, cloves, ginger, cinnamon, and pepper together, then work them into the wet mix until no streaks remain."
  - "Fold in the chopped almonds. Chill the dough 15 minutes so it stops spreading."
  - "Scoop 60 g balls onto a lined sheet, six to a tray. Sprinkle each with flaky salt."
  - "Bake at 180 C for 12-14 minutes, until the edges set and the centers still look soft. Rap the tray on the counter to knock the domes down."

This is a bakery cookie carried as trail food, so all the work happens in a kitchen. The brown butter is the reason: cooking the milk solids drives off water and leaves a nutty fat that keeps the cookie chewy for days instead of going sandy overnight. Chilling before the scoop does the same job from the other side, holding the shape so the cookie stays thick and stays edible after a day in a pack.

The spice load is small on purpose. A quarter teaspoon each of clove, ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper reads as gingerbread without the bitterness a heavier hand gives. Flaky salt on top matters more than it looks: 250 g of sugar needs something to push against. No stove, no water, no cleanup beyond the bag.
