This is a coastal car-camping dinner, and it stays that way for a reason. Live mussels need cold air and drainage, not a bag in a pack, so it only works when you can drive to the water and cook the same day you buy them. The rest of the ingredient list is stove-agnostic and happy over coals.

The cooking is two reductions and a steam. Wine boiled down by half concentrates the acid so the coconut milk has something to push against; skip that and the broth turns flat and sweet. The mussels then release their own liquor into the pot, which is why salt goes in last. Five minutes with a lid is enough — held longer, they tighten into rubber and the broth is all you have left worth eating.
