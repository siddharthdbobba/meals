This is a car-camping dinner, not a pack-in one. Everything about it depends on a cooler and a real fire. What makes it work at a site instead of a kitchen is moving all the fussy work home: the overnight brine, the rub mixing, the apple peeling. In camp you are grilling meat and running one skillet.

The brine matters more than the rub does. Wild hog and lean store pork both dry out fast over coals, and salted cider buys you a margin at 145F. The sauce comes off the heat before the cheese goes in on purpose. Gorgonzola over direct flame breaks into oil and grit; residual pan heat melts it into the apples instead.
