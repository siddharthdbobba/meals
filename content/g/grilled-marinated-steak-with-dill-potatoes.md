This is a car-camping dinner, so nothing is dehydrated and nothing is reconstituted. The whole thing rests on marinating before you leave: acid, salt, and oil in a bag with a cheap cut, then several hours in the cooler doing the work while you drive. London broil is lean and tough, and that is the point. Marinade is what makes it worth eating.

The dairy is what changed. The original potatoes are done in butter, which turns to grease in a warm cooler and coats everything. Olive oil holds up at any temperature and carries dried dill about as well. Cut the tomatoes last, over the plate the steak rested on, so the juice and the drippings end up in the same place.
