This started as a post-holiday soup built on a goose carcass and a pot of homemade broth. Neither travels. Bouillon covers the broth, whole milk powder covers the cream, and the meat gets cooked and dried at home like any other jerky-adjacent protein.

The wild rice is the part people get wrong. Uncooked, it needs 45 minutes and a lot of fuel. Cook it at home, dry it, and it comes back in eight. The flour-and-oil paste is worth the extra thirty seconds: dumped in dry, flour makes lumps you cannot stir out with a spoon in a narrow pot.
