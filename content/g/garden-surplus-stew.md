This is a garden-clearing recipe more than a designed one. Dry whatever came in heaviest that summer, thin and on screens, and the mix changes every year without the recipe changing at all. The one rule is uniform slices. Thick pieces stay leathery no matter how long you simmer them.

Red lentils do the structural work. They cook in the same window the dried vegetables need to rehydrate, so one pot and one boil covers both, and they carry the protein that a bag of dried squash does not. The potato flakes go in last, off heat, to pull the whole thing together into something that stays on a spoon. Tomato powder after the boil keeps its acidity intact; boiled hard it goes flat and dull.
