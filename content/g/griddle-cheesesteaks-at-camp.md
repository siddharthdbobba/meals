This is griddle cooking, which means everything happens on one surface in a fixed order: vegetables until they soften, meat fast and hot, then the two chopped together. The order matters more than the technique. Peppers and onions take three times as long as thin steak, so starting them together gives you either raw vegetables or grey meat.

The foil wrap at the end is the part worth keeping. A sandwich sitting on a grate melts cheese from the bottom and dries the bread from the top. Wrapped, it steams in its own heat and the roll goes soft instead of brittle. It also buys you a holding window, so six sandwiches can be ready at once instead of one at a time. This one needs a cooler and a real fire or grill, so it belongs to car camping, not a pack.
