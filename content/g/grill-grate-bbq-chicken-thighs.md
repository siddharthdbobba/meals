This one stays close to the backyard version because it does not need adapting. Thighs are the cut that forgives a camp fire. They carry enough fat to survive uneven coals, and unlike breast they do not turn to rope if you leave them on two minutes too long.

The marinade happens at home, in the bag, before the cooler is packed. That is the whole trick. An hour on ice does the work while you drive, and the same bag is your marinating vessel, your transport, and your trash. Keep half the sauce back in a separate bottle for basting so you are not brushing raw-chicken sauce onto cooked meat. Coals are not a dial, so the thermometer is the only honest way to know when it is done.
