The source here was a garden-planning chart, not a recipe: how many carrot, bean, corn, tomato, and sweet potato plants feed one person for a year. That is a pantry problem, and a pantry that size ends up in a dehydrator. This is what the dried end of that harvest turns into on a stove.

Dried vegetables need real simmer time, not a soak. Eight minutes gets them tender; four leaves you chewing carrot gravel. The bean flakes and potato flakes go in last, off the heat, because they thicken on contact and will scorch if the flame is still on. They also do the work of turning a thin vegetable broth into something with enough body and fat to count as dinner.
