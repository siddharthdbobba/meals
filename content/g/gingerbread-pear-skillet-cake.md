This is the classic skillet upside-down cake moved outdoors with two swaps. Fresh grated ginger becomes ground ginger, which is stronger by volume and does not go slimy in a food bin. Fresh lemon zest becomes dried peel. Everything else already keeps: molasses, oil, flour, and sugar are all shelf-stable, and the butter survives a weekend out of a cooler.

The boiling water is not optional. It thins the molasses so it disperses, and it wakes up the baking soda against the molasses acid before the pot ever sees heat, which is the only lift this batter gets. Coal placement matters more than time: heavy on the lid, light underneath, or the pear layer burns before the crumb sets.
