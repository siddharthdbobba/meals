Quince is the rare fruit that improves for being stuck in a car for three days. It is too hard and too astringent to eat raw, so nothing is lost by hauling it around, and grating solves the other problem: raw quince takes an hour to soften in chunks, but shreds break down in half that.

The color change is the timer. Quince starts off pale and turns pink as it cooks, which is the tannins doing their work, and it is a better signal than a clock over an uneven camp flame. Sugar and acid are what make this keep without a fridge, so do not cut both. If you cut the sugar, eat it within a couple of days.
