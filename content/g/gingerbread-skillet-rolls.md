This is a home kitchen recipe dragged outside, and most of the work was getting the fridge out of it. Fresh milk, eggs, and butter all became powders and shortening. Powdered egg and milk rehydrate fine inside a dough, where nothing has to look like an egg. Shortening or ghee stands in for butter because both sit in a bear canister for a week without turning. The white chocolate in the original icing is gone; melting chocolate over a fire is a way to burn chocolate.

The molasses is not just color. It is acidic and it holds water, so the crumb stays soft in dry mountain air longer than a plain sweet dough would. Coal placement is the part people get wrong: heat from below cooks the bottoms fast and leaves the tops raw, so weight the coals two to one on the lid and keep turning the skillet.
