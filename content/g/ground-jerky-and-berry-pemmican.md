steps:
  - "At home: slice the trimmed meat thin, toss with the salt and pepper, and dry it in a dehydrator at 160F until it snaps rather than bends. Eight to twelve hours."
  - "Grind the dried meat to a powder in a blender dry container or spice grinder. It should look like coarse flour, not shreds."
  - "Dry the berries separately until they are cracker hard. Halve cranberries first or the skins balloon and never dry through. Grind them and sift out the shell fragments if you want less crunch."

This is trail food that predates trail food. Powdered dry meat has almost no water left in it, and tallow is a fat with no water either, so the two together have nothing for bacteria to work with. That is the whole preservation trick, and it is why the fat has to be clean rendered tallow rather than butter or bacon grease.

Two things sink a batch. Fat left on the meat goes rancid months before the lean does, so trim hard. And store-bought dried fruit is usually soaked in syrup, which stays pliable and adds moisture back in; dry your own until it cracks. Ratio-wise, roughly equal volumes of meat powder and fat, with the fruit as a third of that, gives bars that hold shape in a pack without crumbling into dust.
