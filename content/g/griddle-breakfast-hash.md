steps:
  - "Get the griddle or a large skillet over the fire to about 400°F. Wipe it with oil."
  - "Lay the bacon out flat. Cook, flip once, pull it off and chop when it is done."
  - "Spread the potatoes in a single layer on the oiled surface. Salt them now. Leave them alone for four or five minutes before the first flip, then keep turning until they are brown outside and soft inside, 12 to 15 minutes."
  - "Once the potatoes are about halfway, push them over and add the onion and peppers to the open space. Cook 5 to 7 minutes until they slump and start to color."
  - "Stir the vegetables and bacon into the potatoes. Slide the whole pile to a cooler edge."

This is a griddle recipe and it stays one. A flat top gives you enough surface to brown potatoes in a single layer, which is the whole point — crowded potatoes steam and go grey. A 12-inch cast iron skillet over a grill grate works too, but cook the potatoes in two batches instead of one.

Order matters more than technique. Bacon first leaves fat on the surface, potatoes get the longest run because they take the longest, and the peppers and onion go in late so they soften without turning to mush. Eggs last, off to the side, on lower heat — they overcook in seconds on a hot flat top. It needs a cooler, so it is a car camping breakfast, not a backpacking one.
