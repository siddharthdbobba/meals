steps:
  - "Get a bed of coals going, or run the gas grill to medium. You want steady heat, not flame."
  - "Soak the corn in its husks in a bucket or the cooler melt for ten minutes, then set the ears directly on the grate. Turn every five minutes for about twenty. The husks will char black. That is correct."
  - "Lay each piece of salmon skin down on a sheet of heavy foil. Salt it, pepper it, put a pat of butter and a sliced clove of garlic on top. Fold the foil into a loose packet with room for steam."
  - "Set the packets on the grate and close the lid or tent them with more foil. Twelve to fifteen minutes for thick king. Open one and check the middle: it should flake but still look wet."
  - "Mash the rest of the butter with the remaining garlic, spread it on the cut loaf, and toast it cut side down at the cool edge of the grate. Three minutes, and watch it. Bread goes from pale to ruined fast."
  - "Toss the greens with oil, vinegar, salt, and pepper in the bag they came in. Shuck the corn with a towel, since the husks come off hot."
  - "Serve everything out of the foil. Pour the wine if you brought it."

Foil is doing most of the work. Salmon over open coals sticks, dries out, and falls through the grate; in a sealed packet it steams in its own fat and the butter, which is close to foolproof on unpredictable campfire heat. The packet is also the dish, so cleanup is one wad of foil in the trash bag.

Everything cooks on the same grate at the same time, which is the point of the menu. Corn takes the longest and tolerates neglect, salmon takes the middle, bread goes on last and needs watching. The salad is the only thing that needs a cooler, and it is the first thing to cut if you are short on ice.
