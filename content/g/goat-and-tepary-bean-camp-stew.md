This is a long-simmer stew built around cheap, tough meat and two things that take forever to cook: dried tepary beans and hominy. That combination is why it belongs at a car camp or a basecamp with a real fire, not on a canister stove where fuel is measured out. Cook the beans and hominy at home if you want it to be a weeknight-length dinner instead of an afternoon project.

The original leans on fresh barrel cactus fruit for a sour edge that thickens the broth. That is not something you can pack, so the sour comes from dried orange peel and a shot of tequila added at the deglaze, and the body comes from the beans breaking down. Sage and dried onion go in dry because both hold their flavor in a bag better than anything fresh will hold in a pack. Salt at the end, after the beans soften, or they stay tough.
