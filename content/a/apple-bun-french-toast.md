This is French toast without the fussy business of dipping slices one at a time. Tearing the bread into chunks means every piece gets coated on all sides, and a chunk soaks faster than a slab does. Bread that has gone a little dry works better than fresh, so it is a good use for the buns left over from the first night.

The camp adaptations are the egg and the milk. Powdered whole egg rehydrates into something that sets in a pan just like the real thing, and a tablespoon of milk powder covers the dairy without a cooler. Keep the flame moderate: the apple needs a few minutes to soften, and the egg needs to cook through before the outside of the bread goes dark.
