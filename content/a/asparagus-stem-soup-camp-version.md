The original is a restaurant trick for using up woody asparagus stems: cook them soft, thicken with rice, then blend the fiber into submission. You have no blender, so the fix moves upstream. Slice the stems thin across the grain before drying and the strings get cut into pieces short enough to eat. Skip that and you get a pot of dental floss.

Instant rice replaces the raw rice and cuts a twenty-minute simmer down to a five-minute soak while still doing the thickening. Milk powder stands in for the cream, and the oil goes in last, off the heat, which is where it carries the most flavor and the most calories. Dried parsley at the end is for color more than taste.
