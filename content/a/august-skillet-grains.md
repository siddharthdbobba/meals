The original is a summer skillet: fresh squash and peppers thrown in a pan with a grain. None of that survives a pack, so the vegetables come dried and the pan work disappears. What is left is the part that actually carried the dish, which is salt, fat, acid, and something with texture in it.

Couscous does the heavy lifting because it rehydrates off the heat. The dried vegetables need a real boil first, though. Add them at the same time as the grain and you get soft couscous around pieces of vegetable that are still leathery.
