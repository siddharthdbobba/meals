This started as a home antipasto salad and needed two changes to travel. Fresh tomatoes turn to slush in a cooler and leak water that thins the dressing, so sun-dried tomatoes in oil take their place: same salt and acid, no bleed. Fresh basil goes black within a day in a bag, so dried herbs go in instead and get 15 minutes in the brine to open up.

Everything else was already trail food. Cured salami and aged provolone are preserved on purpose, and the artichokes, peppers, and olives arrive in their own acid. The pepper brine is worth keeping because it does most of the dressing work, and the oil is what makes the whole bag hold together instead of sitting in a puddle at the bottom.
