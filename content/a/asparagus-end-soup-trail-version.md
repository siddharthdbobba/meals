The original is a home soup built to rescue the woody ends of asparagus, and it leans on a blender to hide the fiber. On trail there is no blender, so the fix happens before you leave: slice the ends thin, dry them, and let a long simmer do what the machine would have. Anything thicker than a coin stays stringy no matter how long it sits in the pot.

Flax is the body here. It does what cream would do without a cooler, and it gels fast once the water is hot. Wine becomes a teaspoon of vinegar, which is what the wine was doing anyway, and it goes in off the heat so the acid survives.
