This is a home cake rebuilt around powders. Butter, egg, and sour cream all exist in dried form, and all three do their job here once cold water goes back in, which means the whole cake can sit in a bear canister for a week before anyone lights a fire. The almond butter matters more than it looks: it carries fat that survives the trip and keeps the crumb from drying out in a cast iron pot that runs hotter than an oven.

Heat management is the actual skill. Coals underneath cook the bottom fast and burn it faster, so most of them belong on the lid, where they bake from above the way an oven does. The frosting is meringue powder rather than fresh egg white or fresh dairy, and it sets firm enough that the cake can be cut, wrapped, and eaten cold the next morning.
