The original is a slow cooker dish: seared chicken breast, a litre of stock, most of a carton of cooking cream, pasta stirred in at the end. None of that survives a pack. Freeze-dried chicken replaces the breast, powdered cream replaces the carton, and bouillon replaces the stock, so the whole thing carries dry and comes back with water.

The part worth keeping is the timing. Cooking the pasta in the sauce liquid rather than draining it is what makes this one pot instead of two, and starchy water is what lets cream powder and parmesan emulsify at all. Add the dairy off the heat. Boiled parmesan splits and goes stringy, and there is no fixing it once it does.
