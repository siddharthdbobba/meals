The source is a preserving recipe: fresh apricots, slightly underripe for the pectin, buried in an equal weight of sugar overnight and then boiled down for jars. None of that survives a trip. Fresh stone fruit bruises in a pack, the overnight maceration needs a counter, and canning needs a kitchen.

Dried apricots get you to the same place from the other direction. The water is already gone, so you add a measured amount back instead of boiling it off for an hour. Sugar drops to a third of a cup because the fruit is concentrated sweetness already, and citric acid replaces the tartness that underripe fresh apricots would have brought. Fifteen minutes on a low flame, one pot, spread on whatever bread or crackers survived day three.
