Casseroles are built on a cream-of-something can, an oven, and forty minutes. None of those are coming on a trip. The parts that actually matter are starch, protein, and a sauce thick enough to coat them, and all three have shelf-stable versions that weigh a few ounces.

The cornstarch is doing the work the canned soup used to do. Cheese powder and milk powder alone give you cloudy water; a teaspoon of starch and one minute of real heat pulls it into a sauce that clings. Add the powders at the end, not with the rice, or they scorch on the pot bottom before the rice is done.
