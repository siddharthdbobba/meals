The original is a fresh Mediterranean salad: raw tomato, cucumber, chopped parsley and mint, feta. None of that survives a pack. What does survive is the mechanic underneath it, which is that fine bulgur is parboiled before it is ever sold, so it rehydrates in cold water without a stove. That makes it one of the few grains that actually works as a cold soak.

The swaps are all about moisture. Sun-dried tomatoes replace fresh ones and give back some of the acid and sweetness. Dried herbs and onion do the aromatic work at a fraction of the weight. Lemon powder replaces the juice, which matters more than it sounds: without the acid the whole thing reads as wet cracked wheat. The oil goes in last because fat coats the grain and stalls the soak if you add it up front.
