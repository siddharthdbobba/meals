The home version leans on butter, a flour roux, and half and half. None of that travels. Milk powder and olive oil do the same job at a fraction of the weight, and the flour still thickens as long as you slurry it into hot broth off the heat first. Dump dry flour into a boiling pot and you get lumps you cannot whisk out with a spoon.

Order matters more than technique. Freeze-dried vegetables need water and time before the gnocchi goes in, or they finish crunchy while the gnocchi turns to paste. The parmesan is not a garnish either. It is doing half the thickening and most of the salt.
