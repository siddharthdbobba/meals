  - "Dump the premixed flour and salt into a pot. Boil 170 ml of water and pour it over."
  - "Let it sit five minutes so it stops scalding you, then knead in the pot until the dough is stiff and even. It should feel firm, not tacky."
  - "Cover the pot and set it somewhere cool for at least 30 minutes. Longer is better. This is when you get the fire down to coals."

Flatbrød is flour, salt, and water with no yeast, so nothing in it can go wrong on a cool night in a pot. The boiling water is the whole trick: it gelatinizes some of the starch, which makes the dough stiff enough to roll paper-thin without tearing and springing back. Cold water dough fights you.

The original chills overnight in a fridge and rolls out to 60 cm rounds on a takke. Neither fits a campsite, so this rests half an hour in the shade and rolls to skillet size. A dry pan over coals does the same job as the griddle. Cooked through and cooled, the rounds keep for days in a paper bag and go with cured meat, soup, or anything in a can.
