This is a bakery recipe dragged outdoors, and the only real change is where the heat comes from. The dough is no-knead, so it needs a bowl, a spoon, and time — all things a camp already has. Mixing it directly in the dutch oven means the pot you rise in is the pot you bake in, and there is nothing to wash but the spoon.

The flour blend matters. Straight whole wheat makes a heavy, dry loaf that does not rise well; cutting it with white flour keeps the crumb open. The oil is not a garnish. Half goes under the dough so the bottom fries instead of sticking, and half pools in the dimples and soaks down through the top. Coals on the lid are what brown it — heat only from below gives you a pale slab with a burnt bottom.
