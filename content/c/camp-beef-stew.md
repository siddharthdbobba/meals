A slow cooker runs seven hours because raw chuck needs that long to break down. Nothing on the trail has seven hours or a wall outlet, so the beef comes pre-cooked and freeze-dried and the vegetables come dried in dices. That turns a long braise into a twenty-minute simmer, and the flavor that would have built over an afternoon comes from bouillon, tomato powder, and Worcestershire instead.

The cornstarch is the part people get wrong. Dumped in dry it seizes into lumps that never dissolve, so it has to go in as a cold slurry at the end, after the pot is off a hard boil. Dried potato dices also matter more than they look: flakes would dissolve and turn the whole pot into gravy, while dices stay solid and give the stew something to chew.
