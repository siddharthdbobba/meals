The original is a fridge recipe: cold butter, whole milk, an egg, two pounds of fresh berries, and a bowl of whipped cream. None of that survives a weekend in a car. Shortening replaces the butter because it holds its shape at ambient temperature and still cuts into the flour the same way. Milk powder and water replace the milk, the egg comes out entirely (drop biscuits do not need it), and dried strawberries plus boiling water and sugar rebuild the macerated fruit in twenty minutes.

The cut-in fat is the whole technique. Lumps of solid shortening melt in the heat and leave layers behind, so do the cutting at home while your hands are clean and the fat is cold, then bag it and keep it out of the sun. The whipped cream only works with cold water; warm water will not let the fat set up and you get a glaze instead of a topping.
