The custard is the whole point. Cinnamon rolls baked straight out of the can go dry in a dutch oven because coal heat is dry heat, and the egg-and-milk mixture soaking between the pieces keeps the middle from turning into bread. Cutting the rolls into quarters matters too: more cut surface means the custard actually gets absorbed instead of pooling under an intact spiral.

The home version uses fresh milk and shell eggs. Powdered whole milk and powdered egg do the same job and are one less thing competing for cooler space, which is worth it when the dough already has to stay cold. Coal placement is the part people get wrong. Most of the heat goes on the lid; too much underneath and the bottom is charcoal before the center sets.
