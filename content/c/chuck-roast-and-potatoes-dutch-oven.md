This started as a slow cooker recipe for feeding company on a small budget. The dutch oven does the same job over coals: a chuck roast is tough because it is full of connective tissue, and low heat over several hours turns that tissue into gelatin. That gelatin is what thickens the liquid at the end. There is no flour in here and none is needed.

Two things go wrong most often. Too much water makes soup instead of a braise, so keep the liquid low and let the meat and vegetables release their own. And potatoes added at the start will disintegrate by hour four, which is why they go in late. Coals on top matter more than coals underneath; heat from below scorches the bottom while the meat above it stays cold.
