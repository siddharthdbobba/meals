The original bakes in a water bath at 300°F so the eggs stay creamy. You cannot do that on a canister stove, so the low-and-slow flame does the same job: gentle heat, constant stirring, off the burner before it looks done. The cornstarch is the part worth keeping from the restaurant version. It binds water into the egg and stops it from weeping into the pot.

Cottage cheese and fresh eggs do not travel, so powdered whole milk and egg powder take their place. The hard cheese is doing double duty, adding fat the powders lack and salt the eggs need. Residual heat finishes it after the stove is off.
