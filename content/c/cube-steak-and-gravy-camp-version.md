The original spends eight hours in a slow cooker turning a cheap cut fork-tender. Nothing on a stove can do that, so this recipe drops the raw steak entirely and uses freeze-dried cooked beef, which is already tender and only needs to rehydrate. The canned cream of mushroom soup becomes milk powder plus dried mushrooms, and the sliced onion becomes dried minced onion. What survives the translation is the part that carried the dish anyway: onion soup mix and brown gravy mix, both of which are already dehydrated salt-and-savory concentrate designed to make thin liquid taste like it simmered.

Order matters. Gravy mix thrown in at the start scorches on a thin titanium pot bottom, and milk powder added to boiling water clumps. Rehydrate the solids first, then take the pot off the flame before the powders go in. The potatoes are a separate step because gravy-thickened water will not hydrate flakes evenly, and you end up with dry pockets.
