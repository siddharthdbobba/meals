The USDA is blunt about stuffing a bird: it slows the roast and gives bacteria a warm place to sit while the center crawls toward 165 F. That advice makes the trail version easy, because the safe method and the packable method are the same method. Cook the stuffing on its own, and cook the meat on its own. Here the meat arrives already cooked and sealed in a retort pouch, so the only thing that needs heat is the bread.

Dried bread cubes are the whole trick. They weigh almost nothing, they rehydrate off residual heat with the stove already shut down, and they hold fat well, so the butter powder or oil ends up in the food instead of pooling at the bottom of the pot. Sage does the heavy lifting on flavor; without it this reads as wet bread. Add the meat last so it warms through rather than shredding into mush.
