These are a home-cooking staple adapted for camp heat. The ratio of fat to flour makes them crispy at the edges rather than cake-like, and they bake faster over fire than you might expect. Margarine travels better than butter on short trips because it holds its shape longer in the heat. Self-raising flour saves you from carrying baking powder separately.

The skillet method works if you keep the heat moderate and move the cookies only when the bottom is golden; moving them too early tears the crust. On multi-day trips, swap the fresh egg for powdered egg and carry margarine powder instead; the cookies taste identical. If you don't have a skillet, wrap dough spoonfuls in foil and cook the packets directly over coals for 8–10 minutes.
