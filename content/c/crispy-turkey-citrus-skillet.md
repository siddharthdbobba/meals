This is a fire-cooked stir fry, so the order matters more than the technique. The sauce gets made first and held warm, because once the oil is hot you will not have a free hand or a free burner. Everything after that happens in one skillet in about six minutes.

The frying gives the turkey a crust that survives being sauced, which is the whole point — wild turkey breast is lean and goes dry fast, and a coating buys you a margin. The juice concentrate is doing two jobs: it is the sauce base and it rides down as a frozen block in the cooler. Tangerines go in off the heat so they hold their shape instead of collapsing into the glaze. This one needs a cooler and real dishwater, so it is a car camp or turkey camp meal, not a backpacking one.
