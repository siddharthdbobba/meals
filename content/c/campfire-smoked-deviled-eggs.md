This is the picnic version, moved outside and stripped down to what survives a cooler. The smoking step is the only reason to do it at camp rather than at home: cut egg white is porous and picks up smoke fast, which is why the whites go over the coals alone and the yolks stay out of it. Twenty minutes over a low fire is plenty. Longer and the whites go rubbery and bitter.

The one-minute boil and ten-minute rest is worth following exactly. Eggs held at a rolling boil the whole time get a grey ring and a sulfur smell, and they fight you when you peel them. Everything but the eggs here is shelf-stable, so the cooler is only doing one job. Mayo packets instead of a jar means no half-open jar riding warm in a car for three days.
