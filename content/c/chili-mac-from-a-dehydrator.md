The chili is cooked entirely at home, which is the only way to get 50 minutes of simmering and two kinds of beans into a backcountry dinner. Cracked wheat is doing the work that ground beef usually does: it swells, holds sauce, and gives the spoon something to push against. Lime and soy sauce go in after the heat is off, so the acid and salt survive into the dried version.

At camp the sequence matters more than the heat. Five minutes of cold soak starts the pasta and the dried chili drinking before you spend fuel, two minutes of boil finishes the macaroni, and the ten-minute covered rest does the rest of the rehydration for free. Boiling the whole 20 minutes would burn three times the gas and scorch the bottom of the pot.
