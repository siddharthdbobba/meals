The original is a fresh-vegetable stew: onion, garlic, carrot, celery, peppers, canned tomatoes, cooked beans. All of that has a dry equivalent that weighs a fraction as much. Tomato powder does the heaviest lifting, since a can of chopped tomatoes is mostly water you are already carrying in your bottle. Cooked beans become instant or flaked black beans, which rehydrate in minutes instead of the overnight soak plus long boil the whole dried ones need.

The spices are the whole point, so do not cut them to save a gram. Smoked paprika and cumin are what make a bean pot read as chili rather than as beans. The acid at the end matters just as much: without lime or vinegar the whole thing tastes flat and muddy. Fat comes in as olive oil because there is no sour cream or cheese out here, and a stew this lean is not dinner without it.
