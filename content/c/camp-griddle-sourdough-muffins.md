This is a long recipe with almost no work in it. The overnight ferment does the rising, so the only thing camp has to supply is a flat pan and heat you can hold steady for ten minutes. Skip the fridge entirely: the dough is supposed to sit at room temperature overnight, which is exactly what a cooler lid or a shaded bear box gives you.

The baking soda matters more here than at home. It reacts with the acid the starter built overnight and gives lift fast, which covers for a griddle that never holds one temperature. Cornmeal under the rounds keeps them from gluing themselves to whatever you set them on while they rise. Coals, not flame — direct fire scorches the outside before the middle sets.
