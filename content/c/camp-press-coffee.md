Instant coffee is lighter and faster and nobody drinking it is happy about it. A press earns its six ounces on trips where mornings are slow anyway — basecamps, car camping, any day you are already boiling water for oatmeal. The paper filter and the short contact time are what make it worth carrying: you get the clarity of a pour-over without standing there holding a kettle in the wind for four minutes.

The two-stage pour matters more than the grind does. Pressing a small volume of strong concentrate and then diluting means the water is hot enough to dissolve milk powder, and it means a spill in the chamber costs you a third of a cup instead of all of it. Grind at home; a hand grinder is another half pound and it does not make the coffee better than a fresh bag does.
