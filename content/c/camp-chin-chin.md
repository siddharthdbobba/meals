steps:
  - "At home, whisk the flour, sugar, salt, nutmeg, and egg powder together and bag it. Cut the butter in with your fingers until the mix looks like coarse sand, then seal it."
  - "In camp, add the evaporated milk and 3 tbsp water to the bag or a pot and work it into a stiff dough. It should be firm, not sticky. Add flour a spoonful at a time if it clings."
  - "Dust a cutting board or a clean stuff sack panel and roll the dough out to about 1/4 inch. Cut it into small squares with a knife."
  - "Pour oil into the pot to a depth of about an inch and heat it over the fire or stove until a scrap of dough sizzles and floats within a few seconds."
  - "Fry in small batches, stirring so they brown evenly. Three to four minutes a batch. Pull them when they are deep gold, not pale."

This is a Nigerian party snack, and the only thing standing between it and a trail food is the fridge. The egg becomes powdered egg, the milk is evaporated milk out of a can, and the butter gets cut into the flour at home so all you are doing in camp is adding liquid and rolling. That also means the fat is already distributed, which is what makes the dough short and snappy instead of tough.

Oil depth is the whole game. An inch is enough to float the squares and hold its temperature through a batch; less than that and they sit on the bottom, soak, and come out heavy. Cut the pieces small and uniform so they cook through before the outside darkens. They will seem soft when you pull them out. Wait until they are cold before you decide.
