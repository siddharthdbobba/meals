The original is a stovetop sauce with two cans of tomatoes and a stick of butter. Cans are heavy and butter melts in a pack, so this version trades them for tomato powder and ghee. Powder rehydrates into the same acidic base at a fraction of the carry weight, and the sun-dried pieces give back the texture you lose when the cans go.

Everything happens in one pot, in order: pasta first, then the sauce built in the same water. The reserved pasta water is doing real work — its starch is what keeps the rehydrated tomato from separating into grit and liquid. The fat goes in off the heat, which is where it stays a sauce instead of a slick.
