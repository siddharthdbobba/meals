Coals put out more direct heat than a grate ever will, and contact with the embers is what builds the crust before the interior has time to cook through. Venison backstrap is lean enough that it goes from rare to grey in about a minute, so the speed is the point, not a stunt. Ash brushes off; the meat does not soak it up.

Seasoning goes on afterward for a reason. Salt drawn to the surface before cooking pulls out moisture and burns on contact with the embers. The oil at the end does the same job a fat cap would on a beef cut, which venison simply does not have. This one needs a cooler and a real fire, so it belongs at the truck or at basecamp, not in a pack.
