Meatballs are a fussy thing to build in camp, so most of the fuss happens at home. The dry mix is one bag, and the only wet work left is a spoonful of egg powder and water. Canned chicken is already cooked, which means browning is about crust and structure, not doneness — but check one anyway, since raw pockets are possible if the balls are large.

The pasta cooks in the sauce rather than in a separate pot of water you would have to dump. Measure the 900 ml honestly. Too much and you get soup; too little and the spaghetti stays chalky in the middle with nothing left to fix it. Tomato paste is doing the work canned tomatoes would at home: it is a fraction of the weight and carries the same acidity, which is why the sugar is on the list.
