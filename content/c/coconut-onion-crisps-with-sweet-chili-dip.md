The original is an oven recipe with a fresh onion, fresh milk, and a fresh egg, which is three things a camp kitchen does not have. Dehydrating the onion rings at home solves the biggest one: they rehydrate in ten minutes and hold their shape better than raw onion does in a pan. Powdered milk and egg rebuild the wash at camp with a third of a cup of water.

Baking becomes shallow frying because that is what a stove can do. The coconut is the part that needs watching. It has sugar and oil in it and browns well ahead of the panko, so a low flame and a single turn matter more than time on the clock. The honey dip is stirred cold and keeps for days, so make it first and leave it alone.
