steps:
  - "Warm 300 ml of water until it is barely above skin temperature. Hot water kills the yeast."
  - "Stir the honey into the water until it dissolves."
  - "In your largest pot or a gallon freezer bag, combine 1/2 cup of the flour with the yeast and salt. Pour in the honey water and the olive oil."
  - "Beat it with a spoon for three minutes. It will look like batter. That is correct."
  - "Work in the remaining flour a handful at a time until the dough pulls together and is only barely tacky."
  - "Knead on a floured cutting board or inside the bag for about five minutes, until it stops tearing and goes smooth."
  - "Oil the pot, drop the dough in, cover it, and set it somewhere warm for ten minutes. A sunny rock or beside the fire ring works."
  - "Punch it down, split it in two, and let it rest another ten minutes."

Real dough in camp is mostly a heat problem, not a skill problem. The rapid-rise yeast is what makes it possible: two ten-minute rests instead of an hour and a half, so dinner does not depend on how warm the afternoon is. Honey feeds the yeast faster than plain sugar and gives the crust color, which matters when you are cooking blind under a lid.

The divided flour is the one step worth not skipping. Mixing half a cup with the yeast and liquid first gives you a loose batter you can beat air into; dumping all three cups in at once leaves you kneading a dry lump on a cutting board in the dark. Coals go under the pot and on the lid both, or the top stays raw.
