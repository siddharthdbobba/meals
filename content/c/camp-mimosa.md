This is the brunch mimosa moved to a campsite, which mostly means solving two problems: no fresh-squeezed oranges and no refrigerator. Drink powder handles the first — reconstituted juice is thin on its own, but the wine is doing half the work here anyway, so thin is fine. The cooler handles the second, and it is the part people skip. Carbonation holds in cold liquid and escapes from warm liquid, so a split that spent the drive in a hot car will foam out of the cup and taste flat inside a minute.

The ratio is roughly half and half, which is the traditional one and also the forgiving one. Pour the juice first so the wine's bubbles have something to rise through instead of a dry cup wall. A 375 ml split makes two real drinks and does not leave an open bottle going flat in the cooler overnight — that is the only reason to buy the small size.
