steps:
  - "Morning of, tip the flour into a large zip bag and pour in the water. Squeeze the bag until no dry flour is left, then leave it alone for 30 minutes."
  - "Add the salt and the yeast. Work the bag again for a minute, until the dough is one smooth lump. Press out the air, seal it, and put it somewhere shaded for the day. Eight hours is fine. Twelve is better."
  - "Build the fire early and let it burn down to coals. You want a bed you cannot hold a hand over, not flames."
  - "Open the tomato can, stir in a pinch of salt, a spoon of the oil, and the oregano. No cooking. That is the sauce."

Pizza fails at camp for the same reason it fails at home: the dough is rushed and the heat is weak. Two grams of yeast across 500 g of flour sounds like nothing, and that is the point. A long, cool, slow rise builds flavor that a fast one cannot, and here the rise costs you nothing because it happens in your pack while you paddle or set up camp.

The other three fixes are small. Mixing flour and water first and holding the salt back for half an hour lets the flour hydrate before the salt tightens it up. Stretching by hand instead of adding more flour keeps the dough from drying out. And the sauce is raw crushed tomatoes with oil and salt, which means one less pot and a brighter result than anything simmered. Get the pan properly hot before the first round goes in; a lukewarm skillet gives you flatbread.
