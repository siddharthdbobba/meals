The source recipe presses fresh apples and cans the juice in jars, which is a fine thing to have in a basement and a bad thing to carry uphill. The trail version keeps the same two ingredients — apples and an acid — and removes the water. Run pressed cider through a dehydrator on fruit-leather trays until it goes to a tacky sheet, then grind it. A pint jar of cider becomes about an ounce of powder.

The acid is not a flavoring afterthought. Apple sugars dried alone taste flat and vaguely caramel; the citric acid puts the sharp edge back and keeps the powder from clumping in the bag. If you are buying powder instead of making it, buy unsweetened and add your own sugar, since sweetened versions are mostly maltodextrin by weight.
