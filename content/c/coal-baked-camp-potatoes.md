This is base camp food, not pack food. A raw potato is mostly water and weighs what it weighs, so it only makes sense when the food is riding in a vehicle. In exchange you get a hot dinner that needs no pot, no water, and no washing up. The foil is the cookware and it goes in the fire when you are done.

The coals matter more than the timing. A potato sitting in flame chars on one side and stays raw in the middle, while a bed of spent coals cooks it through by conduction and steam trapped in the foil. Fat is the other half of the recipe. A plain baked potato is a thin dinner after a day on your feet, so the oil and cheese go in while the flesh is still hot enough to absorb them.
