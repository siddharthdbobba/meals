This is the campfire version of a stew, one can per person, and the water is what separates it from a foil dinner. A dry packet steams and browns. A can with a cup of water in it simmers, which means tough cuts and hard root vegetables actually get tender in the same hour, and you end up with broth instead of scorched edges.

The bacon on the bottom is structural, not decorative. It renders into a layer of fat that keeps the rest from welding itself to the steel. Dice size is the other thing that matters: cut the meat to match the carrots, or the potatoes go to mush while the beef is still chewy. This one stays in the cooler and is not a backpacking meal, so nothing here is dried or substituted.
