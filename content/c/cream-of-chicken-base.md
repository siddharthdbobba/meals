The original is a stovetop roux: butter, flour, stock, milk. On trail you have no butter and no fridge, so the fat and the dairy both come as powder and the stock comes as bouillon. That means the roux step disappears, which is fine, because the roux was mostly there to cook the raw taste out of flour and disperse it in fat. Powdered milk does the dispersing job well enough on its own.

The one thing that still matters is the slurry. Flour dumped into hot water seizes into lumps that never break up, so mix a small amount of cold water in first and get it smooth before any heat touches the pot. Thickening happens just below the boil, and it happens fast. Stir the whole time and take it off early — it keeps setting up after the flame is out.
