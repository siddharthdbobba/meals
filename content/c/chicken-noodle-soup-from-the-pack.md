The original is a home soup built on a leftover rotisserie chicken, two hours of stock, and a cutting board of fresh vegetables. None of that travels. Dried carrot, onion, and celery rehydrate in the same water that becomes the broth, and bouillon replaces the six cups of stock you would otherwise carry as liquid.

Order matters more than technique here. Dried vegetables need a few minutes of hard boil before the noodles join them, or you end up with soft noodles and gravel. The oil goes in at the end because there is no sauté step to carry it, and soup without fat leaves you hungry an hour later.
