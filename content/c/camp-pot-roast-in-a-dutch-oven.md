This is a home slow cooker recipe moved onto coals. The slow cooker was doing one thing — holding meat just under a simmer for eight hours — and a dutch oven buried in coals does the same job in about four, because the heat comes from all sides instead of the bottom. Chuck is the cut that rewards it: the connective tissue needs hours of low wet heat before it turns to gelatin, and there is no shortcut around that.

The searing step matters more than the sauce does. Browned meat is where the depth comes from; the soup mixes are mostly salt and thickener. Vegetables go in for the last hour only, otherwise they disintegrate into the gravy. This one needs a cooler, so it is a first-night car camping meal, not something to carry.
