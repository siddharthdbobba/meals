The original is a marinate-overnight, roast-a-whole-chicken affair, which is not happening on a stove with one burner and one pot. What survives the trip is the flavor engine: brine from the olives and capers, sweetness from prunes and brown sugar, and sharpness from vinegar. Those three fight each other in the right proportion and the fight is the dish. Everything else is a delivery mechanism.

Couscous replaces the roasting pan. It absorbs the reduced vinegar liquid that would otherwise pool under a chicken, so nothing is wasted and there is nothing to drain. Blooming the olives and prunes in oil before the water goes in matters more than it looks — dry heat pulls oil into the fruit and rounds the vinegar off. Add the water first and you get a sour, thin bowl instead.
