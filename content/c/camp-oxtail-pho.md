steps:
  - "Light about 20 charcoal briquettes in a chimney over the fire ring. Give them 30 minutes while you prep."
  - "Char the whole onion and the ginger knob directly on the coals until the skins blacken. Scrape off the worst of it and set aside."
  - "Put the oxtail and chuck in the 12-inch dutch oven, cover with water, and bring to a boil over the propane burner. Boil five minutes, then dump that water and rinse the meat and the pot. This is what keeps the broth clear."
  - "Return the meat to the pot. Add both cartons of broth, the charred onion and ginger, the spice packet, and enough hot water to cover by two inches."
  - "Set the pot over coals and hold a bare simmer for three hours. Bank coals on the lid if the wind is stealing your heat. Skim the scum for the first twenty minutes and then leave it alone."
  - "Pull the chuck out around the two hour mark, let it cool, and slice it thin against the grain."
  - "Stir in the fish sauce, bouillon, sugar, and salt. Taste. It should be a little too salty on its own, because the noodles are unseasoned."

This is a whole-afternoon meal, which is the point of a basecamp. Nothing here is technical. The work is parboiling the bones and dumping that first water, which is the difference between a clear broth and a cloudy one, and then keeping the pot at a bare simmer for three hours without letting it roll.

The dutch oven earns its weight because coals hold a steadier low heat than a propane burner does in wind. Dry noodles instead of fresh, and shelf-stable broth cartons, cut the cooler load down to just the meat. Sprouts are the one thing that will not survive a long trip, so treat them as optional rather than planning around them.
