The double dredge is the whole point. Flour, then wash, then flour again, with a rest between each layer so the coating grabs. One pass gives you a thin skin that slides off in the oil. Two gives you a crust with structure, and that crust is what keeps a lean animal from drying out at frying temperature.

Buttermilk and fresh eggs do not travel, so this uses milk powder and egg powder mixed with cold water. The wash comes out slightly thinner than real buttermilk, which is fine here since it only has to be sticky enough to hold flour. Age matters more than technique: young squirrels fry tender, old ones need a long simmer first or they will fight you. Frying needs a stable pot and a lot of oil, so this is a car-camp or basecamp meal, not something to attempt on a canister stove.
