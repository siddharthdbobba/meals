An electric frother makes foam by beating air into fat and protein at a temperature below boiling. You can do the same thing with a fork if you respect the temperature. Boiling water denatures the milk protein too fast and you get a flat brown drink. Water that has just stopped rattling holds the bubbles.

The trick is the two-stage pour. A small amount of near-boiling water makes a thick paste that whips, because there is enough sugar and milk solids per drop of water to trap air. Diluting to full volume first gives you nothing to beat. Whole milk powder is not optional here; nonfat foams and then dies within seconds.
