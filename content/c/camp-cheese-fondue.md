Fondue is stove cooking, not one-pot dump cooking, and the only thing standing between you and a pot of greasy strings is temperature. Cheese proteins tighten and squeeze out fat above a simmer, so the flame stays low from the moment the cheese goes in. The cornstarch and the lemon juice are both insurance: starch holds the emulsion together, acid keeps the protein from knitting into a ball.

The original called for a cup of chicken stock and a fondue pot. Bouillon and water do the same job at a fraction of the weight, and any pot over a low flame works. Hard aged cheese is the one perishable here, and it will sit two or three days out of a cooler without trouble. Grate it at home; grating in camp is misery and you lose half of it.
