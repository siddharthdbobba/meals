The source version simmers a real stock from roasted carcasses and dehydrates it. That is a fine winter project, but the trail-relevant part is the assembly: dried vegetables, a stock base, and dairy added dry at packing time rather than cooked in. Home dehydrators handle fat and milk badly, so the cream comes from milk powder stirred into the finished mix. Bouillon powder from the store does the same job as the homemade stock with none of the oven time.

Potato flakes are the addition that makes this a lunch instead of a hot drink. Two tablespoons give it enough body to stay warm in the pot and enough starch to sit with you through the afternoon. Off-heat water matters more than it sounds: milk powder and potato flakes both clump in boiling water, and there is no fixing that with a spoon.
