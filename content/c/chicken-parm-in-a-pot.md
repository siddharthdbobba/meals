Chicken parm is a breading problem, and breading does not survive a backpack. So the crumbs come off the chicken and go on top, dry and toasted, where they still give the crunch without needing egg wash or a hot oven. The chicken rehydrates on the side rather than in the sauce, because pasta water pulls it apart into threads.

Cooking the pasta in just enough water and never pouring it off is what makes this one pot instead of two. Angel hair is the right shape: it cooks in four minutes and releases enough starch to thicken the tomato powder into something that clings. Longer pasta shapes will leave you with soup and wasted fuel.
