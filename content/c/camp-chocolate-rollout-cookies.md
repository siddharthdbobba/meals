Most cookie recipes fall apart in camp because they need cold butter and eggs. This one never did. Oil is the only fat, water is the only liquid, and both are shelf-stable, so the whole thing collapses into one dry premix and one bottle.

Oil dough behaves differently than butter dough. There is no creaming step and no air, so the cookies come out dense and short rather than puffed. That works in your favor here: they hold their shape on a rough surface, they tolerate uneven coal heat, and they survive a few days in a pack without crumbling to dust. Keep the coals split roughly two-thirds on the lid so the bottoms don't scorch before the tops set.
