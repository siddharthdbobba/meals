This is a car-camping dinner that trades cooking for logistics. Canned spaghetti is already cooked, so there is no pot of water to boil, drain, and dispose of at a site with no sink. All the real cooking is browning meat and vegetables in one skillet, and everything after that is just reheating.

The browned beef, onion, and pepper are what make it edible. Canned spaghetti on its own is thin and sweet; fat, aromatics, and salt from the meat give it something to stand on. Add the garlic at the end so it perfumes the pan instead of turning black in it. If you want this without a cooler, canned or pouched meat works, and the dish stops needing ice entirely.
