The slow cooker does eight hours of work you cannot do on trail, so it happens at home. Braise the shoulder, shred it, pull the fat off, and dry it thoroughly. Lean meat dehydrates and rehydrates; fatty meat goes rancid in a pack. The fat comes back as olive oil at camp, which also does the frying.

The second half of the original recipe survives the transfer intact. Crisping the rehydrated pork against a hot pot is the same move as crisping it in a skillet, and it matters more here, since boiled meat with no browning tastes like nothing. Orange is the other thing worth carrying. Powdered drink mix or dried peel gets you the acid that cuts through a heavy pot of pork.
