Melon jam is normally a canning project: whole fresh cantaloupe, hours on the stove, sterilized jars. None of that travels. Dried melon skips the entire problem. The fruit already lost its water at home, so you are rehydrating and sweetening rather than boiling a pound of liquid off, and fifteen minutes does what an afternoon used to.

The acid matters more than the sugar. Cantaloupe is low-acid and flat on its own, and a quarter teaspoon of citric acid pulls the flavor back into focus the way lemon juice does at home. Powdered lemon works and weighs nothing. Skip it and you get sweet mush.

Made at camp it keeps a day, maybe two in cold weather. This is not preserved jam, so eat it.
