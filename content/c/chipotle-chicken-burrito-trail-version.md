The original bakes assembled burritos under a broiler until the cheese browns. There is no broiler out here, so the heat moves to the inside: hot rice does the melting, and an optional minute in a dry pot does what the oven was doing to the outside. Raw chicken and a marinade become a foil packet and a spoon of adobo, which carries the same smoke and acid without a cooler.

The adobo is the whole reason this works. A tablespoon of it seasons the rice, the chicken, and the fat all at once, so nothing needs a separate sauce step. Dry-aged cheese is deliberate; fresh monterey jack sweats and goes greasy in a pack, while a hard cheese survives a warm day and still melts into hot rice.
