The restaurant version leans on fresh cream, fresh spinach, and a cutting board full of mirepoix. None of that travels. Bouillon and dried vegetable flakes rebuild the base, powdered whole milk covers the half-and-half, and vacuum-packed gnocchi are already shelf-stable off the shelf. Foil-pouch chicken replaces rotisserie and weighs a third of a can.

Order matters more than technique here. Dried vegetables need real simmering time or they stay leathery, so they go in first and alone. The milk powder goes in last, off the heat, because it curdles and clumps in a rolling boil. The gnocchi release starch as they cook, which is what gives the broth body once the pot cools.
