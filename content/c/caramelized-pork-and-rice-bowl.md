The whole point is the caramelization, which most dehydrated dinners cannot do. Rehydrating alone gives you wet mince in sauce. Driving the water back off in the pot with a little oil lets the sugar in the sauce catch on the metal and coat the meat again. That takes low heat and patience, and it is the one step worth not rushing.

Cold-soaking the pork and beans on the trail does the slow half of the work for free. Lean mince matters more than it sounds: fat left in dried meat turns on you after a few warm days in a pack. Add the oil in camp instead. Expect to scrub the pot.
