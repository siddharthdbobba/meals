The original runs in a pressure cooker with fresh carrots, celery, onion, stock, and a rotisserie chicken. None of that travels. Fresh vegetables get replaced by dried ones, stock becomes bouillon and water, and the chicken becomes freeze-dried or a foil pouch. What survives is the shape of the thing: aromatics, thyme, noodles, meat.

Order matters more than technique here. Dried vegetables rehydrate slower than pasta cooks, so they go in first or you get soft noodles and crunchy carrots. The covered rest at the end is not optional either — it finishes the pasta without burning another two minutes of fuel, which is most of what a stove costs you on a long trip.
