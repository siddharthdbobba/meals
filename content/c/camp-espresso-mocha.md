The source this came from is a gear roundup, not a recipe, so what carried over is the method: boil water in the pot you already have, then use a hand press to turn a small part of it into real espresso. That splits the job cleanly. The stove does the heating, the press does the pressure, and neither needs power.

Cocoa and milk powder are here because espresso alone at 6 a.m. in the cold is punishing. The pinch of salt cuts the chalky edge cocoa powder gets when it meets hot water, and stirring the dry mix into a paste before topping up is the difference between a drink and a mug with lumps at the bottom. Powdered whole milk stands in for the fresh milk you would use at home; skim versions read as watery.
