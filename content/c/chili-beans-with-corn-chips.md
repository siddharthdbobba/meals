The dried chilies and the two-hour simmer are the whole recipe. Ground ancho and pasilla give a deep, raisiny heat that jarred chili powder does not, and the long cook is what breaks the kidney beans down into a sauce thick enough to dry flat. Thin, even leather rehydrates evenly. Chunks stay hard in the middle.

Fat is the enemy of a six-month shelf life, so the oil is blotted off before drying and added back in camp from a dropper bottle. The dark chocolate goes in anyway and stores fine once the surface fat is gone. Corn chips are there for texture: everything else you eat on trail is soft.
