Pork tenderloin is the rare cut that suits a campsite. It is lean, it cooks in half an hour, and it takes an acidic marinade well because there is no fat layer to slow the citrus down. The zest matters more than the juice here. Juice mostly adds acid and tenderizes the surface; the zest carries the oils that survive the fire.

This one keeps its cooler. Raw pork does not go in a pack, so it is a first-night car-camping dinner and nothing else. The rest of the work moves home: mince the garlic and shallot in your kitchen, build the marinade in the bag it travels in, and arrive with nothing to do but manage the fire. Pull at 145F and rest it. Lean meat has no margin past that.
