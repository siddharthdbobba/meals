Standard white gravy is butter, flour, and cold milk from the fridge. On the trail the milk is the problem, so it becomes whole milk powder reconstituted in a mug before it ever hits the pot. Whole matters: the fat is what makes gravy read as gravy instead of paste.

The roux is the only step that needs attention. Flour cooked in fat for a minute loses its raw taste and gains the ability to thicken without clumping, but a camp stove concentrates heat in a small ring and will burn it fast. Low flame, constant stirring, and add the liquid slowly. It exists to make instant potatoes, biscuits, or rice into a real dinner.
