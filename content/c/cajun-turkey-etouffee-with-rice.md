The original is a deep-fried turkey leg sitting in a butter roux, which is two things you cannot do on a stove in the dirt. The turkey becomes a foil pouch, the butter becomes oil, and the roux becomes cornstarch. Cornstarch thickens in under a minute and does not need the twenty minutes of stirring a real roux demands, so nothing scorches while you are watching the light go.

The dried trinity is doing more work than it looks like. Onion, celery, and pepper need a real boil, not a soak, or they stay leathery and the sauce tastes like seasoned water. Give them the full five minutes before anything else goes in. Tomato paste from a tube adds body and a little acid without a can to pack out.
