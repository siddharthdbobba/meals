The source for this was a kombucha recipe, which needs a week of room-temperature fermentation, a live culture, a pH strip, and refrigeration afterward. None of that belongs in a pack. What survives the trip is the tea base underneath it: a black-and-green blend, steeped short and below boiling, carrying a heavy dose of sugar.

The temperature matters more than the timing. Green tea leaf dumped into water at a full boil goes astringent in under a minute, and the black tea makes that worse rather than covering it. Sixty seconds off the heat is enough of a drop. The sugar is not a garnish either — two tablespoons is a hundred-odd calories of fast fuel, which is the actual reason to carry sugar instead of drinking plain tea.
