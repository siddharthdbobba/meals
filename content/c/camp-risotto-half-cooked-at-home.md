steps:
  - "At home: warm 1 tbsp oil in a pan, cook the onion until it goes clear, then add the rice and stir for two minutes so every grain is coated."
  - "Pour in the wine and let it cook away. Add hot water with half the bouillon, a splash at a time, waiting for each addition to disappear before the next."
  - "Stop when the rice has taken about 2 cups of liquid. Cut a grain in half: the outside is soft, the center is still chalky white. That is done."
  - "Spread the rice thin on a sheet pan and cut furrows through it so steam escapes. Cool it completely, then dry it fully in a dehydrator or a 170F oven until the grains are hard and loose."

Restaurants do not cook risotto to order. They stop the rice halfway, cool it flat, and finish it in minutes when the ticket comes in. That same split works on a trail, except the pause between the two halves is days instead of hours, so the par-cooked rice gets dried hard at home rather than refrigerated.

The home step is doing the slow work: the onion, the wine, the patient ladling. What is left for camp is one boil and ten minutes of stirring, which is all a small stove can reasonably manage. Keep it looser than you think it needs to be. The starch keeps setting after the pot comes off the flame, and a risotto that looked right in the pot will be a brick in the bowl.
