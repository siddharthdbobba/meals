Instant coffee earned its reputation in an era when it was made from the cheapest beans available and dried with heat. Freeze-dried specialty instant is a different product, and the failure mode on trail is almost never the coffee. It is the water. Boiling water strips the aromatics and pushes the bitter compounds forward, which is why camp coffee so often tastes like ash.

The pinch of salt is the other lever. Sodium suppresses bitterness on the tongue more effectively than sugar does, so a little salt lets you use less sugar and still get something drinkable. Slurry first, then dilute, or you will find a clot of dry granules at the bottom of the mug.
