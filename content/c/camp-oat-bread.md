  - "Night one: boil water, pour 110 g of it over the oat flour in a mug, and stir to a thick paste. Cover it and leave it out overnight. It will be stiff and slightly sweet by morning."

The scald is the part worth keeping. Pouring boiling water over oat flour the night before gelatinizes the starch, and that gelled starch holds water through the bake. The result is a loaf that stays moist on day three instead of turning to sand, which matters more in camp than it does at home.

Everything else got cut down. The original runs a separate overnight preferment; here the yeast goes straight into the dry mix and the rise happens while you drink coffee. One loaf instead of two, one pot instead of a bowl and a sheet pan. The dough is wet and awkward to handle, so the dutch oven doubles as the pan — it gives the loose dough walls to climb and traps enough steam to get a crust.
