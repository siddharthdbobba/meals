The original is a chef's open-fire ribeye with a cream-and-white-wine jalapeno emulsion and beans simmered in beer for hours. The steak and the emulsion do not travel: one needs a cooler and a grill grate, the other needs heavy cream. The beans do travel, and they were the better half anyway. Freeze-dried beef and precooked bacon carry the same smoke and fat into the pot without refrigeration.

What makes it work is the acid at the end. Beans, barbecue sauce, and taco seasoning all pull the same sweet-salty direction, and after twenty minutes in a pot they flatten out. The vinegar and lime go in last, off the heat, so they stay sharp. The dried jalapeno is seeded on purpose — you want the smoke, not a pot that only two people can eat.
