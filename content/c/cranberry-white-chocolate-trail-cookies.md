Camp cookies fail in two predictable places: the egg and the oven. Powdered whole egg solves the first, since it rehydrates in a cup and travels in a bag instead of a shell. The second is a heat problem, not a recipe problem. A dutch oven with coals split roughly evenly top and bottom runs around a moderate oven, and the bottom scorches long before the tops color if you pile them all underneath.

The cornstarch and the pull time are what make these chewy rather than crisp. A teaspoon of starch softens the flour's structure, and taking them off the coals while the middles still look wet lets carryover heat finish them. White chocolate is fragile in a pack, so keep it in the coldest part and expect some clumping; it melts into the dough either way. Cranberries are dry going in and stay dry, which is the point, since they draw a little moisture out of the crumb and keep the cookies from going gummy overnight.
