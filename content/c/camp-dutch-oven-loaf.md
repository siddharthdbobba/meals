This is ordinary sandwich-loaf bread moved onto coals. Nothing in it needs refrigeration, so the whole dry side of the recipe travels as one bag mixed at home. The only real changes from a kitchen version are instant yeast instead of active dry, so there is no proofing step to babysit, and olive oil instead of butter, which means no cooler.

The dutch oven is doing the work of an oven by trapping steam under the lid, which is what gives you a crust instead of a dry brick. Coal placement matters more than count: most of the heat belongs on top, because coals underneath will burn the bottom long before the middle sets. Rotate the pot and the lid in opposite directions every ten minutes and the loaf bakes evenly even over an uneven fire.
