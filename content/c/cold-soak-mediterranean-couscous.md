Couscous is one of the few grains that hydrates fully in cold water, which is why this works without a stove. It softens by capillary action rather than heat, so the only cost is time in the pack. The chickpeas take longest; give them the full soak or they stay chalky.

The fresh parts of the original are the parts that do not travel. Cucumber turns to nothing when dried, so it is gone. Feta is out too, since soft cheese has no business in a pack in summer. What is left carries the dish: sun-dried tomato and olive supply the salt and acid that fresh vegetables were providing, and the oil goes in at the end so it coats the grain instead of sealing water out of it.
