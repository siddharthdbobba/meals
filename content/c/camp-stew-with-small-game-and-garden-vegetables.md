This is a hunting camp dish, not a backpacking one. The point is the bones. Small game carries a lot of them, and a slow simmer turns that into a broth with real body, which is the only reason the stew tastes like anything before the vegetables show up. Pick the meat off after cooking rather than serving joints, and nobody spends dinner spitting bone fragments into the fire.

The vegetables get split in two on purpose. Carrots and anything dense go in with enough time to soften. Squash blossoms, greens, and ripe tomato go on top at the last second, off the heat, so they stay separate from the long-cooked broth instead of dissolving into it. A Dutch oven on coals holds a steadier low simmer than a stove ever will, which is what this needs for two hours.
