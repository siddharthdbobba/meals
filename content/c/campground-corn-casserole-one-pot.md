The original is a baked casserole: butter, eggs, fresh milk, thirty minutes in an oven. None of that travels. The rebuild keeps the structure and swaps the perishables out. Milk powder and flour make the same white sauce, cheese powder stands in for shredded cheddar, and freeze-dried corn rehydrates in the pot instead of coming out of the freezer. The eggs are gone entirely; they were binding a bake that no longer happens.

The sauce is where this succeeds or fails. Slurry the powders with a little hot water before they meet the pot, or the flour seizes into lumps you cannot stir out. The stuffing crumbs go on at the very end and stay dry that way, which is the only part of the crust texture that survives without an oven.
