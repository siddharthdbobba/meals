Cornmeal is the whole point. Camp chili made with a quart of water runs thin, and you cannot simmer it down for an hour over coals without burning the bottom. Stirring cornmeal in at the end takes fifteen minutes to pull the liquid together, and it adds a faint corn sweetness that plays against the cayenne.

This one stays car-camping food. Two pounds of raw beef needs a cooler, and the canned beans are heavy. The beans go in with their liquid on purpose, since that starch is doing half the thickening before the cornmeal ever shows up. Cut the coals hard after the boil. A dutch oven holds heat long after you pull it off the fire, and chili scorches fast at the seam where the wall meets the base.
