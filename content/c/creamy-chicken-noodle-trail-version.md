The home version leans on a butter-and-flour roux, heavy cream, and a fridge full of fresh vegetables. None of that packs. Potato flakes do the same thickening job without needing to be cooked out, and whole milk powder plus parmesan carries the richness that cream was providing. Freeze-dried chicken replaces the rotisserie bird and rehydrates in the same water the noodles cook in.

Order matters more than technique. Vegetables and chicken need the longest soak, noodles go in second, and the dairy powders go in last with the pot off the burner or they turn grainy and stick. Add the potato flakes gradually. Too many at once and you are eating chicken noodle paste.
