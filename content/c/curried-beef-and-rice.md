The original is a Dutch oven dish: two pounds of hamburger browned over coals, fresh onion, fresh apple and pear, a can of tomatoes, and an hour of raw rice simmering while you stir every ten minutes. All of that is fine at a car camp and impossible on a stove that holds fuel for twenty minutes. So the meat is freeze-dried, the tomatoes are powder, the fruit is dried, and the rice is instant.

The fruit is the part worth keeping. Apple in a curry is not decoration; the sugar cuts the bouillon salt and gives the dish somewhere to go besides savory. Dried apple concentrates that, so a quarter cup does the work of a whole one. The oil matters too. Browned hamburger carried its own fat and freeze-dried beef carries none, which leaves the curry powder with nothing to dissolve into. Two tablespoons fixes it.
