steps:
  - "Premix the flour, cornstarch, and salt at home and carry it in one bag."
  - "At camp, beat the softened butter and powdered sugar in a bowl until pale and light. By hand this takes a solid five minutes and a fork works better than a spoon."
  - "Add the vanilla, then the dry mix. Stir only until no dry flour is left. Overworking it makes the cookies tough."
  - "Chill the dough in the cooler for at least an hour. Cold dough holds its shape; warm dough spreads flat."
  - "Roll the dough into balls about an inch across. Press each one down with the back of a fork and scatter a few sprinkles on top so they bake in."

Shortbread is mostly butter held together by flour, which makes it one of the few baked goods that survives camp conditions. There is no leavening to fuss over, no egg to keep cold, and no rise to time. The cornstarch replaces some of the flour's protein, so the crumb stays short instead of chewy. Powdered sugar does the same job from the other direction: it dissolves into the butter rather than staying gritty.

The chill is not optional. Butter that has been sitting near a fire will spread into one flat sheet in the dutch oven. Coals matter too, and the ratio matters more than the count. Weighting the lid two-to-one keeps heat coming from above, which is what browns the tops without scorching the bottoms. If you are backpacking, bake these at home and carry them; they hold up better in a hard container than most cookies.
