The home version leans on a rotisserie carcass and fresh noodles, neither of which fit in a pack. Bouillon paste does the same job for an ounce, and dried carrot and celery rehydrate in the broth while it comes up to temperature. Butter becomes olive oil, which carries more calories per ounce and does not care how warm your pack gets.

Order matters more than technique. Vegetables need a head start or they stay crunchy; noodles go in late or they turn to paste and thicken the broth into glue. The chicken is already cooked, so it goes in off the heat and just warms through.
