steps:
  - "Cut the shortening into half-inch cubes and set it in the cooler, or in the shade if the morning is cold. Warm fat makes a tough crust."
  - "Dump the premixed flour, sugar, and salt into a large pot or bowl. Add the vinegar to the cold water."
  - "Work the cubes into the flour with your fingertips or two forks, about thirty seconds. Stop while the pieces are still pea-sized and visible."
  - "Add the water a splash at a time, tossing with a fork after each one. Stop the moment the dough holds together when squeezed. You may not use it all."

Pie dough is a temperature problem, not a skill problem. Solid fat left in visible pieces turns to steam in the oven and pushes the flour apart into layers. Warm fat blends into the flour instead and you get a cracker. Everything here is aimed at keeping the fat cold in a place with no refrigerator: cubed early, rested in the cooler, handled fast.

Two changes from a home version. Shortening replaces the two sticks of butter because it stays firm at camp temperatures and does not need the cooler to survive the drive. And the stand mixer is gone, so the fat gets cut in by hand for about thirty seconds. The vinegar is not for flavor. It slows gluten from tightening, which buys some margin if you overwork the dough by headlamp.
