The home version builds a bechamel and stirs tomato into it, which is a lot of steps for a soup. The structure survives the trip intact, though: a flour roux is what keeps the milk from separating when it meets acid, and that matters more with powdered milk than with fresh.

Fresh tomatoes, whole milk, and a clove-studded onion do not travel, so tomato powder, milk powder, and dried aromatics stand in. The order is the only fussy part. Milk and tomato go in off the heat, after the roux has already thickened the water, or the powder clumps and the pot scorches. Croutons get toasted at home in butter and carried dry; frying bread at camp is not worth the fuel.
