The original is a microwave recipe with a fresh egg and a splash of milk, which is fine at home and useless at a trailhead. Powdered egg and powdered milk do the same jobs here: the egg sets the crumb, the milk powder keeps it from tasting flat. Both go straight into the dry premix, so at camp you are adding oil and water to one bag.

Steaming replaces the microwave. Direct flame under a mug of batter scorches the bottom before the middle cooks, so the mug sits in a pot of water and the whole thing cooks at boiling temperature and no hotter. That is slower than three minutes but nothing burns. The baking powder is the one addition the source did not have; without a microwave's fast blast of steam, the batter needs something to lift it.
