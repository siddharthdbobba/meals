The original of this is a cooler recipe with fresh diced potatoes and real eggs. Both are replaced here: boxed dried hash browns rehydrate in ten minutes and brown better than fresh ones do on a camp stove, and powdered egg means no shells, no carton, no worry about heat. Cured chorizo is the one thing not to substitute. It is shelf-stable by design, and the fat it renders is what the potatoes cook in.

The soak-and-drain step is the whole recipe. Potatoes carrying water will sit there and steam gray no matter how hot the pot gets, so press them dry and then give them a few undisturbed minutes on each side. Eggs go in last and off heat quickly, because in a thin camp pot they go from set to rubber in about thirty seconds.
