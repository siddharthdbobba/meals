Tamales are a two-day project and a lot of folding. The dough and the filling are the parts that matter; the husk is a wrapper. Layered in a dutch oven, you get the same masa and the same meat with none of the assembly line, and it feeds six off one lid of coals.

The braising happens at home, where a slow cooker can run twelve hours and a freezer can hold the result. In camp you are only doing two things: beating air into the lard so the dough stays light, and holding the oven at a steady moderate heat. Too many coals underneath scorches the bottom crust before the middle sets, so keep most of the heat on the lid.
