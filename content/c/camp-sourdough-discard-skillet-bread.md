A starter is a fermentation project, not a hobby that fits a backpack, so this is the car-camping and boat-galley version: you keep the jar in the cooler and bake with the discard rather than trying to time a rise around a campsite. Discard has flavor and acid but almost no lift left in it, which is why the baking soda is here. The soda reacts with the sourness immediately and gives you a loaf in half an hour instead of overnight.

Coal placement is the whole game. Most of the heat belongs on the lid, because the pot bottom sits directly on the fire and will burn the crust while the middle is still wet. Rotating the pot and the lid in opposite directions evens out whichever side of the fire is running hotter. No scale, no proofing basket, no thermometer — a cup of discard against a cup and a half of flour is close enough.
