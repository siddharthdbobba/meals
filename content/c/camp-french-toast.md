The original calls for eight eggs and most of a quart of milk, neither of which belongs in a cooler you plan to open on day three. Whole egg powder and powdered milk rehydrate into the same custard and weigh a fraction of it. The ratio is what matters: roughly one part liquid egg to one part milk, plus a spoon of sugar for browning.

Two things go wrong with french toast in camp. The pan runs too hot over coals, so the egg sets black on the outside and stays wet inside — cook it over the edge of the fire, not the middle. And the bread sits in the batter too long. A quick dip coats it; a soak fills it, and a filled slice never firms up. The oil beaten into the batter covers for a skillet you have not oiled well enough.
