The pale rind under a watermelon's skin is mostly water held in a firm pectin structure. Sugar swaps places with that water over a long simmer, which is why the cubes go from chalky white to clear. That swap is also what makes them keep: there is not enough free water left for anything to grow in.

The original is a jar of jam in syrup, which is glass and liquid and not going anywhere in a pack. Drying the drained cubes gets you the same thing at a fraction of the weight and no container to protect. Dry them past tacky and they turn to gravel, so pull them while they still give under a thumbnail.
