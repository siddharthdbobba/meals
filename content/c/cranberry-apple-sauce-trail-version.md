The source of this is a canning recipe: fresh apples, fresh cranberries, a food mill, and a water bath. None of that travels. Dried fruit does the same job in reverse — the water comes back at camp instead of being cooked off in a jar, and the skins that a food mill removes are already soft enough in dried fruit to not matter.

The cranberries are the point. Apples alone go flat and cloying; the tartness gives it structure and means you need very little sugar. Citric acid replaces the bottled lemon juice, which in the original was there for canning safety and is here only for flavor. Watch the heat — a pot of fruit sugar over a canister stove scorches before it boils dry.
