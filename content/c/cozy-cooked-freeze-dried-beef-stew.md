Freeze-dried pouches are engineered for boiling water and a stable temperature, and the second part is where most camp dinners fail. Set an open pouch on granite at 30F and the water is lukewarm in four minutes, which is not enough heat to finish rehydrating diced beef or potato. The result is a bag of crunchy chunks in cold gravy. An insulated sleeve fixes the whole problem for about an ounce of gear.

Cut the cozy short — roughly eight inches once the zip strip is torn off — so your spoon can reach the bottom without your sleeve going in with it. The oil is there because the stew's own fat content is low and calories matter more than gravy does. Potato flakes are insurance: pouches often come out soupier than they should, and flakes take the extra water without diluting anything.
