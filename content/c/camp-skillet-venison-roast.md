A pot roast is a sear followed by a long wet cook at low heat. An oven is one way to hold that heat, but not the only one. A heavy skillet with a lid on the cool edge of a fire does the same job — the lid traps the steam, and the low side of the fire keeps the liquid at a simmer instead of a boil. A rolling boil is what makes lean meat seize up and go stringy.

Venison has almost no fat, so the grease and the onions matter more than they would with beef. The onions break down over two hours and turn the stock into something that coats the meat. This one needs a cooler for the roast and a heat source you can control for a couple of hours, which puts it at basecamp or the truck, not in a pack.
