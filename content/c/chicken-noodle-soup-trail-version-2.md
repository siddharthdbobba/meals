The original is a pressure-cooker recipe built around raw chicken, fresh mirepoix, and homemade stock. None of that travels. Bouillon replaces the stock, dried vegetables replace the mirepoix, and freeze-dried chicken replaces the breast. The flavors survive the swap because they were mostly coming from the aromatics and seasoning anyway.

Timing is the only thing to get right. Dried carrot and celery need five minutes of simmering before they stop being crunchy; thin noodles need four. Start them together and you get hard vegetables in mush. Use fine noodles rather than anything thick, since fuel spent boiling pasta is fuel you carried up the hill.
