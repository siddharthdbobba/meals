Cold weather is a fuel problem before it's a comfort problem. You burn more just staying warm, appetite drops, and drinking plain water in freezing air costs you heat you'd rather keep. Building the meal on hot broth solves both ends: the liquid goes in warm, and the salt makes you actually want it.

The fat is the point. Potato flakes and broth alone are a thin, salty dinner — peanut butter powder and a spoon of ghee are what push it past 700 calories without adding much weight. Powdered milk stands in for anything fresh and thickens the mash at the same time. Eating half at night and half in the morning is easier on a shrunken cold-weather appetite than forcing the whole pot down at once.
