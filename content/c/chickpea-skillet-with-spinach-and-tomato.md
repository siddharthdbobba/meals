Chickpeas want to be dry before anything wet touches them. Drain the can well and give them five minutes alone in hot oil first — the outsides firm up and brown, and they hold that texture even after the tomato goes in. Rush this step and you get a soft pile instead of a skillet.

The original wants fresh spinach and a roma tomato, which means a cooler. Dried versions of both do the same job here: they rehydrate in a mug while the chickpeas brown, and squeezing them out first keeps the pan from turning into a stew. Two tablespoons of oil is not optional — it is most of the calories and the reason the chickpeas crisp at all.
