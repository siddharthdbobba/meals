This is a shore-lunch recipe: it only makes sense when you have fish that came out of the water that day and a vehicle to haul oil in. The sparkling water is doing real work in the batter. Carbon dioxide comes out of solution in the hot oil and leaves the coating open and light instead of dense, and the baking powder pushes the same direction.

Temperature is the whole game. At 350°F the crust sets before the oil soaks in; below that the batter drinks oil and goes limp. Frying three or four strips at a time and waiting for the oil to recover between batches is slower than dumping everything in, and it is the difference between crisp and greasy. Fry oil is a pack-out problem, so bring a sealable jug for the cooled oil and do not pour it in the dirt.
