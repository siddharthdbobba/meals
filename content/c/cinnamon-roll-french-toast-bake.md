This is a home casserole moved outdoors with two swaps. Fresh eggs and heavy cream become powdered egg and powdered cream reconstituted with cold water, so the custard is stable in a pack and you are not nursing a carton of cream at 80 degrees. The canned rolls still need a cooler — there is no shelf-stable version of them that behaves the same way, so this is a car-camping or basecamp breakfast, not a backpacking one.

The butter-and-sugar syrup matters more than it looks. Poured into the custard it coats every cut face of dough, which is what keeps the interior from baking into plain bread. Coal placement is the other half: two-thirds of your heat goes on the lid, because heat from below turns the bottom layer to carbon long before the middle sets.
