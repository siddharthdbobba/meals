Pancakes are usually a fridge recipe: fresh milk, a whole egg, a mixing bowl. All three come out on the trail. Powdered milk and powdered egg rehydrate in the same water the batter needs anyway, so the mix travels dry and shelf-stable, and shaking it in the bag means there is no bowl and no whisk to wash out with drinking water.

Heat control matters more than technique. A camp burner runs hot and narrow, so a high flame scorches the center of each pancake before the middle sets. Keep it low, wait for the bubbles to hold their shape, and flip once. The oil in the batter is not optional either — it keeps them from sticking to a thin backpacking pan and adds calories where you want them.
