The original is a diner sandwich, which means deli meat and a fridge. Neither travels. Foil-packet turkey and dry-cured ham do the same job at room temperature, and a block of hard cheddar holds for days in a pack. The mayonnaise packet stays sealed until you use it, so it is not the liability it sounds like.

The assembly order is doing real work. Cheese goes down first against the bread so it melts from below, and the barbecue sauce sits in the middle where it cannot soak into the crust. Butter on the outside faces is what browns the bread; the pan should be hot enough to sizzle when the slice lands but not so hot that it darkens in under a minute. If you are feeding several people, keep one sandwich browning while the next round of meat heats beside it and you get one out every couple of minutes.
