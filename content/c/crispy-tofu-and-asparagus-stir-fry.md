Cast iron and two pounds of fresh asparagus do not go backpacking. What survives the trip is the sauce and the technique: a soy-ginger-cornstarch slurry that glazes everything in the last thirty seconds, and enough heat that the tofu browns rather than stews. Fresh garlic and ginger become powders, and the asparagus gets blanched and dried at home.

The long soak is the part people skip and regret. Dried tofu that goes into hot oil still hard stays hard, and cornstarch needs the sauce to be liquid before it hits the heat. Squeezing the water back out of the tofu matters just as much as putting it in.
