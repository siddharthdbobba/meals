steps:
  - "Unwrap the bird and set it breast up in the dutch oven or a deep pot. Add salt, pepper, and herbs."

  - "Lid on. Set it over low coals or a stove flame turned down until the surface barely moves. A hard boil makes the meat stringy."
  - "Hold that for two and a half to three hours. Check every 45 minutes and top up water if the bird breaks the surface."

A whole bird is the cheapest protein per pound you can bring to a basecamp, and a covered pot over low coals is more forgiving than a fire-roasted chicken. Three hours at a bare simmer is hard to ruin. Late by an hour and it is still fine, just softer.

The water level does the real work. Just covered means the meat poaches and the liquid reduces into stock worth keeping. Fully submerged in a rolling boil gives you thin broth and dry meat. Shred it all at once and you have chicken for tacos, rice bowls, and cold snacking for the next two days, plus a pot of broth to start tomorrow's dinner.
