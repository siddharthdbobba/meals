The original is a home kitchen chowder built on fresh shrimp, fresh crab, and a fridge full of chopped vegetables. None of that survives a weekend outdoors, so the fresh seafood is gone and canned crab and squid do the work instead. The vegetables come dried, which means they rehydrate in the broth you were already boiling rather than costing you a cutting board and a knife.

The flour is what makes this a chowder instead of soup with things in it. Cooking it in the oil for a minute before the liquid goes in is the step people skip, and it is the reason a pot ends up pasty. Evaporated milk holds up to heat far better than fresh milk and needs no cooler until the can is open, so plan to use both cans in one sitting. This is a heavy meal by weight — four full cans — which is why it belongs at a site you drove or paddled to, not one you walked to.
