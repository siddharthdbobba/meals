This started as a slow cooker roast, which is four hours and a refrigerator you do not have. The pork tenderloin becomes a shelf-stable pouch, the broth becomes bouillon, and the mashed potatoes that were a side dish become the body of the meal. The spice rub survives the translation unchanged, since it was already dry.

The glaze is the part worth the extra pot time. Dried cherries, honey, and vinegar reduce into something with real acid in it, which is what keeps a bowl of starch and salt from tasting flat halfway through. Make the glaze first and hold it aside. If you build the potatoes first they set up while you fuss with the cherries.
