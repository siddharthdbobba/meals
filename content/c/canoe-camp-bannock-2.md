This is trip bread. Flour keeps forever, weighs little, and turns into a hot dinner side on day five when the fresh food is long gone. The two flours are doing different jobs: bread flour brings the gluten that keeps the round from crumbling when you flip it, and all-purpose keeps it tender rather than chewy.

The source dough had no leavening, which works if you want a flatbread. Baking powder is the trail change worth making. It costs almost nothing to carry and it lifts the middle enough that the inside cooks through before the crust burns. Heat control matters more than technique here. Coals, low and steady, with a lid on. A roaring fire gives you charcoal outside and raw dough in the center.
