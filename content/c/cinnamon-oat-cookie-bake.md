Cookies at camp usually fail on the dairy. The original calls for two sticks of softened butter and a fresh egg, neither of which survives a weekend in a pack. Shortening and powdered egg do the same structural job, and shortening is more forgiving of a hot spot in the coals than butter is.

Cream of tartar is the ingredient not to leave behind. It is what gives a snickerdoodle its tang and its chew, and without it this is just an oat cookie. The oats matter for a different reason: they absorb some of the moisture and keep the dough from spreading into one sheet when a dutch oven runs hotter than you meant it to. Pull them early. Coals keep working on the bottoms after you take the lid off.
