steps:
  - "Build a fire early and let it burn down. You want coals, not flame, and you want them an hour before you want dinner."
  - "Dump the dough mix in a bowl. Work in the water, 3 tbsp melted butter, and 1 tbsp oil until it holds together as one ball. Knead a few minutes."
  - "Cover and let it rise somewhere warm, 45 minutes to an hour. Near the fire, not in it."
  - "While it rises, stir the tomato sauce with 1 tbsp oil, sugar, salt, pepper, garlic powder, parsley, and flakes. No cooking needed."
  - "Roll or press the dough flat, smear the softened butter over it, roll it into a log, then coil the log and press it out again. That fold is what makes the crust layered instead of bready."
  - "Oil the Dutch oven and press the dough up the sides about two inches. Line the bottom with mozzarella, then sausage and vegetables, then sauce, then the asiago. Cheese under sauce keeps the crust from going wet."
  - "Lid on. Roughly 8 coals underneath, 16 on the lid. Bake 40 to 45 minutes, rotating the pot and lid a quarter turn every 10 minutes so it does not scorch on one side."

Deep dish is a bread recipe wearing a pizza costume, which is why it works in a Dutch oven and not in a skillet over a burner. The pot is a small oven with heat above and below. Coals on the lid brown the top; the few underneath cook the crust without burning it. Get that ratio backwards and you get a scorched base and raw cheese.

Two swaps off the original. Raw Italian sausage is a cooler liability, so dry-cured sausage does the job with no food-safety math. Low-moisture mozzarella in a block travels better than anything fresh and releases less water, which matters when you cannot drain a Dutch oven mid-bake. The rise is the long pole here, not the bake, so start the dough before you start being hungry.
