Sausage gravy is a roux problem, and the roux is the only step that can actually fail out here. The flour needs a minute in hot fat before any liquid arrives, or you get raw-flour taste and lumps that no amount of stirring fixes. Cook the meat, keep the drippings, add butter to make up the difference, then flour, then liquid — in that order, every time.

The camp change is the milk. Fresh milk is a cooler liability and a spill risk, so this uses whole milk powder reconstituted in the pot. Whole, not skim: the fat is doing structural work, and skim gravy is thin and chalky. Everything but the sausage is shelf-stable, so with a precooked pouch this drops off the cooler list entirely.
