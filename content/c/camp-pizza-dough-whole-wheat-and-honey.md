Whole wheat alone makes a stiff, dry crust that fights you when you stretch it. Half all-purpose fixes that. The white flour has more workable gluten, so the dough rises further and takes the pressing without tearing, which matters more when your work surface is a dutch oven lid.

The honey and the oil are doing two jobs each. Honey feeds the yeast and cuts the raw edge of the bran; oil keeps the dough from gluing itself to your hands, so you add less extra flour and end up with something chewy rather than dense. Nothing in it is perishable, so the dry mix can live in the food box all trip and you decide on pizza night.
