The original is a roux soup: butter, flour, milk, and cream cooked into something that coats a spoon. None of that travels. Potato flakes do the same job with no fat to go rancid and no cold chain, and whole milk powder carries enough fat to read as creamy once it hits hot water.

Order matters more than usual here. Milk powder added to a hard boil will curdle and go grainy, so it goes in after the flame is off. The gnocchi is shelf-stable and already cooked, so four minutes is plenty — leave it longer and you get paste.
