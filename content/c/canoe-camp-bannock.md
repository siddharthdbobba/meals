Bannock is the reason a folding-handle frypan keeps earning its place in the pack. Everything in it is shelf-stable and cheap, it needs no yeast and no rise, and the whole thing is flour plus fat plus heat. The baking powder does the lifting the moment water hits it, which is why you mix it last and get it in the pan soon after.

Heat control is the entire skill. Too hot and you get a black crust over raw dough; the fix is coals rather than flame, or a low simmer setting and a lid to trap heat on top. Powdered milk is not required, but the sugars in it are what give the crust color, and the fat you knead in is what keeps it from going to hardtack overnight.
