The original is a skillet dish built on browning: butter, seared thighs, fond scraped up with brandy. None of that survives a canister stove and one titanium pot, so this version skips the browning entirely and rebuilds the sauce from the parts that actually matter. Those are mustard, Worcestershire, acid, and fat. Bouillon covers the pan drippings well enough at 9,000 feet.

Heavy cream powder is the piece worth carrying. Regular milk powder makes a thin, chalky sauce; the cream powder has enough fat to hold the mustard in suspension and gives the dish its calories. Add it off the heat. Boiled hard, it separates into grease and grit, and there is no fixing that in the field.
