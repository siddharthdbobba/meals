The original version of this is a Thanksgiving project: a gallon of vegetable broth, a gallon of ice water, a stock pot big enough to hold a turkey. None of that travels. A dry brine does the same job with three tablespoons of salt. The salt pulls moisture out, dissolves in it, and gets drawn back in carrying the sugar and aromatics with it. Same seasoned, held-together meat, no bucket.

The pot is doing what the wok did in the source: holding heat steady from below while the lid traps it above. That is why the coal ratio matters more than the fire size. Too much heat underneath and the bottom of the breast is leather before the center is safe. Refresh coals on a schedule and check temperature, not the clock, because a windy camp and a still one cook at completely different speeds.
