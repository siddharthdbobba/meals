The original is a fresh-meat, fresh-tomato chili browned in a heavy kettle over a hard fire. Everything perishable here is dried instead: freeze-dried beef, sun-dried tomato, minced onion, garlic granules. The long simmer is what makes that work. Forty minutes over coals gives dried meat and tomato time to come back and give up flavor to the pot, which a ten-minute boil never does.

The spice line is unusual and worth keeping — oregano and curry powder alongside the chile. Curry powder is mostly cumin, coriander, and turmeric, which is not far from a chili blend anyway, and it darkens the whole thing. Bean flakes at the end do the thickening a real fire-braise would have done by evaporation, so you can pull the pot before the bottom burns.
