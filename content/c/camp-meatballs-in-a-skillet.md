An air fryer runs hot air around all sides at once. A skillet over coals does not, so the meatballs need to be turned and they need room. Give each one a gap and a quarter turn every few minutes and you get the same browned shell without the basket.

The breadcrumbs and egg are structure, not filler. They hold the ball together on a surface that moves and tilts. Dried parsley replaces fresh, which wilts to slime in a cooler within a day, and grated parmesan from a can needs no refrigeration on the way out. This is a first-night meal: raw ground meat has one cold day in it, no more.
