The restaurant version is built on a butter-and-flour roux finished with heavy cream, none of which travels. Potato flakes do the same job in a fraction of the weight, and whole milk powder carries the fat that cream was there for. The result is thinner than a roux soup for the first minute and then sets up, so stop adding flakes before it looks right.

Order matters more than anything else here. Dried vegetables need a real simmer, gnocchi needs almost none, and milk powder needs the pot off the boil or it separates into grit. Cook them in the same sequence the ingredients are listed and it holds together in one pot.
