Grits get overlooked on trail, which is odd, because instant grits hydrate faster than instant rice and about as fast as potato flakes. Five minutes in a bag and they are done. The starch is fine enough that it thickens on residual heat alone, so you boil once and shut the stove off.

Dry milk and cheese are doing the work here. Grits alone are wet starch; the milk powder gives them body and the cheese gives them fat, which is what turns a hot carb into a meal. Use whole-fat milk powder rather than nonfat if you can find it. Freeze-dried cheddar is lighter and keeps indefinitely, but diced shelf-stable cheddar tastes better and melts into the bag properly on trips under a week.
