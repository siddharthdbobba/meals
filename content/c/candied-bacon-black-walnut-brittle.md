steps:
  - "At home: heat the oven to 400F. Lay the bacon on a rack over a sheet pan. Mix the cayenne into the brown sugar and dust both sides of every strip."
  - "Bake 15 to 20 minutes, until the fat has rendered and the sugar has gone dark and sticky. Cool on the rack, then chop into rough pieces."
  - "Line a sheet pan with parchment or a silicone mat and set it next to the stove. Once the sugar goes, there is no time to look for things."
  - "Combine the granulated sugar, corn syrup, water, and bourbon in a heavy pot. Stir until the sugar is wet, then stop stirring. Clip on a thermometer."
  - "Boil to 300F. This takes a while and then happens fast. Molten sugar sticks to skin and keeps burning, so long sleeves and closed shoes."
  - "Off the heat, stir in the butter, walnuts, and chopped bacon, then the baking soda. It will foam up pale. Work quickly."

This is home-kitchen work that produces trail food, not a camp recipe. Brittle is one of the better things to carry: sugar at 300F holds essentially no water, so it does not stale, mold, or care about heat until it starts to soften around body temperature. The bacon is rendered dry and then locked in sugar, which is what makes a pork product shelf-stable in a pack for a week.

Two things matter for the texture. The corn syrup interferes with crystal formation, so the finished candy is glassy instead of grainy. The baking soda hits the hot sugar and throws off carbon dioxide, foaming it full of tiny bubbles — that is what makes it snap instead of shatter your teeth. Toast the walnuts first; raw ones go soft in the candy and taste flat.
