The original runs on fresh fruit and a pressure cooker, neither of which fits in a pack. Dried apples and pears do the same job in reverse: instead of cooking water out of fruit, you cook water back in. The result is thicker than stovetop applesauce because dried fruit only takes up so much liquid, which is the point when you are eating out of the pot you cooked in.

The lemon juice becomes citric acid powder. It is not for flavor so much as for keeping the fruit tasting like fruit rather than like sugar. Cloves are the ingredient to underdo. Half the listed amount is still noticeable and the full amount in a small pot goes medicinal fast.
