steps:
  - "Heat the oil in your pot over medium heat."
  - "Add the venison and salt. Brown it on all sides, stirring occasionally. This takes 5-10 minutes."
  - "Pour in 1000 ml of boiling water and stir in the broth powder until fully dissolved."
  - "Add the bay leaf, dried thyme, rosemary, and garlic powder. Stir."
  - "Add the dried potatoes, carrots, mushrooms, onion, and celery. Stir well."
  - "Bring to a simmer, then reduce heat to low. Cover and let it cook gently for 45-60 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the venison is tender and the vegetables are soft."
  - "Mix the cornstarch with 2 tbsp of cold water until smooth. Stir this into the stew. Simmer for 2 more minutes."
  - "Taste and add salt and pepper as needed. Stir in the dried parsley."
  - "Serve hot."

Venison is lean and needs slow, moist cooking to become tender. Long braising breaks down the connective tissue while the broth stays clean and flavorful. Dehydrated vegetables save weight—they rehydrate in the hot broth, releasing their flavor into the liquid as it builds.

Stew feeds more people per pound of meat than any other dish. The liquid carries fat and flavor far more efficiently than broth alone. Thickening it at the end turns it from a thin soup into something with body that fills the bowl.
