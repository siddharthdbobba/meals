The original is a fridge dessert built on strained ricotta and whipped cream, neither of which survives a day in a pack. Both come in powdered form, and both rehydrate into something close enough. Straining was only ever about removing water, so starting from dry skips the step entirely.

The water amount is the part to respect. Sixty milliliters is a ceiling, not a target, and an overshoot turns the filling into a drink you cannot fix in the field. Add most of it, stir, then dose the rest by the spoon. The ten-minute rest does more for texture than extra stirring does.
