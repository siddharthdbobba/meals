Commercial bone broth powder is priced like a supplement. Made at home it costs whatever the bones cost, which is often nothing. The point of the long cook is collagen: it converts to gelatine, which is why a good batch sets solid in the fridge. That set is your quality check before you ever turn the dehydrator on. Broth that stays liquid when cold will dry to something thin and salty.

Skim the fat before drying. Fat does not dehydrate and it will turn the powder rancid in a pack. Protein per spoonful is a guess by nature; homemade broth varies with how long you ran it and how much skin went in. Nine grams is a fair estimate for a heaped tablespoon of a batch that set firm. The powder is hygroscopic, so a clumped jar means air got in and the clock on it is shorter than you think.
