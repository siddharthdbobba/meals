This is home cooking that happens to suit camp better than a kitchen. Nothing needs refrigeration, nothing needs measuring, and the only skill involved is not letting a pot boil dry. The sugar in the condensed milk browns under heat while the milk proteins break down alongside it, and what comes out is caramel with body rather than syrup.

The unopened can does two useful things: it holds pressure so the milk cooks evenly without scorching, and it means no stirring for two hours. The rule that matters is keeping the can submerged. A can that boils dry gets hot enough to burst, which is why the water level is the only thing worth checking on. Cook the can, cool the can, then open it.

Time is the variable. Pull it early for a pourable sauce, leave it longer for something you can stand a spoon in. Once opened it will not keep in warm weather, so plan on finishing it that night.
