steps:
  - "Burn a fire down to coals. You want a bed of embers, not flames. Set the dutch oven directly on them."
  - "Brown the beef with the onion, breaking it up as it goes. Ten minutes, until no pink shows and the onion has gone soft."
  - "Stir in the garlic, mustard, Worcestershire, salt, pepper, chili powder, and cumin. Thirty seconds, no longer, or the spices scorch on cast iron."
  - "Add the tomatoes with their liquid, the drained beans, and 400 ml of water. Stir the bottom of the pot clean."
  - "Lid on. Pull a few coals onto the lid and let it go 30 minutes, stirring every ten so it does not stick."
  - "Taste for salt. Stir in the mayonnaise if you want it richer and slightly thicker. Eat out of the pot."

Cast iron over coals holds heat far better than it takes it, which is the whole reason this works. Once the pot is hot it stays hot without you feeding the fire, so the chili simmers unattended while the fire dies down. Coals on the lid turn it into an oven and keep the top from going cold.

Mustard and Worcestershire are doing more than they look like they are. Both are acidic and salty, and they give a camp chili the depth that a long stovetop simmer would otherwise supply. The mayonnaise at the end is unusual but sound: it is oil and egg, so it thickens and enriches without needing cream you cannot carry.
