The source of this was an oven-and-Instant-Pot holiday ham, which is a refrigerated eight-pound object and a pressure cooker. Neither travels. What survives the trip to camp is the glaze — juice, honey, and three warm spices — and that glaze works just as well on a vacuum-sealed ham steak, which is shelf-stable until you cut the seal and cooks in a fraction of the time.

Foil is doing the work of the pressure cooker. Sealed, the packet traps steam around meat that would otherwise dry out over coals, and it keeps the honey from hitting bare metal and burning. Keep the heat low and indirect. Sugar on direct flame goes bitter well before the ham is warm.
