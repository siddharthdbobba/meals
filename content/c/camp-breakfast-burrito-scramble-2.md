The original version of this leans on a dozen fresh eggs, a carton of milk, and a bag of shredded cheese, which is three refrigerated things and a lot of shell to pack out. Whole egg powder and milk powder replace the first two and weigh a fraction of what they make. Dried hash browns replace the chopped potatoes and cut the cook time roughly in half, since raw potato in a camp pot takes longer than most people expect.

Order matters more than technique here. Bacon fat and fried potato go in first because the pot needs to be hot and greasy before the eggs land; powdered eggs will weld to a dry pot. Take them off the heat early, since the residual heat in the pot finishes them, and cheese stirred into eggs that are already firm just sits there in lumps.
