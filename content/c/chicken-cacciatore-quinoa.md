Cacciatore is a braise, which is the one thing a backpacking stove is bad at. The workaround is to stop braising and start absorbing: quinoa takes on the tomato, bouillon, and oregano while it cooks, so the sauce ends up inside the grain instead of pooled around it. Twelve minutes covered plus a five-minute rest gets there on less fuel than a real simmer.

The wine, the fresh peppers, and the can of paste all became dry goods. Tomato powder is the important one — it does the job of the can at a fraction of the weight, and it thickens as the quinoa swells. Parmesan and olive oil go in off the heat, where they add fat and salt without splitting.
