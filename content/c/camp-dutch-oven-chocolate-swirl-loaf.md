steps:
  - "Whisk 1 tbsp sugar and the yeast into 1/4 cup warm water. Give it ten minutes. If nothing foams, your yeast is dead and you are making a dense sweet brick."
  - "Stir the flour, remaining sugar, salt, milk powder, and egg powder together in the pot. Add the yeast water, the vanilla, half the butter softened, and enough of the remaining water to pull it into a shaggy dough."
  - "Knead on a clean cutting board or inside a large zip bag for ten minutes, until it stops tearing when you stretch it. Cover and let it rise somewhere warm for 60 to 90 minutes. A closed car in the sun works."
  - "Melt the rest of the butter with the cocoa, chocolate chips, and 2 tbsp sugar in a small pan over low coals. Let it cool until it is spreadable, not runny."
  - "Press the dough into a rough rectangle, spread the filling to the edges, roll it up, then twist the roll on itself twice. Coil it into a greased 10-inch dutch oven. Second rise, 30 minutes."

This started as a stand mixer recipe with fresh milk, four eggs, and a two-day attitude. Powdered milk and powdered egg do the same job here without a cooler, and hand kneading gets you to the same window of gluten in about ten minutes. The dough is enriched, so it is slack and sticky; resist adding flour to fix that or the crumb tightens up.

Heat control is the whole game in a dutch oven. Most of the coals go on the lid because bread wants top heat, and the quarter turns keep one hot spot from scorching a stripe through the bottom. If your fire is uneven, pull coals off the base first, not the lid.
