The original is a flour-roux chowder with fresh potatoes and a cup of cream, which is three things a pack cannot carry. Potato flakes do all of that work at once: they thicken like the roux, they taste like the russets, and they go from dry to done in the time it takes to stir. Powdered whole milk supplies the fat the cream was there for.

Bacon bits and olive oil are not garnish. Bouillon and corn are mostly water and salt, so without added fat this lands closer to soup than dinner. Add the potato flakes slowly — you can always thicken further, but there is no un-thickening once it goes to paste.
