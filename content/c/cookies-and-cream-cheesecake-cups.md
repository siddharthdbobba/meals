Cheesecake is mostly fat, sugar, and something acidic holding it together, which means it survives being turned into powder better than most desserts. No-bake cheesecake mix already contains the gelling agents that let it set without heat; the milk and cream powders put back the fat that got carried out with the cream cheese and whipping cream.

The original used a mixer and a fridge. Neither is available, so the water goes in slowly and cold, and the mix does the setting. Butter powder in the crumbs is the one substitution worth explaining: melted butter is what binds a cookie crust, and a spoonful of powder plus a splash of water gets close enough to press into the bottom of a mug.
