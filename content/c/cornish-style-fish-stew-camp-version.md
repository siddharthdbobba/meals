The original is a chef's dish built on a fish counter: live mussels, razor clams, whole gurnard. None of that travels. What does travel is the structure underneath it, which is a tomato and fennel broth with seafood dropped in at the last minute. Tins and pouches carry that structure fine.

The order matters more than the ingredients. Everything in a tin is already cooked, so it goes in at the end and only warms through. Build the broth first and give it real time, because dried leek and sun-dried tomato need ten minutes of simmering before they stop tasting like packaging. The nduja toast is the part worth the trouble: the cured pork fat melts into the bread, and the cold crab on top is the whole point of the contrast.
