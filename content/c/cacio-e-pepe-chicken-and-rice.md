The original browns bone-in thighs and finishes the pan in an oven. Neither travels. Freeze-dried chicken and a bouillon-backed simmer get to the same place over one burner, and the thighs were mostly there for fat and salt anyway, which the oil and cheese cover.

Arborio is doing the structural work. It sheds starch as it cooks, and that starch is what lets a cup of dry cheese turn into sauce instead of a clumped mat. Two things protect it: stir often, and add the pecorino off the heat. Cheese dumped into a boiling pot separates and never comes back.
