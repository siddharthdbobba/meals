The original is a Romanian preserve: whole cornelian cherries, roughly their weight in sugar, boiled down over an afternoon and skimmed as the foam rises. That is a kitchen job with a canning shelf at the end of it. This is the camp version. Dried fruit replaces fresh, which removes the reason for the long boil and cuts the sugar, since dried cherries are already concentrated. Twenty minutes on a stove instead of two hours.

The acid matters more than it looks. Fresh cornelian cherries are sharp enough to balance that much sugar on their own; dried ones have lost some of that, so the lemon powder puts the edge back. Without it the jam tastes flat and only sweet. There is no pectin here and none needed — the fruit skins and the reduction do the thickening, and the pot will look too loose right up until it cools.
