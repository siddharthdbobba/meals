Rabbit is lean enough to go dry and tough if you just throw it on the grate. The buttermilk soak fixes both problems: the acid loosens up the connective tissue in the legs, and the coating that dries onto the meat afterward holds the crust on through the fry. Powdered buttermilk is the only version of this that makes sense away from a kitchen, and it reconstitutes well enough that nobody will know.

The original is an air fryer recipe. Over coals you are shallow-frying instead, which means the oil temperature is your job, not a machine's. Keep the skillet on a steady coal bed rather than open flame, work in batches, and give the pieces room. Crowding is what turns fried rabbit into steamed rabbit with a wet jacket on.
