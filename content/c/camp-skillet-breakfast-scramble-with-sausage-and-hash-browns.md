The original is an oven casserole with a dozen fresh eggs, a bag of frozen potatoes, and two and a half cups of milk. None of that travels. Egg powder and dried hash browns carry the same meal at a quarter of the weight, and powdered milk replaces the dairy that made the baked version set.

The order matters more than the ingredients. Potatoes get rehydrated and browned first because they take the longest and taste like paste otherwise. Eggs go in last over low heat, since powdered eggs turn rubbery faster than fresh ones do. The cheese melts under a lid off the heat, which is the closest you get to a browned casserole top without an oven.
