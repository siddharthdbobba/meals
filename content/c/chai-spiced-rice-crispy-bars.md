Marshmallow is a sugar syrup that has already been whipped and stabilized, which is why this works on a camp stove with no thermometer and no fresh dairy. You are just re-melting it. The honey adds a little invert sugar so the slab stays chewy instead of hardening into candy overnight, and the butter keeps it from gluing itself to the pot.

Heat control is the only real skill here. A canister stove on full throttle puts all its output through a two-inch circle of pot bottom, and scorched marshmallow tastes burnt through the whole batch. Lowest flame, constant stirring, cereal off the heat. Keep the finished bars dry — condensation off a cooler lid softens the cereal faster than time does.
