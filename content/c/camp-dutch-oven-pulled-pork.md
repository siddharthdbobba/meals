This started as a slow cooker recipe, which is a machine most camps do not have. A dutch oven does the same job for the same reason: low, even, enclosed heat over hours, so the collagen in a shoulder has time to turn to gelatin instead of squeezing water out of the muscle. The trade is that you have to feed it coals. Set a rough timer and stop opening the lid.

The vinegar is not there for flavor alone. Half a cup is enough liquid to keep the bottom of the pot from scorching once the onion collapses, and the pork releases plenty more fat and juice as it goes. No added water, so the sauce reduces to something worth eating instead of pale broth. Browning first is optional if you are short on coals, but the crust is where most of the smoked paprika ends up.
