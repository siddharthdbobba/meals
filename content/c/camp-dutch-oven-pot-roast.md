Pot roast is a camp meal disguised as a Sunday dinner. A dutch oven buried in coals holds a low, steady temperature better than most home ovens, and chuck is the cut that wants exactly that: three hours of moist heat to break down the connective tissue into something you can pull apart with a spoon.

The two rules are browning and coal management. Searing the meat first is where the color and most of the flavor in the liquid comes from, and skipping it leaves you with grey beef in salty water. Coals on the lid matter as much as coals underneath — heat from above keeps the top of the roast cooking instead of steaming. Add the vegetables late or they turn to mush an hour before the beef is done.
