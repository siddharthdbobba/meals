This is a two-ingredient cocktail, which is why it survives being made outdoors. The cassis goes in first for a reason: sparkling wine will not stir without losing its carbonation, so the pour has to do the mixing for you. Heavy syrup on the bottom, light wine on top, and the wine mixes itself on the way down.

The changes from the home version are about packaging. Splits instead of a 750, a decanted bottle instead of hauling the cassis, and freeze-dried raspberries instead of fresh ones you foraged that morning. The dried berries rehydrate in the glass over a couple of minutes and hold their shape better than fresh fruit that has been in a pack all day. Cold matters more than anything else here — this is flat and cloying at ambient temperature.
