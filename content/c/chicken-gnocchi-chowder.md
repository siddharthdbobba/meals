The original is a roux soup: butter, flour, milk, and cream, cooked in a kitchen with a fridge. On trail the fat and the dairy both come from whole milk powder, and the roux gets flattened into a single flour-and-powder slurry whisked in off the heat. That order matters. Flour dumped into hot liquid clumps, and a lumpy pot on a canister stove does not recover.

Gnocchi is the reason to bother. Vacuum-sealed potato gnocchi is shelf-stable and cooks in about four minutes, so it does the work of a starch and a thickener at once while the flour handles the body. The dried mirepoix will never taste like sauteed vegetables, but it puts something other than salt in the broth.
