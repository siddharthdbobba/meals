The home version simmers ten cups of broth for half an hour and cooks the pasta in a second pot. Neither of those is happening on a canister stove. So the broth comes from bouillon, the vegetables come dried, and the pasta cooks in the same water it will be eaten in. That last change is the reason this works at all: the starch the pasta sheds gives the broth some body it would otherwise lack.

Water is the variable to watch. Six hundred milliliters sounds like a lot for one pot, but dried carrot and macaroni both drink hard, and you want soup at the end, not paste. If it looks thin at minute ten, leave the lid off. If it looks like a casserole, add another splash of hot water.
