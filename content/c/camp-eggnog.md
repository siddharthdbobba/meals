The original is a custard: yolks blended with honey, hot milk tempered in a spoonful at a time, then cooked back down in the pan. None of that survives a trip. Fresh eggs and a half gallon of milk are not going in a pack, and tempering by headlamp with one spoon is a way to make scrambled eggs.

Powdered whole egg and whole milk powder do the same job dry. Because both go in cold and rehydrate together, there is no tempering step at all — the egg comes up to heat with the milk instead of hitting it. The only real rule is temperature. Hold it under a boil and it thickens smooth; let it rip and the protein sets into curds. Stir the paste first, keep the flame low, and pull it the moment it steams.
