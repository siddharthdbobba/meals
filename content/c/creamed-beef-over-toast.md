This is a roux and a white gravy, which means the only thing that can go wrong is the milk going in too fast. Flour and fat cooked together for a minute lose their raw taste and thicken four cups of liquid without any dairy that needs a cooler. Whole milk powder does the work of fresh milk and weighs a fraction as much, and ghee replaces butter because it sits in a pack at 80 degrees without turning.

The beef is preserved by salt, not by cold, so it rides fine until you cut the seal. That is also why the recipe holds back on salt until the end. Ten minutes of simmering pulls the salt out of the meat and into the gravy, and by then it usually needs nothing.
