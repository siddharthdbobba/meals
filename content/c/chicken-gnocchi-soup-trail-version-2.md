The original is a restaurant soup built on a butter-and-flour roux, fresh dairy, and cubed raw chicken. None of that travels. The roux becomes potato flakes, the milk and cream become powders, and the chicken comes precooked in foil. Vacuum-sealed gnocchi is the one heavy item worth carrying: it is shelf-stable, cooks in three minutes, and gives the bowl something to chew on.

Order matters more than technique here. Vegetables need real simmer time or they stay like gravel, and dairy powder clumps if it hits a rolling boil. Take the pot off the heat before the powders go in, and thicken last so you can stop when it looks right.
