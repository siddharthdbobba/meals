A crawfish boil leaves you with a pile of peeled tails and a fridge, neither of which exists at camp. Canned or vacuum-packed tails get you the same meat off the shelf, and the boxed dehydrated hash browns stand in for the frozen bag. Both changes cost you nothing but a rehydration step.

The drain matters more than anything else here. Potatoes that go into the pan still holding soak water will never crisp, and the whole dish turns into a gray scramble. Ghee replaces the butter because it sits at 90 degrees in a car all day without turning. Crawfish goes in at the very end for the same reason you would not boil it twice.
