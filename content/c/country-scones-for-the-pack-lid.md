This is home baking that happens to travel well, so nothing about it was adapted for camp — it was adapted for the pack lid. A scone is dense, low in moisture, and structurally rigid, which is why it survives being crushed under a rain shell while a muffin turns to crumbs. The whole recipe happens in your kitchen. On the trail you are only unwrapping foil.

Two things decide whether this works. Cold butter rubbed in coarsely leaves fat pockets that steam apart in the oven and give the crumb something to hold onto. And cutting straight down without twisting leaves the sides open, so the scone lifts evenly and splits clean by hand later. The savory version with cheddar and mustard is the more useful one on hot days — it puts salt and fat back in when a sweet snack would just sit in your stomach.
