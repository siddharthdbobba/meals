Most camp cobblers are a cake mix dumped over pie filling and left to sort itself out. This one builds the syrup first, which is the part worth doing. The cornstarch cooks out over direct heat and gives you a thick base instead of fruit sitting in sugar water, so the dough bakes on top of something solid rather than sinking.

Coal placement is the whole game. Most of the heat goes on the lid, because the topping needs to bake from above while the bottom only needs to stay hot. Too many coals underneath and the syrup scorches before the dough sets. The two premixed bags mean the only measuring in camp is water, and that one is by feel.

Canned peaches replace fresh here, and the butter and whipped topping other versions call for are gone. Neither survives a warm crate, and the cobbler does not need them.
