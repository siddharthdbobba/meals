steps:
  - "Build a fire and let it burn down to coals. You want steady heat, not flames."
  - "Set a 10 or 12 inch cast iron skillet over the coals and cook the bacon until the fat renders and the edges crisp, 8 to 10 minutes. Pull the bacon out and leave about a tablespoon of grease behind."
  - "Cook the shallot in that grease until it goes soft and clear, 5 minutes."
  - "Whisk the milk powder into 1 1/4 cups cold water in a bowl or wide-mouth bottle. Add the eggs, salt, nutmeg, and cayenne and whisk until it is one color."
  - "Stir a cup of the cheese and the shallot into the eggs, then pour it into the skillet over the bacon."
  - "Cover with a lid or foil, rake a shallow bed of coals under the skillet, and shovel a layer on top of the lid. Bake 25 to 35 minutes."
  - "Scatter the rest of the cheese on top for the last five minutes. It is done when the center stops sloshing and a knife comes out clean. Let it sit 10 minutes before cutting."

A quiche is a custard that happens to have a crust. Drop the crust and you lose nothing but the pastry work, which is the part you do not want to do on a picnic table. What is left is eggs, dairy, fat, and cheese set slowly in cast iron, and cast iron over coals does that job as well as an oven does.

The half & half becomes powdered whole milk and water, which sets nearly the same and does not need to survive a warm cooler. Bacon fat carries the flavor, so render it first and cook the shallot in it rather than starting clean. Coals on the lid matter: heat from below alone browns the bottom before the middle sets.
