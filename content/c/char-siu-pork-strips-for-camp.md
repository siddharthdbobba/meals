This is a home-oven job that pays off later. Char siu is one of the few cooked pork preparations meant to be eaten cold, which makes it unusual among camp proteins: no reheating, no pot, no stove. Hanging the strips over a water pan is the part worth doing right. It cooks them from all sides and stops the sugary marinade from scorching into the roasting pan.

The sugar and honey are doing double duty. They lacquer the outside during the roast and they keep the meat from tasting flat once it is cold. This still needs a cooler and it is a day-one or day-two food, not shelf-stable — for a long trip, dry the strips into jerky instead and carry the same flavors at a third of the weight.
