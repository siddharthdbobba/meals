The original is a tandoor bread built on milk and whole-milk yogurt, neither of which survives a day in a pack. Milk powder does the same job: it puts sugar and fat into the dough so the crust browns and the crumb stays soft. Olive oil replaces the butter, in the dough and in the finish.

The rise is the only real constraint, and it costs you nothing if you start it when you get to camp and cook when the fire is down to coals. Direct heat is the point. A grate over coals chars the outside faster than the inside dries, which is why the bread puffs instead of turning into a cracker.
