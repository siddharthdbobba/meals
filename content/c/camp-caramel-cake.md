The original is a butter cake with a browned-butter frosting, which is three refrigerated ingredients and a stand mixer. The trail version keeps the part that carries the flavor and drops the part that needs a fridge. Caramel syrup is the whole point here, and it is also the only fussy step, so it happens at home where you can watch the color and start over if it seizes. Once bottled it keeps for months and weighs less than the sugar that went into it.

Shortening replaces the butter and milk powder replaces the cup of milk. Both are structural, not just substitutions of convenience: fat and liquid are what make this crumb tender, and shortening creams cold while butter at camp is either soup or a rock. The glaze is straight confectioner's sugar and syrup, so it needs no heat and sets as the cake cools. Salt it harder than feels right. A pound of sugar and no salt tastes like nothing at all.
