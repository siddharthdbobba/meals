Marinated eggs are an old trick in a lot of places, and the reason they belong on a trip is preservation, not novelty. A hard-boiled egg on its own is a two-day item in a cooler. Sitting in a strong vinegar brine, it holds for weeks, because the acid does the work a refrigerator would otherwise have to do.

Everything here happens at home. In camp there is no stove, no pot, and no water — you open a jar. The brine matters more than it looks: half vinegar keeps the pH low enough to be worth doing, and the garlic, ginger, coriander, and chili only reach the middle of the egg after a day or two, so make them well before you leave. Keep them submerged and keep them cold. Eggs that have been sitting out of the brine are the ones that go wrong.
