This is what a pan of M&M bark turns into when there is no oven, no fridge, and no pan. Chocolate is the only thing doing structural work, so it gets melted low and slow and then pulled early. White chocolate has a lower scorch point than most people expect, and a camp stove has one setting more than you want.

The bag is the mold. Flattening it thin matters more than how long it sits, because a quarter-inch slab firms up in cold water in about the time it takes to clean the pot, while a thick lump stays soft into the night. The M&M's go on last and unmelted, which is the whole point: their shells are the only part of this that survives contact with heat.
