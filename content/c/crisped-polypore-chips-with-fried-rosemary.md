This is a moisture-removal problem, not a browning problem. Firm polypores are most water, and if you cook them fast the water steams out around them and you get rubber. Low heat and a long stir drive the water off gradually, and what's left is concentrated mushroom that crisps in the oil it's sitting in.

Age matters more than technique. Only the outer couple of inches of a young cap are worth slicing; past that the flesh is tough enough to be genuinely unpleasant. The rosemary goes in last because it crisps in seconds and turns bitter not long after. Nothing here keeps, so this is a first-night camp snack, cooked from what you carried out of the woods that day.
