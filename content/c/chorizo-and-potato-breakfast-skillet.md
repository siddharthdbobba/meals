Chorizo is primarily fat and spice. When you cook it, that fat renders into the pot and seasons everything else—you don't need to figure out five different spices because they're already built into the sausage. The vinegar and salt that preserve chorizo don't fade when it dries, so rehydrating brings back the full flavor.

If you make chorizo at home from ground pork, ancho chiles, coriander, cumin, and the other traditional ingredients, cooking it through completely and then dehydrating the crumbles gives you a shelf-stable ingredient that costs half what store-bought does. The vinegar is the preservative; the spices are why it tastes like chorizo rather than just spiced meat.
