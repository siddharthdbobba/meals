This is a fried recipe, so it belongs at a car camp or basecamp where you can carry a quart of oil in and carry it back out. Nothing here needs a cooler. Fresh milk, eggs, and butter all have powdered or clarified versions that hold at ambient temperature, and shelf-stable hot dogs are close enough to the refrigerated ones once they are inside a fried crust. The flour dredge before the batter dip is the part people skip; without it the batter slides off the casing and you get bald spots.

Oil temperature is the whole game over a fire, and you cannot dial a fire down. Cook over coals rather than flames, and use the pan position on the grate as your control: closer for hotter, off to the side to recover. Two inches of oil in a small pan gives you enough depth to float a corn dog without hauling a gallon jug. Plan where cooled oil goes before you start, because pouring it on the ground is not an option.
