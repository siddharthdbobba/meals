The soup packets are doing two jobs: they season the broth and they thicken it, which is what keeps this from tasting like noodles in salt water. The bouillon underneath gives the base something to stand on, since one packet spread across a liter is thin.

Chicken and pepper go in off the heat on purpose. The chicken is already cooked and boiling it just makes it stringy, and the pepper keeps some bite that way. On trips longer than a day or two, drop the fresh pepper for dried flakes — everything else in the pot is already shelf-stable, and the pepper is the only thing that will go soft in a pack.
