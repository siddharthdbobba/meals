The original is a party dish built around fresh chorizo, an oven, and a fridge. Two swaps make it a camp dinner instead: dry-cured Spanish chorizo, which is shelf-stable and gives up the same red fat, and shelf-stable chicken added at the end rather than seared and baked. The onion and garlic go in dried, since neither is doing anything a powder cannot do once the paprika and saffron arrive.

Arborio is the reason this works over a fire. It absorbs a lot of liquid and stays forgiving about uneven heat, so a hot spot on one side of the grate gives you a crust rather than a ruined pot. Leave it alone while it cooks. Stirring releases starch and turns the whole thing into risotto, which is a different dinner.
