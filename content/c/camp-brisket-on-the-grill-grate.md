This is a car-camping recipe and it does not pretend otherwise. Seven hours of low fire, a cooler, and a probe thermometer are not backpacking gear. What makes it work in camp is that almost all the labor happens at home: trim, rub, wrap, and the brisket rides in the cooler doing its own dry brining overnight.

The two-stage heat is the point. The first two hours at low temperature let smoke settle on the surface while the meat is still cold. After that, 250 F is just a slow oven, rendering the fat cap from the bottom up. Sauce goes on at the end because sugar burns long before the brisket is done. Rest it, or the juice ends up on the board instead of in the slices.
