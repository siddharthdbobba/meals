The original is a baked custard: eggs, cream, a water bath, an hour in the oven, four hours to set. None of that travels. Cornstarch does the same job as the eggs without needing gentle even heat, and milk powder replaces the cream, so the whole thing happens in one pot over a burner you can hold your hand near.

The flavors are what carry it, and they are all shelf-stable already: cocoa, strong coffee, cinnamon, almond cookies. The amaretti go in dry and soften from below, which gives you the same soaked-cookie layer the baked version gets from the bottom of the mold. Whisk the powders into cold water before the pot goes on heat, or you will be picking out cornstarch lumps by headlamp.
