The source this came from is not a recipe at all, it is an argument for owning a thermometer. That argument is worth a recipe. Camp cooking is the worst possible place to judge doneness by feel: the light is bad, the coals are uneven, and the meat has been sitting in a cooler at an unknown temperature all afternoon. Poking a chop tells you very little. Sticking a probe in it tells you everything.

The two-zone fire and the early pull do the rest. Sausage and bone-in chicken need more minutes than steak does, so they get the cooler side while the chops take the heat. Pulling three to five degrees short of target sounds like a fussy detail, but a thick chop keeps climbing for several minutes off the fire, and that is the difference between juicy and grey. Chicken is the one number not to negotiate with: it should read 74C after the rest.
