A dutch baby is a popover in skillet form. It rises because the batter is thin and the pan is screaming hot, so the water in it flashes to steam and shoves the whole thing up the walls before the eggs set. That mechanism is what dictates the fussy parts of this recipe: preheat the empty oven, get the coals right, and pour the batter in one motion. Get lazy about any of those and you get a dense pancake instead.

The camp version swaps whole egg powder and powdered milk for the fresh originals, which is a fair trade because both are just liquid and protein here, not structure. Premixing the dry side at home matters more than it looks like it should, since lumps in the batter turn into wet pockets in the finished pancake. The cheese and bacon go on late so they melt rather than sink and weigh down the rise.
