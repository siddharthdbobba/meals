This is a long, low braise, which is exactly the kind of cooking a cast iron dutch oven over coals does better than anything at home. The acid from the orange and lime, plus the sugar, breaks down the connective tissue in the shoulder while the fat renders out and becomes the frying medium for the final crisp. Nothing here needs refrigeration once it is in the pot, and there is only one pot.

The prep is the part that belongs at home. Cubing four pounds of pork shoulder on a picnic table is unpleasant and the spice rub takes five containers. Do both in your kitchen, freeze the seasoned meat flat, and the camp version is sear, pour, wait. The one thing worth watching is the last twenty minutes: the liquid has to actually reduce or you get boiled pork instead of carnitas.

The lager is not gluten-free unless you buy a beer that says so. If the tag matters, use the water-and-citrus version in the variations.
