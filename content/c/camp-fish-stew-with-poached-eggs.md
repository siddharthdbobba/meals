This is a lake-shore recipe, not a backpacking one. Everything in it is heavy and half of it lives in a cooler, so it belongs at a car camp or a paddle-in site where the pot can sit over coals for an hour while people do other things.

The broth carries the whole dish. Frying the tomato paste in bacon fat before the liquid goes in is what turns it from thin tomato water into something with a bottom to it, and the potatoes release enough starch over the simmer to give it body. The fish goes in last and whole, because stirred fish becomes shreds. Eggs poached in the broth at the end are the part that makes it this stew and not any other one: they set into the surface, take on the pepper and the smoke, and stretch eight servings out of two pounds of fillets.
