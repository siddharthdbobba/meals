  - "Brown the meat in the hot oil. Two or three minutes, turning once. Pull it off the heat while it is still pink in the middle; it finishes in the bake. Leave every bit of the oil in the pot."

This is a camp casserole built around whatever came out of the brush. Grouse breast is lean and dries out fast, so it gets a quick sear and then finishes gently in the rice, surrounded by liquid. Browning first is not about doneness. It is about the fond left in the oil, which is most of the flavor in the finished pot.

The original used fresh sour cream and a half gallon of milk, which is a cooler problem. Powdered milk and sour cream powder do the same job and travel in a bag. Ratio matters more than technique here: three cups of dry rice needs roughly five cups of liquid plus what the soup brings, and the lid has to stay shut for the whole bake or the steam that cooks the rice walks off into the fire.
