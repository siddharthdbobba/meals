This is a two-ingredient dessert that lives or dies on heat control. Chocolate seizes and burns well below the temperature a camp fire likes to run at, so the pot goes at the edge of the coals, never over them. Ten minutes of low heat does the job with no stirring; stirring early just breaks the crackers into powder and you end up with chocolate gravel.

The original called for adding water if the mixture got too stiff to stir. Skip that. Water is what makes melted chocolate seize into a grainy paste. If it is not moving, it needs another minute of warmth, not liquid. Powdered milk or a little oil are the honest fixes.
