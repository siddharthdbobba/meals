The restaurant version leans on a butter-and-flour roux, whole milk, and heavy cream. None of that travels. Whole milk powder covers the dairy and most of the fat, and potato flakes do the thickening job the roux was doing, without needing you to cook out raw flour on a stove you cannot control.

Order matters more than technique here. Milk powder goes in off the heat, because a rolling boil will break it into grit. The gnocchi releases its own starch while it simmers, so go easy on the flakes at first and add more only if it is still thin.
