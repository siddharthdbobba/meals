The original is a can of sweetened condensed milk, butter, and fresh milk, none of which survive a weekend without a fridge. Powdered whole milk plus sugar plus a fat rebuilds the same thing dry, and you add the water back at camp. Boiling the water separately matters: chocolate seizes if it heats faster than it melts, so you want a warm pot and a slow trickle of liquid, not a splash of cold water into hot chocolate.

Low heat is the whole game. Chocolate scorches on a canister stove in under a minute at full blast, and there is no rescuing burnt chocolate. If your stove will not simmer, pull the pot off the flame entirely and let residual heat do the work while you stir. Four people can finish 8 oz of chocolate; two people will regret trying.
