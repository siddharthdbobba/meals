The original bakes raw rice under bone-in thighs for an hour, and the bones and skin are doing most of the work: fat renders down into the grain while it absorbs broth. None of that survives a backpacking stove. So the fat comes from a tablespoon of oil, the broth comes from bouillon, and the chicken arrives already cooked in a pouch and gets stirred in at the end instead of roasting on top.

Instant rice is the other required swap. Regular white rice wants twenty minutes of simmering and a lot of fuel; the parcooked kind finishes off the heat under a lid, which is the whole reason this is one boil. Rehydrate the dried onion and greens in the water first, before the rice goes in, or they stay chewy and the rice goes soft around them.
