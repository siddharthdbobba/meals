The coffee-shop version simmers fresh milk in a saucepan and froths it. On trail the milk is powder and the froth is whatever you can beat up with a spoon, but the important part carries over: steeping the chamomile in milk instead of water. Fat holds the floral compounds that water lets escape as steam, so the tea tastes like more than warm water with a smell.

Two things keep it from failing. Mix the powder into hot-but-not-boiling water off the heat, or it clumps and welds to the pot. And the salt is not optional in a sweet drink at altitude, where everything tastes flatter than it did at home.
