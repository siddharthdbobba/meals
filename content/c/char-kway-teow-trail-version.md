This dish is built on wok hei, which needs a screaming hot wok you will not have in camp. So the recipe leans on the other two things that make char kway teow work: rendered sausage fat and dark soy. Cook the sausage first and leave the fat in the pan. Everything after that is cooking in it.

The substitutions are all about the cooler. Fresh shrimp become dried shrimp, which rehydrate in ten minutes and bring more concentrated flavor than the fresh ones did. Fresh eggs become powder. Onions become flakes. The one thing worth keeping intact is the char step: get the pan hot, drop the noodles, and do not touch them. A minute of stillness is the whole difference between this and warm noodle salad.
