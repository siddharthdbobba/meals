A Dutch baby rises on steam, not leavening, which is why the pan has to be screaming hot before the batter lands. That part translates to a fire fine. What does not translate is the oven, so the lid plus coals on top does the job an oven ceiling normally does — without that top heat you get a thick crepe.

The original wants fresh milk and eggs. Powdered whole milk and powdered egg get you close and remove the cooler from the equation entirely. Resting the batter still matters: the flour needs those 15 minutes to take on the water, and a thin batter climbs the pan better than a lumpy one.
