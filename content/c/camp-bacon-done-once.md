Bacon over a campfire fails the same two ways every time: flames instead of coals, and a pan the cook keeps poking. Coals give steady heat that renders the fat out before the meat scorches. Leaving the strips alone lets that fat pool and fry them properly, which is what a stovetop pan does for you at home.

The rendered fat is the actual point. A pound of bacon yields most of a cup, and that carries the rest of the weekend's cooking: potatoes, eggs, a skillet cornbread. This one needs a cooler and ice, so it is a car camping or basecamp breakfast. On foot, carry precooked crumbles instead and accept that you get no fat out of them.
