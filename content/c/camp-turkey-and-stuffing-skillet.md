The source here was a holiday food-safety warning, not a recipe: thaw the bird properly, never cook poultry below 325 F, check three spots for 165 F, do not stuff it the night before. None of that translates to a camp kitchen, so this takes the meal and drops the hazard. Shelf-stable pouched turkey is retort-cooked and sealed. There is nothing to thaw, nothing to hold at temperature, and no raw juices to keep off your cutting board, which you do not have anyway.

The stuffing is the structure. Boxed mix is engineered for exactly this — boiling water, a fat, five minutes covered — so it rehydrates without a stove running underneath it. Oil replaces the butter the box wants. Mix the gravy in your mug rather than the pot, or the starch seizes against the hot metal and you get lumps you cannot stir out.
