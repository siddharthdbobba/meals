The original is a roux soup: oil, flour, a slow whisk, and twenty minutes of simmering diced potato. None of that survives contact with a canister stove. Potato flakes do the thickening the flour and the simmer used to do, and they do it in thirty seconds, so the whole thing collapses into one boil.

Milk powder replaces the two cups of fresh milk, which is the part that would otherwise keep this off the trail. The cheese goes in off the heat and in stages — dumped all at once into a boiling pot it seizes into strings and leaves a greasy slick instead of melting in. Oil is there for calories; the soup works without it but it will not hold you through the night.
