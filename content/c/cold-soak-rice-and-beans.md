Store-bought rice sides cold-soak fine for about three weeks, then the preservative flavor starts registering on every bite and lunch becomes a chore. Drying your own rice and beans fixes that for roughly a dollar a serving. The tradeoff is thirteen hours of dehydrator time at home for something you assemble in twenty seconds on trail.

The beans have to be dried hard, not leathery, or they will still be gritty after three hours in the jar. Canned beans are already cooked through, which is the whole point — you are only removing water, so cold water puts it back. Rice does the same thing, but it needs the clumps broken up during drying or the center of each lump stays wet and never really rehydrates. Salt goes in the bag rather than at camp because it dissolves along with everything else.
