This one is a car-camping dinner and stays that way. A whole raw chicken needs a cooler and a thermometer, so there is no honest trail version of it. What makes it work at a campsite is that the fire does all of it: no pot, no pan, and the foil is the only thing you throw away.

The half-full can is a heat sink more than a flavor trick. Liquid inside the cavity holds at boiling while the outside takes direct radiant heat from the coals, so the breast finishes before the skin turns to carbon. The foil wrap does two jobs — it keeps ash out and it traps enough steam to even out the hot side. Rotating at the 45-minute mark matters more than anything else here, because coals never heat a bird evenly.
