Canned chickpeas are 60 percent water and a can weighs a pound, so the whole recipe happens at home and only the solids get carried. Cooking the spices in oil before the tomatoes go in matters more than it sounds: the flavor compounds are fat-soluble, and a curry built with spices dumped into liquid tastes flat no matter how long it simmers. That step survives dehydration. Fresh lemon does not, so citric acid stands in for the acid at the end.

Chickpeas rehydrate slower than anything else in the pot, which is why they get spread thin and dried hard rather than left whole and soft. Ten minutes covered off the heat is usually enough; twelve is safer in the cold. The tablespoon of oil at the end is not a garnish. Without it this is a low-fat dinner with a long walk after it.
