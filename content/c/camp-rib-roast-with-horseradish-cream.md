This is a home holiday roast moved outdoors, and the parts that had to change are the dairy. The original leans on whole milk, eggs, and sour cream, none of which belong in a cooler for three days. The Yorkshire pudding is gone with them, since it needs flour, eggs, and an oven that holds a real temperature. What is left is the roast itself, which cast iron over coals handles well, and a horseradish sauce rebuilt from powder.

The cook order is reversed on purpose: low heat first, sear last. Coals are uneven, and searing at the end means the crust forms after the interior is already where you want it, instead of burning while you wait. Coal counts are a starting point, not a rule, so rotate the pot and check the meat rather than the clock. This is a car-camping dinner, one night out from the store, and it is not cheap.
