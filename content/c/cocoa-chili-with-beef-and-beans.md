The original is a pressure cooker recipe built around soaking dry beans, which is a non-starter on trail. So the beans get cooked soft and dehydrated at home, and the pot at camp is only rehydrating them. Same for the beef: brown it, rinse the fat off, dry it. Fat is what turns dehydrated meat rancid, so it goes back in as olive oil at the end instead of riding in the bag for a week.

Cocoa powder sounds like a mistake and is not. A teaspoon adds bitterness and depth that reads as long-simmered, which matters when the actual simmer is ten minutes. Salt goes in at home rather than at the start of cooking for the same reason the source holds it back: salted water tightens bean skins. Tomato powder replaces the can and drops most of the carried weight, since a can of tomatoes is mostly water you can add for free at camp.
