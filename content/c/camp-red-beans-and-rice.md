The original is an eight-hour slow cooker dish built on dry kidney beans, and dry beans are a non-starter on a stove that runs on a fuel budget. Instant refried bean flakes do the same job in two minutes: they rehydrate into something creamy and hold the seasoning, which whole beans never manage without long simmering. The rice cooks in the same pot, in the same water, so nothing gets drained.

The smoke has to come from somewhere. Hard smoked sausage is shelf-stable until you open it and renders enough fat to coat the whole pot, and browning it dry first is what pulls that fat out. Smoked paprika backs it up. Go easy on the creole seasoning — between the sausage and the bouillon there is already plenty of salt, and you can always add more.
