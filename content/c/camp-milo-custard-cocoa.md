The original called for two liters of fresh milk, which is a cooler problem nobody needs. Whole milk powder does the same job at a fraction of the weight and rides in the same bag as everything else. Everything here is a dry powder, so the whole drink becomes one premixed bag and one pot of water.

The custard powder is the reason this is worth making instead of just stirring Milo into hot water. It is cornstarch with vanilla and color, and it gives the drink body that a plain cocoa never has. The heat rule matters: starch thickens below a boil and thins out above it, and milk powder scorches fast on a thin camp pot. Keep it steaming, keep the spoon moving, take it off early.
