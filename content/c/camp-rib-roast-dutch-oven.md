This is a car-camping showpiece, so it stays a real roast rather than a trail substitute. The one change that matters is timing: the overnight salt cure happens at home in the fridge, and the roast rides to camp on ice. A dry brine needs cold and about twelve hours, and a cooler on day one gives you neither reliably.

The dutch oven replaces the 450F oven, and the heat goes on the lid rather than under it. That is what keeps the bottom vegetables from scorching while the meat browns from above. A thermometer is not optional here. Coal beds vary too much for a clock, and this is an expensive piece of meat to overshoot.
