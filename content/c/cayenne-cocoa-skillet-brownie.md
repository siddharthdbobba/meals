Cayenne and cinnamon are here for structure, not novelty. Cocoa is bitter and the sugar load is high, so a dessert like this reads flat once it cools. The heat arrives at the back of the throat a second after the chocolate and gives the whole thing an ending. A quarter teaspoon is enough to notice and not enough to argue about.

The outdoor risk is scorching, not underbaking. Coals under a cast iron pan run far hotter than an oven floor, so most of them belong on the lid. Sifting the dry mix at home matters more than it sounds: cocoa clumps, and unsifted cayenne clumps worse, which is how one person gets a mouthful of pepper. Butter and eggs need a cooler, so this is a car-camping dessert unless you go the powdered route.
