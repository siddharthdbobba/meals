The source is a fresh emulsified sausage: ground chicken, cold cream, curing salt, and a stuffing step, all of it needing refrigeration and twelve-hour rests. None of that survives a trip. What is worth keeping is the seasoning ratio, which is a good one, and the coarse-thigh-plus-fine-breast grind that gives the meat body.

So this is the same mixture cooked loose in a pan. Milk powder plus a little hot water replaces the cream and does the same job of holding fat and moisture in the meat rather than letting it render out. The curing salt, antioxidant, and emulsifier are gone; they exist to make a stable sausage that sits for days, and this pan gets eaten in twenty minutes. Ketchup with more curry stirred in is the part the dish is actually named for, so it stays.
