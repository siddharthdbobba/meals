Burgoo is a stew defined by its method, not its list. The traditional pot takes whatever game is left over at the end of a season, three or four kinds of meat, and cooks them down for hours in a tomato broth until nothing is identifiable anymore. That last part is the point. The long simmer is what turns a pile of odds and ends into one thing.

The trail version keeps the structure and swaps the freezer for the pantry. Canned chicken, cured sausage, and jerky give you the three-meat character without a cooler, and each contributes something different: the chicken shreds into the broth, the sausage holds its shape and leaks fat, the jerky rehydrates into chewy strands and carries smoke. Cans of vegetables are heavy, which is why this is a car-camping recipe. The vinegar at the end is not optional. Four cans of vegetables and a lot of cured meat go flat and sweet without acid to cut it.

Cook time is 90 minutes at a bare simmer. Shorter and the jerky stays leathery.
