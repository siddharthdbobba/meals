The original is a Norwegian weeknight pot: onion, carrot, pepper and chili softened in oil, spices bloomed, two cans of tomatoes down, then beans and chickpeas. Nothing in it needs meat, and almost nothing in it needs a refrigerator once the water is gone. Canned beans dry well on trays at 125°F overnight, and they rehydrate far faster than dry beans ever will. Tomato powder is what replaces the cans and saves about two pounds of water weight per pot.

The spices go in with the water rather than into hot oil, which loses a little depth. You get most of it back by simmering longer and adding the oil at the end, off the heat, where it stays in the sauce instead of cooking off. The lime is not garnish here — a pot of beans and tomato goes flat without acid, and lime powder weighs nothing.
