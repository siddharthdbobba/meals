Coffee and cocoa are here for color and bitterness, not flavor you can name. They push the broth dark and give it a floor that plain bouillon does not have. The porcini soaking liquid does the same job from the other direction — it is the strongest ingredient in the pot, which is why the grit at the bottom of the mug matters enough to leave behind.

The original is a restaurant braise with fresh stock, butter, and grated horseradish. On the trail the stock becomes bouillon, the brewed coffee becomes instant, and the butter goes away because it will not survive the drive. The flour dredge stays: it browns on the meat and thickens the stew later, so one ingredient does two jobs. The meat is the only cooler item, and it needs the full 45 minutes at a real simmer to stop being tough.
