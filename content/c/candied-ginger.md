steps:
  - "At home: peel the ginger and slice it into thin coins, about an eighth of an inch. Thicker slices stay woody in the middle."
  - "Cover the coins with plain water in a pot and boil ten minutes. Drain. Repeat once. This pulls out enough heat that you can eat more than one piece."
  - "Return the ginger to the pot with 2 cups of water and 1 cup of sugar. Simmer at a low bubble for 40 minutes, until the syrup thickens and the coins turn translucent."

This is home cooking that happens entirely before you leave. What you carry is the finished product: sugar-cured ginger that shrugs off heat, cold, and a week of getting crushed in a hipbelt pocket.

The two blanching boils matter more than the simmer. Raw ginger is aggressive enough that one piece is the limit; boiling and draining twice knocks the burn down to something you can chew on for an hour of switchbacks. The sugar does double duty, sweetening it and pulling water out so nothing molds. Chew one when altitude or a swaying canoe starts working on your stomach.
