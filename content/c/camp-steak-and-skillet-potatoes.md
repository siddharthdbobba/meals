This is a brasserie dish rebuilt around one pan and a fire. The original deep-fries the potatoes in a quart and a half of oil, which is a bad idea in a campsite: it is heavy to carry, dangerous to move once hot, and impossible to dispose of responsibly. Half a cup of oil in a hot skillet gets most of the same crust if you keep the sticks moving and give them the full 25 minutes.

Order matters. Potatoes first, steak second, in the same fat. The pan is already seasoned with starch and oil by the time the meat hits it, and the steak's rest time is exactly long enough to keep the potatoes from going cold. Thick steaks are worth the money here because coals run hot and uneven, and a thin cut is grey through before the crust forms.
