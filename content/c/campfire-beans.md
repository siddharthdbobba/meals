Dried beans are the thriftiest protein in camp: a pound costs a dollar and feeds half a dozen people. The catch is time. At home you'd use a slow cooker. At camp, you use a gentle fire and patience. A low, steady simmer for two hours softens hard beans and lets the bouillon, turmeric, and ginger work in. The quick boil-and-drain step isn't essential but cuts cooking time and reduces intestinal complaints.

You need a pot large enough to stir and add water as it simmers down. On a campfire, this meal only makes sense if you're staying put for the evening. For backpacking, you would need to pre-cook and dehydrate beans at home, which is more work than most thru-hikers will do.
