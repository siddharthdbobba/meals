This is a stove recipe more than a camp recipe. A windscreened burner with a real low setting will hold a simmer, and a simmer is the only thing that matters here — the beans need ten quiet minutes to take on the sausage fat and the tomato. Crank it and you get scorched paste on the bottom of the pot and raw-tasting beans on top.

Everything in it sits on a shelf at home. Dry-cured sausage does not need a cooler, tomato paste in a tube survives being opened, and the can supplies both the beans and most of the water, so you are not hauling extra. The dried onion is doing more work than it looks like: rehydrated in the fat, it gives the dish the sweetness a fresh onion would, without the fridge.
