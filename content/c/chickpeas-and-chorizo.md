Tapas menus are mostly small plates built on cured meat, olive oil, and paprika, which is convenient: none of those need refrigeration. This is the chickpea-and-chorizo end of that menu, scaled to one pot and one boil.

The chickpeas are the part that needs work at home. Dry chickpeas will never soften on a canister stove, so start from canned ones, rinse them, and run them through a dehydrator until they rattle. They come back in about eight minutes of simmering. Tomato powder does the job of a sauce without carrying water, and the chorizo fat is what makes the whole pot taste like more than beans.
