The original is a fridge-and-freezer soup: frozen vegetable mix, a can of tomatoes, fresh onion and celery. All of that dries well, which is the whole reason this one travels. Tomato powder and bouillon rebuild the broth, and dried celery does more work than its weight suggests.

Dry shelf-stable tortellini is the part to get right. Refrigerated tortellini cooks in three minutes and spoils in a day; the dried kind sits in a pack for a month and wants closer to seven. Give the vegetables their eight minutes first, then the pasta, or you get soft pasta and hard carrots. The oil goes in off the heat because that is where the calories are.
