The original is a double-crusted Easter pie built on drained ricotta, which needs a refrigerator and two hours of hanging cheesecloth. Neither travels. Milk powder and egg powder rehydrated in a mug do the same job of binding the filling, and a biscuit lid replaces the pastry without a rolling pin or a cold slab to work on.

Provolone and fontina are the point. Cubed rather than grated, they stay as distinct melted pockets instead of turning the whole thing into one uniform mass. Venison is lean enough that it will dry out on its own, so the fat from the salami and the cheese is what keeps the filling together. Bottom heat browns the meat; lid heat bakes the biscuit, which is why most of the coals go on top.
