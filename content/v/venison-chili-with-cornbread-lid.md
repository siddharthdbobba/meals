The original bakes chili in a dish and pours cornbread batter over it, which needs an oven and a fridge full of cans. Neither travels. Dehydrating the cooked chili at home cuts most of the weight and all of the cans, and the cornbread turns out to cook fine as a steamed lid on a covered pot. It comes out denser than oven cornbread, closer to a dumpling. That is the trade.

Low heat and a sealed lid are the whole technique. Too hot and the chili underneath burns before the batter sets; the flame should be barely on. The freeze-dried corn and milk powder replace the can of cream corn and the half cup of milk, and they carry the same job: moisture and body in the batter. The sugar is halved from the source, on the theory that a sweet chili topping is a matter of taste and yours may differ.
