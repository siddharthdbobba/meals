The original is a layered casserole: fried plantain sheets, sofrito-cooked meat, cheese, eggs, all baked in a dish. None of that survives a campsite, so the layers get folded into one skillet instead. The plantains still get browned separately, because that step is where their sugar caramelizes and it will not happen if they go in with the sauce.

The sofrito becomes dried onion and garlic bloomed in the meat juices. It is not the same, but a food processor and a bunch of cilantro are not coming on this trip. The raisins, olives, and capers do most of the actual work here anyway — sweet, salty, and sharp all landing on lean meat that has no fat of its own to carry flavor.
