This is a camp translation of a cast-iron wok dish. The fresh produce is the part that does not travel, so broccoli and mushrooms come dried and get a ten-minute soak first. Save that soaking water — it carries the mushroom flavor and gives the cornstarch something to thicken.

The sear matters more than anything else here. Venison is lean enough that a slow pan turns it grey and tough, so the pot has to be genuinely hot and the meat has to go in dry and in batches. Two minutes total, then out. The sauce goes in last and off high heat, because cornstarch that boils hard breaks back down into liquid.

Meat in a cooler makes this a day-one or day-two meal, not a resupply staple.
