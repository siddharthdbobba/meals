steps:
  - "Build a fire and let it burn down, or light the grill. You want a hot bed, not flames."
  - "Rub the steaks with the cumin-coriander mix and the chipotle grind. Wrap each one in bacon and pin it with a toothpick."
  - "Set a skillet on the grate. Melt the butter, stir in the flour, and cook it a minute until it smells like toast."
  - "Add the dried onion, garlic powder, cumin, and bouillon, then pour in 350 ml of hot water a little at a time, stirring so it does not lump."
  - "Whisk in the powdered milk and the drained chiles. Let it thicken at a low simmer, then pull it to the edge of the grate."
  - "Grill the steaks over the coals, four to five minutes a side. Pull them at medium rare; venison is lean and goes to leather fast."

Backstrap is the leanest cut on the animal, which is why it gets bacon and why it comes off the fire early. The bacon bastes it while it cooks and the ten-minute rest keeps the juice in the meat instead of on the cutting board.

The original sauce runs on heavy cream and fresh-roasted poblanos, neither of which survives a weekend in a cooler. Powdered whole milk whisked into a butter-and-flour roux gets close, and canned roasted green chiles are already peeled and charred, which saves you the broiler you do not have. Cook the flour before the liquid goes in or the gravy tastes like paste.
