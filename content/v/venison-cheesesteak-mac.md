The original is an oven casserole with two cups of milk, a cup of heavy cream, and four cups of grated cheese. None of that travels. Whole milk powder and cheese powder rebuild the sauce dry, and a spoonful of flour stands in for the body the cream used to provide. The starch left in the undrained pasta water does the rest of the work, which is why you never pour it off.

Soaking the venison and the dried peppers separately matters. Jerky dropped straight into boiling pasta stays leathery, and dried bell pepper needs a full fifteen minutes before it reads as vegetable instead of confetti. Add the cheese powder off the heat; boiled cheese powder goes grainy and will not come back.
