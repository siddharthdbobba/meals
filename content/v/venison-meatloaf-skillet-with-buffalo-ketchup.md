Meatloaf is a bad camp dish on paper: an hour of oven heat, a loaf pan, a fridge full of binders. Most of that is solvable. Cooked flat in a covered skillet with coals on the lid, it heats from both sides and finishes in about the time it takes to eat the appetizer. Pressing it thin instead of shaping a loaf is the whole trick.

Venison has almost no fat, which is why this needs help. The bacon goes into the mix rather than draped on top so its fat ends up throughout the meat instead of pooling on the surface. Eggs, parmesan, and milk-soaked bread hold it together without a pan. Powdered milk replaces fresh here because it does the same job to the bread and does not take up cooler space. The glaze goes on in two passes so the sugar in it does not scorch over direct coals.
