Shanks are the cut nobody wants at home and the right cut for a camp fire. They are mostly bone and connective tissue, which means they need hours of low wet heat before they give up anything — and hours of low wet heat is what a coal bed does well. Brown them first. The crust is where most of the flavor in the finished broth comes from.

The barley cooks separately and goes in at the end on purpose. Left in the pot for five hours it turns to paste and soaks up all the liquid, and you end up with wet grain instead of soup. Cooking it on its own keeps the grains whole and lets you decide how thick the finished pot gets. This is a car-camping or basecamp dish: heavy, cooler-dependent, and worth the fuel only when you have the time and the fire to spend.
