The raw rice is the whole structural trick. It swells inside each meatball as it simmers, which both binds the lean venison and keeps it from packing into a dense lump. That means the meatballs need real simmer time, not a quick poach, and it means you should not stir the pot like you mean it.

This is a car camp or basecamp dinner, not a pack-in one. It needs a cooler for the meat and egg, a big pot, and over an hour of steady heat. The changes from a home kitchen are small: bouillon instead of cartons of broth, dried parsley and oregano instead of fresh, and the meatballs go straight from the mixing bowl onto a plate rather than into a fridge. Skip that rest and they still hold, as long as you drop them into liquid that is barely moving.
