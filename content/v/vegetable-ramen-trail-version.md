The original simmers a stock for twenty minutes, strains out the aromatics, and finishes with fresh vegetables and a soft egg. None of that survives a pack. Dried kale, beans, cabbage, and onion rehydrate in the broth instead of being fried separately, and the aromatics stay in the pot because straining in the field means a second container and a wasted ounce of liquid.

The sweet soy is the part worth carrying. Bouillon alone gives you salt and not much else; ketjap manis adds sugar and body, which is what makes this read as ramen rather than as instant soup. Cook the noodles last and short. They keep absorbing after the stove is off, and a pot left standing turns into a solid block.
