Venison has almost no fat, so the whole plan is to keep it out of the pan. Thin strips, a hot skillet, two minutes a side, then pull it. The corn starch coat helps a little by giving the surface something to brown before the inside overcooks.

The citrus is doing two jobs. Ponzu and lime in the marinade tenderize and cut the gaminess, and the mandarin segments go in at the end unheated so they stay intact instead of collapsing into the sauce. Canned mandarins travel better than fresh tangerines and drain dry, which matters when your pan is already thin on liquid. Dried mushrooms and freeze-dried spinach replace the fresh produce and their soaking water becomes the sauce base.
