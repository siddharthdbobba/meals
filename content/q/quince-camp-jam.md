Quince is hard and astringent raw, which is why it almost always ends up cooked in syrup. Dried at home it becomes trail food: the cubes rehydrate in about ten minutes and the sugar goes in at the end, once they are already soft.

The peel and cores carry most of the pectin, so simmering them in a bag is what makes the syrup set instead of staying watery. Skip them and you get sweet fruit in sugar water, which is fine but not jam. Adding the sugar late matters for the same reason a canning recipe adds it late: sugar in the pot early firms the fruit and it never gives up.
