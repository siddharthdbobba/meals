  - "At home: put the onion or garlic in the bottom of a clean quart jar, add the salt, and pack in raw cubed meat to within half an inch of the rim. Add no water. Wipe the rim, set the lid, and finger-tighten the ring."
  - "Process in a pressure canner at 10 lb for 90 minutes with a weighted gauge, adjusting pressure for your altitude. A dial gauge and higher elevations need more pressure, so check your canner's chart. This is the step that makes the jar safe at room temperature, and it is not optional."
  - "Let the canner depressurize on its own. Check that every lid has sealed before the jars leave the counter. Anything that did not seal goes in the fridge and gets eaten that week."

Raw-pack canning works because the meat is never really cooked twice. It goes in the jar cold and cures under pressure for ninety minutes, which both cooks it through and sterilizes the contents. The meat also gives up enough liquid to cover itself, so you add no water at the start and end up with a jar of broth that is already most of a sauce.

The cornstarch and potato flakes are camp additions. Jar broth is thin and salty on its own, and the two of them turn it into something with body that will not slosh out of a bowl. Ninety minutes at proper pressure is the whole safety margin here. Cutting it short, or skipping the altitude adjustment, is how a shelf-stable jar becomes a botulism problem.
