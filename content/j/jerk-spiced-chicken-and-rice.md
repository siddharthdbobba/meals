Jerk is normally wet work: fresh garlic, onion, and hot peppers blitzed into a marinade, then hours on a fire. None of that survives a pack. What does survive is the spice half of the equation, which is where most of the character lives anyway. Thyme, clove, nutmeg, cinnamon, and pepper carry fine as powder, and garlic and onion powder cover the aromatics without a cutting board.

The wet ingredients get cut down to four small carries: honey, soy sauce, oil, and lime. Honey balances the pepper, soy replaces the salt-and-savory depth a long marinade would have built, and the lime keeps the whole thing from tasting flat and heavy. Bloom the spices in boiling water before the rice goes in. Dumped on at the end, ground spice tastes dusty.
