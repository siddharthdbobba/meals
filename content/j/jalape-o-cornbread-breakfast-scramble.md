The original is a baked casserole built on day-old cornbread, a pound of sausage, and a dozen fresh eggs. None of that travels. What survives the translation is the structure: a starch that soaks up egg, fat from meat and cheese, and enough jalapeño to keep it from tasting like beige powder.

Cooking the cornbread batter into crumbles first is the part worth doing carefully. Dumped in raw, the mix turns the eggs to paste. Cooked out and broken up, it stays as distinct bits with body, which is what the stale cornbread was doing in the oven version. Low heat throughout — powdered eggs go rubbery fast, and cheese powder scorches on a hot pot wall.
