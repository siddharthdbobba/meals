Jerk is normally a wet marinade with onion, scallion, and chiles blended into a paste. None of that keeps in a pack, so this is the dry version: the same spice backbone — allspice, thyme, cinnamon, nutmeg, ginger — carried premixed and bound to the meat with oil instead of a puree. The oil matters more than it looks. Squirrel has almost no fat of its own, and without a coat it goes stringy over coals.

Coals, not flame, is the other thing to get right. A dry rub this heavy on cinnamon and sugar-adjacent spice burns fast over open fire, and burnt allspice tastes like ash. Instant rice on the side soaks up the drippings and stretches four small carcasses into four real dinners.
